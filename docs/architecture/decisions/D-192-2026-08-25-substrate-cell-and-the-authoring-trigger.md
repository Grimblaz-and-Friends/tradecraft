## Decision

**The substrate standards get a cell, `substrate`, and take the text-mode reference with them** — `skills/authoring/references/text-mode.md` at `da2e11b`, now `skills/substrate/references/text-mode.md`. `skills/authoring/references/routing.md` supplies the test and answers it: *"A piece is its own skill when it has an independent trigger: a situation where it should fire without the parent job underway... Where no cell serves it at all, the answer is a new cell — the burden sits on cramming rather than on creating."* A session writing `tools/lint.py` is not authoring a governing document, and no cell on the roster — read at `da2e11b`, all eight — fired for it; `persist-changes` is the nearest and governs committing, not writing.

**The scope widens, and that is a meaning change rather than a relocation.** From *code a cell carries* to the code this practice ships and runs. The two guards already enforce that scope for the **text-mode** rules; for the other four the warrant is the same 11 of 15 files and not a mechanism, which is why [#195](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/195) carries the two shipped files the widening leaves in violation of it.

**`authoring`'s description is re-derived, not trimmed to fit.** Two triggers were subsets of a third and fold into it: *"writing or revising a skill's description"* and *"deciding whether a skill's prose should move behind a pointer"* become *"including a description alone or what sheds behind a pointer"* on the writing clause, which also absorbs *"revising governing prose"*. The code job is stated as a non-trigger in the excluding form the cell's own standard permits — the job, not the cell, because the sideways-dependency guard refuses the reserved name form between siblings. Landed: **632 of 700, headroom 68**, against the affirmed bar of 61. That bar is **not** derivable: the merge-base description carried **six** trigger clauses measuring `[54, 41, 61, 62, 24, 33]`, whose median is 47.5 bare and 52.5 prefixed, and no five-element subset has median 61. The verdict is unaffected — 68 clears every candidate. Its body landed at **6,469 of 7,359, headroom 890**, against a bar of 387 — the median of its twelve bullets, which the review confirmed is unaffected by the basis error above, bullet lengths being per-line.

**`AGENTS.md` repoints two citations at no change in size.** The calling-contract line and the substrate line move from the `authoring` cell to the `substrate` cell; the two names are nine characters each, so a doctrine file that had five characters of headroom at `da2e11b` absorbed the edit exactly. The review's fixes then rewrote that line's enforcement claim twice and compressed it, so the file lands at 5,987 with headroom 13.

**`substrate` gets no body budget.** [D-184](D-184-2026-08-25-outflow-and-the-always-on-total.md) holds that a cell absent from `CELL_BODY_BUDGET_CHARS` is unbudgeted on purpose: a number chosen for a cell nobody has argued about is a ruling on its size arriving as a constant. Its description landed at **555** and its body at **1,686**, both measured after the review's fix batch.

**The outflow produced one move and one refusal, and the price is stated rather than absorbed.** The move is the second of the three and is this change's whole content — a rule that binds only inside one activity goes to that activity's cell. The refusal is `AGENTS.md`'s CRLF clause, which #186 placed there: `substrate` fires for a session *writing* a script, and that clause is for a session confused by ` M` against an empty diff after *running* one, so moving it would strand the reader it was written for. **A ninth name-and-description pair is a net addition to the always-on surface**, and `skills/authoring/references/routing.md` is explicit that a body-to-body move is not a reduction. Measured by `python tools/figures.py` after the review's fix batch: the adopter's total goes **9,747 → 10,253, +506**; this repository's **15,753 → 16,258**; `authoring`'s own pair falls 699 → 641 while `substrate`'s is 564. The argument for paying it is that a rule nobody loads costs the same in bytes and delivers nothing — the surface's job is to fire, and for 11 of 15 Python files these rules did not.

**One affirmed check does not do what it says, and is recorded rather than quietly relied on.** Criterion 4 names `python tools/lint.py`'s citation check as settling that any moved `[D-N]` still resolves. It cannot: `check_doctrine_citations` iterates `("AGENTS.md", "CLAUDE.md")` by a decision its own docstring records, and both moved markers are in a cell — the review probed `[D-186]`→`[D-999]` in `skills/substrate/SKILL.md` and got a green lint, against a `doctrine-citation` finding for the same substitution in `AGENTS.md`. Both markers do resolve; what is false is that the green says so. The guard's scope is an owner-level decision and stays as it is; the claim is what was wrong.

**The cell was scoped to this practice's own code, and that is the review's correction rather than this change's design.** As first written, `substrate`'s Purpose claimed *"the code this practice ships and runs"* while its Audience reached *"a repo that adopts the practice"* — so **Stdlib-first Python** shipped as a standard for an adopter's own scripts, which is `skills/authoring/references/routing.md` run backwards: the shipped standard stating the local application while `AGENTS.md` derives the local application from it. The panel found it, and the repository's external reviewer found it independently as its highest-priority comment. The remedy is split across two sites because the collision was: the Audience line now says an adopting repository's substrate is its own choice, and the rule itself says the pick is this practice's own. **This narrows the inversion rather than removing it** — the cell and `AGENTS.md` now state the same pick twice with different referents — and the durable answer, stating the standard generally so the language pick lives only where it is made, is [#197](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/197). The description's *"or a dependency"* trigger went in the same batch: the rule behind it was cut before commit, so the trigger was charging always-on cost for a question the body does not answer.

**An affirmed acceptance criterion was amended after the fact, by the owner, and that is recorded here because it is the kind of thing a later reader would otherwise reconstruct wrongly.** Criterion 3 read *"a session deciding where a rule about how code is written belongs is not routed to `authoring`"*. The landed design violates it and should: `authoring` owns routing, `substrate`'s description disclaims *where a rule belongs*, and the first experience session's consumer routed **through** `authoring` and correctly landed its rule in `substrate`. Meeting the criterion literally leaves code-rule routing unowned, or puts `skills/authoring/references/routing.md`'s discriminator in two cells — the two-half-owners shape `authoring` bans and which already costs [#196](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/196). The review put the fork to the owner argued rather than editing the affirmed record; he ruled that the criterion names the code-**writing** job and that the disambiguating clause lands. `authoring`'s description now reads *"not for how code is written, though where such a rule belongs is here"*.

**Its outflow is two deletions, and neither is one of the three moves.** The clause costs 41 always-on characters. Paid by dropping *"(adversarial review's job)"* — a cell-naming aside this cell's own standard says is unnecessary, since a description excluding a neighbouring job states the job rather than the cell that holds it — and by trimming the blurb's *"enough to survive review"*, which is not a trigger. Stated plainly rather than dressed as a move: the surface still shrank, 632 → **622**, so the description lands smaller than it was before the amendment and headroom rises 68 → **78**, against criterion 2's bar of 61.

## Rejected

- **A `references/` file inside `authoring` plus a code trigger on its description** (fork 1, owner-ruled). Cheaper on the always-on surface by roughly the difference between a trigger clause and a whole pair, and it fixes the body budget the same way — but it makes `authoring` a two-job cell whose purpose header has to widen from prose to prose-and-code, and it answers the routing rule's independent-trigger test by declining to apply it. A session writing `tools/lint.py` would still have to fire a cell called *authoring* to learn how.
- **Adding one trigger to the full string.** This is what #186's terminal stage refused and filed #188 over. At 10 characters of headroom the trade is not an edit; it is a deletion chosen under end-of-batch pressure, which is how [D-184](D-184-2026-08-25-outflow-and-the-always-on-total.md) already recorded losing D-141's recognisability examples.
- **Raising `CELL_BODY_BUDGET_CHARS`.** It answers neither the trigger gap nor the ceiling and ratifies the cell's size by editing a constant — the shape D-184 created that guard to make visible.
- **Ratcheting the 700-character ceiling in this change.** Measured at `da2e11b`, it binds exactly one cell: the other seven descriptions are 592, 590, 588, 529, 511, 497, 308. Ratcheting it inside the change that resizes the one cell it binds would pin the ceiling to a number this change chose for itself. That ground is this entry's own and **not** D-184's: the review found that D-184 ratcheted `AGENTS_BUDGET_CHARS` inside the very change that spent 112 characters of that file, and its margin note calls the resulting tightness *"the ceiling doing its work rather than an argument against it."* The landed headroom is on the PR so a later change can ratchet against a settled tree.
- **Re-expanding the calling-contract reason that #186 compressed to `[D-156]`.** A cell body carries no budget, so the pressure that forced the compression is gone — but the compression was itself a lawful outflow move, and undoing it because room appeared is churn. **The landed rule does not honour this in full, and the review caught it:** #186 left zero reason clauses, and this change restored one, so the bullet is a 0→1 restoration rather than a retention. It stands, on `skills/authoring/references/revising.md`'s rule that a rule moves with its reasons; what is corrected here is the record of it.
- **Three clauses in the first draft of the cell**, removed before commit: a rule about admitting dependencies, a claim about where a test may live, and a reason bullet 5 already carried. The first two were agent-proposed rules with no incident behind them, which the charter's admission order refuses outright; the third was net growth for nothing.
