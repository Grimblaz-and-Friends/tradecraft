# D-1013 — The builder route

**Purpose:** record why the second of #1011's two pull requests routes a change through one handoff to a builder that answers for readiness, how the route is chosen, what the holder may still do once the builder is live, and how the release report shows the owner what the builder decided, and what was chosen over what. **Audience:** a future session changing the route decision, `run handoff`, the readiness record, the builder-route gates, the send-back or catch-up resume, the release report's builder-route payload, the route's cost split or the five-change comparison. **Success:** that reader can recover what was chosen over what without the issue.

The route exists because every product change paid for the holder relaying each step of the run on the lab's largest context: in the 30 days the brief measured, holders spent 83% of their cost from the first build on, and 71% of the points they sent builders after a first build were process steps or a check's finding passed on, which a builder can take from the source. So the builder takes the checks as its own tools, and the holder returns for one final pass.

The owner's [affirmed brief](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1011#issuecomment-7071427121) governs, including its Delivery paragraph, which gives this pull request the holder-written plan, the handoff, the readiness record, the final pass and send-back, the release report's additions, the cost split and row 7's default. Its pre-implementation artifact was [settled on the cap route](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1011#issuecomment-7085587515), with the holder's [whole-change reading](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1011#issuecomment-7085596192) and its readings of the [first](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1011#issuecomment-7087075226) and [second](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1011#issuecomment-7088529044) build turns. The lane is elevated / routine-panel. It builds on [D-1012](D-1012-2026-11-21-a-builders-own-checks-and-writes.md), whose builder tools this route composes, and supersedes none. The plan's decisions are E1 to E12; each call below names the decision it sits in.

## Calls made inside the rows

- **C1. The route is read from the record, and the work's first launch fixes it for good** (E1). Row 7 makes the new route the default with no word from the owner, lets him ask for today's route, and has a change in flight finish on the route it started, in either direction. So `in-flight` comes before the owner's `route` marker, and is judged over the whole work rather than the term, so an amended brief can't move a change. A cloud build stays on today's route first of all, because Not this keeps cloud builds there.
- **C2. A `builder-session` marker decides nothing by itself** (E1). It counts toward the relay route only where it agrees with a `build` bundle, because a builder on the builder route posts one too.
- **C3. The mechanical lane takes the builder route, and the builder maps its rows** (E1, E5). The brief leaves only cloud builds on today's route. Which row needs a fresh reader stays the builder's mapping; what that reader is told and reads comes from the brief's row alone (`run acceptance-criterion --row N`), because a builder choosing either would break row 3. The readiness record carries the mapping, so an unmapped row is visible. Its handoff carries the lane reason in place of a plan, because charter §3 owes that lane no artifact.
- **C4. Stages that start a builder are confined to their route** (E1, E7). On the builder route, `run build` at `handoff-due` would move a default change onto the relay route without the owner's ask, and at `stretch-stopped` would start a second writer; `run artifact`, `floor` and `review-disposition` are refused there for the same reasons. On the relay route, a holder-written handoff would start a stretch on a change the owner kept on today's route. The `route` marker, at the owner's word and before anything is launched, is the one way across.
- **C5. The cold seat keeps today's vendor and model** (E2). Not this keeps the entrance's model choices, so a Claude holder's plan is judged by a Claude seat, recorded `degraded` with `holder-written-plan`. The seat takes its producer vendor from the draft marker's `author=holder vendor=…`, because no launch produced the plan.
- **C6. No holder reading on the builder route** (E2, E3). Row 2's holder cell says the holder posts no separate reading of its plan, so a `holder-reading` there counts for nothing and the handoff carries none. A correction to the plan is a new draft, settled again before the handoff; a ruling on a row is a new affirmed brief; after the handoff, the holder's one further word is the send-back.
- **C7. The plan names every check by carrying it** (E2). Its criteria with `Fresh reader:` lines, its `Use session job` block and its consumer tree declarations are the formats D-1012's composed checks read, and the checks owed are computed from them, the lane and the use rules. A separate list of checks would be a second statement of the same thing.
- **C8. The handoff is one composed text, started once and detached** (E3). The entrance composes it deterministically from the brief, the settled plan and the owed checks, so a builder never receives instructions for a check the entrance didn't compose, and the received text can be recomposed byte for byte. `run handoff` makes and publishes the builder's tree before composing, because the launcher needs a registered tree and the allowance sentence names the branch, and a refusal after that point removes what the same call made. The launcher starts detached so the holder doesn't wait on the builder.
- **C9. The stretch's limit is its own 28,800 s, and a stopped stretch resumes only on the owner's word** (E3). A stretch carries build turns, checks and the waits on CI and reviewers in one launch; on #1011's first pull request those came to about ten hours, against a four-hour budget meant for build turns alone. Cutting the stretch to that budget would stop most real changes at the ceiling and bring the owner in, against row 1's owner cell. A figure above the default takes an override reason, which the release report lists. A resume that didn't follow the owner's message would break row 1's third check; where a session can't be resumed, the entrance's existing recovery restart is the one way on, in the same tree and lineage, so no second writer starts. Nothing resumes a stretch on its own.
- **C10. The builder tells the holder with one message** (E3). A launched session could list its holder by name; delivery wasn't shown, so the owner stays the fallback row 6's holder cell names. The holder's turn ends at the handoff and makes no call until a message arrives.
- **C11. A hand-started builder that can't go on has a lawful exit** (E3, E5). A `cannot-launch` stop carries row 6's second check and isn't a stop by choice under row 3, which still allows a chosen stop only when Shape is wrong. `run handoff --ended-reason`, on the owner's word, closes a hand record whose session ended without a record; without it the holder would be refused `with-builder` with no way out.
- **C12. Seats under a hand-started builder are staffed against its declared vendor** (E4). Row 6 staffs a seat against the builder's actual vendor, and for a hand record the declared one is all the practice has at launch. The record and the report say it was declared, not observed, so no one reads it as proved; where the builder's own usage log can be read, its observed vendor and model govern the readiness reading.
- **C13. Builder mode takes the holder session from the builder's own launch** (E4). The builder catches up with the base itself, and the session the registration names is its launch's holder. A command line the builder writes can't supply it, and shouldn't.
- **C14. While a builder is live, the holder runs only the read-only decision and the two stopped-builder exits** (E4). Row 1's holder cell has the holder launch nothing and send nothing until the ready report.
- **C15. Findings are counted from a fixed return tail** (E5). Row 3's first check needs a count both the builder and the entrance can make. An unreadable tail counts as one finding, so a malformed report's findings can't vanish.
- **C16. `reconciled` reads every report the builder launched** (E5). Row 3's first check counts findings across the posted check reports, not across those the record chose to name, so a builder that records only its final runs can't drop an earlier report's findings unseen.
- **C17. No gate before the ready record reads that record, and the record is the builder's last post** (E5, E6). A hand record completes at its readiness record, so a gate that waited on the record before proof would close the record before the builder had finished. Reconciliation is judged once, at ready; an unreconciled record still reaches the final pass with what is unreconciled shown, and the send-back is its remedy.
- **C18. The builder posts the markers the holder used to post** (E6). A builder that holds the run can't wait on a holder who sends nothing. Each marker is checked as before, by login, bundle and head, and the release report lists them; the counting `use` report stands for the holder's `use` note.
- **C19. The holder accounts for the stretch's own launch findings in its final pass, #1009's bytecode included** (E6). Once the stretch has ended no other party remains, and no launch-account party fits bytecode. Adding a party would be fixing #1009, which this change doesn't own.
- **C20. What ends the cold reader's reread loop** (E6). The builder may decline any flag with its reason; an accepted flag owes no reread; a flag that comes back is evidence about the point and is named false, wrong shape or fork; and the stretch's limit bounds the whole. The release report lists every acceptance for the owner.
- **C21. The source-verification launch stays open in the final pass** (E7). It verifies the holder's own accounts, the release report included; it isn't a review of the change.
- **C22. A bought panel runs after ready and isn't counted against row 4's first check** (E7). Not this keeps the panel with the holder, and row 1 names a bought panel among what stays owed after ready, so read together the rows exempt it. `holder_after_ready` still lists it, by class.
- **C23. After ready, catching up stays the builder's, and the holder only resumes it** (E7). Row 1 has the builder catch up and keep the record current, and row 5 has the holder post no proof on this route. A catch-up resume carries no problem the holder found, so it isn't a send-back and doesn't spend one; the release report lists each one, so the owner sees how often the builder was resumed. A moved base therefore can't strand the change.
- **C24. A send-back and a catch-up resume each have their own limit, 3,600 s by default, raised as the handoff's is** (E7, E8). Each is one resume of the same session, and a longer one takes the same override reason, which the release report lists. A second send-back is refused, because row 4 allows one.
- **C25. `handoff_plan` recomposes the whole handoff** (E9). Row 2's first check is about the text the builder was actually given, so a digest of the plan alone isn't enough. A handoff the holder wrote reads `differs` with the reason `holder-written`, and the owner sees it under "Needs your decision".
- **C26. Release-report placement follows row 4's words** (E9). Row 4's owner cell puts what the holder checked, the send-back and what stays open under what needs the owner's decision. Declines that need no decision go in their own uncollapsed list, so they don't bury the rest, and none is only in the collapsed section.
- **C27. Connected reviewers' declines reach the owner as check declines do** (E9). Row 3's owner cell covers every finding the builder declined, whoever raised it, with the holder's view.
- **C28. `holder_after_ready` reads the holder's transcript as well as the bundle store** (E9, E10). Row 4's first check covers any review the holder starts, and a review agent started outside the launchers leaves no bundle. The holder's own send-back and catch-up resumes have classes of their own, so they don't fail the check.
- **C29. The five-change comparison is switched on by a file on the machine** (E10). With no `~/.tradecraft/route-comparison.json` the release report counts nothing and writes nothing, so nothing specific to one owner's comparison ships; the repo-only Steward cell says who writes the file and where the comparison goes.
- **C30. Store against posted stays exact** (E11). The holder's post-and-read found GitHub keeping every byte it was sent, so any difference is an edit, and no normalisation is added.
- **C31. The first real builder checks run before the merge on a real launch against stand-in GitHub, and after it in a real repository** (E11, E12). Each of the first pull request's live launches found a defect its tests had passed, and the Delivery paragraph rules out carrying a change on the new route in a real repository before the merge, so the first builder-route change records the real-repository run. The use session drives the built entrance through a fixture driver over the suite's fakes, run from an exported copy with no network, because a stand-in `gh` first on the path then carries a whole run.
- **C32. Quoted check-report markers are set aside** (E11). A holder reads `check_reports` in its final pass, and a list padded with quotations of the marker would hide the reports that matter; the entrance already separates quotations from posted markers for every other marker it reads.

## From the holder's reading of the first build turn

- **Use at handoff.** The handoff lists the use check when the settled plan carries a `Use session job` block, and says the entrance decides at the builder's head whether use is owed, because the use rules read changed paths that don't exist at the handoff.
- **The reach entry.** No new stage: the entry names the read-only decision, which prints the reach report, and the builder posts the `reach-reading` comment in the markers reference's form for every flagged item.
- **The floor command is supplied once, at the handoff.** `run handoff --floor-command TEXT` carries it word for word into the handoff and the request record's `handoff` block. With none, the handoff says the declared CI jobs are the floor and no command was supplied, and a builder floor that falls due becomes a `cannot-launch` stop naming the floor. The builder never writes or picks the command, because a check's instructions are out of its reach (row 3).
- **Reach is listed before `ready-reviewers` and `proof`,** since both are held on it. The list of owed checks is otherwise not a sequence.
- **A resumed builder is priced reply by reply, from its session's transcript.** A send-back, a catch-up resume and `--resume-reason` each resume the session, and the cost module's rule for resumed Claude usage would make the whole figure `unknown`, though row 7's third check has each of the first five release reports state the builder's whole cost. So each local Claude launch of a launched builder names its replies by the message ids in its own retained stream (`<output>.claude.stdout.log`), and those replies are priced from the session's transcript at each reply's time, as the holder's are. The stream can't price them itself, because its `output_tokens` is the count as each reply began: in one launch the holder measured, the stream gave 860 where the launch's result gave 94,394. A stream or transcript that can't be read, or a reply whose input-side counts the two disagree on, leaves the figure `unknown` with its reason. A Codex resume keeps its share of the session the stretch began, and the existing cost report and today's route are priced as they were.
- **`handoff_quiet` lists a reply that follows only a task notification or an automated event, and names what each reply followed,** so the owner can tell a reply to a watcher from one the holder chose to make.

## From the holder's reading of the second build turn

- **The `route` marker may stand in the issue body as well as a comment** (E1). The owner's ask can come with the issue when it is filed, and the shared test fixture needed a place for the marker that moves no comment. A later comment governs over the body, as the latest lawful marker does.
- **A Claude builder started by hand must work** (E4). Row 6 sets no vendor or model for a builder the owner starts, and E4's conditions carry no exception for Claude, but a hand record has no session id, so comparing a present one with it refused every entrance run of a hand-started Claude session. Under a hand record a present session id is accepted and recorded as declared; it is refused only where it is the holder session the handoff names, because the holder can't be its own builder.
- **The price join is accepted as built:** reply ids from each launch's stream, priced from the session transcript, `unknown` with its reason where either can't be read or they disagree. One `unknown` is known and left: a subagent transcript that writes one message id twice, its partial output and then its final one, is refused by the transcript reader as conflicting usage, so that session's builder figure is `unknown`.

## From the holder's reading of the third build turn

- **A send-back is spent by its launch, not by its marker,** read from the store's record of that launch, so a failed post can't re-arm it. A `run send-back` that finds the launch made and the marker missing posts the marker and launches nothing.
- **`handoff_plan` for a hand-started builder compares the recorded digest,** and a stretch whose record carries no `handoff` block reads `differs`.
- **The doctrine:** `run handoff` is the one stage not refused `with-builder`, and it refuses `builder-live` itself unless it carries `--resume-reason` or `--ended-reason`.
- **Reconciliation reads every counting report any stretch or hand record of the term launched,** and a ready record naming a dispatch that is none of them is invalid.
- **An unreadable cloud switch is reported, not raised:** the read-only decision names it in a holder-owned decision and the report's `route` reads `unknown`.

## From the holder's reading of the repair turn

- **The final pass is a decision state,** `ready-reported` or `owed-at-ready` until `run release-report`, saying if the send-back is spent; it accepts a send-back while none is, `run release-report` when nothing is owed.
- **A stretch proves delivery of its handoff's terms,** a resume counting with its launch; no answer names `run build`.
- **The handoff names what the entrance would refuse:** the closing reference alone on a line; the cold reading's `open` before `run use`, `clean` before `run proof`.
- **`implementing-pr` and `builder-session` stay out of the handoff;** the entrance needs neither.

## From the holder's reading of the final pass

- **`handoff_plan` across a practice update reads `unknown`, `composed-by-another-version`,** naming both, because other code's recomposition proves nothing.
- **Row check 5.2 pairs logged and reported refusals in order,** failing only on a logged one left unpaired, because the launch logs the command whole and the record words it its own way.
- **A send-back doesn't wait on the gate, a connected reviewer or a panel,** whose findings are what it carries.
- **A `stopped` record completes a hand-started record**, so it reads `stretch-stopped`; `builder-live` refuses `--resume-reason`, which is for a stopped builder.
- **`placement` is removed:** where each decline goes (C26) is the release-report reference's rule, applied by the holder who writes the report.

## Rejected

- **A Codex cold seat for a Claude holder's plan,** which D-1012's plan recommended. It would change which model the entrance launches, against Not this (C5).
- **A stretch cut to the four-hour build budget** (C9's reason).
- **A gate before ready that waits on the readiness record** (C17's reason).
- **A `handoff_plan` that compares only the plan's digest.** An altered handoff, or one the holder wrote, would read true (C25).
- **Reconciling only the reports the record names** (C16's reason).
- **A launch-account party for bytecode** (C19's reason).
- **Normalising a posted report before comparing it with the stored return** (C30's reason).
- **A catch-up after ready that counts as the send-back, or that the holder merges itself.** The first would spend row 4's one send-back on no problem the holder found; the second would have the holder post proof (C23).
