# D-304: A second adverse verdict on the same point stops the rework — and the carrier that makes the second one visible is the load-bearing half, not the remedies

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-01 (PR #304)

## Context

[#290](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290) recorded two convergences on 2026-09-01 in which a session, told *no* by a cold seat, repaired the sentence the verdict named and resubmitted — repeatedly, on the same passage. [#260](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260) ran five adverse verdicts on one budget paragraph, each asserting a property of four budgeted quantities that they did not share; the sixth draft replaced the paragraph with a table and passed. [#286](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/286) ran twelve adverse verdicts across ten drafts, seven of them on one clause whose four successive wordings each classified the same matters differently; it ended when the owner ruled in one sentence, because the question underneath the clause was his and no wording would have passed.

Both were stopped by the owner asking whether the rounds were still buying anything. `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` stated what an adverse verdict does *not* do — it settles nothing, and an artifact edited after its verdict is a draft again — and nothing about how to respond to one. The default was therefore local repair. `skills/authoring/SKILL.md`'s *"restate the sentence more plainly rather than appending a qualifier"*, at `945fe6b`, governs a writer facing a hypothetical misreader, not a writer an instrument has told no twice, and it reaches only one of the three remedies the incidents needed.

## Decision

**1. The trigger is keyed to the point, never to the text.** A second adverse verdict on the same claim, criterion or choice stops the rework. What distinguishes point-keying is not that a location key would have been inert — #260 ran five consecutive verdicts on one budget paragraph, so a paragraph key fires there at the second. It is that a point survives what a location does not: a full re-draft, and a move between sections. #286's clause was rewritten four times, and in the experience session run for this change the recurring point moved from a criterion to a boundary statement between rounds. #290's own deferred list asks *"What counts as 'the same passage' — a paragraph, a criterion, a claim"*; the claim, criterion or choice is this entry's answer to that question rather than a rejection of it.

**2. Three remedies, named, and the session picks.** The claim is false about the tree, which no wording repairs — probe it. The passage is the wrong shape for what it covers — restate it whole. Or each draft decides the same thing differently, which is what a fork looks like when a session keeps closing it silently — it goes to the owner argued. #260 is the second; #286 is the third; the filing's own deferred list asked whether the real rule was about spikes instead, and #286 answers no for its own incident, since no probe settles who decides something. A restate-only rule would have sent #286 into a fifth shape, which [the incident comment on #290](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290#issuecomment-5489029938) names as the failure a one-remedy trigger produces.

**3. The carrier is the load-bearing half, and this reverses the design the brief was affirmed on.** A four-seat cold A/B spike, run before the artifact's criteria were written, put two reconstructed incidents to two control seats (today's cell, no new rule) and two treatment seats (the same plus the candidate paragraph). **All four stopped reworking and diagnosed correctly**; both controls named a probe unprompted and one named the owner-fork branch unprompted. So a rule that only names the trigger and the remedies buys close to nothing. Its bound is severe and stated: each prompt laid the two rounds out adjacently, which is itself the diagnosis, so the spike tested whether the remedy is *known* and not whether the repetition is *noticed*. The spike is recorded in full in [the settled artifact](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290#issuecomment-5495179525), under *What was checked*. That a recurring point arrives among a round's other findings is not attributed per round by [#260's settled artifact](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5487906386), which records its five verdicts as one aggregate and its enumeration of them after settling, in retrospect; what supplies the premise directly is the A/B below, whose control seats, handed a four-finding round with no lines, each reported being unable to tell whether any of the four was a repeat.

**The carrier was then measured, in the review this change's own pull request ran.** Four fresh seats, no repository access, identical rules text and identical four-finding round-2 verdict, the recurrence third of four and sharing no wording with round 1; the single variable is whether the inherited draft carries a round-1 point line. **With the line, 2 of 2 stopped, named the fork branch and went to the owner; without it, 0 of 2 detected the repeat and both wrote round 3.** Both control seats named the missing mechanism themselves. Three limits: the arms were handed the rules text directly rather than letting the cell load, so this measures the **carrier** and not the **trigger**; n is 2 per arm; and the arms' round-1 block was built under the unit this change later abandoned, one line for a three-point verdict, so what the A/B varied was a per-verdict carrier rather than the per-point one that shipped. The re-run ordered against the fixed paragraph inherited that construction unchanged. It is [in the review's defense report](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/304), which is what shows it. **It also measures the half the owner did not see** — both control seats reconstructed the carrier unprompted and recorded honestly that round 1's points were unrecoverable rather than inventing them, which is a second argument for *from the first verdict onward* beyond the one below.

So the draft carries every adverse verdict it receives, one line per point a verdict lands on, from the first verdict onward, collected directly under the draft's purpose header. The unit is the point rather than the verdict because a verdict lands on several at once, and under a per-verdict line a bundled round's other points go unrecorded — so the stop cannot fire for them, which is the population the rule exists for. That is the form [D-261](D-261-2026-08-30-visible-compliance.md) landed as the standard, on the merged-history sweep [#256](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/256) that entry names as carrying the measurement: compliance visible on the artifact its reader is producing, rather than a judgment resolved against a document not in front of them. **The first verdict earns a line too** — the second has to be adjacent to something, so a carrier starting at the second records a repetition with nothing to repeat against.

**4. No guard, and no round cap.** "The same point" is a judgment about verdict content that no script decides, and a guard counting rounds would fire on the first-verdict case the rule exempts. No cap on rounds either: #290 is explicit that the waste is attributable to the response and not to the gate, and #260's round 5 produced that change's highest-value find, so the instrument was not in diminishing returns when the repetition happened.

**5. The second remedy is written out in full rather than pointed at `authoring`.** It overlaps that cell's restate-rather-than-qualify rule, and a pointer would be the ordinary move — but `tools/lint.py`'s `CHARTER_CELL` and `check_sideways_deps` permit a shipped cell exactly one reference target at depth one, the charter, so `engagement` naming `authoring` is not a lawful shape.

## What the owner affirmed, and the call this session made under it

The [affirmed brief](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290#issuecomment-5494906958) names a trigger with three remedies, in `engagement`, at zero always-on cost, touching no review-stage instrument. He was offered and **declined** a general rule spanning instruments — cold seat, review, guards, external reviewers — whose natural home is the charter; [#95](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/95) is the same shape one stage later and its remedy already landed per-incident. He was also offered admitting nothing and recording the incidents.

**The carrier is an obligation he did not see.** It is reported rather than re-agreed, on the ground that every term of the affirmation is intact and the carrier is the mechanism by which the affirmed trigger fires rather than a further end — so it falls to the residual in the fork test, everything that is not a genuine fork being the session's. It remains the most contestable thing in the change: a standing cost on every future draft in the practice, from the first verdict, on a population wider than the repeated-verdict case the brief describes, and it makes the rule longer than the "short rule" the brief names. Stated in the settled artifact, in the amendment, and in the pull request body, which is where he reads at merge.

## The meaning changes this makes, as revising governing prose requires

Named here because `skills/authoring/references/revising.md` obliges naming every one where amendments are recorded, including a sentence left verbatim while a term it turns on is redefined. All five were found by this change's own review and are recorded rather than remedied, each on the ground that the only remedy is more prose on a governing surface and the consequence has not earned the words.

- **The mid-artifact owner fork's default.** The third remedy sends a silently-closed fork to the owner. The cell's existing rule carries such a fork *"marked as contingent"* and proceeds; the section on the unattended stretch says *"The work does not wait on the ruling: proceed on your recommendation."* The remedy is written to agree with both — *"takes the contingent form below and goes to the owner argued, the work proceeding on the recommendation"* —. The review's A/B observed a seat halting the artifact's settling pending a ruling, which is the work waiting on it; the clause exists to make the three sentences agree, and it is written on that ground rather than on the frequency of the halt.
- **What the artifact carries about a check.** *"Where a check bears on the artifact, the artifact carries what came back rather than the fact that you looked"* is generic and reaches the cold-seat check. The new rule scopes that one check's record to the point rather than the finding's prose. A session may read the narrower rule as discharging the wider one; no run to date has produced that failure, across both experience sessions and every seat the review's A/B and its re-run dispatched.
- **Where the lines go when the draft's identity moves.** An amendment *"is a further comment, never an edit"*, and an amended brief obliges settling again. Nothing says which comment then carries the accumulated lines. Re-derived across every run to date rather than restated: neither experience session moved a draft's identity mid-settling, and the review's A/B arms stayed inside one artifact, so the case is still unobserved. The nearest thing to it is the second session settling an artifact and then carrying five corrections in an amendment beside it — its own words in [the second session note](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/304#issuecomment-5498349079), *"a settled artifact whose reading is corrected in a document beside it"* — which moves the reading rather than the draft, and leaves this unobserved rather than answering it.
- **The cell's Success clause.** It enumerates four things — the fourth arriving with D-300 in this change's own merge — and this obligation is none of them. The recheck `revising.md` obliges is conditional on the revision changing what the document is for, which one paragraph inside an existing section arguably does not — recorded because the comparator is adjacent: [D-300](D-300-2026-09-01-the-boundary-and-its-two-crossings.md), the previous change to this cell, extended Success when it added a section.
- **The level at which a point is named.** The rule says the claim, criterion or choice and does not say at what altitude. In the experience session the recurring point was named at two different levels in two rounds and the consumer bridged them by re-abstracting. Every session observed to meet the case has bridged unaided — both experience sessions, the second of them across three drafts pitched at three different altitudes, and both arm-A seats in the A/B and its re-run. Naming the level would need an example.

**One divergence this change deliberately does not repair.** The settled artifact's acceptance criterion 1 says *"one line per adverse verdict naming its point"*, and the rule as shipped says one line per point a verdict lands on — the review found six seats split 3–3 on the singular reading and that under it a bundled round's other points go unrecorded, so the stop cannot fire for them. The artifact freezes once settled and is not maintained, so the divergence is named here rather than edited there.

## What was rejected, and why

- **A rule about spikes instead of about verdicts.** The filing's own strongest alternative — every one of #260's five findings was cheap arithmetic, and its artifact says no spike was run. Rejected because it covers one incident and not the other, and because `engagement` already routes a session to a spike for an untested premise; what was missing was the trigger that sends it there.
- **Admitting nothing.** The honest alternative the filing names, on the ground that a competent session should notice. The spike is what settles it: capable seats do know the remedy, and two capable sessions still failed to notice on the same day, with the owner as the only instrument that caught it.
- **Criterion 3 as a claim about behaviour.** Written as a criterion about the diff arguing its consumer-facing load instead, because at a baseline where the no-rule control already selects among the branches, a behaviour criterion would be passed by the current tree and measure nothing.
- **Pointing the verdict lines at the artifact's *What was checked* section.** Drafted that way and reversed by the settling seat's finding: that section sits at the bottom, which is the burying form this change exists to prevent, while the artifact's own instance put its verdict record near the top. The rule now names a position — directly under the draft's purpose header — rather than a section at the artifact's foot.
