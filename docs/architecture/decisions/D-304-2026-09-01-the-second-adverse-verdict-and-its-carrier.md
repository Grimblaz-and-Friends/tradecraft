# D-304: A second adverse verdict on the same point stops the rework — and the carrier that makes the second one visible is the load-bearing half, not the remedies

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-01 (PR #304)

## Context

[#290](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290) recorded two convergences on 2026-09-01 in which a session, told *no* by a cold seat, repaired the sentence the verdict named and resubmitted — repeatedly, on the same passage. [#260](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260) ran five adverse verdicts on one budget paragraph, each asserting a property of four budgeted quantities that they did not share; the sixth draft replaced the paragraph with a table and passed. [#286](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/286) ran twelve adverse verdicts across ten drafts, seven of them on one clause whose four successive wordings each classified the same matters differently; it ended when the owner ruled in one sentence, because the question underneath the clause was his and no wording would have passed.

Both were stopped by the owner asking whether the rounds were still buying anything. `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` stated what an adverse verdict does *not* do — it settles nothing, and an artifact edited after its verdict is a draft again — and nothing about how to respond to one. The default was therefore local repair. `skills/authoring/SKILL.md`'s *"restate the sentence more plainly rather than appending a qualifier"* governs a writer facing a hypothetical misreader, not a writer an instrument has told no twice, and it reaches only one of the three remedies the incidents needed.

## Decision

**1. The trigger is keyed to the point, never to the text.** A second adverse verdict on the same claim, criterion or choice stops the rework. Both incidents rewrote the sentence every round, so a trigger keyed to a paragraph or a passage would have fired in neither; the wording having changed in between is the norm rather than a reason the verdict does not count.

**2. Three remedies, named, and the session picks.** The claim is false about the tree, which no wording repairs — probe it. The passage is the wrong shape for what it covers — restate it whole. Or each draft decides the same thing differently, which is what a fork looks like when a session keeps closing it silently — it goes to the owner argued. #260 is the second; #286 is the third; the filing's own deferred list asked whether the real rule was about spikes instead, and #286 answers no for its own incident, since no probe settles who decides something. A restate-only rule would have sent #286 into a fifth shape, which [the incident comment on #290](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290#issuecomment-5489029938) names as the failure a one-remedy trigger produces.

**3. The carrier is the load-bearing half, and this reverses the design the brief was affirmed on.** A four-seat cold A/B spike, run before the artifact's criteria were written, put two reconstructed incidents to two control seats (today's cell, no new rule) and two treatment seats (the same plus the candidate paragraph). **All four stopped reworking and diagnosed correctly**; both controls named a probe unprompted and one named the owner-fork branch unprompted. So a rule that only names the trigger and the remedies buys close to nothing. Its bound is severe and stated: each prompt laid the two rounds out adjacently, which is itself the diagnosis, so the spike tested whether the remedy is *known* and not whether the repetition is *noticed*. The incidents say the second is what fails — [#260's settled artifact](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5487906386) shows each adverse round arriving bundled with unrelated findings, and its enumeration of the five rejections written after settling, in retrospect.

So the draft carries each adverse verdict as one line naming its point, from the first onward, collected in one place. That is the form [D-261](D-261-2026-08-30-visible-compliance.md) measured strongest on this repository's own merged history: compliance visible on the artifact its reader is producing, rather than a judgment resolved against a document not in front of them. **The first verdict earns a line too** — the second has to be adjacent to something, so a carrier starting at the second records a repetition with nothing to repeat against.

**4. No guard, and no round cap.** "The same point" is a judgment about verdict content that no script decides, and a guard counting rounds would fire on the first-verdict case the rule exempts. No cap on rounds either: #290 is explicit that the waste is attributable to the response and not to the gate, and #260's round 5 produced that change's highest-value find, so the instrument was not in diminishing returns when the repetition happened.

**5. The second remedy is written out in full rather than pointed at `authoring`.** It overlaps that cell's restate-rather-than-qualify rule, and a pointer would be the ordinary move — but `tools/lint.py`'s `CHARTER_CELL` and `check_sideways_deps` permit a shipped cell exactly one reference target at depth one, the charter, so `engagement` naming `authoring` is not a lawful shape.

## What the owner affirmed, and the call this session made under it

The [affirmed brief](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/290#issuecomment-5494906958) names a trigger with three remedies, in `engagement`, at zero always-on cost, touching no review-stage instrument. He was offered and **declined** a general rule spanning instruments — cold seat, review, guards, external reviewers — whose natural home is the charter; [#95](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/95) is the same shape one stage later and its remedy already landed per-incident. He was also offered admitting nothing and recording the incidents.

**The carrier is an obligation he did not see.** It is reported rather than re-agreed, on the ground that every term of the affirmation is intact and the carrier is a *means* to the affirmed trigger rather than a new end, which `engagement` makes the session's. It remains the most contestable thing in the change: a standing cost on every future draft in the practice, from the first verdict, on a population wider than the repeated-verdict case the brief describes, and it makes the rule longer than the "short rule" the brief names. Stated in the settled artifact, in the amendment, and in the pull request body, which is where he reads at merge.

## What was rejected, and why

- **A rule about spikes instead of about verdicts.** The filing's own strongest alternative — every one of #260's five findings was cheap arithmetic, and its artifact says no spike was run. Rejected because it covers one incident and not the other, and because `engagement` already routes a session to a spike for an untested premise; what was missing was the trigger that sends it there.
- **Admitting nothing.** The honest alternative the filing names, on the ground that a competent session should notice. The spike is what settles it: capable seats do know the remedy, and two capable sessions still failed to notice on the same day, with the owner as the only instrument that caught it.
- **Criterion 3 as a claim about behaviour.** Written as a criterion about the diff arguing its consumer-facing load instead, because at a baseline where the no-rule control already selects among the branches, a behaviour criterion would be passed by the current tree and measure nothing.
- **Pointing the verdict lines at the artifact's *What was checked* section.** Drafted that way and reversed by the settling seat's finding: that section sits at the bottom, which is the burying form this change exists to prevent, while the artifact's own instance put its verdict record near the top. The rule now names collection rather than a section.
