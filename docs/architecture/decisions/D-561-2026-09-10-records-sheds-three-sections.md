## What was decided

`docs/cells/records/SKILL.md` at `6c2125a` was one file of 8,820 characters below its frontmatter with no depth at all, and the cell's description names five triggers. Three of its five sections each serve one of those triggers and nothing else, so a session firing the cell for any other reason loaded them: `## Review, here` at 4,212 characters, `## How a brief reached settled` at 1,818, and `## Admissions, at a ceiling` at 1,546. Each becomes one file under `docs/cells/records/references/`, and the body keeps the purpose header, a three-entry depth index, and the two sections every firing needs. **2,744 characters**, and `tools/lint.py`'s ceiling for this body is rebaselined to it, as that map's own comment requires of a cell that sheds depth.

**Nothing is cut, and the claim is checked rather than asserted — against the landed tree, not against the split alone.** Of the 26 non-blank lines below the frontmatter at `6c2125a`, **17 appear verbatim exactly once** across the four files as they land, and **9 do not**. **The split accounts for five of the nine** and they are **two headings promoted to file titles, one heading replaced** — `## Review, here` named a position rather than a file, so `# What a review records` is fresh prose in its place — **and two clauses repaired for position**: one said *below* about a section three headings down, and one named its section by title while relying on it being the next one. **The class is a clause that depended on where it sat, not a clause containing the word *below*.** Reading it the narrower way missed a third member — *exactly as an admission's bank row does*, in `docs/cells/records/references/the-settling-row.md` — which this change's review found and its fix batch repaired. **The review's fix batch accounts for the other four**, each an edit in place to a line the split had moved verbatim: three in `docs/cells/records/references/what-a-review-records.md`, qualifying a bare cross-cell filename by the cell that owns it, and one in `docs/cells/records/references/the-settling-row.md`, the bank-row analogy above. A fifth line differs for a reason the split did not create either — `## Records are exhaust`'s tripwire sentence gained the clause naming its two exceptions, which is the review's high. The figure moved from five to nine because the batch edited the tree the figure describes; it is re-derived here against that tree rather than carried forward.

**`## Decisions` stays in the body, and that is this change's one judgment call.** The disqualifier in `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md` — *a cell is too big when a session loads prose it had no use for* — reaches part of it: its locator and its `[D-N]` marker serve *writing or citing a decision entry* alone. It stays for three reasons that are recorded here so a later session finishing the split does not read it as a paragraph somebody missed.

- **The arithmetic runs the wrong way.** The section is 306 characters. An index entry written the way the other three are — naming the path, the freeze and the citation marker, so a reader can decide whether to open it — costs about as much. Moving it would relieve a reader who does not need it by roughly nothing and cost a reader who does a file open, which is the disqualifier's own measure made worse in both directions. The estimate was put at *roughly 250* in the artifact and is low: the shortest of the three entries this change writes is 287 characters, which strengthens the case rather than weakening it.
- **It is not purely one-trigger prose.** Its freeze clause — *frozen on landing but for the two narrow repairs bounded in the log's README* — is what the description's fifth trigger reaches for, a session tempted to correct something already recorded being sent here for the one record whose freeze has named exceptions.
- **A live pointer lands on it.** `docs/cells/siting/SKILL.md:17` sends a reader for where a decision entry lives and under what freeze; after the split they land in a body whose first section is the depth index and whose second is that one, rather than in a depth file the index would first have to route them to.

**What would change the call:** the section growing to where an index entry is materially cheaper than it, or a second trigger's worth of decision-log material arriving beside it. Either makes a fourth depth file the right shape.

**Sites.** `docs/cells/records/SKILL.md` (the three sections out, the depth index in); the three new files under `docs/cells/records/references/`; `tools/lint.py` (the `records` row of `CELL_BODY_CEILING_CHARS`, and the `_check_cost` docstring, which named the body as where the cost exclusions bind and now names the depth file that carries them).

**Three entries in `docs/recorded-findings.jsonl` are stranded by this move and stay stranded.** All three name `docs/cells/records/SKILL.md` as their `artifact`, and the sentences they were keyed to now sit in `docs/cells/records/references/what-a-review-records.md` (the two shapes a reader of the review index meets) and `docs/cells/records/references/the-settling-row.md` (the `reversed` field, and one row per change). That record carries no repair permission — nothing maintains it — so this is stated rather than repaired. What keeps them findable is that `artifact` names the durable subject the entry survives to be met on, and the cell is that subject whichever of its files holds the sentence.

**A split that moves prose into a cell's own `references/` does not trigger the decision log's mover-pays repoint permission — the owner ruled it on 2026-09-10**, choosing between two argued options put to him in conversation. His word was *"B"*, which was the option that it does not; the rest of this paragraph is the session's account of what that settles.

The permission reads *the repair rides with a change that moves the target*, and this change's review raised the question because a split moves a sentence without moving the file that holds it. Five live unpinned references now characterise this body by content that left it — `D-371:46`, `D-371:49`, `D-441:64`, `D-545:17`, `D-544:30` — and `tools/lint.py`'s repoint check fires only on a path that resolves to nothing, so nothing reddens. **They stay as they are, and that is the ruling's accepted cost:** each is recoverable from a depth index one line into the file it names. What the other reading would have cost is why the question was his — it obliges every remaining split in the [#476](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/476) programme to repoint, and cannot reach the already-merged `filing` split, whose identical strandings the permission bars from ever being repaired alone. **The ruling binds the programme's seven remaining splits**, and is recorded on that cause so the next mover reads it without re-deriving it.

## What was rejected

**Splitting `## Records are exhaust` out with the others.** It binds any append whatever the trigger, and it is the general answer to the *tempted to correct* trigger. A body that shed it would route every firing through a file open to learn the one rule that governs all of them.

**Subdividing `docs/cells/records/references/what-a-review-records.md`.** At 4,417 characters it is much the largest of the three, but nothing in it serves fewer triggers than the file as a whole: `cost` and `target` are both read while recording one review's outcome.

**Giving the new depth files a ceiling of their own.** The lint prices a cell's `SKILL.md` and not its depth, so a cell can shed its way under a constant without shedding anything a reader loads. That asymmetry is [#457](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/457)'s subject and is deliberately not resolved here — this change would otherwise be deciding the ceiling mechanism, which the affirmed brief puts out of scope.

**Repairing the two wordings the second cold seat named.** It returned `would` and reported both as observations: the artifact says three headings were *promoted* where two were promoted and one replaced, and the *roughly 250* estimate above sits below the real shortest entry. Both cut in favour of the calls they bear on, and editing a settled artifact makes it a draft again under the `engagement` cell's narrowing. They are recorded on the pull request instead.

## Cost

**The body shed 6,076 characters and the cell as a whole gained 2,039.** Decoded UTF-8 characters, which is what `tools/lint.py` measures: the body 8,820 → 2,744, against 8,115 characters across the three new files. What the cell gained over the three sections that moved is the three titles, the three load conditions, the two repaired clauses, and what the review's fix batch added to all of them. The depth index accounts for most of the body's remainder. `tools/lint.py` grew 27 characters, all of it the rebaselined constant and the repointed docstring.

**The growth is the point rather than a cost overrun.** A cell body is charged to every firing of the cell and its depth to none, so 2,039 characters added off the body buys 6,076 characters no session pays unless its trigger is the one that needs them.

## What the review found, recorded because it changed the text

One `cold-read` seat and a `revision-diff` seat, each in its own worktree at `d328c1e`, then the defense as the terminal stage on the routine lane. **16 raw findings merged to 13; eleven fixed, one recorded, one handed to the owner.** Two were high.

**The high in the artifact is the one this change's own subject should have caught.** `## Records are exhaust` keeps the tripwire — *a PR whose only content is record bookkeeping is the tripwire: delete the record it books* — and both sentences carving an exception to it went to depth, where nothing routed a reader back. Pre-change all three sat in the file a firing session loads. A session meeting a pull request whose only content is a bank row would have read a destructive instruction with no exception; the carve-out itself names the damage. The body now names both exceptions and where each states itself. **In a change whose whole subject was routing, the one rule left unrouted was the destructive one.**

**The high in this entry is a false class definition.** The sentence above about the five exceptions read *each a clause that said* below, in this entry, in the log's index row and in the commit message — and only one of the two clauses said *below*. Reading the class that narrowly is what missed a third member of it, the bank-row analogy in `docs/cells/records/references/the-settling-row.md`, which the review found and the batch repaired. The settled artifact's own errata list had already flagged the neighbouring *three headings promoted* error, and it was written into three fresh surfaces anyway.

**The external pass reached one thing neither seat did.** CodeRabbit hit its OSS rate limit and produced nothing. Codex posted two comments: one duplicating the routing findings, and one correct on a figure — this change's settling row called itself the third for its brief where it is the second, and the pull request body called this the third of #476's symptoms to land where only `#477` has landed.

**The batch departs from two priced remedies and says so.** The ordered fix for the bare cross-cell filenames was to write them as paths from the repository root; the tree refuses that outright — `check_sideways_deps` reds on a cell naming another cell's files by path, five findings on the first run — so each file's first mention is qualified by the `adversarial-review` cell in the name form the wall permits, and the bare names stay, which is what `tools/lint.py`'s own comment says a bare filename is for. The second departure: The defense priced the body-touching fixes on the assumption that the ceiling constant is not rebaselined a second time, leaving `records` reported as over where it stood. The constant is instead set to where the body stands at landing, 2,744, because the rebaseline the map requires happens once per change and this change has not landed: a constant of 2,347 would describe a tree that never reached `main` and would report the cell as over a mark it never stood at.
