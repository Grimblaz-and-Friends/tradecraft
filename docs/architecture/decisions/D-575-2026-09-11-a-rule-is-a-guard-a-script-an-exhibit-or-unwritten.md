# D-575 — Governing prose states the concept and its reason; a rule is a guard, a script, an exhibit, or unwritten

**Landed by** [PR #575](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/575). Closes [#571](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/571), the owner's, framed at birth. Evidence pinned at `568e7de` unless stated. **Supersedes [D-261] on the fourth answer's gate**, and nothing else in it.

## What was decided

The charter's `Admitting a new requirement` sentence becomes the principle it was a rule about. **Governing prose states the concept and its reason and carries at most one example; a rule is a guard, a script, an exhibit, or unwritten** — a concept reaches the case nobody anticipated, a rule reaches only the case it names, and only if it was read first. With one guard on the principle itself: **a guard or a script is built only for a concept prose already states in one sentence.** `skills/authoring/references/routing.md` is rewritten as that principle's application — the four homes a concept takes, the four materials a rule takes instead, and the outflow read backwards off the same order.

The owner's direction was *"A bunch of rules that must be followed shouldn't really be in prose... we need better ways to handle them. prose should be high level concepts only"*, and the brief he affirmed on 2026-09-10 carries the two halves and the guard. His own sequencing follows: every other cell is swept under the principle one change at a time, each after its split has landed, except `adversarial-review` and `filing`, which #572 rewrites.

**The admission order stays named in the charter, and that is a choice.** The principle *is* the concept the order was a rule about, so restating it as a concept is what #571's `supersedes #140` tie describes; and it keeps `docs/cells/siting/SKILL.md`'s *"The charter names the admission order"* true, which a charter that dropped the phrase would falsify.

**The charter carries no example, and that is the principle applied to its own statement.** The one example the principle allows is in `routing.md`, where the guard clause needs it: the merge job built on the cell-body ceiling, retired while the question of what a body ought to weigh stays open [#328] [D-544].

### The meaning change: the fourth answer's gate reverses

**This is the one thing a reader could miss, and it is the brief's own term.** The fourth answer at an outflow read *"lawful against evidence that the rule does not bind"* — measured non-compliance, a falsified premise, a superseding mechanism. It is now **lawful where the sentence is none of the four materials and states no concept**, the change naming which it was; a second example of a concept already illustrated is such a sentence.

[D-261] is read and knowingly superseded, not dropped. Its reasoning stands on its own terms: ungated deletion takes the cheapest line rather than the one that has earned it, and the *never drop* clause before it had to move because every commit that reduced the surface it governs grew this practice's governing prose overall in the same commit. What changes is that the gate is no longer *evidence about the sentence's effect*, which nobody can produce for a sentence nobody has measured, but *the sentence's form*, which the reader in front of it can apply. The argument for the old gate goes to D-261 rather than being retold here, which is the principle's third material doing its job. Its own history — the measurement, and the clause that read *never drop* — was retold in `routing.md` and is now the citation alone.

### Three sites the principle falsifies, repaired with it

Two are the same sentence in two voices. `skills/authoring/SKILL.md`'s routing summary said content routes *"a binding rule"* to the always-on surface, which the principle makes false — what goes there is what must hold before any context loads, and a rule goes to one of the four materials. `tools/lint.py`'s always-on-row finding told a session at a full ceiling that the fourth answer is *"reached only against evidence that the rule does not bind"*; that is the sentence such a session actually reads, and under this change it is the second material doing its own job, so it now states the form test. The third is `docs/cells/siting/SKILL.md`'s *"the three moves, the evidence-gated fourth"*.

### A call the session made: `siting` was touched against the brief's letter

The affirmed brief says `routing.md` *"is the only cell this change touches"*, and `siting` is a cell. **The reading taken is that the sentence scopes the sweep** — which cells get rewritten under the principle — **and that repairing a pointer this change itself falsifies is not a sweep but the cost of the change.** The alternative was to ship a false sentence in the repo-only surface that routes every siting question here. Under the `engagement` cell's fork test nothing the owner lives with turns on it, so it was decided and is reported rather than put as an ask. The repair is confined to the stale clause; no other sentence in `siting` moves.

## What was rejected

- **Dropping the admission-order clause from the charter** on the ground that the principle replaces it. It would have falsified `siting`'s opening sentence and lost the one thing the paragraph did that the principle does not state on its own: the ordering.
- **Putting the example on the charter.** The principle allows one; the charter is the most expensive surface there is, and a principle that spends its own example on its own statement is the argument against itself.
- **Writing the guard clause as *a mechanism*.** The third cold seat observed it was wider than the brief's *"a check or a script"*, which is what the owner affirmed. Narrowed back. *Mechanism* is the better word and this change is not the place to widen a rule past what was affirmed.
- **Building a guard for the principle.** Its own guard clause forbids exactly that until a concept is stated in one sentence — which this change is what states, so the earliest a mechanism could be argued is after it lands.
