## The condition

The question *is the review pipeline worth what it costs* had been argued five times since 2026-08-18 and answered each time with *it finds true things*, which is not the question. What the record showed by 2026-09-03:

- **Cost concentrates after round one.** PR #336's close-out: the panel saturated in one round; **six cycles ran against a budget of two, and five of the six fix batches introduced a fresh false or imprecise claim — every one in the decision entry or its index row, none in the shipped skill text** ([issuecomment-5521380432](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/138#issuecomment-5521380432)). The batch that ended the loop was the one that deleted claims rather than repairing them.
- **#143's field data:** *"the panel earned its cost in round one and the two post-fix cycles did not."*
- **Most findings hit the record, not what ships.** #122's deposit on PR #132: 24 of 42 merged findings against the change's record, 18 against shipped text, both highs in the record. PR #314's review note states it verbatim — *"what failed was the write-up"*; #320's and #322's say it in their own words; #336's four highs were in the shipped width paragraph and cut the other way.
- **Volume measures the instrument.** #138 spike 3: six findings before a fifty-fix batch and six after; severity moved, volume did not.
- **Nothing automated ever ended a runaway cycle.** #48, #93, #290 and #305 each ended with the owner asking whether it was still buying anything.

**D-96 already named the mechanism, and stopped one step short of this conclusion.** It classified a post-fix cycle's findings by generator and wrote of the third — the review's own conduct — that it *"does not deplete at all while the artifact's author runs the review; each cycle generates fresh conduct for the next to find,"* and that *"asking generator 3 to reach zero is asking for something unreachable while the author reviews their own work."* D-96's answer was to scope the terminus so generator 3 could not extend the cycle. This change's answer is that a loop whose only non-depleting generator is the reviewing itself should not be run at all past the round that pays.

**What the record does not support cutting, carried so a later session does not read this entry as licence for more.** The width recomputation puts four seats at 90–96% retention of attributed sustained highs ([issuecomment-5519548671](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/138#issuecomment-5519548671)). Round one of a differentiated panel is not shown to be waste, and this change does not touch it.

## The decision

**A review is one round.** The panel or the routine lane's cold pass, the defense, the terminal stage, the fix batch, and an executable floor on the fixes — once each, then the review closes. What removed: the post-fix prosecution look and its defense, the floor that could never be recorded or handed on, that look's scope bound, the count terminus, the cycle, the cycle budget round one's ruling named, the relevance terminus, and the escalation to the owner at budget.

**What replaces them is use, not another pass.** The executable floor — suites, lint, and each sustained finding's own probe re-derived against the fixed tree; then the experience session the batch buys on that tree [D-295]; then friction met under merged material in ordinary later work as the standing intake, routed to the board as a filing.

**Direction and the three riders are the owner's, admitted rather than argued** ([#360](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/360), 2026-09-03). His counter to the session's first proposal — which was to drop the panel to a single cold pass — was to keep the panel and cut everything after it, on the ground that it isolates the variable. He was right and the session said so at the time.

- **The change's own record is held to honesty, not precision** — decision entry, pull request body, commit message, index row. A false claim there is a finding; an imprecise or unfavourably worded one is not.
- **A finding enters the fix batch only where its stated consequence is one a consumer would live** — an action taken wrongly, or a failure that would occur. Otherwise `record`.
- **`operational` retargets** to the deletion lens: what could be removed without any consumer behaving differently.

## The four calls the artifact made, and what each rejected

- **The floor owes nothing for a sustained finding that carried no probe**, and the report names what the floor could not reach. Rejected: having the floor manufacture a check at fix time, which is a fresh instrument answering a question no stage asked and would return a prosecution pass under the floor's name.
- **The experience session's note routes to a fix by the session that reads it, and buys no further look** — its own batch owes the floor and nothing else; a decline needs a stage that is not the session that built the change, which outside a review the charter already places. Rejected: routing the note back into a chartered fix batch, which reopens the cycle under another name.
- **The honesty standard is worded on the axis false/imprecise rather than on load.** Rejected: a load test, because on the record every claim is low-load by construction and such a test would exclude the false ones too.
- **Carried recorded findings are ruled by the terminal stage**, once per review rather than once per seat, while every seat still reads them. Rejected: the merge, which is the dispatching session's and may be the change's author, where the charter reserves a decline to a stage that is not. [#343]

## Meaning changes, named because a diff will not show them

- **A retired lens was rehomed, not dropped.** *Prosecuting the review's own record is a retired lens* lived inside the deleted post-fix paragraph, scoped to that look. It now sits in the evidence standards, where it binds every stage. Its reason moved with it.
- **The honesty rule reaches neither the pre-implementation artifact nor the brief it carries.** The artifact's first revision included them and a cold seat returned `would not`: those are what an implementer builds from and what every review stage judges against, so a false or imprecise claim there misaims all of them, which is the opposite of the read-once reason the rule rests on.
- **`arbitration.md`'s consequence-shape paragraph lost two clauses** — the relevance-terminus reference, and *"so a cycle is never stopped early by a finding that also reaches the work,"* whose subject no longer exists. The axis survives; the report's split reads it, and so does `docs/cells/records/SKILL.md`'s `target` field. **Whether it should survive is a live question**, recorded on the amendment above rather than settled here, and put to this review's `operational` seat, which makes the change its own first test of the lens it lands.
- **`roster.md`'s width table is qualified, not recomputed.** Its corpus is historical and was produced by panels whose `operational` seat walked the artifact as its consumer; what the deletion lens does to those figures is unmeasured. Deleting the table would drop the only measurement the width question has.
- **`dispatch.md` lost three instructions** naming the removed stage. The first artifact revision missed the file entirely; the cold seat's second blocking point is what found it.
- **`docs/cells/records/SKILL.md`'s `cost.dispatches`** no longer counts the cycles.

## The ceiling, and the outflow that was applied rather than asserted

`skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`'s body went **937 characters over** its ceiling — the figure re-derived against the tree this change's review-fix batch produced, which is 195 characters shorter than the tree the panel read — and the overage is **admitted** on `docs/admissions.jsonl`. The outflow found no move for either new rule: the honesty rider binds what a seat files, and `arbitration.md` — the only reference that could host a disposition rule — states outright that a prosecution seat does not need it, so a rule seats must obey cannot live where seats never read; the one-round shape must be readable when the lane is chosen, before any reference opens. It found two shed candidates in *existing* sections, `Choosing the shape` and `Closing a recurring class`, both of which pass the disqualifier and either of which would have fitted this addition. Both are restructurings of the cell that this change's affirmed boundary does not carry, and the restructuring — not an absence of candidates — is the reason recorded on the row. [#357]'s row said nothing sheds without restructuring; that was re-checked here rather than inherited.

## What is knowingly given up

**The post-fix look on PR #336 caught four defects in that change's record that were about to freeze on merge.** Under this rule nothing catches those, and the duty moves onto the fix batch's writer, which is why the re-derivation duty stays and why a batch departing from an ordered remedy must now say so [#208] — with no look after it, the departure is stated in the batch or nowhere.

**This is a trial, and it is scored.** #360 opens the scoring when this merges and closes it at three weeks or fifteen scored reviews, comparing defects found in use against shipped cells with the three weeks before. If the rate does not rise, the cycles were the waste; if it rises, the price is known and the look is bought back where it earned it. Nothing here claims the look was worthless — [PR #263](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/263)'s two post-fix cycles repaired a false claim and an unquoted command placeholder in `skills/experience-session/`, both shipped — only that the record does not show it paying for what it costs, and that the way to find out is to run the arm nobody has run.

**This change's own review runs under the rule it lands — one round** — as the owner ruled, rather than paying the old price to land the new.
