# D-376: The charter holds what must hold before any cell loads, and a compressed copy of a cell's rule is worse than a pointer

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-04 (PR #376)

## Context

[#303](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/303) was filed on the reading that the charter is *silent* in four places — that a session holding it alone has no route — and every comment on it argued for adding prose. The owner corrected the premise in conversation: the success criterion is about an adopting repository that has no doctrine of its own, not about a session that has loaded no skills, and *"the charter should never be able to function without skills."*

Two things already on the record say the same and neither had been cited on the issue. [D-184](D-184-2026-08-25-outflow-and-the-always-on-total.md) defines the adopter surface — *"An adopter reads the charter and the roster's descriptions and nothing else of this practice's"* — which is confirmed by arithmetic: on `fcbb27d` `python tools/lint.py` reports 11,046 for an adopter, and the nine shipped cells' `name` plus `description` sum to 5,195 against a charter body of 5,851. Exactly. **The population that holds the charter and no roster does not exist**, so all four recorded gaps argue from an empty set. And [D-169](D-169-2026-08-25-charter-exemption-and-cell-standards.md) records the owner's ruling on the axis in his own words: *"without its references the charter's value drops immensely."*

## Decision

**The charter states an obligation and names the cell that owns the rest.** What belongs in it is *what must hold before any context loads*; a rule a cell can hold goes to that cell. This is not a new rule and is admitted as none: `skills/authoring/references/routing.md:6` already routes binding rules there as *"the last resort, after a mechanical check and after skill prose"* and defines the residency test in those words, and `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md:7` gives the charter its privilege *"because it is loaded in every session already, so the citation costs no loading and cannot drift as a second copy can."* The charter was the surface breaking it.

**The failure mode is compression, not size, and that is what makes a copy worse than a pointer.** A compressed copy answers a session's own check — *do I know this rule?* — and thereby suppresses the read of the full text, while what compression drops is the limb that decides the case. Five instances were verified by reading each charter passage against its owning cell. The sharpest is the argued ask, whose *"the live options"* omitted `engagement`'s *"Where exactly one option is live, say so… a fabricated second option under-informs worse than none"* and so pointed a reader at the defect the full rule forbids. The fork test omitted *"Both halves are required"* and read as either/or.

**The incident is this change's own drafting session**, which is why this is admitted at all — the charter refuses an agent-proposed rule absent an incident from real work, and *"a review finding about governing prose is not an incident."* That session wrote three briefs against the charter's compressed form spec. Scored against the six rules at `skills/engagement/SKILL.md:16-21`, the two the charter carried were broken once between them and the four carried only in the cell were broken seven times.

**The test keeps as well as it cuts, and that is load-bearing rather than incidental.** What has no other home stayed whole: the positioning paragraph; Authority; the mechanical-work exemption, whose only other carrier is repo-only `docs/cells/landing` and so reaches no adopter; and the whole fix-bar paragraph, every sentence of which fires *outside* a review where no cell is loaded — including its own *"Outside a review no such stage exists."*

**One statement was corrected rather than routed.** `exactly` left *"Process weight concentrates at exactly two moments"*, which PR #364's two-round cold-check cap had made false and which recorded row 194 names along with two further claims narrowed by the same cap.

**Two shared standards got one owner apiece.** The both-polarities rule moved whole to `skills/substrate` — the cell a session fires while *writing* a guard, which [#196](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/196) established was carrying the weaker half — taking its negative-control requirement and the [#142](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/142) incident with it, since that incident is a guard's probe being masked and `skills/authoring/references/revising.md` holds that a rule moving without its reason strands the clause a later reader needs. The general figure rule stays with `authoring`; `engagement` dropped its copy and kept only the artifact's own freeze, which is that cell's. `cell-structure.md:7` stopped offering *"routes up to the charter"* as coequal with single-owner, which the last-resort rule already contradicted.

**Removing duplicated text is answered at the ceiling by banking, not by spending.** The move put `adversarial-review` back under its 9,000-character body ceiling with 121 characters still admitted against it, and `tools/lint.py` raised that itself as *"room nobody argued for"*. A row was appended to `docs/admissions.jsonl` with `chars: -121`.

## Rejected

- **Adding the four recorded gaps as charter prose**, which is what #303 was filed to do. Each asks the charter to carry *more* compressed procedure; granting them would have manufactured further instances of the class producing the failures. Rows 82, 85/93 and 94 are dispositioned here rather than fixed.
- **Record-search licensing as the method for removing a passage**, which the first affirmed brief named and which was run before being abandoned. Applying `skills/spikes/references/licensing-a-deletion.md`'s record search to seven candidate passages totalling 1,633 characters, one returned no citation — it would have licensed 135. **The search finds text that was argued about and cannot find text that was obeyed**, because compliance leaves no citation; the deletion rule states the asymmetry itself. The one passage it cleared was the charter's statement of the brief's form, whose class was producing that session's defects as it read.
- **Narrowing the deletion rule to its stated scope so passages could be removed on judgment**, recommended and then withdrawn: it inherits the same blind spot and gives up the over-keeping safety, and it turned out unnecessary once replacing a compressed copy with a route was seen not to be an ablation at all. `licensing-a-deletion.md`'s stated harm is *"a session routed where the text existed to stop it"*, and a route sends the session to **more** of the rule than the copy held.
- **Relocating the positioning paragraph to `README.md`.** Asserted, then falsified: root files land in a consumer's cache as inert files and are never loaded ([D-156](D-156-2026-08-24-installable-plugin-and-shipped-charter.md), D-184, both owner-verified with a live control), and `tools/lint.py:1572` calls the README *"the front door"* — for someone browsing the repository, not a session in someone else's.
- **Relocating the fix-bar paragraph to `adversarial-review`.** Asserted, falsified by grep — that cell carries neither *"whatever its scope"* nor *"recorded, not filed"* — and then found unnecessary on the residency test. Both this and the bullet above were the same error: checking that text exists somewhere rather than that the reader receives it.

## Left open

**Whether a route is lawful only where its destination reaches the reader the source was serving.** Two routes in this change failed exactly that and were caught by hand. `cell-structure.md`'s one-owner rule says nothing about it, and the check that caught them is criterion 1 of this change's artifact rather than a rule anywhere. Not filed as its own issue; it is the natural next reading of the rule this entry lands.

**The cold-seat standard**, stated operationally in four cells with no two alike and none owning it. Put out of scope by the owner in the affirmed brief and filed as [#375](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/375) with the divergence measured; `sequenced-after` this issue, since the residency rule constrains one of the shapes it can take.

**The residual on the both-polarities move.** `adversarial-review` keeps the red-pin rule and the behaviour-claim standard at `:40` and no longer states the negative control itself. #196's deferred list had left open whether the two are one rule or two; this settled them as one, on the ground that #142 is the requirement's reason rather than a neighbouring incident.
