## What was decided

[#542](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/542) shipped four rules and left two terms unsettled, both marked in its affirmed brief as the session's rather than the owner's. He ruled both on 2026-09-09, each put to him argued with options and a recommendation.

**An amendment to governing prose seats `revision-diff` beside the cold pass — one seat, not a panel.** And the two undefined names in that bullet collapse to one, `substantially new prose`, which is the roster's own term for the shape it selects on.

The bullet carried *"newly written foundational prose"* as the panel's trigger and *"substantially new prose"* as half of the fifth seat's, neither defined anywhere. Before #542 the ambiguity was absorbed by *"when in doubt, this lane"* pointing at the panel: a session that could not tell fell **up** to four seats. #542 moved that clause to the routine lane, so doubt fell **down** to one and the undefined term became the sole gate.

**The evidence is measured, and it is not the incident this entry first cited.** A recorded finding on the term already had a consumer session filing a change as the routine lane and reaching five only by reasoning from claim density. Then [PR #545](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/545) — itself an amendment to governing prose — took the one-seat lane under its own new rule and **did not seat this lens**. Two of its sustained findings were nonetheless that class, and its own cold pass and defense found them; its external pass reached one of the two independently. **The lane did not lose them, and this entry's first draft said it did** — `docs/reviews.jsonl`'s row for that review records the external comments as duplicates of internal findings, and its own decision entry records the two as internally found. The review of *this* change is the stronger datum, and it is the first run with the lens seated: **six of its seventeen merged findings are the class** — a sentence whose characters never changed while a term it turns on was redefined elsewhere in the change — against the two a one-seat pass found on #545. That is the rule measured on the axis it was written for.

**Which way the collapse moved the panel's population is undetermined, and this records that rather than claiming it.** The two names are not nested: the retired one required prose to be *foundational*, the surviving one requires only that it be *substantially new*. So a small piece of foundational prose stops buying a panel and a large non-foundational one starts, and no count exists either way. The surviving name is defined nowhere, and that is filed as a cause rather than settled here.

**The trigger for the seat, as against the panel, needs no new definition.** `roster.md` already fixes *governing prose* for this choice in the broad sense — *"prose a later session is expected to act on, reaching a docstring that states a calling contract and not confined to always-on surfaces and cells"* — which is what [#236](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/236) closed on.

**Two further edits inside that bullet, recorded here rather than left to be re-derived.** *Five where a panel already bought is both…* returns to *Five where the artifact is both…* — the dropped precondition is entailed once one name buys the panel, and the hedge existed only to paper over the contradiction the collapse now removes. And *It also buys a seat beyond the applicable count* becomes *A declared risk also buys…*, restoring the referent the sentence carried before #545 pronominalised it.

**The cap of three causes per review goes.** Two grounds, and the second is the one that decides it.

- **It never bound.** Two experience sessions on #542's material, twelve findings each, one filing each; and #545's own review disposed of nine findings and filed nothing at all. The three rules ahead of it emptied the queue before it was ever reached. There is no run in which it did anything.
- **Where it would bind, it wrote a falsehood into a record nothing may correct.** A fourth cause reaches the cap only by having *passed* the filing bar — for governing prose, an incident from real work or a run — so somebody has judged it worth someone's time. The cap then ruled it `record`, which `arbitration.md` defines as *"the evidence that acting is worth it not yet there"*, and `the-record.md` as *"by construction a worth-it judgment the evidence did not settle."* Both are false of a capped-out cause. Its only exit was recurrence, which for a one-off subject may never arrive.

**#545's review sustained that as a finding and did not fix it**, on the terminal stage's own recommendation that it ride the owner's ruling on the cap instead. Struck, the finding is discharged with no fix owed — which is what that deferral was for.

## What was rejected

**Adding *amendment to governing prose* to the panel buyers.** It was rejected while #542 was being built and again here. Nearly every change in this repository amends governing prose, so it re-inflates panel use to something close to universal and defeats what #542 was for. Seating one extra seat is a fraction of that cost and targets the same lens.

**Defining the two names as two separate bars.** More precise and more words, on a cell already at its constant. One name for one bar is what the roster already assumes.

**Keeping the cap with a reconciling clause.** That was the alternative if the owner kept it: a sentence distinguishing a capped-out cause from an ordinary record entry. It buys prose on a governing surface to repair a rule that has never fired.

**Replacing the cap with a reporting duty** — *a review filing more than three causes says why in its report* — was offered and not taken. Nothing now caps filings; the three rules that do the work are the record route, the text-only limb, and filing by cause.
