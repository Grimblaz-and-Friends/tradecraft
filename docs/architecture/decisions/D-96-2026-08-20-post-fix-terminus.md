# D-96: The post-fix cycle's terminus, and a budget that asks rather than stops

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-20 (PR #96)

## Context

`skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md` ended the post-fix cycle "when a look's findings are all dismissed or all priced out." That condition has no reachable terminus, and [PR #90](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/90)'s review is the exhibit: two full cycles in which it moved *away* from satisfaction. Round 1 sustained one high; post-fix cycle 1 sustained two. **The basis, because the raw labels give a different number:** these are highs whose remedy changes the artifact, counted as merged findings per the terminal stage's one-ruling-per-merged-finding rule. Cycle 1's ruling carries three merged sustained highs — P1, {P9 + O-D1}, P10 — of which P1's remedy was the PR body and not the artifact, leaving two. Reading the ruling's `Sustained, high` labels without merging returns four. Severity rose between rounds. The session discovered this only by running long enough to hit it.

It also contradicted a rule twelve lines below it. The next-pass default states the same decision as "stop once a pass has sustained no high it will fix." Two conditions for one decision, with nothing saying which governs.

**Three generators, one rule.** Findings in a post-fix cycle come from sources that behave nothing alike, and the old sentence treated them identically.

1. **The artifact.** Genuine defects in the text under review. **Depletes** — round 1 found F1, cycle 1 found its residue.
2. **The record.** Stale claims in the decision entry, the PR body, the index row, the issues. **Regenerated by every fix**: change a rule and every site describing it goes stale. Converges only if each batch fixes all description sites at once.
3. **The review's own conduct.** Charter compression in dispatches, findings buried in the merge, aimed attention delivered to a cold seat. **Does not deplete at all** while the artifact's author runs the review; each cycle generates fresh conduct for the next to find. #90 produced **seven** instances of one sub-class alone — aimed attention reaching a cold seat under a relabelled slot — the last being the judge finding the defect in the dispatch sent to the judge; and its final report books two further classes fitting this same generator, the parent-owned merge altering what reached the terminal stage three times, and eight consecutive dispatches breaking the four words amendment 2 added.

Asking generator 3 to reach zero is asking for something unreachable while the author reviews their own work. That is why the cycle did not terminate. Filed as [#93](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/93).

## Decision

**The cycle ends when a look sustains no high it will fix whose remedy changes the artifact**, and a finding whose remedy is not a change to the artifact is recorded and routed, never buying a further round. **The mandatory floor is scoped to match**: every fix batch *the cycle produces before it ends* gets its prosecution look, which exempts the closing batch the terminating ruling orders and nothing else. **Until then the cycle continues without asking**, so round one's terminal ruling names the cycle budget, and **exceeding it puts the review to the owner in that cycle's ruling rather than ending it.**

The severity clause borrows the next-pass default's exact phrase — *"no high it will fix"* — rather than paraphrasing it, so the severity half of the two conditions is identical rather than agreeing by luck. **They are not one condition, and the first version of this paragraph said they were.** What the post-fix cycle adds on top of the borrowed phrase is the remedy restriction, and that restriction is exactly where the two diverge: a look sustaining one high the review will fix whose remedy is record-only ends the cycle under this rule while the next-pass default would not stop. This change's own review found the claim and struck it from the rule's gloss; it is corrected here too, because a session is sent to this entry before it changes what the rule governs. A high sustained-and-priced-out produces no fix batch, so there is nothing for a post-fix look to validate; the shared wording carries that without a second clause.

**The remedy test keys on the remedy, never on the evidence.** *Does the fix change the artifact?* is checkable; *is this about conduct?* is not. #90's O-D1 was discovered through the review's conduct but names a genuine artifact gap, and must still extend the cycle; #90's P12 named only conduct — the judge wrote that the artifact is right and the dispatch is wrong — and must not. #90's judge already reasoned this way with no rule authorising it, which is the usual sign a rule is describing practice rather than inventing it.

**The rule names the vehicle — the cycle's own ruling — and this is the second version of that clause, because the first one was wrong.** As first landed it said only "puts the review to the owner", on the reasoning that the skill's pause discipline already owned the mechanism. It does not. Typed-halt's atomic window runs "from first dispatch to final ruling", spanning every post-fix cycle; its vehicle is the final report, which does not exist at a cycle boundary; and its subject is a *finding*, which a budget overrun is not. This change's own review found it, and the sharper form of the objection is that a check deferred to the final report is not a check at all but a postscript on a review that has already run to completion — which is the owner's "continuing forever" with a footnote. The vehicle it now names is one the terminal stage already mandates: rulings are posted to the PR before the fix batch begins, and both of #90's cycle rulings carried an owner section at a cycle boundary. The practice was already right; the skill did not say so.

## The live test failed, and that is why the budget is here

#93 scheduled #90's post-fix cycle 2 as the proposal's live test, before the rule was asserted. It ran. [The cycle-2 ruling](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/90#issuecomment-5362809986) answered negatively, and the result is recorded rather than smoothed:

- The **remedy test** routed out **5 of 15** findings — the first mechanism that has ever closed generator 3 at all.
- The **severity floor** did **not** stop the cycle: two highs were sustained and fixed, so cycle 3 ran regardless.
- What kept the cycle alive was **generator 2** — six of ten artifact-remedy findings were stale claims inside one unlanded decision entry, regenerated by earlier fixes to that same entry.
- **Neither rule terminated the review.** The owner did, after cycle 3, at a cycle boundary, on argued options.

**Generator 2 gets no rule.** The cycle-2 judge ruled it a discipline failure against the evidence standard already on the books — *"land the fix where the claim is read as well as where it is written"* — and the admission order says the cheapest reliable material wins. A second rule for a rule already written is the wrong material.

**The budget's shape is inverted from the one #93 held in reserve.** #93 described a terminus: exceeding the budget "goes to the owner" as a way of *ending* a runaway review, and priced it as arbitrary and largely redundant against the owner as merge gate. The owner amended in conversation on 2026-08-20 — the cycle continues without asking, and the budget triggers a check rather than a stop. That inversion cost the "arbitrary" objection most of its force: an arbitrary number that decides *when to ask a human* is a far cheaper thing to be wrong about than one that decides when a review is over. And it is not a hypothetical mechanism — what actually ended #90 was the owner answering a question at a cycle boundary. The rule writes down the thing that already worked, instead of hoping a findings-based condition reaches a case that has now demonstrably outrun one.

**Declared in round one's ruling, before anyone knows how bad the review will get.** Declaring up front is the whole of its unfakeability; a budget named after the cycles are counted is a rationalization. **The unit is cycles** — countable and declarable in advance, where findings or tokens would be more precise and less checkable, and the number's only job is to time a question.

## Rejected and dropped

**A findings-based terminus alone**, without the budget. Rejected on #90: it did not terminate a review that ran four rounds, and nothing in the two rules would have.

**Patching the collision inside the new sentence, rather than scoping the floor.** Tried and rejected on evidence, in that order. The floor's sentence is an emphatic universal — "**Every** fix batch … **never priced away**" — and the terminus fires while a look is still ordering mediums and lows it will fix, so something had to give. The first attempt asserted that a terminating look's fixes are validated by the ruling that orders them, and this change's own post-fix cycle sustained a high showing the collision survived it: the clause carried no exception operator, so both mandates still applied on their face. Hardening it to "rather than by a further look" closed that, but at the cost of two clauses doing one rule's work and a sentence carved out of a rule that says it has no exceptions. The repository's external reviewer had prescribed the scoping fix from the text alone, thirty-one minutes before the ruling that chose the patch and without sight of any internal finding: *"the floor must explicitly exempt these fixes."* The owner took the scoping option; the patch clause came back out, and the change shrank by thirteen words in its last fix batch.

**What that decides, stated rather than smuggled: the closing fix batch of a review no longer gets a mandatory prosecution look.** That is a real reduction in coverage. It is accepted because the terminal stage has, by rule, just re-read the artifact and re-executed the contested probes in order to reach that ruling; because the closing batch is by construction the smallest and least structural of the review; and because the owner is the merge gate behind it. The floor's guarantee is untouched in the sense that matters — what changed is which batches it reaches, never whether it can be priced away.

**A rule for generator 2.** Rejected on the admission order; the evidence standard exists and was breached, which is a discipline problem, not a coverage gap.

**A default budget number in the skill.** Rejected: reviews differ by lane and blast radius, and a number in standing prose would be obeyed where a ruling's number is argued.

**Dropped from the old sentence:** *"it turns on what findings are worth, not on a clean look, which the record shows is an asymptote."* The warning against demanding a clean look survives inside "no high **it will fix**". The asymptote evidence does not, and is recorded here rather than deleted — carefully, because the instrument is thinner than it looks. `docs/reviews.jsonl` books a `post-fix` seat in only **2 of its 6 rows** (PR #81 at 7 raw, PR #80 at 16); the other four, PR #90 among them, record no post-fix seat at all, so the index cannot answer the general question and no universal is claimed from it. What is on the record: every post-fix look whose count is written down anywhere returned findings — those two, plus #90's three cycles at 25, 19 and 8 raw. Five looks, none clean. That is why a clean look was never the bar and must not become one.

## What this does not fix

Generator 3 itself. The durable answer is [#91](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/91) — moving review orchestration off the artifact's author. Seven instances of one sub-class in a single review, alongside three merge alterations and a 0/8 obedience record, is the strongest evidence that issue will get. This decision bounds the symptom; #91 removes the cause.

## Cost

+49 standing words, 2,652 → 2,701 — the sentence itself 34 → 77, plus six words on the floor sentence. It peaked at +62 mid-review and came back down: option (C) deleted a nineteen-word patch clause against a six-word insertion, so the last fix batch of this change **removed** standing prose. The skill's own charter makes net growth a finding with the burden on keeping the words. The defense, recounted against the shipped sentence rather than against the draft: it carries **five** rules where it carried one — ends-when, recorded-and-routed, continues-without-asking, round-one-names-the-budget, and exceeding-goes-to-the-owner — three of them instructions a session acts on directly, plus the floor's scope clause. **The compression argument that stood here has been withdrawn rather than repaired.** It claimed the drafting had shrunk a +51 draft by deleting what the pause discipline and the next-pass default already said. Both halves failed: the pause discipline said nothing about a budget overrun at a cycle boundary, as recorded above, so there was nothing of its to delete and the vehicle had to be written back in; and the sentence briefly grew past the draft the clause claimed to improve on. The honest cost statement is the figure itself, defended on what it buys — a terminus a review can actually reach, agreeing with the next-pass default instead of contradicting it, and an interrupt that bounds the case where neither fires.
