# D-166: Acceptance criteria state consumer behavior, and each names the check that would falsify it

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-25 (PR #166)

## Context

The owner asked, on 2026-08-23, why judging findings against a document's purpose was not helping. [#144](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/144) is the answer: the review's terminal clause (a) reads its success criteria from the affirmed acceptance criteria, and the rule producing those criteria was asking for the wrong thing.

### The rule as it stood

`skills/engagement/SKILL.md` at `b9bcdeb`: *"Write each one so a reader can tell from the diff whether it is met — a criterion nobody can check is a wish."*

True as far as it goes, and **no decision entry establishes it** — [D-155](D-155-2026-08-24-measured-figure-lawful-in-the-artifact.md) cites it only to decline touching it, ceding it here — so this entry supersedes nothing by number. What it reaches, though, is conformance: what a diff can show is that a count is right, a section is present, a wording is faithful, a file is untouched. `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md:14` at `b9bcdeb` then makes those criteria the success definition every review stage inherits, and `:69` at the same commit has clause (a) dismiss only what impairs no criterion. **`skills/charter/SKILL.md:31` at `b9bcdeb` carries the same hinge** — *"for an implementation, the affirmed acceptance criteria are the success definition"* — in the always-on surface an adopting repository receives first and its owner may amend; it is byte-identical here, and it is the second surface this mechanism turns on. A bookkeeping finding impairs a conformance criterion, so the filter sustains it — **because the criteria told it to**. The volume the practice has been paying for at review time was ratified upstream, at convergence.

### What was measured

The deliverable was a wording, so `skills/spikes`' trigger fired and a cold-seat A/B ran during drafting. Two arms differing in exactly the criteria bullet; two real dockets; then clause (a) applied to twelve of **PR #132's own** independently-verified findings, once against each arm's criteria. Six dispatches, Opus 5, Claude Code (Windows), throwaway.

Arm B wrote **12, every one behavioral with a named check**, and kept the executable class.

**Clause (a) on D1, D24, D5, D15, D20, D28, D31, D32, D33, D23, D22, D3:** Arm A dismissed **5 of 12**, Arm B **8 of 12**. The delta is three findings: **two record-accuracy** — D28 (a seat count restated 2-of-6 as 2-of-2) and D32 (a docket size stated as nine) — and **one against shipped prose**, D23, the compression at `skills/spikes/SKILL.md:18` that PR #132's ruling classed as dropping *argument, not history* and fixed under its own *"Stage 3 — the shipped cell"* header. Under Arm A each was sustained *by naming Arm A's own criteria 13 or 14*, both conformance. **The executable class survived both arms** (D1). Of the five shipped-prose findings on the docket — D5, D15, D20, D22, D23 — **Arm A kept four and Arm B kept three**; both dismissed D20, a false claim in the shipped cell. **The narrowing's measured cost is that one shipped-prose finding, and it is not zero.** This paragraph is a correction: the affirmed artifact and this entry's first draft both said the delta was record-accuracy throughout, and PR #166's review panel falsified it against PR #132's own stage headers.

**On the second docket** — issue #133, which carries an open fork — Arm B's seat wrote unprompted *"If the owner rules that the table has no order, this criterion is empty on that branch"* and stated a per-branch falsifier on two more; Arm A produced defensible contingent criteria and no per-branch falsifier. 9 criteria against 7.

**The shape was already being invented by hand.** PR #155's own [artifact](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/152#issuecomment-5390811885) carries behavioral criteria 1–4 and assigns them to the experience session in **one closing line covering the group** — *"Criteria 1–4 are checked by the experience session the boundary buys. 5–7 are diff- or command-checkable"* — with no instrument named on any individual criterion. The classification is the shape this change generalises; the per-criterion naming bullet 2 now requires is **not** what that artifact does, and its own criterion 5 is pure conformance. [D-155](D-155-2026-08-24-measured-figure-lawful-in-the-artifact.md) separately records a cold seat reaching this thesis from the author's side and rewriting a criterion D-155 describes as *"passes on any wording"*.

## Decision

**1. Criteria state consumer behavior; conformance is the exception that argues for itself.** Each criterion is something whoever works under the built result can do, or no longer does wrongly — most often a session, and the gloss is exemplary rather than definitional, because this practice ships to repositories whose result a person or a service consumes. A criterion about the artifact conforming is lawful **where it argues its own consumer-facing load, which it states** — written for its own sake it ratifies the bookkeeping class the review can then no longer dismiss, for want of a criterion it impairs. **The cell states that consequence in its own words rather than naming the review's clause**, which a self-contained cell may not reach. This is what gives clause (a) teeth, and **it buys them with no change to the review**: `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md` is byte-identical to `main`, which the measurement above is the whole warrant for.

**2. Diff-checkability narrows to falsifiability with a named instrument.** *Each criterion names the check that would falsify it* — the diff for what the diff settles, a command for what a command runs, and — for what only use shows — putting the situations to a session that has none of the change's history, or the built result in use. **The third instrument is glossed operationally rather than named**: *cold docket* was the drafting term and it is constructible only from cells this one may not reference, so a criterion could have carried a named check no session could build. **This narrows the old sentence rather than repealing it**, and *a criterion nobody can check is a wish* survives verbatim: what changes is that the diff stops being the only lawful instrument, not that checkability stops being required. **Two sentences in the bullet are byte-unchanged and stay so**: *"They state what must be true of the result, not what it may cost"*, which carries [D-119]'s and [D-155]'s cost rule and not the checkability axis; and the figure rule, **relocated into its own bullet with no change to its text**. The relocation is why the bullet the artifact called one is four in the diff, and `skills/engagement/SKILL.md:51` permits the work covering more.

**3. A criterion a plausible wrong implementation would also pass measures nothing** — say what failing looks like before writing it. Where the artifact carries a fork, a criterion held to survive it must be falsifiable on **every** branch it survives, because the cheapest cross-branch criterion is one that is empty on a branch — stated as a diagnosis, never as a method, which is the form the affirmed artifact used and which an external reviewer read the first shipped wording as inverting. This closes the second surface **PR #155's terminal ruling routed to #144 as N8** — [D-155] cedes only the diff-checkability sentence and says nothing about branches. **#152's affirmed artifact**, not that entry, carried the criterion requiring the sentence be byte-identical to `main`, and it was, so nothing here contradicts it.

**4. Naming the check does not order it run.** Put to the owner as the one fork, because the Arm B seat declared "Run owed" against 8 of its 12 criteria unprompted — up to eight cold dockets per change. **He ruled no.** The criterion states how the claim could be falsified and stops there; what a change owes in runs is owned by `skills/experience-session` and by `AGENTS.md`'s flow line, and a third statement of one duty is the drift [D-155]'s item 4 closed.

## Rejected

- **A rule about how many criteria to write.** #144 left count guidance open. Declined by the session and reported: the shape rule cut counts on its own in both dockets — 15→12 and 9→7 — so a number is a second rule for an effect the first already produces, and [#143](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/143)'s evidence is that instruments game counts rather than obey them.
- **Strengthening clause (a) to dismiss the conformance class directly.** This was the alternative to changing the criteria, and the run is the argument against it: the filter needed no amendment to shed three findings, and two findings both arms dismissed (D24 and D20, sustained by the real panel at high and strong-medium) say one A/B is not enough evidence to touch a review stage.
- **The change owing a run, or a recorded decline, per behavioral criterion.** Put to the owner and declined. Priced at up to eight cold dockets per change — or eight decline lines, which is the bookkeeping class this change exists to shrink.
- **One run per change, the experience session naming the criteria it covered.** Also put to him and declined. It is what PR #155 did in practice, but it restates the neighbouring cell's duty at a discount rather than avoiding it.
- **A guard.** Nothing mechanical distinguishes a behavioral criterion from a conformance one.
- **An offsetting deletion to hold the cell's size flat.** The change is net growth on `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` and the PR body carries the measured delta with its justification. `skills/authoring` asks that net growth be justified, not balanced, and deleting text to make a size claim true is the failure PR #132's ruling named at D9(a).

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **Both arms dismissed two findings the real panel sustained at high and strong-medium** — D24, a false superlative in a frozen entry, and D20, a claim in the shipped cell falsified by the change itself. D20 is a claim in the shipped cell and the control dismissed it too, so that half belongs to clause (a) against any criteria set. **This bullet's reopen condition has already fired, inside the measurement rather than after it:** D23 — shipped prose, per PR #132's own *"Stage 3 — the shipped cell"* header — was dismissed by Arm B and kept by Arm A, so the amended rule sheds a shipped-prose finding the old one caught. The watch is therefore open on arrival, not conditional. **Reopen further on:** a later session acting wrongly on shipped prose a review dismissed under these criteria — which is the harm neither D20 nor D23 has yet produced, and the thing that would turn a measured cost into a reason to amend.
- **The rule asserts what a cold session does under it, and the arms that measured it were prompt-contained rather than tree-isolated** — seats were instructed not to open repository files, not prevented. **Reopen on:** the experience session this change buys finding a seat that still writes a conformance checklist under the amended bullet, or one that reads it as licensing an unfalsifiable criterion.
- **Nothing distinguishes a criterion that genuinely argues its consumer-facing load from one that asserts it in a clause.** The exception in decision 1 is stated as a duty to argue, and a session can discharge it with a sentence. **This condition also fired before landing, twice.** A cold consumer wrote such a criterion and named the problem itself — *"whether that load is real or whether I just wrote a justification for a criterion I wanted anyway is exactly the call the cell warns about"* — and **this change's own affirmed criterion 7** is an instance: *"A consumer's plugin update carries the amended rule"*, checked by two commands neither of which falsifies that consumer claim. A rule change was **priced out** at review: it costs a shipped-cell edit with an unbounded obligation term — every conformance criterion gaining an adjudication duty — on one run's evidence. **Reopen on:** a second occurrence in real work, or an owner-stated requirement.
- **A criterion *withdrawn* on a branch is neither the criterion `:45` covers nor the one `:49` covers.** `skills/engagement/SKILL.md:45` covers a contingent criterion **replaced** on the branch that is not taken; decision 3's bar covers one **held** across branches; neither addresses one struck outright. A cold consumer hit exactly this, wrote a struck criterion, and flagged its own guess — *"'replaced by nothing' is uncomfortably close to the empty-on-one-side cheat."* Legislating it was declined at review: roughly a dozen words on a cell already carrying justified net growth, for a case observed once, by a consumer whose guess-and-flag is the rule working. **Reopen on:** a second artifact whose author cannot tell whether a struck criterion is lawful.
- **Decision 3 requires a criterion be falsifiable per branch and does not require the per-branch test be *recorded*,** so the property is one only the author can assert. Fixing that was **priced out**: it puts a positive obligation on every future fork-carrying artifact, and the behavior it would enforce is already being produced — the #133 docket's seat stated per-branch falsifiers unprompted, and the cold consumer carried a fork with per-branch coverage stated. **Reopen on:** a fork-carrying artifact reaching review with a criterion silently empty on a branch.
- **One seat per arm per docket.** The counts above are two runs, not a distribution. **Reopen on:** a second A/B whose arms do not separate.
