# D-128: Filing gets a cell of its own, ties are closed by what a ranking does with them, and a filing never asserts that it searched

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-23 (PR #128)

## Context

[#103](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/103) was filed at the owner's direction on 2026-08-20 against two failure modes with independent evidence.

**Ties discovered at ranking instead of filing.** The board's rankings have repeatedly had to group after the fact what filing could have tied: [#20](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/20) and [#33](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/33) are the same file (`tools/check_version_bump.py`) in the same defect shape, filed a day apart with no reference between them; [#94](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/94) and [#95](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/95) were filed by the same review against the same sentence and merged into one PR later; [#101](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/101) arrived with no stated relationship to the paragraph #102 was actively reviewing. The practice exists uninstructed — [#52](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/52) names #35 as *"a sibling looseness in the same guard file"* — so it fires by session temperament.

**The over-filed filing goes stale.** [#38](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/38) arrived richly framed — layers, monopolies, coupling maps — and every one of those terms was dead vocabulary by pickup; it was rescoped into [#89](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/89). Its *evidence* survived perfectly: three ADRs assigning owned responsibilities to a layer with no files in it is still checkable today. The filings that survived the reset best were the deliberately minimal ones, a convention several issues stated individually and nothing taught.

### One of the issue's exhibits did not hold

#103 offers the lost priority-labels design as evidence for extending the search to closed issues. It is not that. [#83](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/83) records the design as *"drafted in the #68 and #11 sessions, **never filed**, and orphaned when the reset retired the vocabulary it was written in"* — a loss to never filing, which is the second rule's exhibit rather than the first's. The closed-state search is decided below on its own grounds; the [#26](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/26)/[#27](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/27) residue is the exhibit that stands.

## Decision

**A new cell, `skills/filing`, carrying two rules. Nothing else ships** — no other skill is edited, no doctrine line is added, no mechanism is built.

1. **A filing searches the board before it lands**, with three lawful outcomes: extend an existing issue (a comment, not a new number); file new with named ties; file standalone.
2. **The search covers issues in every state.** A closed issue is the record of what was already decided, already tried, or left behind when its parent closed, and the extra reach is a flag on one command — so the question is signal, not cost.
3. **A closed vocabulary of four tie verbs** — `same-subject`, `same-class`, `sequenced-after`, `supersedes / superseded-by` — each stated with the ranking consequence that earns it its place, under the extension test that **a tie name earns its place by changing what a ranking does with the pair.** The verbs read identically for open and closed issues. Written in one block directly under the filing's opening, one per line as `<verb> #N — <one clause of why>`.
4. **No filing states that the search was performed.** The ties are its artifact: a filing naming them has demonstrably searched. Where the search found nothing the filing states *no siblings on the board* — a fact about the board a ranking uses, not a claim about the filer.
5. **Creation carries the want; pickup does the work**, as two lists. Carried: the want or defect in plain terms, the incident or evidence making it real, why it will actually get picked up, the ties, and what discovery must settle named as deliberately deferred. Left: the framing, the options and their argument, the remedy design, the pre-implementation artifact.
6. **The evidence/design boundary is a kind, not a quantity** — *record what happened; do not decide what to do.* Evidence stays true however far the vocabulary moves; design is written in today's vocabulary and decays with it. The floor, which is what stops this reading as licence to file bare titles: carry enough that a picker-up can confirm the problem is real without redoing the discovery that found it.
7. **The "Minimal filing" announcement retires.** Thinness is rule 5 now, and announcing it is a filing asserting compliance with a rule — the shape item 4 already refuses. Provenance is a different thing and stays: who directed the work, or which review sustained it, is a fact the picker-up uses.

### Why a cell rather than `authoring`

#103 proposed `authoring`, and `authoring`'s own routing rule is what argues against it: *"A piece is its own skill when it has an independent trigger: a situation where it should fire without the parent job underway. […] Where no cell serves it at all, the answer is a new cell — the burden sits on cramming rather than on creating."*

**Filing's trigger is independent and is not authoring's.** Every incident #103 cites happened mid-review or mid-ranking — #52 and #33 out of review panels, #101 out of an external-pass reconciliation, #94 and #95 out of the same review. No session in that list was writing a governing document.

**Cramming also costs `authoring` its success criterion**, which reads *"every document it governs can say what it is for, who reads it, and what its review should judge it against — and contains nothing that fails that test."* An issue filing has no purpose header, no audience statement and no review; hosting filings turns that criterion into a disjunction over two unlike jobs and aims every future review of `authoring` at a blurrier target. [D-104](D-104-2026-08-22-engagement-cell.md) is the one-commit-old precedent: `engagement` was created rather than crammed for the same shape of concern, and it is the entry that flipped the burden from creating to cramming.

**The name is `filing`** — honest about what the cell holds today. `backlog` or `board` would promise the ranking of #83, which this does not build. If #83 later lands beside it, a rename costs a version bump and nothing else: no skill may reference another, so there are no inbound links to break.

### Why no platform mechanism

The doctrine's admission ladder puts a platform or CI mechanism ahead of skill prose, so a GitHub issue template is the first candidate, and the reason it fails is specific rather than aesthetic: **it never reaches the filer.** Filings here are made by sessions calling `gh issue create` with `--title` and `--body`, and that path applies no template unless `-T` is passed explicitly — read off the command's own flag list rather than assumed. A template would fire for a human filing in the web UI and never for the party that files nearly everything. A mechanism opt-in for the party it is meant to bind is not a mechanism.

## Rejected

- **`skills/authoring` as the home**, per #103's own proposal. It was the cheaper diff and a filing genuinely is authored content, which is a real argument and not enough: it fails the skill's stated trigger test and forces an amendment to `authoring`'s purpose and success statements in the same change. Argued at the convergence gate with both options priced; the owner ruled the cell.
- **`skills/engagement` as the home.** Rejected on subject: engagement governs surfaces the owner enters, and a filing's audience is the session that picks the work up.
- **A fifth tie verb for closed siblings** (`residue-of`). It fails the extension test — it changes nothing about what a ranking does; a closed sibling is read by whoever picks the work up, which `same-subject` already delivers.
- **A machine-parseable tie syntax.** #103 asked for ties written *"so a later ranking can read them mechanically."* There is no tool that parses the board and none is built here, so the reader is a session, which reads prose. Consistent placement is the whole of what mechanical readability can mean at this scale; a syntax built for a machine that does not exist is dead weight.
- **A GitHub issue template**, argued above. Considered first because the admission ladder requires it, not skipped.
- **Requiring a filing to state that it searched.** This is the shape [#94](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/94) is this repo's worked example of: a rule with a 0/8 obedience record that nothing could observe. The ties are a checkable artifact; the sentence is not.
- **A quantity bar for how much a filing carries** — a word count, a section list, a template. It would have rejected good short filings and admitted long empty ones; the failure #38 exhibits is a kind of content, not an amount of it.
- **Retro-tying the open board.** Board bookkeeping is the tripwire the doctrine names, and the ties earn their value at filing time, which has passed for everything currently open.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **Whether an issue filing is a surface the owner enters,** and therefore owes a plain brief. Filings carry one in practice and nothing here removes it, but the rule is not stated: reaching into `engagement`'s list of surfaces from this cell would either duplicate that cell's prose or take a cross-cell reference, both forbidden. **Reopen on:** the owner reading a filing that lacks one and having to reconstruct it, or `engagement` amending its surface list.
- **The ties have no reader yet.** They are written for the ranking [#83](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/83) describes, which does not exist; today a session ranking by hand is the whole audience. If #83 lands with a different shape than a session reading the board, the placement rule may want revisiting. **Reopen on:** #83 landing, or a ranking that could not find a filing's ties where the rule puts them.
- **Nothing observes compliance with either rule,** which is deliberate for the search half and simply true for the rest. The ties are visible when present and indistinguishable from a temperament that would have named them anyway; a filing that skips the search leaves no trace. **Reopen on:** a duplicate pair filed after this lands, which would be the first evidence that a visible artifact is not enough.
- **The four verbs are drawn from one repo's board at roughly 130 issues.** A fifth may earn its place under the stated test; the test, not the list, is what this entry commits to. **Reopen on:** a ranking that had to invent a relationship the four cannot express.
- **The cell carries no `references/`.** At forty lines it does not need depth loaded on demand, and adding a directory for its own sake would be cost without a reader. **Reopen on:** the cell growing past what a session should read to file one issue.
