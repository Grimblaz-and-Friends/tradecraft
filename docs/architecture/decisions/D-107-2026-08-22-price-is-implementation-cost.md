# D-107: A remedy's price is what implementing it costs, and a real finding at the wrong moment is routed

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-22 (PR #107)

## Context

**`SKILL.md:59` names two axes and says neither absorbs the other** — *"Severity is a claim too, and it measures harm-if-unfixed; the remedy's price is its separate half, and neither absorbs the other."* `:65` then defined the second axis, and defined it wrong in half: *"its complexity delta — whether the remedy adds a rule, guard, or **standing prose every later reader pays for**, or removes one."* The first half of that clause is implementation cost. The bolded half prices the artifact's **steady state** — what it costs for the words to exist — not the work of changing it.

So reviews priced remedies by word count, on the face of the record rather than by drift in practice. [PR #102](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102)'s round-one ruling led the prices for M2, M4 and M24 with a word figure; `postfix-8` was declined partly on word churn; and the [§7A owner decision](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102#issuecomment-5380511632) was substantially about thirteen words. **No count of how many grounds were size-driven is stated here**: the figure has been written three different ways across three artifacts, and `:57` warns that a count over a corpus the change itself writes into is a query, not a number. Read the ruling.

**The worst of it was upstream of every ruling.** [#91](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/91#issuecomment-5375316133)'s affirmed acceptance criteria carried a word budget as criterion 5, so cost became a first-class success criterion; five seats judged against it, the defense priced against it, and the judge ruled against it. The distortion was written into the charter and propagated by the review's own machinery.

**A session reading `:65` got the reading the owner says was never intended**, which is why this is an amendment and not a session behaving better.

**The second defect is in the same neighbourhood and is the reason these landed together.** `:67`'s clause (b) offered exactly two outcomes: sustain and fix, or sustain and price out. `AGENTS.md` carries the third path — *"Filing an issue instead requires that the remedy belongs neither in this change nor in a guard, and that it would genuinely get picked up"* — but it appears nowhere in the paragraph the terminal stage reads. By [D-90](D-90-2026-08-20-dispatch-contract.md)'s own placement argument, `:67` is precisely what a session occupying the terminal stage reads *without* the doctrine.

`postfix-8` is the exhibit. Its remedy is **−1 word** — negative complexity delta, one-sentence regression surface, the cheapest remedy on that entire docket, and negative in cost. **It cannot be declined on price.** What actually declined it were facts about the moment, not the remedy: it was the only shipped-zone remedy on the docket and landed in the sentence Collision A had closed; a terminating ruling's fix batch takes no prosecution look (`:71`'s own exemption), so the edit would land unreviewed; it would move an approved cost figure at two sites; and it would diverge permanently from that figure in an unamendable commit message. **The ruling names the second as decisive**, and the third and fourth are the size-pricing habit this entry's first half corrects.

**The owner's test is what makes the mislabel visible: a price-out is time-invariant.** If a remedy costs too much, that is true in this change and every future one, and only new evidence about value reopens it — which is why a price-out reads as permanent and generates no follow-up. `postfix-8`'s ruling had to write itself a reopen condition (*"any future shipped edit to `:67` for another reason — at which point the −1 rides free"*), and **a genuine price-out needs no clause like that**, because nothing about the world has to change for it to stay declined.

## Decision

**Landed in the change, no new stage and no mechanism.**

1. **`:65` defines price as implementation cost** — *"the cost of implementing it: what the fix touches (its regression surface) and what it obliges later sessions to do. A remedy that removes a rule or guard prices negative."* The old wording's add/remove sign survives as *prices negative*.
2. **`:67`'s clause (b) gains `sustain and route`** — *"Worth it but wrong for this change → sustain and route, naming or filing the vehicle that would genuinely pick it up"* — with the discriminator that makes the three-way choice decidable rather than a matter of taste: *a price-out holds in every future change; what only this change's circumstances decline is routed, not priced out.* **Arm 1 carries its own condition** (*"Yes, and it belongs in this change"*) so the general case does not swallow the special one.
3. **`:67`'s docket sentence takes `postfix-8`'s −1**, its em-dash becoming a colon.
4. **`:75`'s external-pass enumeration gains `sustained and routed with the vehicle`**, so the disposition exists on the lane the skill runs *"every time."*
5. **`:71` gains the route in its anti-evasion clause** (*"never priced or routed away"*) and **loses its own overloaded `routed`** (*"is recorded, never a further round"*).
6. **`skills/engagement` takes the upstream rule**, extending its existing acceptance-criteria bullet: criteria state what must be true of the result, not what it may cost, and a cost estimate belongs **outside the numbered criteria**.

**The definition's own test, and why this wording rather than a shorter one.** M4's price in PR #102 was stated as *"a standing production obligation on every seat in every review"* — genuine implementation cost, and the part of that ruling that survives the clarification untouched. The replacement had to keep that expressible, which is what *what it obliges later sessions to do* buys and what a bare *what the fix touches* would have lost. An ongoing behavioural obligation is a cost of implementing, not a cost of existing.

**The conduct-shaped wording is a repair the review forced, and the evidence is the review itself.** The affirmed draft read *"any standing obligation it lays on later sessions"*, which reads as a widening of *"standing prose every later reader pays for"* as easily as a repeal — prose a session must carry is, on a plain read, a standing obligation laid on it. That is not hypothetical: **in the first review conducted under the amended clause, three of five seats led remedy prices with word counts**, one of them deriving *"prices negative"* from the count alone for a remedy that widens a guard and is not negative. This entry's own reopen condition fired before merge. The response is to make the limb name conduct, not to add a boundary clause — see Rejected.

**Why one change and not two.** The two amendments are one idea. Once price means implementation cost, a price-out is permanent **by construction** — so the time-dependent case has nowhere to go unless it is named in the same sentence, and a judge left with two labels reaches for the nearest. Landing them separately would have amended the file's most collision-loaded sentence twice in two days. **The owner settled the bundling explicitly** before the artifact was posted.

**Size is not renamed, not promoted, and not moved.** It keeps exactly two homes: `:8` (*"leaves the artifact no larger than that answer requires"*) and `:17` (*"net growth is itself a finding, and the burden of argument sits on keeping the words"*). `:17` is the right home **because a finding is prosecuted once** — one severity, one remedy, that remedy's own implementation price, one ruling — instead of leaking into every other remedy's price line, which is the failure mode PR #102 exhibited.

**`:17` confers standing, not a price credit**, and the distinction matters because an earlier draft of this entry got it wrong. `:17` gives deletions equal standing and shifts the burden of argument; the *credit* lived in `:65`'s `or removes one`. **That credit narrowed**: base `:65` reached {rule, guard, standing prose} and the landed clause reaches {rule, guard}. Prose removal no longer prices negative, because prose's cost of existing is not a price under the new definition. The narrowing is principled and is recorded here rather than asserted away as continuity.

**The addition side narrowed too, and is recorded rather than repaired.** Base `:65` named *adds a rule, guard, or standing prose* as a cost outright; the landed clause reaches an addition only through what it obliges later sessions to do. A new test pin obliges CI rather than sessions, so it now prices at its regression surface alone. Symmetrising the clause was rejected — see below.

**Two sentences the new disposition reaches, named because they did not move on their own.** `:71`'s cycle terminus and `:79`'s next-pass default both turn on *"no high it will fix"*, whose extension is `:67`'s disposition set. That set gained a third and cheaper member, so both tests narrow: a routed high is not a high it will fix. **This is intended**, and `:71`'s anti-evasion clause is amended to say *priced or routed away* so the mandatory re-validation floor is not the place it costs. `:79` is left alone; its default is a default, and a routed high still appears in the report under *every finding's disposition*.

**`route` at `:67` and `routed` at `:71` named different things.** `:67`'s is a finding whose remedy **is** an artifact change belonging elsewhere; `:71`'s was a finding whose remedy is **not** an artifact change at all. `:71`'s word is removed rather than `:67`'s renamed, so one term now has one sense in this file.

**`postfix-8`'s reopen condition is discharged by this entry**, not by editing the frozen [cycle-1 ruling](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102#issuecomment-5381165417). **The residue is recorded rather than smoothed:** the colon does not close the misreading the em-dash allowed — the bold terminator is still the sole delimiter and a colon runs to the sentence end. The finding is discharged on its second reopen disjunct (a shipped edit to `:67` for another reason), which fired; the first disjunct guards a risk this edit did not change. Nothing in PR #102's review is re-dispositioned; records are append-only.

**The cost is +106 standing words across two shipped files**, derived from the finished tree after the fix batch:

**The estimate's misses are named, both of them.** The affirmed artifact estimated +38 in `adversarial-review` and ≈40 for the criteria rule. The rule as drafted was already **47** words when the ≈40 was written — a 17.5% miss on prose the artifact itself contained verbatim, carrying no citation and no punctuation change to explain it. The landed +55 adds the structural test the review required on top of that. In `adversarial-review`, +38 became +51 through two `[D-107]` citations, one token from `price**:` → `price** —`, and the review's own sustained fixes at `:67` and `:75`. **No offset is claimed, because none was made.**

**The figure is recorded here as a fact and not as a criterion — and the affirmed criterion 7 still carried the estimate**, in the same shape as the #91 criterion 5 it was written to convict, and it propagated verbatim to all five seats through the review's shared block. That is this rule's own exemplar recurring one last time before the rule binds, and it is why the landed wording names a **structural** test — the figure sits outside the numbered criteria — rather than asking an author to self-certify that a criterion is really a fact.

**One divergence from the judge's ordered wording, recorded because the affirmed artifact and the ruling are both frozen.** The ruling ordered a standalone paragraph opening *"Acceptance criteria state what must be true of the result…"*. `engagement`'s existing bullet already opens *"**Acceptance criteria** are the change's success definition…"*, two lines up. Landing both would put near-identical openers in adjacent sentences, which is the legibility cost `:8` makes the review's own criterion. The rule is instead appended to that bullet, carrying the same structural test in the same cell. Same function, one opener.

## Rejected

- **Size as a named third axis.** It reads as symmetry and would invite exactly what `:65` forbids — scoring. Three axes also give a stage that wants to decline a finding a second cost channel, which is the failure this entry exists to close, restored under a new name.
- **A boundary clause at `:17` or `:65` saying size is not a price** — including after this entry's own reopen condition fired mid-review. `authoring` is explicit that *"A clause defending against a reader who would not actually err is pure cost"*, and its remedy for a reader who genuinely could err is to **restate the sentence more plainly**, which is what the conduct-shaped limb does. The boundary lives here, where a session changing `:65` will look.
- **Bounding the negative-sign sentence** (*"prices negative on that obligation"*). The terminal stage priced this out: a qualifier appended in the file's second most-loaded sentence, against a reader who would have to ignore the immediately preceding clause naming regression surface. **An external reviewer subsequently produced exactly that reading unprompted**, which is evidence the price-out was weighed on incomplete information; it is docketed for the post-fix cycle rather than re-decided here.
- **Symmetrising the sign clause** (*"adds a rule or guard prices positive"*). It restores a cost channel against every guard-adding remedy, in a repo whose doctrine puts *"a platform or CI mechanism"* first in the admission order. Deterring guards is the wrong direction.
- **Requiring a routed ruling to state the circumstance that declined it.** Real, and priced out: four separate findings each proposed adding a clause to the route arm, and sustaining the documentation ones alongside the decidability ones makes route the longest branch in clause (b), at the salience cost the three-arm structure depends on.
- **Amending the frontmatter `description`**, which still says the terminal stage *"weighs value against price"*. It carries the trigger, not the disposition set, and it enumerates none of the other dispositions either; changing a triggering string has a cross-repo regression surface untestable in this review.
- **Amending `:79`'s report list** to name a routed finding's vehicle. `:67` already requires the vehicle be named and `:79` requires *every finding's disposition*.
- **The acceptance-criteria rule in `authoring` or the doctrine.** `AGENTS.md` is budgeted and last-resort. `authoring` was the affirmed choice and was **wrong by the time the change landed**: [D-104](D-104-2026-08-22-engagement-cell.md) moved the pre-implementation artifact into `engagement` and set `authoring`'s trigger to *"not for a pre-implementation artifact"*. A rule for artifact authors placed in a cell that disclaims artifact authors ships where its reader will not be — D-90's placement argument, which this entry invokes twice, turned on this change. Caught by the defense re-running against a base that moved mid-review; the owner chose the move.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **Nothing verifies that a routed finding actually reached a vehicle.** The disposition names one; no stage checks it exists or that anything picked it up. **Reopen on:** a routed finding with no filed vehicle, or a vehicle filed and never picked up.
- **Nothing verifies that a `[D-N]` citation resolves to an existing entry.** Probed: `[D-999]` passes lint and the full suite. The zone wall makes the bare marker the only handle a shipped consumer has, so a bad citation is undetectable *and* unresolvable. **Routed, not priced out** — the remedy is a lint check and this change's affirmed boundary excludes new mechanisms. Vehicle: [#109](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/109).
- **`check_version_bump.py` cannot see a version already taken on the base ref's tip.** It re-derives the merge base, so a branch cut before another change landed reports a clean bump onto a version `main` already carries. This change hit it. **Routed** for the same boundary reason. Vehicle: [#110](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/110).
- **`:67` carries the second of `AGENTS.md`'s two filing conjuncts and not the first.** The doctrine requires that a remedy belong *neither in this change nor in a guard*; `:67` carries only *"would genuinely pick it up"*. The divergence exists **at landing**, not as future drift — so a reopen condition phrased as drift would never fire. It is accepted because `:67` says **vehicle**, not *issue*: a guard-building change is a lawful vehicle, so `:67` does not authorise skipping a guard, it merely does not warn about one. **Reopen on:** a finding whose remedy belonged in a guard being routed to an issue.
- **The defense prices findings; the docket carries entries it never saw.** [D-102](D-102-2026-08-21-merged-list-is-an-index.md) deferred this; clause (b) now asks a three-way question without widening who supplies a price. **Reopen on:** a terminal stage unable to choose between route and price-out for want of a stated price.
- **The terminal stage cannot originate a net-growth finding on a panel.** `:17` addresses seats; `:65` lets the defense originate, which covers the routine lane, but the judge has no origination power. `:8` binds its conduct directly and the judge in this review applied it. **Reopen on:** a terminal stage sustaining a remedy whose only material cost is prose growth with no `:17` finding on the docket.
- **Comprehension cost — salience, legibility, an enumeration demoted to illustration — is deliberately not a price.** It is a steady-state property of the resulting text, which this change excludes. `:17` and `:8` carry it. **Reopen on:** a fix that degrades legibility at word-neutral cost and prices as free.
- **Word-pricing recurred under the affirmed wording and the response was to tighten the wording, not to relocate the fix.** Three of five seats did it in this review. **Reopen on:** word-pricing recurring *after* the conduct-shaped limb is in force — which would mean the habit does not live in `:65` and the text is not where the fix belongs.
