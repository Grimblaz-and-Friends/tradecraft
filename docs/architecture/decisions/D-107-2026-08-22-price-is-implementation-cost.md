# D-107: A remedy's price is what implementing it costs, and a real finding at the wrong moment is routed

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-22 (PR #107)

## Context

**`SKILL.md:59` names two axes and says neither absorbs the other** — *"Severity is a claim too, and it measures harm-if-unfixed; the remedy's price is its separate half."* `:65` then defined the second axis, and defined it wrong in half: *"its complexity delta — whether the remedy adds a rule, guard, or **standing prose every later reader pays for**, or removes one."* The first half of that clause is implementation cost. The bolded half prices the artifact's **steady state** — what it costs for the words to exist — not the work of changing it.

So reviews priced remedies by word count, and did it on the face of the record rather than by drift in practice. [PR #102](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102)'s round-one ruling led three prices with a word figure — M2 (+9), M4 (+12), M24 (+8) — `postfix-8` cited word churn as one of three declining reasons, and the [§7A owner decision](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102#issuecomment-5380511632) was substantially about thirteen words. **The worst of it was upstream of every ruling.** [#91](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/91#issuecomment-5375316133)'s affirmed acceptance criteria carried a word budget as criterion 5, so cost became a first-class success criterion; five seats judged against it, the defense priced against it, and the judge ruled against it. The distortion was written into the charter and propagated by the review's own machinery.

**A session reading `:65` got the reading the owner says was never intended**, which is why this is an amendment and not a session behaving better.

**The second defect is in the same neighbourhood and is the reason these landed together.** `:67`'s clause (b) offered exactly two outcomes: sustain and fix, or sustain and price out. `AGENTS.md` carries the third path — *"Filing an issue instead requires that the remedy belongs neither in this change nor in a guard, and that it would genuinely get picked up"* — but it appears nowhere in the paragraph the terminal stage reads. By [D-90](D-90-2026-08-20-dispatch-contract.md)'s own placement argument, `:67` is precisely what a session occupying the terminal stage reads *without* the doctrine.

`postfix-8` is the exhibit. Its remedy is **−1 word** — negative complexity delta, one-sentence regression surface, the cheapest remedy on that entire docket and the only one with negative cost. **It cannot be declined on price.** What actually declined it were three facts about the moment: a terminating ruling's fix batch takes no prosecution look (`:71`'s own exemption), so the edit would land unreviewed; it would move an approved cost figure at two sites; and it would diverge permanently from that figure in an unamendable commit message. **Only the first is load-bearing**, and the other two are the size-pricing habit this entry's first half corrects.

**The owner's test is what makes the mislabel visible: a price-out is time-invariant.** If a remedy costs too much, that is true in this change and every future one, and only new evidence about value reopens it — which is why a price-out reads as permanent and generates no follow-up. `postfix-8`'s ruling had to write itself a reopen condition (*"any future shipped edit to `:67` for another reason — at which point the −1 rides free"*), and **a genuine price-out needs no clause like that**, because nothing about the world has to change for it to stay declined.

## Decision

**Four changes, no new stage, no mechanism.**

1. **`:65` defines price as implementation cost** — *"the cost of implementing it: what the fix touches (its regression surface) and any standing obligation it lays on later sessions. A remedy that removes a rule or guard prices negative."* The old wording's add/remove sign survives as *prices negative*, so deletions keep the credit `:17` gives them.
2. **`:67`'s clause (b) gains `sustain and route`**, naming the vehicle that would genuinely pick the finding up, with the discriminator that makes the three-way choice decidable rather than a matter of taste: *a price-out holds in every future change; what only this change's circumstances decline is routed, not priced out.*
3. **`:67`'s docket sentence takes `postfix-8`'s −1**, its em-dash becoming a colon.
4. **`skills/authoring` takes the upstream rule:** acceptance criteria state what must be true of the result, not what it may cost; a cost estimate is recorded as a fact.

**The definition's own test, and why this wording rather than a shorter one.** M4's price in PR #102 was stated as *"a standing production obligation on every seat in every review"* — genuine implementation cost, and the part of that ruling that survives the clarification untouched. The replacement had to keep that expressible, which is what `any standing obligation it lays on later sessions` buys and what a bare *what the fix touches* would have lost. An ongoing behavioural obligation is a cost of implementing, not a cost of existing; the two are separable and the wording separates them.

**Why one change and not two.** The two amendments are one idea. Once price means implementation cost, a price-out is permanent **by construction** — so the time-dependent case has nowhere to go unless it is named in the same sentence, and a judge left with two labels reaches for the nearest. Landing them separately would have amended the file's most collision-loaded sentence twice in two days, with the second review reasoning about text a day old, and the first half would have dissolved two of the three grounds that declined the second half's own exemplar. **The owner settled the bundling explicitly** before the artifact was posted.

**Size is not renamed, not promoted, and not moved.** It keeps exactly two homes: `:8` (*"leaves the artifact no larger than that answer requires"*) and `:17` (*"net growth is itself a finding, and the burden of argument sits on keeping the words"*). `:17` is the right home **because a finding is prosecuted once** — one severity, one remedy, that remedy's own implementation price, one ruling — instead of leaking into every other remedy's price line, which is precisely the failure mode PR #102 exhibited. Size is a real concern and the owner says so; it is simply not the currency remedies are priced in.

**`postfix-8`'s reopen condition is discharged by this entry**, not by editing the frozen [cycle-1 ruling](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/102#issuecomment-5381165417). Nothing in PR #102's review is re-dispositioned; records are append-only, and this binds what comes next.

**The cost is +39 standing words in `adversarial-review/SKILL.md`, 2782 → 2821**, and +47 in `authoring/SKILL.md` — derived from the finished tree rather than carried from the estimate. `:65` took +2 and `:67` +37 net of the −1. The affirmed artifact estimated +38 and ≈40; `:65` came in one over because replacing `price**:` with `price** —` adds a token the clause-body count did not carry. No offset is claimed, because none was made. **The figure is recorded here as a fact and not as a criterion**, which is this change's own new rule applied to itself.

## Rejected

- **Size as a named third axis.** It reads as symmetry and would invite exactly what `:65` forbids — scoring. Three axes also give a stage that wants to decline a finding a second cost channel to reach for, which is the failure this entry exists to close, restored under a new name.
- **A boundary clause at `:17`, or at `:65`, saying size is not a price.** The positive definition is unambiguous, size already has two homes, and `authoring:37` is explicit: *"A clause defending against a reader who would not actually err is pure cost."* The misreading is on the record, so this was a genuine call rather than a reflex — it was made on the ground that the clause it would defend against no longer exists in the text. **The decision entry carries the boundary instead**, which is where a session changing `:65` will look.
- **The acceptance-criteria rule in the doctrine.** `AGENTS.md` is budgeted and last-resort; `authoring` governs the pre-implementation artifact and already carries the boundary statement, so the rule sits where its reader already is. The doctrine's admission order stops at skill prose here.
- **`:67` citing `AGENTS.md`'s file-an-issue bar rather than restating it.** A cross-reference costs more words than the four it saves, and `:67` is read by a stage that by D-90's argument does not have the doctrine open. See the drift risk below, which is the price of this choice and is disclosed rather than absorbed.
- **Amending `:79`'s final-report list to name a routed finding's vehicle.** `:67` already requires the vehicle be named, and `:79` requires *every finding's disposition* — the vehicle is part of the disposition. Adding a third itemised case to that sentence buys nothing the general clause does not already carry.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **`:67`'s *"would genuinely pick it up"* restates the doctrine's *"would genuinely get picked up"**, and `authoring:31` warns against duplicated prose that can drift. Accepted on differing scope: the doctrine sets the bar for filing an issue at all, including outside a review; `:67` gives the terminal stage a disposition. **Reopen on:** either sentence's bar being amended without the other moving, which is the drift itself becoming visible.
- **Nothing verifies that a routed finding actually reached a vehicle.** The disposition names one; no stage checks that it exists or that anything picked it up, so a routing is a promise on the record and `AGENTS.md`'s *"would genuinely get picked up"* is judged by the stage making it. Priced at a mechanism this change declines to build. **Reopen on:** a routed finding with no filed vehicle, or a vehicle filed and never picked up.
- **The defense prices findings; `:65` is finding-scoped, and clause (b) now asks a three-way question.** [D-102](D-102-2026-08-21-merged-list-is-an-index.md) already deferred the case of an uncarried docket entry reaching the terminal stage with no price; this entry widens what the terminal stage does with a price without widening who supplies one. **Reopen on:** a terminal stage unable to choose between route and price-out for want of a stated price.
- **`:17` may still be over-read into a word-denominated pricing habit** now that `:65` no longer mentions prose size at all. Judged unlikely: `:17` makes net growth a finding, and a finding goes through the docket. **Reopen on:** a review pricing a remedy by word count after this change is in force, which would mean the habit does not live in `:65` and the text is not where the fix belongs.
