# D-388: A standard moves only to a cell that reaches the reader it is taken from, and a search for the source's own words is not that check

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-04 (PR #388)

## Context

[D-376](D-376-2026-09-04-the-charter-holds-what-loads-first.md) landed the residency rule — the charter states an obligation and names the cell that owns the rest — and left one passage unmoved: the fix-bar paragraph in the charter's `Review` section. That change was built to move it. Its own affirmed brief said so, in the material under the term the owner affirmed: *"the disposition rules fire during a review, when `adversarial-review` is loaded, and go to the cell."* Its draft artifact specified the relocation at item 3 while its drafted charter text kept the paragraph whole, and its cold seat returned `would not` on exactly that contradiction.

**The contradiction was resolved in the direction a false check supported.** The settled artifact deleted the relocation on this ground: `grep -rn "whatever its scope\|needs no decision\|Real but not yet worth" skills/adversarial-review/` returns nothing, therefore *"those rules exist only in the charter, so the route would have deleted them."* The grep searches for the charter's own phrasings. `skills/adversarial-review/references/arbitration.md` at `379adc7` states the whole fix/decision/record trichotomy at `:15` and carries *"Fixing needs no permission; declining to fix does"* verbatim at `:25`; the rule-level check in this PR's body returns both. The premise was false when written, and PR #376's own review established it — which is why that entry's Rejected section concedes the duplication while its Decision section keeps the paragraph because *"every sentence of which fires outside a review where no cell is loaded."*

**The gap that let the false check stand is that nothing required a different one.** `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md:7` at `379adc7` gives a shared standard exactly one owner and says nothing about the reader the losing cell was serving, so a mover has no stated obligation beyond finding the text somewhere.

## Decision

**Four sentences leave the charter and the fifth stays.** The owner's requirement, stated in conversation on 2026-09-04 and quoted in the affirmation record: *"rules that don't fit anywhere else can be contained in the charter, others need to be in the appropriate skill and pointed there by the charter... we should have very little in the charter, and only those things that can't go somewhere else."* Run against the five sentences that test is not close — four have a fuller home, one has none. **The fifth is reworded rather than kept verbatim** because *"no such stage exists"* loses its referent when the permission asymmetry it depends on moves; the replacement states the fact inline.

**A standard moves only to a cell that reaches the reader it is taken from, and the check is a read rather than a search.** The mover names the route the losing reader now takes — the destination's own load condition covering their situation, or an always-on surface naming the destination — and checks it by reading what that condition says. Finding the words in the destination is not that check: a search for the source's phrasing answers a question about text where the question is about the rule.

**The check is an obligation on the mover rather than a measured claim, and that is forced rather than chosen.** [#272](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/272) closed with the finding that no instrument can attribute a routing effect to a cell description, so any criterion of the form *a session reaches this cell* is unfalsifiable. What remains checkable is what a load condition says, which is what the failed grep was standing in for.

**Where no route holds, the destination's trigger is widened; the rule does not go back.** This is the owner's ruling inside PR #376 generalised. The negative-control requirement moved to `skills/substrate/SKILL.md` and landed behind that cell's own non-trigger, stranding the review stages that must judge a probe's credibility — observed twice on that change's own review, its defense and its judge receiving the requirement only from hand-written briefs. He amended the description rather than returning the text, on the ground that returning it restores the duplication the move existed to remove. **That incident is what admits this rule**: it is a failure in the running of real work, not a review finding about governing prose, which the charter's admission bar refuses.

**The rule is applied to this change itself, which is why two hops move and not one.** `skills/adversarial-review/references/arbitration.md:3` and `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md:32`'s pointer both read inside-review-only at `379adc7`, so a session the charter now routes to that cell would have met two stated conditions excluding it. Both cover a finding met outside a review.

**No guard is built, for either polarity `tools/lint.py` ignores** — a standard with no home, and one stated in two cells at once. *A standard* is not an object that lint can recognise: its checks are structural, over paths, frontmatter, citations, ceilings and byte-level properties, and none has a notion of a rule. A guard could only test a proxy — a phrase, a heading, a count — which is the same instrument class that produced the failure above, and one that passes a real violation while reading as coverage is worse than none. [#378](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/378) names a recorded decision not to build one as a lawful outcome, and this is it.

**D-376's keeping ground is superseded by reading.** Its Decision section's *"every sentence of which fires outside a review"* is false of the four that had a fuller home. That entry is frozen and is not repaired: the falsifier is not its own — the duplication its Rejected section concedes is a different sentence conceding a different thing — so this is supersession rather than the log's narrow correction.

## Rejected

- **Keeping the paragraph on the two-readers ground** — that the charter serves a session outside a review while `arbitration.md` serves review stages, so the duplication is lawful. Rejected against the owner's stated requirement above: a sentence with a fuller home elsewhere leaves and is pointed at, and the reader is served by the pointer rather than by a second copy. This was the session's own first recommendation, built by scoring the options against the reader-reach test imported from #378 rather than against the requirement the owner had already stated; the owner caught it.
- **Lifting the trichotomy out of `arbitration.md:15` into `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`'s body**, which would put it one hop earlier for an out-of-review reader. Rejected on three grounds: the text is woven into the terminal stage's clause (b) and would have to be split rather than moved; measured at `379adc7` the trichotomy span alone exceeds that body's remaining headroom, so it does not fit and would buy an admission row — the command and trees are in this PR's body; and restructuring the review cell is not what this change is for.
- **Amending `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`'s description to reach the out-of-review reader.** This was the session's proposal until the owner ruled it out: *"our changes should go inside skills and not in descriptions."* The session did not argue against it, because the exclusion costs this change nothing — a description is always loaded, so routing text from the charter body into one is the same always-on cost under a different heading, which `skills/authoring/references/routing.md` already states; and the charter is itself always-on and carries the pointer, so a charter-to-cell move needs no description text to reach its reader. **The reader test's teeth are on cell-to-cell moves**, where no always-on surface names the destination — which is the `substrate` case, not this one.
- **Returning a rule to its source when the destination does not reach.** Rejected as the general remedy for the reason the owner gave on the `substrate` instance: it restores the second copy, which is the failure the one-owner rule exists to prevent.

## Left open

**Whether the `record` disposition is available outside a review at all.** [D-269](D-269-2026-08-30-description-names-stages-not-tests.md) already records that the charter's decline rule and `skills/adversarial-review/references/after-the-fix.md` disagree about who may drop an external comment, with a stated reopening condition. This change moves where the rule is read and not what it says, so that disagreement is carried unchanged and stays that entry's.

**Whether a mechanism could ever hold the weaker property** — that a standard has exactly one home — without reaching the reader question. The decision above declines to build one now on the ground that lint has no notion of a standard; it does not establish that no instrument could.

**What a cell description is for.** The exclusion above raised it and it is filed as [#384](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/384): descriptions are the largest single term in what an adopter always loads and two are at their ceiling, while #272 holds that the effect of shortening one cannot be attributed.
