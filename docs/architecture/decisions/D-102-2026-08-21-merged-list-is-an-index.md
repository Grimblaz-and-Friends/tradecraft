# D-102: The merged list is an index, not the record

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-21 (PR #102)

## Context

[D-90](D-90-2026-08-20-dispatch-contract.md) took the dispatcher's pen away at every stage but one. It deferred the merge — the point where the dispatching session, in this repo's single-session lanes the artifact's author, reduces several seat reports to one list — and wrote itself a reopen condition: *the class recurring through the merge.* Its stated reason was that dedup can bury a finding and the narrow duplicate definition, same failure mode at the same location, was guard enough until something proved otherwise.

**The condition fired inside D-90's own review, three times, across three stages.** Round 1's O3: `revision-diff` filed seven findings and the merge folded the seventh into F12 as a parenthetical word count, losing a **deletion remedy** — a proposal the charter gives equal standing with additions, on a change whose net growth was itself a finding. Cycle 1's O-D2: a seat's "Checked and found no finding" paragraph, three reasons declining the very question P11 raised, dropped entirely while the merge recorded P11 as sole-findered; and a co-finder credited on a finding that seat never filed. Cycle 2's O-C2-1: a correction appended for an error the seat did not make, **in the discrediting direction**, attached to the one finding the merge itself called independently verified — alongside a silent 12→13 change to that seat's LCP figure that was correct, and unflagged, which is the same class.

The merge that lost the first one was performed by the session implementing the change whose subject is *the author's control over what later stages see*, while the author was watching for exactly this. That is the most favourable condition the parent-owned merge will ever get.

**What caught all three was not the merge's guard.** It was the terminal stage reading the raw seat reports and ruling against those rather than against the merge's account of itself — cycle 1's judge: *"reading the seat reports rather than the merge is what caught this — that is the docket-set-by-rule change working, on its own review."* That was a dispatcher's instruction on those two occasions, not a rule. `SKILL.md:41` relayed every predecessor's output whole, but `:67` defined the docket as "every **merged** finding," so the reports arrived and the merged list still decided what got ruled on.

## Decision

**Two clauses, no new stage and no new dispatch.** At the merge paragraph: *the merged list indexes the seat reports; it never replaces them*, and content the merge adds that no seat wrote — a correction, a co-finder, an adverse note — is labeled as the merge's own. At the docket sentence: the terminal stage's docket carries *anything in a seat's report that no merged finding carries*, alongside every merged finding, every defense verdict, and every flagged collision.

**The merge keeps the pen and loses the authority.** This is the axis the remedy turns on, and it is not the axis the issue was filed on. D-90's deferral, [#88](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/88), and [#91](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/91)'s own title all read the class as author-interest — the charter-compression class one layer up. **The four modes say otherwise.** Run them against a clerk with no stake: mode 1 is a dedup judgment call it could make identically, and mode 2 it would produce *more* reliably, because "checked and found no finding" is not a finding and a merge-of-findings discards it by construction. Only the manufactured finder and the fabricated error plausibly turn on interest. Two of four are what any summary does to a source — so the load-bearing defect is **a summary standing in for its source**, and the source being right does not help when nothing rules against it. That is why the remedy is not a clerk, and why a clerk would not retire it.

**The second clause is not a new rule.** It is D-90's own dispatcher's-note rule — calling attention is lawful, filtering and restating are not, and what the dispatcher adds is labeled and additive — reaching the one packet it never covered. The merge is a dispatch product that escaped the contract governing dispatches.

**Placement is part of the decision**, on D-90's own argument that a rule binding one stage, buried in a paragraph about another, stops firing for the stage that needs it. The merger reads the merge paragraph; the session dispatching the terminal stage reads the docket sentence. Splitting them costs nothing and puts each half where its reader already is.

**The affirmed draft said `anything a seat filed`; what landed is `anything in a seat's report`.** "Filed" is readable as "filed as a finding," which excludes mode 2 — a paragraph that examined a question and declined to file one, which is precisely the mode a findings-shaped instrument cannot see. Changed during implementation against the affirmed acceptance criterion that the text would have caught its own exemplars; +1 word.

**The cost is +45 standing words, 2702 → 2747**, against an affirmed estimate of ≈+45. Net growth in this file was a sustained finding in three of PR #90's four rounds. No offset is claimed, because none was made.

**D-90's deferral is resolved by this entry**, not by editing D-90, which is frozen. A reader arriving at that deferral should read this.

## Rejected

- **A per-seat mapping table** — each seat's raw findings mapped to the merged findings that carry them, so a drop shows as an unmapped row. It was #91's own cheap option and it covers **mode 1 alone**: mode 2 produces no finding row to leave unmapped, mode 4 is content arriving from no seat at all, which a seat→merged map is structurally silent about, and mode 3 only lands if the table demands a citable finding id *and* someone checks the cited row says what it is credited with. An instrument that looks like a guard and covers one of four is worse than none, because the next session reads the absence of unmapped rows as proof.
- **Deleting the merge as a stage.** The terminal stage already receives every seat report whole, so the merged list is arguably redundant there — but the defense does not receive them deduped, and it prices 23 findings rather than 40 because the merge ran. Deleting the stage moves that cost onto the defense; it does not remove it.
- **A mechanism.** Nothing gates what a merge writes, and no script can tell a faithful reduction from a lossy one. The doctrine's admission order stops at skill prose here, as it did for D-90.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **A clerk that is not the author** — [#88](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/88)'s structural escalation, which D-90 recorded as a decision waiting on evidence. This change is that evidence and still declines it, on three grounds: it covers modes 3 and 4 only; it costs an extra dispatch every round; and until [#85](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/85)'s self-contained-artifact requirement lands, the author must brief the clerk, which is the same defect one step upstream. **Reopen on:** a recurrence of modes 3 or 4 after this change is in force, or a merge defect reaching a landed artifact — at which point the argument that realized harm has been zero dies, and the clerk should be taken regardless of #85's status.
- **`one ruling per merged finding`, with a docket that now carries entries which are not merged findings.** The natural reading is one ruling each, and this change does not multiply rulings over merged findings — but the sentence does not say so, and the seam is named here rather than papered over. It is a candidate for [#95](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/95)'s restatement rather than a further qualifier now. **Reopen on:** a terminal stage that leaves an unmerged docket entry unruled, or one that reads the clause as licence to rule only on merged findings.
