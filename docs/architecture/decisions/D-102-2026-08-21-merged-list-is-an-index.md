# D-102: The merged list is an index, not the record

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-21 (PR #102)

## Context

[D-90](D-90-2026-08-20-dispatch-contract.md) took the dispatcher's pen away at every stage but one. It deferred the merge — the point where the dispatching session, in this repo's single-session lanes the artifact's author, reduces several seat reports to one list — and wrote itself a reopen condition: *the class recurring through the merge.* Its stated reason was that dedup can bury a finding and the narrow duplicate definition, same failure mode at the same location, was guard enough until something proved otherwise.

**The condition fired inside D-90's own review, three times, across three stages.** Round 1's O3, **mode 1**: `revision-diff` filed seven findings and the merge folded the seventh into F12 as a parenthetical word count, losing a **deletion remedy** — a proposal the charter gives equal standing with additions, on a change whose net growth was itself a finding. Cycle 1's O-D2, **modes 2 and 3**: a seat's "Checked and found no finding" paragraph, three reasons declining the very question P11 raised, dropped entirely while the merge recorded P11 as sole-findered; and a co-finder credited on a finding that seat never filed. Cycle 2's O-C2-1, **mode 4**: a correction appended for an error the seat did not make, **in the discrediting direction**, attached to the one finding the merge itself called independently verified — alongside a silent 12→13 change to that seat's LCP figure that was correct, and unflagged, which is the same class.

The merge that lost the first one was performed by the session implementing the change whose subject is *the author's control over what later stages see*, while the author was watching for exactly this. That is the most favourable condition the parent-owned merge will ever get.

**What caught all three was not the merge's guard.** It was a downstream stage reading the raw seat reports and ruling against those rather than against the merge's account of itself — cycle 1's judge: *"reading the seat reports rather than the merge is what caught this — that is the docket-set-by-rule change working, on its own review."* That was a dispatcher's instruction on those two occasions, not a rule. `SKILL.md:41` relayed every predecessor's output whole, but `:67` defined the docket as "every **merged** finding," so the reports arrived and the merged list still decided what got ruled on.

## Decision

**Two clauses, no new stage and no new dispatch.** At the merge paragraph: *the merged list indexes the seat reports; it never replaces, edits, or restates them*, and content the merge adds that no seat wrote — a correction, a co-finder, an adverse note — is labeled as the merge's own. At the docket sentence: the terminal stage's docket carries *anything in a seat's report that no merged finding carries — a remedy, a dissent, an examination that declined to file*, alongside every merged finding, every defense verdict, and every collision or open matter any stage flagged; and the ruling obligation follows the docket, *one ruling per merged finding and per uncarried seat entry*.

**Three of those five elements were added by this change's own review, and the review is why they are here.** The clause first landed unbounded (`anything in a seat's report`, no enumeration), with no ruling obligation attached, and reaching omission and addition but not alteration. Each gap was found, and all three were demonstrated by the review's own conduct rather than argued — see below.

**The merge keeps the pen and loses the authority.** This is the axis the remedy turns on, and it is not the axis the issue was filed on. D-90's deferral, [#88](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/88), and [#91](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/91)'s own title all read the class as author-interest — the charter-compression class one layer up. **The four modes say otherwise.** Run them against a clerk with no stake: mode 1 is a dedup judgment call it could make identically, and mode 2 it would produce *more* reliably, because "checked and found no finding" is not a finding and a merge-of-findings discards it by construction. Only the manufactured finder and the fabricated error plausibly turn on interest. Two of four are what any summary does to a source — so the load-bearing defect is **a summary standing in for its source**, and the source being right does not help when nothing rules against it. That is why the remedy is not a clerk, and why a clerk would not retire it.

**The second clause is not a new rule.** It is D-90's own dispatcher's-note rule — calling attention is lawful, filtering and restating are not, and what the dispatcher adds is labeled and additive — reaching the one packet it never covered. The merge is a dispatch product that escaped the contract governing dispatches.

**Placement is part of the decision**, on D-90's own placement argument — a session dispatching one stage does not read the paragraph governing another. The merger reads the merge paragraph; the session dispatching the terminal stage reads the docket sentence. Splitting them costs nothing and puts each half where its reader already is.

**The affirmed draft said `anything a seat filed`; what landed is `anything in a seat's report`.** "Filed" is readable as "filed as a finding," which excludes mode 2 — a paragraph that examined a question and declined to file one, which is precisely the mode a findings-shaped instrument cannot see. Changed during implementation against the affirmed acceptance criterion that the text would have caught its own exemplars; +1 word. **Four further divergences followed from the review**, all recorded here because the affirmed artifact is frozen and cannot carry them: the affirmed draft's enumeration (*a remedy, a dissent, an examination that declined to file*) was dropped in implementation and restored by ruling; the ruling-count clause gained *and per uncarried seat entry*; `never replaces them` became `never replaces, edits, or restates them`; and both clauses gained `[D-102]`, the affirmed draft having none. A fifth: the placement argument was re-grounded on D-90's reader-locality clause, the affirmed draft having inverted D-90's quantifiers — it reads *every* stage buried in a rule about *one*, not the reverse.

**The cost is +66 standing words, 2716 → 2782 against this PR's merge base**, and the figure is derived from the finished tree rather than carried forward: every ruling moved it, so it was re-run last. It is **47% over the affirmed estimate of ≈+45**, and over the terminal stage's own ≈+58 projection, which under-counted the enumeration by 3 words and mis-summed its own itemisation by 5. The affirmed ≈+45 bought the two clauses as first drafted; the overshoot is what the review added — the enumeration that bounds the class, the ruling obligation that makes it bind, the alteration clause, and two citations. The owner approved that set on 2026-08-22 with the derivation expressly pending. PR #90's final report records net growth in this file as a sustained finding in three of its four rounds. No offset is claimed, because none was made.

**The one available offset was priced and kept, not overlooked.** The entry says, in the paragraph above, that no offset was made; a seat found one — deleting `:63`'s em-dash enumeration, measured at **−9 words**. It is kept: that enumeration is the only place in the shipped artifact naming modes 3 and 4 for a cold reader of the merge paragraph, and the alteration clause extends the same sentence, so the deletion would fight the fix.

**The clause holds on both lanes.** On the routine lane a single prosecution seat makes mode 1 structurally impossible, but modes 2, 3 and 4 survive — a summary standing in for its source needs one source, not several — and the record shows routine reviews running two prosecution seats. Scoping the clause to panels would strip three modes from the lane carrying three of ten recorded reviews to save three words.

**A zero-finding panel now yields a non-empty docket**, because declined examinations are docket content and no merged finding carries them. That is the enumeration working as intended, and it is the accepted cost of covering mode 2.

**`tools/lint.py` lost both `merged >= sustained` and `raw >= sustained`.** Its per-seat nesting invariant encoded exactly the premise this change repeals: that nothing could be sustained which the seat's *finding* counts included. Under it, **nine of the ten recorded reviews** — this review's own row among them — had a seat one sustained entry from a red required check on an honest row, and the cheapest escape available to a recording session, folding the entry into `merged`, would relocate the very loss this change exists to stop. **Dropping only the first conjunct was not enough, and this change's own post-fix look caught it:** a declined examination is not a finding, so it is in neither `raw` nor `merged`, and a zero-finding seat with one sustained decline is `raw 0, sustained 1` — the shape the paragraph above says is now normal. `raw >= merged` and `sustained >= high` are kept; **`sustained` has no upper bound expressible in these four fields**, and re-adding either dropped conjunct looks right and is wrong. A test pins the newly lawful shapes green and goes red against the pre-fix revision.

**D-90's deferral is resolved by this entry**, not by editing D-90, which is frozen. A reader arriving at that deferral should read this.

## Rejected

- **A per-seat mapping table** — each seat's raw findings mapped to the merged findings that carry them, so a drop shows as an unmapped row. It was #91's own cheap option and it covers **mode 1 alone**: mode 2 produces no finding row to leave unmapped, mode 4 is content arriving from no seat at all, which a seat→merged map is structurally silent about, and mode 3 only lands if the table demands a citable finding id *and* someone checks the cited row says what it is credited with. An instrument that looks like a guard and covers one of four is worse than none, because the next session reads the absence of unmapped rows as proof.
- **Deleting the merge as a stage.** The terminal stage already receives every seat report whole, so the merged list is arguably redundant there — but the defense would not receive them deduped, and it prices 23 findings rather than 40 because the merge ran. Deleting the stage moves that cost onto the defense; it does not remove it.
- **A mechanism.** Nothing gates what a merge writes, and no script can tell a faithful reduction from a lossy one. The doctrine's admission order stops at skill prose here, as it did for D-90.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **A clerk that is not the author** — [#88](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/88)'s structural escalation (that issue is closed; D-90 and this entry are what carry it), which D-90 recorded as a decision waiting on evidence. This change is that evidence and still declines it, on three grounds: it covers modes 3 and 4 only; it costs an extra dispatch every round; and until [#85](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/85)'s self-contained-artifact requirement lands, the author must brief the clerk, which is the same defect one step upstream. **Reopen on:** a recurrence of modes 3 or 4 after this change is in force, or a merge defect reaching a landed artifact — at which point the argument that realized harm has been zero dies, and the clerk should be taken regardless of #85's status.
- **Mode 4 is not fully closed, and this change's own review is the exhibit.** Acceptance criterion 3 required that all four exemplars become breaches readable on the face of the artifacts. Three do. `O-C2-1`'s mode — the merge *altering* what a seat said — does not, and the merge that assembled this review's docket committed it: three seats each stated correctly that no `references/` directory exists **scoped to `skills/adversarial-review/`**, and the merged list widened that into an unscoped universal, which is false (`skills/authoring/references/spikes.md`). An absence claim strengthened by the summarizer omits nothing and adds nothing at finding granularity. `never replaces, edits, or restates them` narrows the gap; it does not close it, because a restatement broader than its source is still a restatement the reader cannot detect without the source. Closing it outright needs a fidelity requirement, which edges into the verification step the affirmed boundary excludes. **Owner-decided 2026-08-22:** land the narrowing, record the criterion as partially met. **Reopen on:** any merge restatement broader than what the seat wrote, this instance being the first.
- **The defense prices findings; the docket now carries entries it never saw.** `:65` is finding-scoped, so an uncarried seat entry reaches the terminal stage with no verdict and no price, and `:67`'s clause (b) asks a question with no input. Priced out at ~+9 standing words to state what `:67`'s *settle severity and price at the evidence* already permits the terminal stage to do — and it did, in this review. **Reopen on:** a terminal stage that dismisses an uncarried docket entry for want of a price.
- **Nothing requires a seat to record what it examined and declined**, so mode 2's coverage reaches only what seats volunteer. Priced out at ~+12 standing words **plus a standing production obligation on every seat in every review** — the most expensive recurring remedy on this docket, against a practice all five seats followed unprompted by rule. **Reopen on:** a seat report arriving with no declined-examination content.
- **No stage is named as the enumerator** of the uncarried class. Priced out: the enumeration makes the class determinate, and a stage receiving a short list can now check it against the reports, which is what this review's terminal stage did (55 declined bullets against 8 docketed). **Reopen on:** a terminal stage ruling on a merge-supplied docket list without checking it against the reports.
