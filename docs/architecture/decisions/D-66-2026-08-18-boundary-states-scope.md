# D-66: The boundary statement states scope, and moving it is the owner's

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-18 (PR #66)

## Context

**Dates and counts in this entry are on the repository's local basis (EDT) unless a sentence says otherwise**, stated because [#51](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/51) is open against exactly this gap and an entry that reproduces the defect it cites is worth less than one that names its basis.

§6 states what the pre-implementation artifact's boundary statement is *for* — *"what makes a later scope question answerable from the artifact instead of from memory"* — and stops there. It never says whether the boundary **binds**, and the convergence gate's own `amended` outcome has no post-affirmation form: the gate ends *affirmed, amended, or rejected*, and nothing says how an affirmed boundary changes afterward.

**Sessions read a wall into that silence, and said so in their own filings — five of them in one day, across three different surfaces.** **Three route findings out on the boundary statement**: [#54](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/54) carries the sentence exactly — *"routed out as sitting outside that change's boundary"* — and [#55](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/55) and [#56](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/56) say *"routed out as outside that change's boundary."* [#51](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/51) makes the same move against a **what-statement**, and [#63](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/63) against an **acceptance criterion**: *"which #59's affirmed pre-implementation artifact requires byte-unchanged ... acceptance criterion 1. Landing it there would have made the work nonconform to its own affirmation."* The word *boundary* appears nowhere in #63. **Only the three are exhibits of what this decision repairs**; #51 and #63 are the same wall reading on surfaces this decision does not reach, and they are named here rather than counted in, because a decision that repairs one surface and reports three claims a coverage it does not have. Each of those sessions followed the text faithfully, which is what makes this a defect in the text rather than in the sessions.

**The wall reading closes §7's exits by elimination.** With fix-here walled off, the guard home is closed a second time by §2's recurrence-and-stability road wherever the remedy is new code rather than the hardening of a guard the statute already assigns, `recorded` makes a claim that is false of a finding someone does intend to act on, and `owner-pending` reaches a finding whose *disposition* turns on the owner rather than one whose scope a fixed boundary had already settled. `filed` is what remains — reached by arithmetic rather than by anyone's appetite.

**The measurement, taken 2026-08-18** over the 26 open issues numbered through #64. This change's own work item, [#65](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/65), falls outside that bound. The bound does not make the corpus inert — [#63](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/63) is inside it and this change's own commits move its counted state, which is why the figure below is given at an instant.

- **41 issues created since 2026-08-15; 26 open.**
- **17 carry zero comments.**
- **20 of 26 — 77% — showed no activity after the UTC day they were filed**, measured at `2026-08-19T00:55Z`, where *activity* means a comment or a commit reference and excludes GitHub's automatic cross-references. The definition, the clock and the instant are all stated because the figure is otherwise not reconstructible, and §2 requires that a figure offered as a rule's basis be reconstructible from the record. **The same query returns 19 of 26 — 73% — at this change's own head**, because this branch's first commit referenced [#63](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/63) and moved it out of the set. That is stated rather than quietly adopted: the count is a query over a corpus this change writes into, and the lower figure is one the change itself caused. The measurement figure is the one the argument rests on, and it is the worse of the two.

Set against the target ADR-006's Context names — the predecessor's *"324 open issues; 40% untouched for 90+ days"* — the trajectory is the one §7 exists to prevent, reached in days rather than over the predecessor's eight-month life. Work items are picked up more often than routed-out findings, though not uniformly: [#40](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/40) and [#60](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/60) are both work items sitting untouched inside the measured set, so the split is a tendency and not a clean partition.

**The defect is an asymmetry, not merely a silence.** Widening the boundary changes what the owner affirmed and therefore feels costly; routing a finding out costs nothing and requires nobody. The free move is the one that manufactures issues, and no decision ever chose that gradient — it fell out of a rule that never said what it bound.

## Decision

**Statute delta:** §6's boundary statement gains what it binds and a form for changing it after affirmation — it states scope rather than prohibiting more, a finding is outside it only where remedying it would falsify an enumerated exclusion, widening it is a re-affirmation the owner gives, both directions arrive as one recorded question when the review report lands, and an unattended run records such findings `owner-pending` rather than widening or asking — with `AGENTS.md` carrying the obligation and its unattended exemption, and `tools/check_constitution.py` gaining the two `**Displaces:**` shape checks the change's own review surfaced.

**Displaces:** —

**The test applied is §12's:** displacement's object is a **frozen ADR rule**, and its trigger is that rule being retired or its citation moved. Neither happens here, so the obligatory status-line append is not owed and none is written. The nearest contestable case is §5's commitment-scoping rule: this decision provides that after a widening the announcement names the re-affirmation rather than the affirmed artifact, which changes that rule's answer in a case without editing it. That is recorded here rather than settled by an append, because a supersession pointer written on a contested reading is irreversible under §12's freeze and worse than the ambiguity it would resolve. No existing rule is retired or loses a citation. A lead-in-keyed diff of the statute returns 274 → 278 rules: four added, none removed, no rule body changed, no citation token lost. The boundary-statement rule keeps [ADR-006:46] and is byte-identical, as are §5's commitment-scoping rule and §6's convergence-gate rule.

### The four rule units, and why four rather than three

The affirmed artifact described three moves. They land as **four rule units** because §12 keys rule identity on the bold lead-in, so a rule with no lead-in of its own *"cannot be cited, superseded, or found"* (D-61, rejected option 3). The unattended route is a distinct rule a later change will need to cite; folding it into the batching rule as a trailing sentence would make it unfindable. **The substance is identical to what was affirmed** — this is the shape of the landing, not its scope.

1. **What the boundary binds.** Stated as the purpose sentence's own consequence: a scope that is *answerable* is not thereby *fixed*. A change that remedies something adjacent is not nonconforming for having remedied it; it is nonconforming for moving the boundary **silently**. A merely permissive wording was rejected: the sessions reached the wall from silence, and a permission that leaves the prohibition readable changes nothing. The reason clause names **both** remedy homes §7 gives, and states why each of the other exits is unavailable rather than asserting a universal about `recorded` that §7 contradicts — a sustained finding may lawfully take `recorded` where it was priced out, and the clause is narrowed to the finding someone does intend to act on.

2. **Widening is a re-affirmation.** The boundary is part of what was affirmed *as a whole*, so moving it is §5's convergence question put again over a smaller surface. **This is the same gate, not a new one**, so §5's cut test is satisfied by convergence's own answer rather than escaped — the looser "does not reach it" was rejected because a later session could cite it as an exemption from stating what the human uniquely decides. The record is **given in conversation like any affirmation**, carries the affirmation-record rule's contents by pointer rather than restating a shorter form, and **names the pull request** so its anchor identifies a commit on multi-pull-request work, which is the case §6 chose the issue thread to survive. From that point the re-affirmation is the recorded decision covering the work's scope and is what commitment's announcement names — without which a session announces commitment as covered by an approval at a **narrower** scope than the work, which §5 forbids.

3. **Both directions are one decision, and the trigger is defined.** **A finding is outside the affirmed boundary when remedying it would falsify an item of the boundary statement's enumerated exclusions; one matching neither the acceptance criteria nor that list is not outside it, and puts no question.** That is the reading §6's own text compels — the boundary statement is *"the explicit list of what the work is not doing"*, and a finding on neither list falsifies nothing — and it had to be written down because it is the **trigger** of the mechanism this decision installs. A residual-is-outside fail-closed was rejected: it would install a standing owner round-trip on the most frequent case, spending the resource §5 exists to conserve. The question names its actor (**the session running the change**) and its seam (**the review report's delivery**), where the comparable rules in §6 and §7 each name theirs, and **the report records which way it went in every polarity** — widened, routed out, or nothing outside the boundary to ask about — because a question skipped by rule and one forgotten are otherwise the same silence, which is what would make a mis-fire of the trigger undetectable.

4. **The unattended lane records `owner-pending`.** It widens nothing, puts no question, and does not halt. The first draft said it *"routes out, records"* — a novel term appearing nowhere else in the governing corpus, pointing at `filed` in one rule and `recorded` in the next. Naming §7's existing disposition instead is the whole repair, and it is a **subtraction**: the row keeps, it ages under §7's own backstop, the fix-here home stays available to whichever change is in flight when the question is answered, and §7's filing precondition is no longer discharged by a lane rule rather than by the judgment it demands. It also makes `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`'s existing instruction — record such a finding `owner-pending`, *"never a filed follow-up"* — correct as written, with no edit to the shipped zone. That sentence governs what the **pipeline** writes at surfacing time; §7 separately lets the owner convert the row in conversation, and a converted row may lawfully reach `filed`. The skill forbids the pipeline filing, not the owner deciding.

### The guard, and the boundary this decision moved on itself

The change's own review sustained two defects in `tools/check_constitution.py`, both with executable red pins: a **second `**Displaces:**` line is silently ignored**, because the declaration is read with a first-match search, so an innocent `—` in front lets a real declaration ride through and the record it names never gets the supersession pointer §12 makes obligatory; and an **unbracketed target** reads to a person as a declaration and to the guard as the empty set, so the cross-check silently does not run. Both are the collapse the `**Status:**` twin already checks, one field over, and §12 already assigns this validator to that file — so hardening it is discharge rather than promotion.

Their remedy lands in files the affirmed boundary statement excluded. **Under the rule this decision installs, that is a widen-or-route-out question and it is the owner's** — so the review's terminal stage put it rather than deciding it, in §5's surfacing form, recommending route-out. **The owner chose to widen**, and the re-affirmation is recorded on [#65](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/65) before the commit that relies on it. This entry records that the recommendation was not the choice taken, because a decision surface that only ever ratifies its recommendation is not one.

The third defect in the same family — that `docs/architecture/decisions/README.md` is read by no guard at all, so the row this change adds could be deleted or made to contradict its entry — was **not** offered for widening and stays priced out: validating that index means deciding what its columns mean, which no rule states, making it a rule change before a guard change.

### What is unchanged, and stated because a reader will ask

§7's five exits, the filing precondition and the pickup test all stand — what changes is how often the fix-here home is genuinely available, not the test for leaving it. The pricing rules are untouched on both surfaces: §6's *"argued rather than scored"* and §7's *"No field, threshold or query ships with this."* **No boundary edit reaches a finding a review sustained and priced out**, which was in scope by construction; that is now stated in the statute rather than only here.

### Not a trial

§2 opens the trial road where a mechanism's **absence is silent**, and is explicit that *"where the absence produces its own incident, the ordinary road is open and the trial road is not needed."* This absence produced its own incidents — three filings routing findings out on the boundary statement in one day, alongside two more reading a what-statement and an acceptance criterion the same way. The ordinary promotion road is open and is the one taken.

## Rejected

1. **Making the boundary binding in text, and building an explicit exception process.** The honest inverse: it would settle the ambiguity in the other direction and preserve reviewability by construction. Rejected because it ratifies the gradient — an exception process is a cost on widening with nothing added to routing out — and because no rule ever decided the boundary should bind; ratifying it would be adopting an accident.
2. **Leaving widening to the session's judgment**, with no re-affirmation. Removes the wall and the owner in one stroke: the boundary is part of what was affirmed, and letting the author move it unilaterally makes the affirmation cover a scope the owner never saw.
3. **A new gate type for scope changes.** Fails §5's cut test on its own terms — it could not say what the human uniquely decides that convergence does not already decide — and §5's gate set *"expected to evolve by evidence, not by accretion."*
4. **Per-finding questions as they arise.** §5 requires questions *"batched at phase seams, never dribbled mid-stream,"* and the per-finding shape is the interruption cost [#40](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/40)'s approach would have carried.
5. **Gating widening only.** Strictly worse than the silence it replaces: routing out would stay free while widening acquired a cost, sharpening the gradient this decision exists to level.
6. **A typed halt in the unattended lane.** Dominated — the halt delivers the decision no sooner than the durable row, and costs the unattended lane its purpose. The same reasoning D-61 applied to the seam's option B.
7. **Terminating the unattended route in this change** — the first draft's *"routes out, records"*. Rejected on review: it made the analogy to §7's opening seam false on the operative property, since that seam's justification is that the row **keeps** and a terminal route's does not; it left §7's filing precondition dischargeable by lane rather than by judgment; and it reinstated the wall reading for the unattended lane while unit 1 stated its repeal without qualification.
8. **A residual-is-outside fail-closed direction** for the trigger. It closes the same gap as a definition and installs a standing owner round-trip on the most frequent case — a finding matching neither the criteria nor the exclusions — which is the resource §5 exists to conserve.
9. **A guard on whether the question was put.** Whether a change *could* lawfully have covered a finding is a judgment about scope, and no artifact a lint reads distinguishes a boundary honoured from one that should have moved. The polarity line in the review report is the checkable half and is required instead. §2's promotion road stays open if a checkable shape emerges.
10. **Defining `attended`.** The gap is real and recorded in D-61, which declined a test on §2 grounds fifty-one minutes before this change's first commit, the same day. Unit 4's non-terminal route makes the term carry only the weight the owner already accepted, so no reversal is needed here.
11. **A guard for the `AGENTS.md`-to-statute correspondence.** New code on §2's recurrence road at one occurrence. The divergence is real and is recorded rather than mechanised.
12. **Re-dispositioning or closing the issues already filed.** They are this decision's exhibit, and re-judging them under a rule that did not exist when they were written is the retroactive reclassification §8 declined at corpus scale.
13. **Bundling the prioritisation work** — a read-only bucketing skill and its label — into this change. Sequenced after it by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-18, on a different road.
14. **Landing the three moves as three rule units.** §12's identity model would leave the unattended route uncitable.
15. **Editing `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`.** The twin-site duty binds only the two rules already stated at both sites and saying so; §4 governs the general case the other way — *"never duplicated prose that can drift"* — and D-61 rejected a third forced twin. Unit 4 makes the skill's existing text correct without an edit.
16. **Editing §5's commitment-scoping rule or §6's boundary-statement rule directly.** Both are frozen-cited bodies this change leaves byte-identical; the repairs land in this decision's own new units instead, which is what keeps `Displaces:` honest.
