# D-167: A behavior claim carries a probe at every stage, and the obligation term loses its sign

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-25 (PR #167)

## Context

Two issues land together because one of them said so. [#125](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/125) narrows the review's jurisdiction to what it can arbitrate; its boundary declares that `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`'s price clause is not reopened *"except as #121's disposition requires, coordinated in this same opening"*, so [#121](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/121) rides the same track rather than waiting for a change that would open the paragraph a second time.

**The defect #125 names.** The evidence standards demand probes from *findings*, and one seat carries an execution charter. Nothing stopped a behavior question — what a cold reader does under a wording, whether a mechanism fires, what using the result is like — from being argued across defense, judge, and post-fix cycles as if prose could settle it. Two runs on this repo's own record are the warrant: the [#115 spike](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/115#issuecomment-5384143581) ended in an afternoon what argument had circled for days, and [#138's experiments](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/138#issuecomment-5389959566) falsified the harm claims of the only two sustained highs ever tested — PR #127's, where four cold seats produced the predicted mis-sorting zero times.

**The defect #121 names.** The obligation term took one sign, negative where a remedy removes a rule or guard. Remedies routinely produce both signs at once on different carriers, releasing an obligation from one population while creating one for another. Four cold seats priced a four-remedy docket and, asked what was hardest to state, all four named this. None produced a wrong price; the clause was awkward, not wrong, which is evidence about urgency rather than about whether the defect is real.

**Line numbers moved under both issues.** Both cite `:65` for the price clause; [D-153](D-153-2026-08-24-consequence-field-and-the-batching-cut.md) put the consequence-shape paragraph there and pushed the price clause down. The work was done against the tree, not the citations.

## Decision

### The five clauses, and why each landed where it did

**A behavior claim is a hypothesis until a probe answers it**, added as its own bullet in the evidence standards rather than folded into the load-bearing-claim bullet above it. The existing bullet already reaches a behavior claim in principle — *"a load-bearing claim requires the check that would falsify it"* — and what it does not reach is the obligation to **commission**: a defense contesting behavior argues, and a ruling may settle on that argument. The new bullet is what binds the stages after the finder, and folding it upward would have buried the marginal content inside a sentence that reads as already saying it.

**Severity is reached, and the executable class is excluded in the same breath.** #138's [yield report](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/138#issuecomment-5389959440) is why: the same cold pass returned 1 high / 1 medium on a defective tree and 0 / 0 on the fixed one while volume stayed flat, so the severity channel is the part of the pipeline demonstrably carrying information, and a probe protects the one channel that discriminates. The clause is keyed to the harm claim being behavioral, not to how often it is: the same corpus records most highs naming record-integrity failures, so a frequency claim here would have been false. The exclusion is stated because a high in the executable class — red lint, a guard that cannot fail — already arrives with its probe, and re-probing it is pure cost.

**`:60` was not amended, and that is a decision rather than an omission.** *"Severity is a claim too"* sits five bullets below the new one, in the same list, and a reader reaching one reaches the other. Restating the reach at `:60` would duplicate a rule inside one file, which is the drift `authoring` forbids and the failure the plain brief's own duplication already cost this repo once.

**Probe hygiene rides on the existing probe bullet** rather than opening a new one, because it is a condition on the same object. `:57` already pins a new test red pre-fix and demands both polarities; neither reaches a probe that reports a result it never took. [#142](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/142) is the incident and is linked rather than retold, per `authoring`'s state-the-rule-link-the-evidence standard: a size-preserving mutation masked by stale bytecode reported SURVIVES for a guard it never broke, a defense published the false result, and the retraction was also wrong. The rule's reason clause carries what the incident proves — a lying probe is *trusted*, which argument at least is not. **The condition is that the negative control be drawn from the probe's own class, and the review is what forced that wording.** As first landed the clause asked for *its own negative control or equivalent*, and a rebuild of the incident showed that letter is satisfiable by a control that cannot fire: a size-changing control invalidates the stale bytecode, reports KILLED, and so demonstrates a difference — while the size-preserving arm it was meant to vouch for stays masked. A same-class control instead reports the same value as the probe, the demonstration fails, and the report is blocked, which is the clause working. The same edit replaced *the post-change question* with *the question it is credited with*, discharging a two-vocabulary collision with `:59`'s *pre-fix question* at the cost of widening the duty's reach from post-change probes to any probe at any stage — recorded here because that widening was not separately ordered.

**Route-to-spike is a species of `routed`, not a fifth disposition.** The four disposition values are copied into `docs/reviews.jsonl` by every review and guarded there; a fifth would have rippled into the index schema [D-136](D-136-2026-08-23-index-dispositions-and-staffing.md) landed two days earlier, for a distinction the existing disposition already expresses — the spike is the vehicle, and the shipped clause names who runs it and by when.

**The timing seam is answered by typed-halt rather than by an exception to it.** #125's routed finding recorded that `skills/spikes/SKILL.md` admits a disputed review thesis and says nothing about *when*, while this file forbids interruption from first dispatch to final ruling, and that the cells cannot resolve it between themselves because no skill may reference another. They do not have to, but the first answer to this was wrong and the record says so rather than hiding it. **As landed in round one, the clause routed a dispute to a spike that ran *after* the final ruling** — decided by the session from typed-halt, and reported on the PR rather than put to the owner. The review falsified it: `:55` forbids ruling on a cheap-to-probe behavior claim without the probe, routing is itself a sustained disposition, and so no compliant ruling existed. Five of five cold judges at the tier that staffs the seat routed rather than probed, and four of five across two tiers then invented a downstream vehicle the same sentence forecloses; an external reviewer reached the contradiction independently from the diff alone. **The shipped clause instead routes only what the terminal stage cannot itself arbitrate cheaply, and names a runner and a deadline** — the dispatching session, before it closes the review, or it records why not — which was itself read cold by two fresh seats against a control arm before it landed. The atomic window is still never entered, and typed-halt is untouched.

**The coverage exception D-132 left open is closed in `skills/spikes/SKILL.md`.** [D-132](D-132-2026-08-23-spikes-graduate.md) narrowed *"reviewed with the work that carries it"* to *"wherever that work is itself reviewed"*, because under the broadened trigger the carrier may be a review's final report, which is posted rather than reviewed. The clause now names that carrier and says where coverage falls instead: on the change the result drives, which is reviewed like any other change — and where it drives none, the clause says plainly that the report is the whole of what covers it rather than leaving a universal standing over a case it does not reach.

**The session note gets reachability before standing, in that order, because standing without reachability is moot.** The note is added to the shared block's assignment enumeration, and the cold-boundary paragraph says why carrying it does not spend a cold vantage — it reports what using the artifact was like rather than what the review has said about it. Standing is then stated in the behavior bullet, where behavior claims are already governed: real-use evidence outranks panel hypothesis **on behavior and on nothing else**, and on reasoning the panel's standards rule unchanged. The first full run had already proved the clause operable and the mechanism unlawful — the note decided two rulings against the panel ([field data](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/122#issuecomment-5390224102)) after reaching the seats by a move no rule authorised.

### #121: the sign is dropped, and the term is criterion 10 as affirmed

**The owner ruled option B at the convergence gate**, from four argued options: carry both signs explicitly with their carriers; drop the sign; keep one sign and name the dominant carrier; do nothing. B was recommended and taken. With no sign, a defense naming what a remedy releases *and* what it creates reads as a benefit without sign machinery — which is option A's substance at negative word cost — and `:68` already says *"Argued, never scored"*, so the sign buys nothing a scored system would need.

**Round one shipped a wider wording than the criterion, and the review reverted it.** The reasoning was that pure deletion leaves the second term reading *what it obliges later sessions to do*, and a remedy that **releases** an obligation obliges nothing — so the noun was widened to *what it changes in what later sessions must do — a release as readily as a new obligation*. Two things falsified that. The criterion's own next sentence, which the argument never quoted, already answers it: *"A defence is then free to name both what a remedy releases and what it creates, each with its carrier, without the clause forcing one sign and suppressing the other half."* And the widened noun states no reach at all, where the deleted appositive carried the `{rule, guard}` narrowing [D-107] [D-113] — a reach the affirmed boundary listed among what is **not** reopened, and `releases an obligation later sessions carry` is close to the construct [#115](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/115)'s spike withdrew, where one seat of two priced prose read-weight as a release. **The shipped term is criterion 10 as affirmed: the appositive deleted, nothing in its place.** Under pure deletion the reach question dissolves rather than needing a home, which is why the owner could list it as not reopened.

**What the loss costs, stated rather than netted away.** [#111](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/111) existed to protect the removal credit, and this weakens it: an expensive deletion no longer carries an explicit discount. Two things mitigate and neither cancels it. [D-113](D-113-2026-08-22-component-sign-and-purpose-statement.md) already dismantled the total-sign reading that made a costly deletion price as free, which was #111's actual harm; and `:17` continues to put the burden of argument on keeping the words, so the thumb on deletions survives at the charter level where it is about the artifact rather than about a remedy's price.

**D-113's grammar survives and its appositive does not.** *"in two terms"* is untouched, which is why `[D-113]` stays on the line beside `[D-107]` and `[D-167]`: item 1 of that entry is superseded by being read, and the rest of it still governs.

### Criterion 7 was dropped by the owner, and D-132's routed seam is answered in the other direction

D-132 recorded a second untested joint with no falsifier and routed it here: `skills/spikes/SKILL.md`'s consume exemption supplies its only worked account of what makes something a spike — *an artifact is affirmed and handed off on the strength of it* — a property no review thesis has, so a session mid-review reading it for the discriminating test may conclude its case is not the kind the file means. The affirmed artifact required that any rewrite be cold-checked first, per that cell's own trigger.

**The check ran and the premise fell.** Two arms differing in exactly that sentence, four fresh Opus seats on Claude Code (Windows), six situations each asked twice — *what would you do* before *does the practice apply*. **Four of four seats, both arms, routed the mid-review dispute to a spike, and not one of them decided it on the sentence under test.** They decided it on three sentences the arms share — `:12`, `:16` and `:22`, two of them *below* the exemption rather than above it — on the trigger's own naming of *"a thesis still disputed after a round of review"*, on the executor-is-a-reader reframe, and on the tiebreak *"what a reader will do under the wording is a spike; where the wording belongs is not"* — all identical across arms. The consume exemption's actual job in every seat's hands is discriminating an idle check from a load-bearing one; a session holding a review thesis never arrives at it. 24 of 24 calls matched expectation, nothing over-fired, and the negative control — the file's own paradigm case, which #132's first attempted fix had swallowed — held in both arms. The [full report](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/125#issuecomment-5404978774) carries the docket, the arms, and the limits.

**So the rewrite is not shipped.** A replacement whose stated purpose is that the tail no longer under-fires, for a tail four of four seats say does not under-fire, is a clause defending against a reader who does not err — `authoring`'s definition of armor — in the run's most-iterated sentence, at the joint #132 already spent five arms on. Because criterion 7 is affirmed, dropping it was the owner's call and not the session's. **He ruled it dropped** ([the ruling](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/125#issuecomment-5405141900)), from three argued options — drop, ship the candidate on the weaker discrimination signal, or re-scope onto the residual — and the residual was filed as [#168](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/168).

**The residual, so the next session does not re-derive it.** One seat per arm named that sentence the hardest in the file to apply, for a reason the rewrite does not fix and neither wording changes: the test is settled downstream of the question — *"applying it honestly means forecasting what I will do with the answer, not judging the question in front of me."* The weak signal favouring the candidate was self-reported ease on the idle-versus-load-bearing discrimination, two seats against two, with every verdict identical across arms — too thin to buy the rewrite. **The falsifier that would reopen this:** a session mid-review reading the consume exemption for the discriminating test and concluding its thesis is not the kind the file means.

## Rejected

- **Amending `:60` to restate severity's reach.** Duplication inside one file; the bullets sit in one list.
- **A fifth disposition value for route-to-spike.** Ripples into #136's index schema and its guard, for a distinction `routed` already carries.
- **A named exception to typed-halt** so a routed spike could run mid-review. Unnecessary once routing is seen to be a ruling's act, and it would open the atomic window for the first time.
- **Deferring a routed spike past the review's close.** Strands the result outside the cycle that asked for it.
- **The widened obligation noun** — *what it changes in what later sessions must do* — shipped in round one and reverted by the review. It states no reach where the deleted appositive carried `{rule, guard}`, and the criterion it departed from had already granted what it was reaching for.
- **Naming the carrier for each half of the obligation term**, as #121's option A proposed. That is the option the owner did not take, and it is the one that grows the file's densest paragraph.
- **Shipping the `spikes` trigger rewrite.** Above — armor on this evidence.
- **A third arm or a wider docket** to press the under-fire premise further. Four seats agreeing across both arms is not a result a fifth seat overturns, and #132 records five rounds of rewriting one paragraph on apparatus-facing evidence as the self-referential defect supply [#122](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/122) warns against.
