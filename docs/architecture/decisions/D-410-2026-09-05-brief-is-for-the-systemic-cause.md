# D-410: A problem's brief is for its systemic cause, and a brief that asks nothing says so first

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-05 (PR #410)

## Context

Two issues against one cell, worked as one change because both are rules about the plain brief and both were settled in one conversation with the owner on 2026-09-05.

Both are children of [#391](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/391), a map of where the owner-facing interaction design is missing. That map's thesis is that `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` governs the two *endpoints* of the interaction — the brief's lock, the artifact, the cold seat — and says nothing about the stretch that produces them. [#401](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/401) is its aspect 1 taken at pickup; [#403](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/403) is aspects 1 and 7 at the brief's first line.

**What the owner was reacting to.** Asked what makes him wince, on 2026-09-05: *"it's bringing things up that I thought we'd already sorted out. For example I don't know how many discussions I've had with you on having very slim always-on components… but it seems to keep coming up."* The record shows the opposite of a lost ruling — 48 of 87 decision entries mention it, 91 closed issues match the term, and about 17 open ones are about it by title. Read by title, the open ones are **breaches being caught and holes in the enforcement mechanism**, not proposals to relax the rule. So the rule applies; applying it by discovery sends him one brief per breach, and each brief re-explains the rule to him. #401 fixes the cause of the volume, #403 fixes the cost of each one.

**Where the rule surface was silent.** The cell began the brief at the issue as filed — *"the brief is drafted and revised with them in conversation and posted as its own comment when it locks"* — with nothing sending a session to what produced the issue. `grep -rn -iE "root cause|systemic|why-chain|symptom"` over `skills/`, `docs/cells/` and `AGENTS.md` at the base returned one hit, the CRLF paragraph in the doctrine. Nothing competed with either rule and nothing obliged recording what a cause explains.

**The brief was amended mid-change.** After the artifact had been settled by a cold seat, the owner amended #401's brief to add the sibling-tying sentence, on the question *"do we have anything that checks the rest of the board when we've identified a root cause, to see how it impacts what remains?"* The amendment is a new term, so the settlement was void and the artifact was settled again against the amended brief. Decision 6 records what that cost and why the superseded verdict is still on the record.

## Decision

**1. The rule sits after the premise-test sentence, not in *The plain brief*.** Placement is file layout and therefore the session's under this cell's own list, and the owner saw this placement when he affirmed the wording. The reason it is not merely aesthetic: the rule's own sentence *"A cause still a hypothesis when the asking stops is a premise that could make the brief wrong"* routes an unevidenced cause into the premise test **by naming it**, and the premise test is the sentence it now follows. Separating the two leaves the untested-cause clause pointing at a sentence three screens away, and that pointer is the only thing making the clause enforceable. The cost is real and is accepted: a reader hunting for brief rules goes to *The plain brief* and will not find this one there. It is bounded by both sections sitting in one file a session loads whole.

**2. The declaration fires only on a brief that asks the owner nothing, and the rejected option is recorded here because reversing this looks like an improvement.** The fork was put argued: **A**, every brief declares which kind it is; **B**, only the ones that ask him nothing. He ruled B. **A's advantage was real and is the reason this decision is written down** — under A the rule is self-checking, because a brief missing its declaration is visibly malformed, whereas under B a session that judged correctly and a session that simply forgot produce the same silence. B was chosen because on a brief that *does* put a decision the declaration only repeats the ask the brief already ends with, and the signal he lacks is the negative one. **A future session that "fixes" the ambiguity by making every brief declare has reversed an owner ruling, not tightened a rule.** The shipped text therefore states the negative half explicitly — *"A brief that does ask them something says nothing of the kind"* — so the omission is a rule rather than an oversight.

**3. Silence carries a meaning, and that is not the guard the owner excluded.** #403's affirmed brief puts out of scope both how a session decides which kind a change is and any catcher for getting it wrong. The shipped clause *"a brief that applies a settled ruling silently is a defect in the brief"* adds no classifier and fires no check; it makes an omission reviewable, which is what the rule needs to be enforceable at all. The residual ambiguity is the accepted cost of the ruled option, recorded on #403 with A's case.

**4. The tying obligation uses the `filing` cell's existing candidate-tie route, and the tie set gains no row.** None of that cell's four rows asserts *this cause produced that symptom*. The cell provides the escape itself — *"a relationship none of the four expresses is carried in the same block in prose and flagged as a candidate for the set"* — which is what [#404](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/404) already does with its fourteen symptoms, and where a search finds none the same cell's *no siblings on the board* form applies. So the amendment is executable at this revision with **no edit to `skills/filing/SKILL.md`**. Settling whether *supersedes* carries the relationship or the set gains a row is [#409](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/409)'s, along with what the ties do at the refresh and at landing.

**5. This lands the producing half of the tie mechanism and nothing that consumes it.** A cause does not outrank its symptoms at the refresh and a landing does not walk them; both are #409's want. The order is deliberate rather than incidental: **#409 has nothing to act on until the ties exist**, so the producer lands first. The consequence to hold is that between this merging and #409 landing, sessions write ties that nothing reads.

**6. Two things the owner's amendment left unstated were settled as the session's rather than by asking him to re-amend.** His sentence says the siblings are *"tied to the cause"*, which presumes something on the board to tie to. Where the why-chain climbs past the issue as filed — the ordinary case — **the session files the cause first and ties the siblings to it**, which is the shape #404 and #405 already took; *"before the brief locks"* is what fixes that ordering against the affirmation. And the naming in #403's rule is **plain English, never an issue number or decision citation**, because the *Plain English* bullet two above it sends exactly those below the blockquote — so a declaration naming its ruling by number would breach the bullet it sits beneath. Both were checked against the tree by the seat that settled the artifact.

**7. #402 is not in this change, and the cut is by kind rather than by convenience.** #401 and #403 are rules about the brief — what it is about, and what its first line declares. [#402](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/402), the design sitting, is a rule about the conversation that produces the brief, plus a `references/` file that loads. Its wording is deferred by its own filing, and its decision procedure names #401's why-chain, so it wants this landed first. Bundling it would have put an unsettled shape into a change whose other two halves were affirmed.

**8. The artifact reached settled twice, against two different terms, and both verdicts stay on the record.** A seat said `would` on the pre-amendment text; the owner then amended the brief; a third seat said `would` on the amended text. The first settlement is void — an amended brief is a new term, so that verdict judged something else — but deleting it would hide that this artifact was once settled against a term that has since changed. A round-1 `would not` also stays: it caught a locating claim that was false about the tree, the artifact having said the premise-test sentence closed the *first* paragraph of its section when it closes the second.

## Rejected

**Every brief declaring which kind it is.** Decision 2. Self-checking, and rejected by the owner because most briefs put a decision and would carry a clause repeating their own ask.

**Placing #401's rule in *The plain brief*.** Decision 1. Better routing for a reader hunting brief rules; rejected because it breaks the chain onto the premise-test sentence that makes the untested-cause clause enforceable.

**Bundling #402 into this change.** Decision 7. Rejected on its shape being unsettled and its dependence on this rule landing first.

**Re-amending the owner's text to cover what his sentence left unstated.** Decision 6. Rejected because both gaps are implementer-facing detail an artifact can settle, and re-amending an affirmed sentence spends his attention on something no fork turns on.

**Adding a `symptom-of` row to the tie set now.** Decision 4. It was discussed on #401 as a candidate and parked; the `filing` cell's own prose route carries the relationship at this revision, and #409 owns settling whether a row is warranted.
