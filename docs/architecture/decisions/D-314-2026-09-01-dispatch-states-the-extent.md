# D-314: The dispatch states the job's extent, because the box does not

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-01 (PR #314)

## Context

`skills/experience-session/SKILL.md` binds the consumer's side of the time-box — *"Expiry without an answer means stop and report what the box bought — never a silent extension"* — and its dispatch guidance says what a job must and must not name. Neither says what a box does **not** do.

[PR #263's second experience session](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/263#issuecomment-5472614246) is the incident. Its dispatch set a box of one working pass and separately asked the consumer to work out what the change owed and do it. Under this practice's own standards the answer to the second is a review and an experience session, each buying a further look. The consumer derived that correctly from `AGENTS.md` and the charter, ran it, stopped short of the terminus, and named the shortfall rather than disposing of it — citing `after-the-fix.md`'s rule that the floor *"is never recorded or handed on in place of being run."* The consumer was right and the dispatch was wrong; the dispatcher wrote it up against itself in that same note.

**The incident has two halves and this entry is only one of them.** The material-side half — that the fix-and-look cycle's only real bound is a budget a review's ruling names, so outside a review the flow describes a loop with no exit — is [#274](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/274), and is untouched here. That filing expressly does not rest on the dispatch defect this entry fixes, so a ruling closing it would not dispose of #289; the independence the two records state is #274's, and this entry's own independence from #274 is argued below rather than cited.

## Decision

One paragraph in **"Running one"**, immediately after *"Give the job, not the test"* — plus two sentences this change's own review ordered, recorded under *What the review changed* below:

> **State the job's extent, because the box does not.** A job that defers its extent to the material — *work out what this change owes and do it* — is realistic here and unbounded, since this practice's answer to it is a review and a session, each buying a further look; the time-box caps how long a run may take, never how much it takes on, so a consumer holding both resolves toward the job — [one ran well past its box and still stopped short of what it had correctly worked out it owed](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/263#issuecomment-5472614246). Name which of the owed steps are in scope, or make working out what is owed the deliverable and doing them out of scope. [D-314]

**Placement is "Running one", not the Time-box bullet.** The defect is written into the job statement, and "Running one" is where a dispatcher composes one. The Time-box bullet makes no scope claim, so it is not the defective text; it also sits under *"The instrument — three pieces, nothing more"*, a heading whose own closing line resists growth. A rule about how to write a dispatch is not a property of the instrument's second piece.

**The rule names two moves rather than asserting only that the box does not bound the job.** That is the whole of what the spike bought, and the reason is below.

## The spike reversed the reasoning; the premise it recorded testing held

A cold-seat A/B ran **before the criteria were written** — after the brief was affirmed, which is later than this practice's ordering asks and is recorded below — six seats, three per arm, each handed the cell body inline as its whole world and stopped at the dispatch it would write. The arms differed in exactly this paragraph. [The report](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/289#issuecomment-5501042628) carries the docket, the arms, the stop point and the quoted returns.

**The premise the spike recorded testing held, and the reasoning is what reversed.** That premise was *“a session composing an experience-session dispatch, holding the cell as it stands, pairs a time-box with a job whose extent the material sets and does not bound the job”* — and the control arm confirmed it: not one of the three stated an extent. What the run overturned is the *justification*. Three of three control seats named the contradiction unprompted, one writing that the pairing “is a contradiction the consumer resolves toward the job” — so the rule cannot be argued from the clash being invisible. **A stricter form of that correction, because this entry teaches the `spikes` method by example:** the *“and does not notice”* wording, which an earlier draft of this entry called the falsified premise, appears in no document written before the run — not the brief, not the affirmation record, not the issue, and not the report's own statement of its premise. It first appears in the artifact, ninety-four seconds after the report. The record was fitted to the result, and the review caught it.

**What the control arm could not reach was the remedy.** None of the three stated an extent. Two declared a tiebreak — *the box wins, stop and report unfinished* — and one removed the time-box from its dispatch altogether. A tiebreak leaves the consumer holding an unbounded job and a clock, which is the #263 shape by construction rather than by accident: spend the box on a job that cannot be finished, hand back a truncation. Three of three treatment seats instead produced an extent — an in-scope enumeration, an explicit out-of-scope clause naming the recursive steps, and *working out what is owed* promoted from task to deliverable.

So the rule earns its place on **which remedy a dispatcher reaches**, never on whether the clash is visible. A version asserting only that the box does not bound the job is what the control arm already had, and it lands a dispatcher at the tiebreak.

**Two limits are recorded rather than smoothed.** The docket put the two desires in adjacent sentences and asked each seat for its own rationale, which is far more salient than the real case, where job and budget are settled at different moments and no dispatcher writes a rationale — so *3 of 3 noticed* is an upper bound on how visible this is in the wild, and it does not touch the arm difference, both arms having had the identical docket. And the shipped paragraph differs from the tested one in **two** ways, both of which add a pointer and alter no instruction: the seats received the incident clause unlinked, so that none could follow a URL out to this change's own record, and the shipped line also ends `[D-314]`. The second was declared in [an amendment to the pin](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/289#issuecomment-5501309445), and stripping both from the shipped line reproduces the tested paragraph byte-exactly — 608 characters, verified independently by three review stages.

**The acceptance criteria are pinned to this wording because of that.** Criteria 1–3 name the completed A/B as their falsifier, which measures nothing unless the built text is the tested text; [the settlement comment](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/289#issuecomment-5501106599) states that any other wording of the two moves or the reason clause voids them until the A/B is re-run. A cold seat found that hole in the artifact and it is adopted rather than argued away.

## Rejected

**Stating the rule once for every cold dispatch in the practice**, whose home would be the charter. `skills/spikes/SKILL.md` already carries a sibling — *"Every bound in this cell is yours to impose on the seat, because it cannot infer one"* ([D-307](D-307-2026-09-01-a-commissioned-probe-stops-at-its-datum.md)) — and both rest on the same observation about what a seat handed an ordinary job does. Rejected on three grounds. They are not one standard, so the one-owner rule does not bite: a spike seat must dispatch nothing, stop at its datum, and **not do the job**, where an experience-session consumer is meant to do one, so what the two share is an observation rather than a standard. Routing up would spend an outflow against a near-full always-on surface to buy a sentence neither cell shares. And the evidence is one incident, in an experience-session dispatch. The charter requires an incident from real work before an agent-proposed rule is admitted at all; it sets no ceiling on reach, so what bounds this rule to its own case is the evidence rather than a rule of the charter's. A cold seat checked this against `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md` at `e333441` and both cells, and cleared it.

**A rule prescribing the tiebreak** — *say which of the job and the box wins*. This is what two of the three control seats invented unaided — the third removed the box instead — so writing it down buys little that is not already there, and, the weightier ground, it writes down the worse remedy: it holds the overshoot only where the box is hard, and it guarantees a truncated deliverable.

**Banning the deferred-extent job.** It is a job this repository's own flow makes routine, and the incident's own consumer produced the finding [#274](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/274) records precisely by working out what was owed — which is the ground, the ranking against other jobs having no count behind it. A criterion guards the job's survival.

**Placing it in the Time-box bullet**, argued above.

**Making the stated extent a required field of the note.** The cell's own text says a template or field list is *"how it stops being cheap enough to run"*; a criterion guards the instrument at three pieces.

## What this does not fix

**[#274](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/274).** Whether the fix-and-look cycle terminates at all outside a chartered review is a property of the material, not of a dispatch. **The rule holds either way**, which is why this did not wait on it: even with a terminus, the owed work is a review plus a session plus the looks those buy, which no single working pass contains.

**And it does not make a dispatcher bound anything it has not thought of.** The paragraph fires on a job whose extent the material sets. A job that is unbounded for some other reason gets nothing from it.

The defence of the words is the spike: the paragraph carries two named moves and a reason clause, and the arm without them left three of three dispatchers at a remedy that reproduces the incident.

## What the review changed

A five-seat panel, a defense and a judge ran against this change. The rule itself survived: the terminal ruling was **fit for purpose once the named fixes land**, no sustained finding showed the paragraph failing its purpose, and the four findings arguing it would misfire were each dropped on the change's own use-evidence — the A/B treatment arm and the experience session answered them. **The shipped paragraph is unchanged from the tested text**; every ordered fix was sited to keep it so.

**Two sentences were added to the cell, neither in the pinned paragraph.**

- **The dispatcher inspects the extent as well as the tree** (in the `:48` paragraph). The rule as first shipped required an extent and never required it to be *performable*. An owed step carries parties and permissions, not only a stretch of time, so a dispatch can name a step in scope while capping what performs it, and the consumer discovers the contradiction only after spending the box. This change's own experience session did exactly that — it put the review in scope, capped sub-agents at one, and the cold seat that should have settled its artifact went unperformed. The cell already gave the *tree* a pre-dispatch check and gave the new object none.
- **The note discloses a step the dispatch excluded** (widening the trigger at `:33`). That sentence was keyed to a failure of *authority*; a step bounded out by the dispatch lacks none, so the rule made deliberate exclusion the common cause of an unperformed step and left it disclosed by nothing. The trigger widened rather than a field being added: an instrument that grows a required field stops being cheap enough to run, and a note with nothing unperformed still owes nothing.

**Nine findings were recorded rather than fixed**, in `docs/recorded-findings.jsonl`. The largest class is real imprecision inside the pinned wording — `:54` calls the owed chain *unbounded* while `:42` says the session limb *fires once*; it says the box caps *how long* where the cell admits units that are not duration; and "extent" is a term the tree defines once. Each remedy would reword the tested paragraph and void the criteria the A/B earned, which buys a six-seat re-run for a low. They wait for a second instance.

**The ordering this practice asks for was inverted, and the record then drifted toward the result.** The `engagement` cell says a premise that could make the brief wrong is tested before it locks. Here the brief was affirmed at 21:55:30Z and the A/B reported at 22:03:24Z. It came out harmless — the brief's claims survived the run — but harmless by outcome rather than by construction: had the control arm bounded the job unprompted, the change would have bought nothing. Then the entry's first draft named as the falsified premise a *"does not notice"* formulation that appears in no document written before the run, and first appears in the artifact ninety-four seconds after the report. Both were caught by prosecution rather than by the session that wrote them, which is the argument for running the prosecution.
