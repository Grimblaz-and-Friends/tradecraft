# D-322: The cold interpretation check gets a written procedure, and its verdict gains a third thing to say

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-01 (PR #322)

## Context

`skills/charter/SKILL.md` mandates that a pre-implementation artifact be settled by a seat with none of the conversation's history, this repository's `landing` cell orders it in the flow, and `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` names that seat and points at nothing. At `fbb6cc4` there is no `skills/engagement/references/` directory, no `commands/` and no `agents/`. So every session invents the dispatch, and the inventions diverge. **An earlier draft of this entry said `AGENTS.md`'s flow line carried that mandate; it carries neither the word *cold* nor the word *flow*.** The claim was inherited from the recorded finding this change promotes and restated rather than re-derived, which is the failing this entry's last section is about, caught by the review one paragraph away from where it is confessed.

Three filings are the consequences of that one absence, and each defers a question only the others answer.

- **[#308](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/308)** promotes a recorded finding on the first of its two disjunctive limbs, met on PR #304 — the second limb is about being unable to *dispatch* a check, and what the incident shows is an inability to *resume* one. One consumer, never told anything was under test, spent fourteen cold-seat readings across three rounds, twice tried to resume a finished seat to ask whether a verdict still stood, got no reply either time, paid a full fresh read both times, and volunteered that this was *"the single thing I'd change about how this practice's artifact stage runs."* A second consumer on the same tree improvised a different shape across four verdicts and roughly 340k subagent tokens, and reported its own result unsatisfactory.
- **[#296](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/296)**: the verdict vocabulary is two-valued and only one value is terminal, so a seat that has correctly identified a question the session cannot settle has no move but to fail the artifact again. #286 ran twelve adverse verdicts across ten revisions from four seats; rounds 1–5 caught defects that would have shipped real damage, and **rounds 6–12 found nothing that would have shipped** — four successive rewrites of one clause, each correctly rejected, on a question the owner settled in one sentence when finally asked.
- **[#297](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/297)**: under repeated check the artifact accumulates material aimed at the checker — a verdict-history status line, a twenty-item list of what eleven rejections changed, per-section notes on earlier false claims, a paragraph on abandoned drafting approaches — and gets worse as the handoff contract the cell says it is. At least two of those additions existed *because verdicts asked for them*. The obligations were sound; **the defect is siting.**

## The decision

**One new file, `skills/engagement/references/cold-seat.md`,** loaded only when the check runs, carrying four things. The pointer and one corrected sentence are all that `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` takes.

**The dispatch gives the seat the draft artifact and the brief it carries and nothing else that aims attention.** This is derived rather than chosen: the cell already states the artifact's own test — *"an implementer who was not there builds the agreed thing from the artifact and the brief it carries, alone"* — so a seat given more settles the reading against material the implementer will not have, and the check would pass documents that fail the test it is run to apply. **The seat therefore does not read the work's issue thread**, which #308 records as answered nowhere. It **does** read the working root: the artifact is judged on whether its reading holds against the tree, so the tree must be reachable, and what is bounded is the aiming, not the reading. The dispatch names that root and the revision it expects the seat to confirm — or, where the tree has no commit to name, says so and asks the seat to report what it finds, since a tree built for a run of its own commonly has no history and the flat form sends every such seat an unsatisfiable first instruction. It states outright that any always-on text arriving otherwise is not governing — a fresh dispatch commonly starts against the repository default while the tree under judgement is on a branch. The seat **dispatches nothing of its own**, a bound it cannot infer because it does not know it is an instrument. **One seat per verdict**; every count observed before this was an invention.

**A third verdict: `not settleable` — the artifact turns on a fork that is the owner's.** The cell already specifies the answer — *"the artifact carries the fork rather than closing it"*, criteria holding whichever way they rule, the turning ones written against the recommendation and marked contingent — so what the verdict obliges is a revision the cell already describes. **What that revision costs was got wrong first and the review corrected it.** The entry originally called it a reading amendment, borrowing the cell's *"needing no second verdict"*; but that clause governs an amendment recorded as a further comment, and the cell's sentence opens *"An amendment is a further comment, never an edit"* — while this revision edits the artifact's criteria. It is now stated as its own exception with its own reason: the seat's finding was that no reading settles the artifact, so a revision performing exactly the remedy it named cannot fail a reading the verdict never affirmed. One exception, named as one, rather than an instance of a rule that excludes it. **Because the issue thread is withheld, the file states what the seat judges a closed fork against**: a criterion turning on an answer *the brief* does not settle, where what turns on the pick is a genuine fork under the charter's test. The cell's own statement of that defect is keyed to the issue, because it addresses the session, which has the issue; a seat that never sees one would find the third verdict unreachable by construction. **That gap was found by a cold seat and not by the session** — it is the third of the three points round one returned, and it is the clearest evidence that the withholding needed the sentence rather than the sentence needing the withholding.

**The bound on re-runs is not a count.** A count is crude and licenses shipping an unsettled artifact at round N, which is worse than the loop; #296 argues the same and #290 argues the repetition is itself the signal. The bound is [D-304]'s repeated-point rule — a second verdict on the same point being evidence about the point, one of whose three remedies is the fork above — plus **a carve-out for a revision that neither changes nor adds a claim the verdict rested on.** The session states what it changed and which claim of the verdict's it did not touch, and proceeds. **Adding is in the condition because an external pass found it missing** — a new acceptance criterion alters no existing claim, so a carve-out keyed to change alone would admit an unjudged criterion into a settled artifact under the verdict's own hash. No seat and no defense probe reached that direction. That is the cheap re-verdict path PR #304's consumer reported missing in its own words: *"there is no cheap 're-verdict this one-word correction' path in the cell."*

**The [D-304] verdict lines are the whole of the provenance a round leaves in the artifact**, and the file tables where each other kind goes — the pull request body for what changed, the decision entry for rationale and abandoned approaches. **The seat is told the list is closed**, because #297's evidence is that the accretion was partly demanded by the instrument. What a later seat needs beyond the closed list travels in the dispatch, which answers #297's own stated crux and is answerable only because #308 builds a dispatch to put it in.

## The meaning changes this makes, as revising governing prose requires

**`skills/engagement/SKILL.md`'s *"What settles the artifact is a verdict that it would"* gains *"or a verdict of `not settleable` discharged by the fork being carried"*, and the label that route produces.** Before this, exactly one verdict was terminal. After it, two routes settle an artifact, and the second settles it without any seat ever having said *would* — a real widening of what a downstream stage may inherit as a judged reading, bounded by the third verdict's own condition: the fork must be the owner's, and the revision must be the contingent form the cell already specifies. **Two review findings shaped that sentence.** It first named the verdict by its ordinal — *the third verdict* — which the body defines nowhere; a probe rotating the three bullets in the reference file left `lint`, `roster` and the suite green while turning the sentence into a licence to settle on a rejection, so the verdict is named rather than counted. And the cell already required the other non-affirmed route to carry a qualified label — *settled by a recorded truncation, never the word bare* — which this route did not; it now carries **settled by a discharged `not settleable`**, so no stage inherits it as a reading a seat affirmed.

**The narrowing of *"an artifact edited after its verdict is a draft again."*** That sentence was written to hold a line the wait paragraph beside it says breaks under competent sessions acting reasonably. The carve-out is stated as a narrowing rather than folded in, its condition is the verdict's own basis rather than the size of the edit, and it carries a recording obligation so a later reader can test the claim. **The wrong implementation is a carve-out loose enough to be read as the general licence**, and this change names that rather than assuming against it.

## What was rejected

- **A count of rounds.** Crude, and licenses shipping unsettled work at the cap.
- **Giving the seat the issue thread.** It would settle the fork question directly, at the cost of breaking the derivation the whole dispatch rests on and of the check passing artifacts that fail their own handoff test.
- **Resuming a finished seat for a re-verdict.** Observed not to deliver on PR #304, and a seat that judged revision one is not a cold reader of revision two.
- **Pointing at another cell's definition of cold.** `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md` forbids naming a sibling cell, so this file owns its own. **An earlier draft justified that by calling the roster's the *only* operational definition, which is false** — `skills/spikes/SKILL.md` and `skills/experience-session/SKILL.md` each state one, and the charter states the first limb. The conclusion survives the correction and the ground does not, which is what a superlative costs. That there are several is itself the subject of a filing this review sent to the board: `cell-structure.md` names the charter as the one lawful home for a standard two cells need, and this change neither considered it nor may reach it.
- **Widening to [#274](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/274)**, the same unbounded-cycle defect on the fix-and-look cycle. A third instrument; the affirmed brief excludes it.
- **Pinning any wording about what an attached recommendation implies**, which would pre-empt [#288](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/288).

**No always-on surface is edited, so no outflow is owed.** The cell's name and description are untouched, and its body is not always-on — the always-on rows are the doctrine files, the charter body, and each cell's name and description. The new material lands one directory down, which is the depth-shedding `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md` prescribes rather than a dodge of a budget: `engagement` carries no entry in `CELL_BODY_BUDGET_CHARS` and its body is unbudgeted.

## What the artifact cost, and the claim this session got wrong

Two cold seats, two rounds, both run under the contract this change proposes — which is the closest thing to a use-check available before the file existed. Round one returned **WOULD NOT** on three points; round two **WOULD**.

**Round one's sharpest point was a false claim about the term the artifact is held to.** The draft quoted *"gains at most a pointer"* inside quotation marks as a clause of the affirmed brief. The brief carries no such clause: the phrase was in this session's supporting material around the brief, not in the blockquote the owner affirmed. The seat's argument is recorded here because it is the more useful half — under `skills/engagement/SKILL.md`, what the affirmed brief put out is the owner's and what only the artifact excluded is the session's, so presenting a self-imposed edge as an owner-imposed one **puts a question in front of the owner at release that they never set**, which the same cell names as a defect rather than politeness. It was also unjudgeable by the instrument it was flagged for: the seat was asked to test the reading against the brief's words, and the words were not there.

On the brief's actual words — *"a file loaded only when the step runs rather than in the rules every session carries"* — there was no boundary question at all. The cell body is not always-on; only its name and description are, and those are byte-identical to `fbb6cc4` in git's bytes. **The edge was kept because this session had undertaken it to the owner, and it is recorded as the session's rather than as a term of the brief.**

One of the three round-one points was a criteria defect the artifact's own standards should have caught: acceptance criterion 3's falsifier was a presence test on the diff that **the wrong implementation the artifact itself names would also have passed**, which is the bar *a criterion a plausible wrong implementation would also pass measures nothing* exists to enforce. The third point was the design gap named above, and it is not a criteria defect — an earlier draft of this entry counted two, and the review caught the count.

## What the review cost, and what it changed

A five-seat panel — the artifact is both shapes the roster names, so `claims-vs-evidence` and `revision-diff` both sat — then a defense, then a judge. **44 raw findings merged to 27**, plus four the defense originated and two from an external pass. Terminal ruling: fit once the named fixes land, cycle budget two.

**The review's most useful half was not the design.** The spine survived prosecution: the third verdict, the no-count bound, the closed provenance list and the siting were sustained. What did not survive was this entry and one section of the file. Five claims here were false and are corrected above, four of them inherited or carried rather than derived — the failing this entry's own last section confesses, committed twice in one document.

**Two of the four inventions the purpose statement names were still being invented.** The file said what the dispatch carries and never named a **carrier** for the artifact itself, which is committed nowhere and posted nowhere at the moment it is dispatched; two of two observed runs invented one, and the two inventions differed. And §2's enumeration omitted the verdict vocabulary, so one of two observed dispatch-writers asked a three-valued question in a two-valued voice. Both are now in §2, with sha256-over-the-inlined-bytes as the identifier, because a digest whose algorithm and unit are left to the session is one the later reader cannot recompute — which an external pass convicted independently, naming this tree's line-ending normalisation as the concrete case.

**The fix batch is net-shedding on the new file.** Three clauses left: a near-copy of a sentence `skills/spikes/SKILL.md` owns; an uncited claim about improvised seat counts whose figures did not support what they were cited for; and a second, uncited statement of the repeated-point rule under the unit [D-304] explicitly rejected, replaced by a pointer to the cell body's cited one.

**What the panel got wrong, ruled by the judge against the majority:** two seats claimed this change promoted the open recorded finding on the verdict carrier's unit. It does not — a finding *about* the axis is not a sighting *on* it, and the entry stays open.
