# D-320: The roster's exclusion clauses are measured, nearly all of them are live, and the deletion rule keeps on a single citation

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-02 (PR #320)

**Evidence.** The run's record and its addendum, carrying the per-clause table, every control row, the docket and the elicitation verbatim: [run record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5503370780), [addendum](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5504131649). Every figure below is measured on `skills/` at `fbb6cc4`, the tip of `main` this branch was cut from and the base the run was dispatched against — `origin/main` has since moved to `0b81d27`, which is this change's merge base and where the version guard reads it, except where a head figure is named beside it.

## The condition

Each of the nine shipped cells' `description` ends with one or more clauses saying what the cell is **not** for — twenty-one of them, 1,176 characters of an adopter's 11,079. Two begin a sentence and a case-sensitive search misses them, which is how the count read nineteen for as long as anyone had counted.

**The enumeration, complete, because the figures depend on every part of it:** `roster.cell_sources` filtered to `source == 'skills'`, each cell's `description` from `lint._frontmatter_fields`, matched on `(?i)\b(?:not|never) for\b`, **each clause running from its match to the next match or to the end of the description**. Without the filter the recipe returns 27 across all twelve cells; without the span rule neither 1,176 nor the per-clause figures reproduce. The always-on totals are `python tools/figures.py`; `python tools/lint.py` prints the same rows at the mandated checkpoint.

**Nothing had ever established that any of them works.** A fence is a *negative* trigger, and the only measurement touching them — [#217](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/217)'s routing harness, 6 of 6 on near-misses drawn from these very clauses — was an outcome measure in an intact arm only, so it could not say whether the fence caused the correct route or the description's opening summary did. [#272](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/272) is the demonstration that those two come apart: 6 of 6 identical across arms, including the arm whose trigger was deleted, while the *stated warrant* separated three-for-three against three-for-three with no overlap.

So the surface could only grow. Adding cost one anecdote; removing required proving a negative, and no instrument answered *does this clause fire?* for a clause that had already landed.

## The decision

**The measure is the warrant, not the outcome.** What is recorded is whether a seat's stated reason for *declining* the excluded cell is the exclusion — the clause named, or its substance given. A decline for another reason counts as not cited, even where the route was right. An instrument built on reach would have reported every clause dead whether or not it worked, which is the reading #272 exists to forbid.

**Applying that needed one distinction the instrument did not state, and it decided the only deletion: a decline reasoned from the absence of a positive trigger is not a citation, even where its content coincides with the exclusion's.** Against that, a seat that attributes an exclusion *to the entry* — *"its entry explicitly excludes push-rejection recovery"* — is citing, in whichever arm it appears. The scorer was the dispatching session, not blind to arm, with no adjudicator; a reader applying "or its substance given" as generously as the keeps received it would score the deletion as a keep. That is stated rather than defended.

**Deletion requires a per-clause run in which no seat, in either arm, cited the fence clause or its substance. Anything else keeps** — one citation by one seat keeps it, recorded as tested-and-kept. The rule's subject is a **fence clause**; it says nothing about a positive trigger, where it would contradict #272's own recommendation on `substrate`'s language trigger. **That asymmetry is this change's central judgement call.** Three seats per arm can show that a clause nobody reaches for is not carrying its situation; they cannot show that a clause one seat leaned on is safe to remove. The cost of keeping an inert clause is characters. The cost of deleting a live one is a session routed into the cell the clause existed to keep it out of — the failure the surface exists to prevent, which no guard here catches and which surfaces only as somebody's bad change months later.

**Every licensing run carries a planted fence, built to the same near-miss shape as the items it certifies, and never landed.** A control calibrated easier than the measurements passes while they stay blind — excluding a cell from a situation it *plainly* fits forces the rejection to be narrated, where every real item is a near-miss that does not. **Where it rides differs by run shape, and the difference is not cosmetic.** In the **screen**, whose ablated arm strips every fence at once, the plant goes with them, so it is present in the intact arm only. In each **confirm**, only the clause under test is removed and the plant rides **unchanged in both arms** — which is what makes the two arms differ in exactly that clause, as `skills/spikes/SKILL.md` requires of an A/B. The intact-arm-only form would make a confirm's arms differ in two lines and break that rule. In the control table, the `—` on the two confirm rows means the ablated column carried no contrast — the plant rode identically in both arms — not that nothing was read there: the control went to all six seats of each confirm, and the three ablated-arm seats returned 3/3 citing the plant on D2's and 2/3 on H1's, the third opening the cell while naming it. Those readings are reported from the dispatching session's transcript rather than from a retained seat output, and are appended to the [addendum](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5504485191) so a later reader can consult them; they are what shows the ablated arm could still read a fence of that shape, which is what a both-arms null rests on.

**If no seat cites the planted control in a run's intact arm, that run is void** and licenses nothing. **The control certifies the run, not the item**: a run on an item no seat treated as a live candidate is *uninformative* rather than null, and the item is rebuilt. `spikes` already carries that rule's substance on its docket-door bullet [D-173].

**The elicitation forces a verdict on every cell, so a silence is evidence.** A seat asked only what it would do and why never mentions a cell it did not reach for, and on a near-miss a correct route needs no mention of the excluded one.

**Isolation is no repository.** The always-on surface is reconstructed as prompt text, as #272 built it, because a subagent dispatched inside this repository loads the real fenced roster and both arms become the same arm. Bodies stay closed — with the one exception the ceiling section below names.

## What the run returned, and why it matters more than the deletion

Per-clause results and the six deciding seat reasons are in the [run record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5503370780); the docket and elicitation are in its [addendum](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5504131649).

**Twenty-one tested, twenty kept, one deleted.** `engagement`'s *"not for how a review is run."* — 28 characters as the unit above cuts it, terminal stop included; 33 off each runtime's surface once its connector goes. On its own confirm run all six seats declined `engagement`, none on the exclusion; every one gave a **cell-specific reason naming `engagement`'s own positive trigger** — *"when delivering a review's outcome to them"*, **four clauses above** the deleted one — and resolved the cell on *timing* rather than on topic, identically in both arms. That, and not the seats' compliance with a mandated per-cell walk, is what shows the cell was a live candidate: a walk that requires a reasoned verdict on every cell cannot return the negative, so compliance certifies nothing.

**The deletion has a second, independent warrant the routing result does not supply.** At base the description disclaimed review procedure while the cell's body specified what a review's final report carries (`:25`), the input to its fix bar (`:20`), and what every stage of a review inherits (`:58`). That tension is not a flat contradiction — a routing exclusion and a body that says what a review consumes can coexist — but it is a reason to doubt the fence that stands whatever the seats did.

**The exclusion list went from two clauses to one**, so the surviving fence is now the whole of what the cell disclaims. Only one of the review-procedure situations the deleted clause could bear on was ever put to a seat — staffing and staging. *Deciding review depth* and *dispositioning what an automated reviewer posted* were not.

**The ground, under `skills/authoring/references/routing.md`'s fourth answer:** evidence that the rule does not bind. Measured non-citation across two arms is not among the three grounds that paragraph names, and whether its list is closed or illustrative is ambiguous on the text; recorded against that file rather than settled here.

**Five clauses changed conduct, not merely warrant** — seats declined the cell with the fence present and *opened* it without it. One is confirmed at clause resolution: `spikes` #0, on its own rebuilt confirm, whose arms differ in that clause alone. The other four — `authoring` #1, `filing` #0, `substrate` #0 and #1 — are screen results, and the screen's ablated arm carries no fence at all, so each is attributable to its cell's fence set rather than to the single clause. What argues against reading them as generalised opening under total ablation is the screen's own rate: the same fence-free arm was put to twenty informative near-misses and the excluded cell opened in four. That is a stronger result than the instrument was built to detect, and only one of the five is at the instrument's own per-clause resolution.

**So the stage's premise is falsified.** [#260](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260) picked this up expecting the fencing to be shed weight; it is load-bearing, and the roster half of the adopter surface is not where the slack is. The charter body — 5,851 characters, **52.81%** of the adopter total at this change's base and 52.97% at its head, larger than all nine names and descriptions combined, never ablated — is, and that measurement went to [#303](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/303) rather than being acted on here, the owner having set that edge.

**And what it does to #272**, which is cited above four times: that issue named *"a paired arm run outside this repository"* among the instruments whose buildability was open. This change built it, and it separated in five runs out of five. #272's second objection is not answered — see the ceiling below — so the issue stays open on that half.

## The ceilings on this instrument, which a later session will hit

**Seats reconstruct clauses that are not in front of them.** In five places (`authoring` #0, `charter` #0, `experience-session` #2, `persist-changes` #0, `substrate` #2) an *ablated* seat gave a removed clause's substance as its reason. The rule counts a citation in either arm, so those keep — and the consequence is general: **a clause whose substance a seat can reconstruct from the cell name alone can never clear this instrument.** Part of why the answer is 1 of 21 is that the instrument cannot see such a clause as dead, not that the clause is doing work.

**The charter body was carried unablated in every arm, and it names four of the nine cells.** For the deleted clause's situation this matters directly: `skills/charter/SKILL.md` reads *"its own charter, roster, evidence standards and dispositions travel with it, in the `adversarial-review` cell"* — a second, always-on, non-description route to the correct cell, present in both arms. **#272 raised exactly this** — *"any description whose subject the charter names is in the same position"* — and it was not carried into the instrument's design. Here it is a residual rather than a defeater: inside the same arms the charter names `engagement` three times to `adversarial-review`'s once, so on its own logic it pushed toward the cell the seats declined, and no seat or consumer cited it. **For [#303](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/303), where the ablation target is the charter body itself, it is structural rather than residual**, and the nine descriptions become the intact second route. Settling it needs a third arm with that sentence ablated; none was run.

**And the docket is where this fails silently.** `spikes`'s screen item echoed the substance of the cell's own positive trigger — *"a review thesis still disputed after a round"* against an item saying *"two mechanism designs still disputed after a round of argument"* — so it was the cell's door rather than a situation it excludes, and every seat in both arms opened the cell. Rebuilt as a first-round disagreement, the same clause came back 3/3 cited and 0/3 ablated. **A malformed item reads exactly like a dead clause**, and the near-miss rule plus the uninformative-run rule are the only things standing between the two. The contradiction that item exposed is [#321](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/321). The deleted clause's own item has the mirror of that defect — it opens with `adversarial-review`'s first positive trigger, that of the *correct destination* rather than the tested cell — which four review seats raised and which is why the warrant above rests on the six cell-specific declines rather than on the routing outcome.

## What the record carries, and what it does not

The docket's twenty-one situations and the elicitation are in the addendum; nineteen of the twenty-one appear nowhere else. **The thirty seat outputs were not retained** — only the rendered prompts. So the per-arm counts rest on this session's scoring; four of the five reconstruction instances above are in screen transcripts nobody can now consult; and the screen licenses no deletion, so nothing that landed rests on the unreproduced half. What a reader loses is the ability to re-score the twenty keeps independently.

**One run of the changed surface exists outside prompt text.** An experience session put a cold consumer, in a real runtime with the roster loading normally, in the deleted clause's own situation: it considered `engagement`, declined it on the same positive trigger the six seats gave, and separately cited kept clause `persist-changes` #0 verbatim as routing it away from a cell that would have misled it. That is the change's only evidence from a runtime's own skill loading, and its only partial answer to the prompt-text isolation at *Isolation is no repository* above, which the run record carries among the run's stated limits. Its isolation leaked — the consumer's skill invocation resolved to the dispatcher's tree, which carried this change — and that is recorded in the note rather than smoothed.

## What was rejected

**Re-partitioning the roster.** Dropped on measurement rather than deferred — all nine triggers are moment-shaped already — seven open on the phrase `Use when`, `charter` on `Use this before`, and `persist-changes` on `Use whenever`, so the entries fire on moments; the merges that would remove most of the fence text pair a shipped cell with a repo-only one, which stage 1's zone wall forbids, and the one legal large merge — `adversarial-review` + `experience-session` + `spikes`, summed from `python tools/lint.py`'s body rows — would load 28,785 characters of body at that base, 29,956 at this change's head, where a session loads roughly ten thousand. The owner set this edge in the affirmed brief.

**Deciding what the charter carries**, and **inventing any size limit.** Both the owner's edges. No charter text and no budget constant moved.

**Editing the summary half of any description.** A description reading badly once its fence went would have been a review finding, not a licence to rewrite the sentence above. None did.

**Admitting the instrument's four rules to `skills/spikes/SKILL.md`** — the planted control, the void rule, keep-on-any-citation, and the forced per-cell walk; the fifth, the uninformative rule, is already there in substance. Which of them bind universally, which are conditions on a deletion-licensing run, and whether they land as body prose or behind a pointer are design calls this change did not undertake, and they put standing obligations on every later cold-seat A/B. [#325](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/325) carries it, and it matters most for #303.

**Treating [#217](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/217)'s price as binding.** It put deletion-by-null at 200–400 runs per clause and set the guard *"deletion-by-null-result only where coverage exists, or the instrument becomes a machine for deleting the untested."* That price was a behavioural readout with two-thirds of runs scorable; the warrant readout separated cleanly at three seats per arm, five runs out of five. The claim made here is narrow: the warrant readout **is** the coverage #217 required, the planted control is what makes a null on it interpretable, and the keep-on-any-citation asymmetry is what stops it becoming that machine. A later session that thinks n=3 cannot license a deletion at all is disagreeing with this entry, not correcting it.

Three cold-seat verdicts on the artifact, two NO — both landing on the instrument rather than the prose, and both fatal as first written: the measure was reach, which the cited precedent had already proved does not separate; and the positive control was a *trigger* clause sitting before the fence, byte-identical in both arms, which under the void rule would have voided every run. A five-seat review then found the instrument's arm placement written backwards, its near-miss certification stated in a form that cannot fail, and this entry's evidence resting on one off-tree link no guard can see ([#324](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/324)).
