# D-320: The roster's exclusion clauses are measured, nearly all of them are live, and the deletion rule keeps on a single citation

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-02 (PR #320)

## The condition

Each of the nine shipped cells' `description` ends with one or more clauses saying what the cell is **not** for — twenty-one of them, 1,176 characters of an adopter's 11,079, enumerated case-insensitively over `roster.cell_sources` with `lint._frontmatter_fields`. Two begin a sentence and a case-sensitive search misses them, which is how the count read nineteen for as long as anyone had counted.

**Nothing had ever established that any of them works.** A fence is a *negative* trigger, and the only measurement touching them — [#217](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/217)'s routing harness, 6 of 6 on near-misses drawn from these very clauses — was an outcome measure in an intact arm only, so it could not say whether the fence caused the correct route or the description's opening summary did. [#272](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/272) is the demonstration that those two come apart: 6 of 6 identical across arms, including the arm whose trigger was deleted, while the *stated warrant* separated three-for-three against three-for-three with no overlap.

So the surface could only grow. Adding cost one anecdote; removing required proving a negative, and no instrument answered *does this clause fire?* for a clause that had already landed.

## The decision

**The measure is the warrant, not the outcome.** What is recorded is whether a seat's stated reason for *declining* the excluded cell is the exclusion — the clause named, or its substance given. A decline for another reason counts as not cited, even where the route was right. An instrument built on reach would have reported every clause dead whether or not it worked, which is the reading #272 exists to forbid.

**Deletion requires a per-clause run in which no seat, in either arm, cited the clause or its substance. Anything else keeps** — one citation by one seat keeps it, recorded as tested-and-kept. **That asymmetry is this change's central judgement call.** Three seats per arm can show that a clause nobody reaches for is not carrying its situation; they cannot show that a clause one seat leaned on is safe to remove. The cost of keeping an inert clause is characters. The cost of deleting a live one is a session routed into the cell the clause existed to keep it out of — the failure the surface exists to prevent, which no guard here catches and which surfaces only as somebody's bad change months later.

**Every licensing run carries a planted fence, built to the same near-miss shape as the items it certifies, present in the intact arm only and never landed.** A control calibrated easier than the measurements passes while they stay blind — excluding a cell from a situation it *plainly* fits forces the rejection to be narrated, where every real item is a near-miss that does not. If no seat cites the planted fence in a run's intact arm, that run is void and licenses nothing. **It certifies the run, not the item**: a run on an item no seat treated as a live candidate is *uninformative* rather than null, and the item is rebuilt.

**The elicitation forces a verdict on every cell, so a silence is evidence.** A seat asked only what it would do and why never mentions a cell it did not reach for, and on a near-miss a correct route needs no mention of the excluded one. Every real item is exactly that shape, so without the forced walk the instrument measures the prompt.

**Isolation is no repository.** The always-on surface is reconstructed as prompt text, as #272 built it, because a subagent dispatched inside this repository loads the real fenced roster and both arms become the same arm. Bodies stay closed: a body restating its own exclusion lets a seat route correctly for a reason the always-on surface had nothing to do with.

## What the run returned, and why it matters more than the deletion

**Twenty-one tested, twenty kept, one deleted.** `engagement`'s *"not for how a review is run"* — 28 characters of clause, 33 off each runtime's surface with its connector. On its own confirm run all six seats declined `engagement`, none on the exclusion; every one reasoned from the positive trigger list two clauses above it (*"when delivering a review's outcome to them"*), and the two arms were indistinguishable. The work the clause was written to do was already done.

**Five clauses changed conduct, not merely warrant** — `authoring` #1, `filing` #0, `spikes` #0, `substrate` #0 and #1: seats declined the cell with the fence present and *opened* it with the fence gone. That is a stronger result than the instrument was built to detect.

**So the stage's premise is falsified.** [#260](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260) picked this up expecting the fencing to be shed weight; it is load-bearing, and the roster half of the adopter surface is not where the slack is. The charter body — 5,851 characters, **52.8%** of the adopter total, larger than all nine names and descriptions combined, never ablated — is, and that measurement went to [#303](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/303) rather than being acted on here, the owner having set that edge.

## The ceiling on this instrument, which a later session will hit

**Seats reconstruct clauses that are not in front of them.** In five places (`authoring` #0, `charter` #0, `experience-session` #2, `persist-changes` #0, `substrate` #2) an *ablated* seat gave a removed clause's substance as its reason, twice almost verbatim. The rule counts a citation in either arm, so those keep — and the consequence is general: **a clause whose substance a seat can reconstruct from the cell name alone can never clear this instrument.** Part of why the answer is 1 of 21 rather than more is that the instrument cannot see such a clause as dead, not that the clause is doing work.

**And the docket is where this fails silently.** `spikes`'s screen item quoted the cell's own positive trigger — *"a review thesis still disputed after a round"* — so it was the cell's door rather than a situation it excludes, and every seat in both arms opened the cell. Rebuilt as a first-round disagreement, the same clause came back 3/3 cited and 0/3 ablated. **A malformed item reads exactly like a dead clause**, and the near-miss rule plus the uninformative-run rule are the only things standing between the two. The contradiction that item exposed is [#321](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/321).

## What was rejected

**Re-partitioning the roster.** Dropped on measurement rather than deferred — all nine triggers already read `Use when `, so the entries fire on moments already; the merges that would remove most of the fence text pair a shipped cell with a repo-only one, which stage 1's zone wall forbids, and the one legal large merge would load 28,785 characters of body where a session loads about ten thousand. The owner set this edge in the affirmed brief.

**Deciding what the charter carries**, and **inventing any size limit.** Both the owner's edges. No charter text and no budget constant moved.

**Editing the summary half of any description.** A description reading badly once its fence went would have been a review finding, not a licence to rewrite the sentence above. None did.

**Recording the eighteen screen seats' full nine-cell walks** — roughly 120 KB. The screen licenses no deletion, so nothing that landed rests on them; what a reader loses is independent re-scoring of the twenty keeps, and the [run record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/260#issuecomment-5503370780) says so rather than leaving the gap to be discovered.

**Treating [#217](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/217)'s price as binding.** It put deletion-by-null at 200–400 runs per clause and set the guard *"deletion-by-null-result only where coverage exists, or the instrument becomes a machine for deleting the untested."* That price was a behavioural readout with two-thirds of runs scorable; the warrant readout separated cleanly at three seats per arm, five runs out of five. The claim made here is narrow: the warrant readout **is** the coverage #217 required, the planted control is what makes a null on it interpretable, and the keep-on-any-citation asymmetry is what stops it becoming that machine. A later session that thinks n=3 cannot license a deletion at all is disagreeing with this entry, not correcting it.

Three cold-seat verdicts on the artifact, two NO — both landing on the instrument rather than the prose, and both fatal as first written: the measure was reach, which the cited precedent had already proved does not separate; and the positive control was a *trigger* clause sitting before the fence, byte-identical in both arms, which under the void rule would have voided every run.
