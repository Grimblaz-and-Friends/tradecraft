# D-436: A successor's systemic-cause belief stops the work, and the amendment route says only how the disagreement is settled

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-06 (PR #436)

## Context

[#432](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/432), worked alone. The [affirmed brief](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/432#issuecomment-5561408243), its [affirmation record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/432#issuecomment-5561409288) and the [settled artifact](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/432#issuecomment-5561487541) are on that issue.

**The seam this sits on is the owner's split**, affirmed 2026-09-06 as GitHub dates it, which is 2026-09-05 on this log's local basis — the same two bases [D-431] separates in its own Context: the session that settles a brief in conversation and the session that builds from it are different sessions, and [D-431] wrote down what has to survive the crossing. It is a defect on the arriving side of that seam, found by using it. **It is not the first**: an arriving-side entry recorded by an experience session on the same pull request already sits in `docs/recorded-findings.jsonl`, and that log carries no dates, so the order of the two is not determinable from the tree.

**The incident.** A cold consumer, given an issue whose brief another session had affirmed and told nothing was under test, reached an unresolved question about whether the brief was aimed at the right cause, found two rules reaching it, and surfaced the fork in its run report. **That report quotes the consumer twice and narrates the rest in the reporting session's own voice; both are given here as they stand at [the experience session on PR #431](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/431#issuecomment-5556649099), because an earlier revision of this entry presented the narration as the consumer's speech and appended a sentence the source does not contain.**

The consumer's own words:

> that obligation falls on the session that drafted the brief, before it locked, and this brief is already affirmed.

and:

> a successor amends rather than replaces, and amending a brief means re-agreeing it with the owner, who is not reachable

The reporting session's narration of what the consumer then did:

> It reasoned from the new arriving rule — [the second fragment above] — recorded the question in its artifact's boundary statement, and proceeded. It flagged that another session might have read this as the map's test 1 and stopped the work entirely.

**The word *belief*, which this change's rule turns on, is PR #431's terminal ruling's rather than the consumer's** — *"a successor holding a live systemic-cause belief against an affirmed brief"*. What the consumer records holding is a question it recorded and proceeded past. That is a weaker warrant than a formed belief, and it is stated so rather than dressed up. PR #431's [terminal review stage](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/431#issuecomment-5556974034) sustained it and dispositioned it a decision rather than a fix, which is what sent it to the board.

**What the tree held at the base.** At `07511c362979649c7711dd9a54d5de7c078d9fe6`, `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` carried both of these and nothing ordering them:

- `:119`, the first of the three tests a question inside the unattended stretch takes, first match governing — *"Does answering it change what the change is for?"* → *"The work **stops** — the one thing that stops it."*
- `:95`, in the handoff at the affirmation — *"A successor that believes the affirmed brief is wrong amends it rather than replacing it … That is what the amendment route above is for"*, pointing at `:66`, which re-agrees an amended brief with the owner and is silent on the work's state meanwhile.

`:95` is the passage a successor reaches, because the handoff section is what it is told to read on arrival.

## Decision

**1. The ordering is the owner's, ruled from three argued options.** The stop governs; the route that would have carried on comes second. Rejected: the amendment route governing, with the work proceeding and the disagreement carried to release; and the successor's case being neither of those and taking its own sentence — proceeding on the affirmed term, recording the belief as a disputed parentage, and stopping only where the affirmed term could not be built at all. **The session recommended the third and carried the strongest case against it**; he ruled the first, in one word — *"A"* — and affirmed the re-put brief carrying it. The case against the recommendation was that it adds a fourth rule to a stretch already carrying three tests plus an amendment route, and that the line it turns on is itself a judgment, so it may relocate the ambiguity rather than remove it. The ruling avoids that cost entirely.

**2. The ordering is stated at one site, `:95`, and test 1 is untouched.** `:95` is where a successor is told to read on arrival, and it is the site the incident's consumer was reasoning from. **It is not the only site the fork is reachable from** — an earlier revision of this entry said it was, and the consumer's own run report falsifies that: it had test 1 available, named it as the other reading, and took the amendment route anyway. What decides the placement is the next sentence rather than reachability. Giving test 1 a successor clause would put a second statement of the ordering in the file — **which is the shape that produced this defect**, two sites stating a successor's obligation differently. A fix that reproduces its own cause is not a fix. This was written as an acceptance criterion and checked on the diff, not left to care.

**3. The two passages are not two answers to one question, and the wording says so.** The amendment route answers *how* a disagreement about an affirmed brief is settled — by re-agreeing it with the owner, never by the successor settling it alone. Test 1 answers *what the work does* while that is unsettled. Read that way the ruling is not one rule overriding another but a statement of what each was already about, which is why the change is an ordering and not a new rule.

**4. The stop terminates in machinery the cell already carries, and no route was added.** The standing objection to this option, raised while it was argued, is that a stop has nowhere to end when the owner is unreachable — which is also the deferred question #432's filing called *"the one no route currently terminates"*. The file already ends it: the rule that a session which cannot reach the owner for an affirmation its work owes waits, the *waiting is not idling* clause with its closed list, and the hand-back form. The new text points at those rather than restating them. **The closed list is worded throughout for a session with no affirmed brief yet, while a successor here has one, and that mismatch is recorded rather than repaired.** An earlier revision of this entry attributed the observation to the second cold seat; the settled artifact's list of that seat's observations does not carry it, so the attribution is withdrawn and the observation kept on its own merits. **Whether the pointer nonetheless yields a coherent instruction is not established** — see 7a below.

**5. No site was added for an arriving session to run the why-chain, and that is the owner's own exclusion**, carried in the affirmed brief: *"This change is not adding a way for an arriving session to go looking for a bigger cause — only what it does with the belief once it has one."* Both existing why-chain sites remain scoped away from a successor — `grep -rn "systemic" skills/` returns two, one behind *"Before drafting"* and one behind the gate that no sitting opens where an affirmed brief is recorded. Whether an arriving session needs such a site at all is [#427](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/427)'s and stays open. `skills/engagement/references/design-sitting.md` is untouched.

**6. The why-chain ran and reached no systemic cause, which is recorded rather than assumed.** #432 is a problem, so the chain ran before drafting. It found one open issue of the same shape — [#170](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/170), a rule meeting a situation it does not route, so each session improvises and the same facts produce different outcomes — and **no cause whose single fix covers both**: #170's fix is a lawful partial discharge for the defence's re-run rule, and this one's is an ordering sentence. Failing the test that a cause's fix would have prevented the instance and its siblings, the chain stopped at this issue's own level. **The resemblance was recorded on the issue and deliberately not written as a tie**, a tie being what tells the next ranking session to plan one pull request for two unrelated mechanisms.

**7. One question is left open, and the reading is built so it does not load-bear.** Whether a systemic-cause belief a successor forms is the same object a drafting session's why-chain produces — #432's second deferred item — is unanswered. The ordering keys on the belief's **effect**, *an answer that changes what the change is for*, and not on its provenance, so it holds either way. That is the session's call and not the owner's, because nothing about the answer could have made the brief wrong.

**7a. Acceptance criterion 2 is unexercised, and criterion 1 passed on a run whose antecedent never obtained.** Criterion 2 — that a successor whose owner is unreachable terminates the stop in the waiting rule — is the only check on decision 4's terminus claim, which is itself the answer to the standing objection this option was argued against. **Two runs were made and neither reached the antecedent**: one consumer ran the brief-is-wrong question explicitly and answered no, the other never raised it, and both were halted early by the same no-dispatch bound the dispatching session set in each dispatch. So the terminus claim rests on argument, and that is recorded here rather than left to be discovered. What the two runs do establish, on independent consumers, is that a competent successor handed material undercutting the affirmed brief's remedy **does not readily form the belief the stop fires on** — both absorbed the tension as design inside the affirmed term, which the cell assigns to the session. Criterion 1 is reported passing on the first of those runs; its antecedent did not obtain there either, so what that run establishes is reach rather than what a session holding the belief does. A third run would need the consumer free to dispatch its own cold seat, or the belief given rather than formed.

**8. No mechanism, guard or script.** The obligation is discharged by a session's behaviour on arriving at an issue, and no guard in this tree reads that. The same weakness [D-431] recorded applies here and is not newly answered.
