# D-431: The affirmation becomes a handoff between sessions, and both sides of it are written down

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-05 (PR #431)

## Context

[#422](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422) and [#408](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/408), worked as one change under the owner's ruling of 2026-09-06 — *"no on both, keep it one issue"* — recorded on the [affirmation record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422#issuecomment-5556450249) beside the [affirmed brief](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422#issuecomment-5556447889). Dates on GitHub are UTC and dates in this log are local; the affirmation is 2026-09-06 there and this entry lands on the evening of 2026-09-05 here.

**What the split is, and why the seam exists at all.** On 2026-09-05 the owner proposed a division of labour in his own words — *"what if I have you do the interactive pieces and new sessions just pick up once you've published the artifacts?"*, revised the same day to switch at the brief — and re-affirmed it at this sitting: *"I do want to move forward with the split, so this is about making it work."* Under it, the session that settles a brief in conversation and the session that builds from it are different sessions, and the issue is the only thing that crosses between them. `skills/engagement/SKILL.md` already called the affirmation *"the boundary"*, meaning a boundary of authority. Nothing said it was now also a boundary of sessions, or what has to survive the crossing.

**Four incidents, all at that one seam, three of them recorded by the sessions that made them.**

- [#402's filing carried three sufficient tells for opening a sitting where the brief the owner then affirmed says two](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422). The filer and the sitting's session were the same session; the implementer read both and built from the issue's three. One cold-seat round was spent on the contradiction.
- [An ordering the owner ruled at a sitting was recorded as prose in the brief and the affirmation record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422#issuecomment-5556031322). The refresh that night reads ties, not comments, and placed the pair the other way round.
- [The successor on #405 fetched the issue with a `--jq` filter that selected `{number,title,state,labels,body}`, discarding the comments before reading them](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/422#issuecomment-5556367974). The departing session had left all three comments correctly — brief, affirmation record, placement reading. The successor re-ran convergence, produced a second brief, and built from it; the owner adjudicated two competing terms and issued an amendment. That comment records nine dispatches spent against the superseded brief, the eight of them that completed returning 943,473 subagent tokens, and names why nothing downstream caught it: the cold seats were dispatched with the wrong brief, and the panel judged against criteria derived from it.
- [PR #393's second experience session](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/393#issuecomment-5551051054), a cold consumer resuming a convergence past an affirmation: *"the text that binds does not exist in any form I can quote, and the comment that is supposed to carry it had to be written by me, after the fact, and then quoted by me as if it were the record."* It named the alternative reading — hand back rather than write it — and called it the thing it was least sure it had right.

**What the tree held at the base.** At `bab184c`: `skills/engagement/SKILL.md`'s *"What is posted on the work's issue is the affirmed brief, as the record of what was agreed"*, with nothing about where on the issue; *"It opens with the affirmed brief, quoted verbatim from the comment that carries it"*, which presupposes the comment exists; the amendment rule, keyed to a session amending its own brief rather than to a successor arriving at one; the waiting-session rule's *"labelled a draft, marking which sentences are the session's restatement rather than the owner's own words"*, keyed to a brief the owner has **not** affirmed; and the systemic-cause paragraph's *"The siblings the test found are tied to the cause before the brief locks"*. `skills/engagement/references/design-sitting.md`'s pickup order opened *"Read the issue or the bundle"*. `skills/filing/SKILL.md` pinned the tie block as *"the first element of the issue body, before any heading or prose"* so *"a session ranking the board [can] find ties without reading every body"* — the same argument this change needed, one class of reader over.

## Decision

**1. All six obligations land in the `engagement` cell, in one new section, and none of them lands in `filing`.** The seam is the affirmation; the cell owns the affirmation; and the audience is that cell's — a session settling a brief, or one picking work up to build from one. The filing cell's stated audience is *"any session about to create an issue"*, which is not a moment any of these obligations fires in. **The pointer rule was the close call**, because the filing cell owns the issue body's opening block and states, for a different reader, exactly the argument the pointer rests on. It was decided against splitting: `skills/authoring/references/cell-structure.md` gives a shared standard exactly one owner, and *"two half-owners of one sentence kept in agreement by hand"* is named there as the mechanism by which copies drift. What the filing cell already holds is untouched and is not wrong — its *"Design … is written in today's vocabulary and decays with it"* is the warning the supersession obligation enforces.

**2. The pointer sits alongside the tie block, not above it, and the relative order is deliberately left unstated.** The new rule says *"the issue body's opening block, alongside the ties already there"*. Saying *first element* would have contradicted the filing cell's pin; saying *second* would have been a rule about layout with no consequence anyone can name. The cold seat that settled the artifact observed the gap and did not fail it: either order builds and either order works, because what makes the pointer work is being in the body at all — the ordinary read (`gh issue view N`) returns the body and no comments.

**3. The wording avoids naming any sibling cell, and this shaped three sentences.** A shipped cell may name no sibling, and may name nothing repo-only; the board's refresh lives in a repo-only cell. So the tie obligation is written as *"a ranking reads ties and not prose"* rather than as a pointer at the mechanism that does the reading, and the pointer's placement is described in the engagement cell's own words rather than by reference to the tie block's owner. The precedent for using the tie vocabulary without naming its owner was already in the cell: *"The siblings the test found are tied to the cause."*

**4. The tie obligation is the sibling-tying obligation over a second class of ruling, at the same moment.** The cell already made the sitting's session tie siblings before the brief locks. The second incident is that same session tying siblings and not the sequence it had just ruled. Nothing new was needed in the tie vocabulary — `sequenced-after` / `blocks` already carry an ordering — so what was missing was only the obligation to write the ruling as one, which is now stated where the sibling obligation already falls.

**5. `skills/engagement/references/design-sitting.md` gains the matching negative, and it yields to the owner.** No sitting opens on work whose brief is already affirmed. That file owns the conditions under which a sitting opens, and its *"Read the issue or the bundle"* is the exact sentence the third incident's successor was acting on. It is not a second copy of the arriving-side rule: the cell body says what an arriving session reads and that it may not replace the term; this answers the sitting file's own question. **As first drafted the clause sat ahead of the existing *"where the owner … has asked for a sitting, open one and stop here"* branch and would have overridden it**, which is a rule of this practice blocking a decision of the owner's — the thing the charter's Authority section forbids. It now ends *"only the owner asking for one reopens it."*

**6. A successor may not author the record of an affirmation it was not present for; it reconstructs, marks, and gets one line.** The owner ruled this at the sitting. #408 deferred the question in both directions — write it and mark the seam, or hand back for want of a term — and the second reading is what the ruling rejects, because refusing to record an affirmation that genuinely happened leaves the work with no term at all. **The marking reuses the waiting-session clause rather than adding a second one**, per #408's own deferred question about whether it could be reused: the machinery needed is *marking which sentences are the session's restatement rather than the owner's own words*, and that is exactly what the existing clause supplies. What does **not** carry over is the surrounding sentence — *"neither the post nor the owner's reading it there supplies the affirmation"* — which is true of a brief never affirmed and false here, where the affirmation happened and only its record is missing. **The artifact's *"quoted verbatim from the comment that carries it"* needed no new clause**, which was #408's fourth deferred question: once the owner confirms the reconstruction, that comment *is* the comment that carries the brief, and the existing sentence reads correctly over it.

**7. No always-on surface was edited, and the outflow it would have owed was therefore not triggered — but the description is part of that surface and the look was run anyway.** The requirement climbs from the cheapest material that can hold it, and the cell is the material: the session that produced the third and most expensive incident **had** loaded the `engagement` cell, so a rule placed in it reaches the reader who needs it. `AGENTS.md` and the charter are untouched. **The outflow look over the whole always-on surface is a refusal, recorded here because a refusal leaves no other trace.** Nothing on it has a cheaper home that its own stated reason does not already defeat: the CRLF paragraph and the wall paragraph in `AGENTS.md` each state, in the file, that they are there because a session can meet them before any cell fires; the *Worth doing* pointer states that a session deciding whether work is worth doing has at that moment loaded nothing; and the charter already carries *"Nothing reaches this charter that a cell can hold"* as its own standing test. No rule moved out, none was deleted, and none compressed to a citation.

**8. No mechanism, guard or script.** Every obligation here is discharged on a GitHub issue, and no guard in this tree reads issue bodies. That is a real weakness — the writing standard prefers a rule whose compliance is visible on the artifact its reader is producing, and the artifact here is a GitHub comment rather than a file in the diff. The carrier that partly answers it is the issue body itself: the departing obligations are visible on the thing the departing session is already editing. A guard over issue bodies is a different change and was not proposed.
