# D-97: The dispatch contract, restated

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-21 (PR #97)

## Context

[D-90](D-90-2026-08-20-dispatch-contract.md) landed the dispatch contract and then records, in its own Decision section, that the sentence carrying it had **five versions in one day**. It names one word as load-bearing in terms: *"Under `each`, the fidelity clause reached only the predecessor outputs by nearest antecedent, so `each` had to become **`both`**, which cannot distribute over a non-pair; tidying it back reopens a high."*

The second word is [#95](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/95)'s finding, not D-90's, and this entry originally mis-sourced it. #95's amendment table records it: *"cycle 2 | the identity phrase could bind to 'evidence standards' | `itself`"*. D-90 does re-scope byte-identity in the same cycle, but **for a different defect** — *"made the contract unsatisfiable at any stage holding both a cold recipient and a briefed one … so it was re-scoped onto the assignment alone"* — which is scope-of-application, not anaphora. The distinction matters because a session following the citation to D-90 for the `itself` reasoning will not find it there.

A sentence whose correctness depends on a reader not tidying it is a sentence that will be tidied. `skills/authoring/SKILL.md` names the remedy — *"If a competent reader genuinely could go wrong, restate the sentence more plainly rather than appending a qualifier to it"* — and all five amendments had been qualifiers. #95 asked for the restatement; [#94](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/94) asked whether one clause of the same sentence should be reworded, reasoned, mechanized, or withdrawn. **They are one change**: #94's clause sits inside #95's sentence, and settling it separately would rewrite that sentence under a gate that had affirmed half of it — D-90's own reason for not splitting #82 and #88.

## Decision

**The fidelity clause leads the pair instead of trailing it, and byte-identity stands alone.** `both` and `itself` are **gone rather than replaced**: a clause that precedes both items needs no distributive word, so nearest-antecedent has nothing to strand, and byte-identity as its own sentence with `the assignment` as subject needs no anchoring pronoun. That is the point of a restatement — swapping one qualifier for a better one would have been the sixth amendment, not the remedy.

**The restatement was sound; four gap closures bolted onto the same sentence were not.** The review found the mechanism above intact and every one of the paragraph's seventeen prior obligations preserved, and it found three of the four gap closures had introduced new defects. That is the lesson this entry exists to carry forward: **bundling five decisions into one sentence is what produced D-90's five versions, and doing it again produced four more defects in one pass.** The closures below are the repaired forms.

**Position zero — the absolute, with its one exception named.** `and nothing before it` becomes **`with nothing before it but a byte-identical title line — a preamble puts per-recipient framing ahead of the shared block`**. The first draft narrowed the bar to *per-recipient* content, which left byte-identical aiming prose lawful ahead of the block — the change's own motivating incident, reproducible one line higher. Three seats reached that reading independently. The narrowing also rested on a premise the review could not establish: that the old clause forbade a byte-identical title. **D-90's own text undercuts it** — it names the hazard as a preamble carrying unique bytes and says *"This repo has no such preamble, so for today's structure the two formulations coincide."* Naming the title line explicitly moots the premise, because it stops doing any work. The reason clause states a hazard rather than an affordance, its object is the shared block rather than the assignment (the bar's own subject), and it says `per-recipient framing` rather than an anaphor — all three restoring D-90:21's own wording, which this entry's first draft had degraded on each count.

**Part one's closure is scoped to the hazard, not to arity.** `and nothing else` becomes **`nothing else in it aims attention`**. An absolute closure barred the merged finding list that `:67` makes mandatory input at the terminal stage — the merge is parent-owned, so it is neither the assignment nor a *stage's* output — along with the charter block `:14` requires. That was a live regression on every panel-lane review. The recorded gap was always aiming prose in the byte-identical span, and the closure now says so. `two things` is gone with it: a numeral welded to a list amended five times in one day is one more thing a sixth amendment must remember, and the arithmetic was already false for a cold dispatch, which receives one thing.

**The fronted modifier stops at a sentence boundary.** `in this skill's own words` governs identity and lens brief — the relabelling defeat closes at both nouns — and part three's remaining members move to their own sentence. A fronted adverbial otherwise scopes over its whole clause, reaching `output format`, which D-90 records as sitting per-recipient **because it is stage-varying**: *"seats emit findings, the defense emits verdicts and prices, the terminal stage emits rulings."* Re-welding it by scope is the unsatisfiability D-90 logged. Closing the scope with a distributive word would have reinstated the retired defect at the exact site; a sentence break costs nothing and does not.

**The note bar names what it binds and gives a reason that fits it.** `the seat stage`, which occurred once in the shipped zone and was defined nowhere, becomes **`no seat or post-fix prosecution dispatch`**, and the reason becomes `a cold vantage sits among them`. Three separate defects closed: the first draft deleted the antecedent while keeping — and strengthening — the demonstrative pointing at it, which is the `itself` failure reappearing in the sentence written to retire it; it re-scoped the bar from a stage to a role while leaving a stage-level reason, so the reason told a reader the rule stopped at four of five seats; and it reached no further than the panel, while `:71` calls the post-fix pass a *prosecution look* and never a seat — leaving the one dispatch shape all eight recorded violations came from outside the bar it was written for. `on either lane` is deleted: `lane` carries three senses in this file (`:10` attended/unattended, `:22`–`:24` routine/substantial, `:43` single-session), so it replaced one undefined term with another, and `either` under a universally negative subject could not narrow the bar under any reading.

**#94's clause is reworded and given its reason inline**, and the position-zero absolute above is that reword. The old wording barred the wrong thing: it forbade a byte-identical title, which every compliant dispatch in the corpus uses, and never named per-recipient framing, which is the hazard every time.

**`[D-90] [D-97]` cites both.** The shipped zone used the form nowhere despite `AGENTS.md` sanctioning it and using it twice. `[D-90]` alone routes a future editor to a frozen entry whose warnings concern `both`, `itself` and `each` — three words this change deletes — with nothing pointing forward, since D-90 cannot. Both tokens are lint-safe: `tools/lint.py`'s `ROOTED_ZONE` and `RELATIVE_REF` patterns each require a path separator.

## Rejected

- **A sixth qualifier.** Five amendments had been qualifiers and each exposed the next weld. D-90's own row in the decisions README is an existence proof for *half* the plainer construction — it drops `itself` but retains `both`, still trailing its pair, the exact configuration D-90 records as load-bearing. This entry's first draft called that row unambiguous, which overstates it.
- **Deleting the terminal stage's second docket sentence**, which #95 nominated as a 17-word offset from the **shipped** zone (`skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md:67`; this entry's first draft called it `docs`-side, which is wrong). It splits into a permission — *"arrive as a labeled note on that docket"* — that exists nowhere else at that stage, and an emphatic half that duplicates the sentence before it but is worded to catch #88's exemplar. Deleting the pair costs a compliant dispatcher the findings it noticed; trimming the emphatic half opens a recorded meaning change in a sentence nobody complained about. Owner ruled it untouched.
- **Withdrawing the position-zero clause.** #94 put it up seriously. It falls on the measurement: 6/6 obedience in the next review, and the hazard D-90 admitted it on is precisely what all eight breaks did.
- **Restoring the deleted justification tail to the skill.** The finding that its removal left byte-identity as the paragraph's only reasonless rule is real, and this entry's first draft justified the deletion on a false ground — *"which D-90 carries whole."* D-90 carries the label and none of the words. But **[D-99](D-99-2026-08-21-dispatch-prompt-caching.md) does carry it**, in its own Rejected section and in both halves: it quotes *"one literal string cannot drift"* and states the reason as *"a dispatcher that retypes the assignment per recipient decides one recipient at a time what gets reviewed."* Restoring it to the shipped paragraph is +5 words of standing prose against the burden on keeping words, for a reason that now has a durable home. Priced out, not dropped.
- **Naming a lens-brief conditional** for the `cold-read` seat, which by `:32` has none. The clause is equally unconditional in the pre-change text; the fix is +4 words and a third conditional in one sentence for a case a dispatcher resolves by reading `:32`. Priced out.
- **Re-scoping byte-identity across post-fix cycles.** The phrase is verbatim from the pre-change text, and any fix re-litigates D-90's diff-base anchor — chosen deliberately over a "review state" it rejected as *"a stage label the dispatcher assigns"* — for a defect no dispatch in a 295-record corpus has hit. Priced out.

## Deferred, with the evidence that would reopen them

- **The post-hoc dispatch guard.** The mechanism premise **held**: a script can read every dispatch's position-zero content and compute shared-prefix identity across a stage, and five independent rebuilds from this entry's prose description alone reproduced every figure above. It is held rather than built because it is post-hoc and advisory — it cannot gate what a dispatcher writes — it reads a harness-private path that is no stable interface, and #94's premise for needing it fell. This is the owner's ruling, not the session's: the doctrine's admission order prefers a mechanism to prose where one is available, and one *is* available here. **Reopen on:** a dispatch breaking any clause of this paragraph. The first draft named only the reworded position-zero clause, which is the clause that **held** on its first application — this review's own five seat prompts share a 606-character byte-identical prefix with nothing per-recipient before it. What broke were two of the clauses this change added: two of those same prompts carry unlabeled aiming prose in the lens-brief slot, not in this skill's words, barred by the fronted modifier and by the note bar alike. A trigger that names the clause that worked cannot fire on the failure the change actually produces.

## A citation this change strands

[D-99](D-99-2026-08-21-dispatch-prompt-caching.md), landed hours before this entry and frozen, quotes `"one literal string cannot drift"` as the skill's live text and states that the byte-identity requirement *"never carried a caching rationale and does not lose one here."* This change deletes that phrase from `skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md`. Two concurrent sessions, one preserving a reason in a frozen entry and one removing it from the shipped text, neither aware of the other. D-99's argument is unaffected — its point is that no caching rationale was ever there — and the reason it preserves is exactly what makes the deletion affordable above. Recorded because a reader arriving at D-99's quotation will not find it in the skill, and because it is a worked example of what the records-are-exhaust rule costs when two changes land the same day.
