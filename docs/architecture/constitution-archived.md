# The constitution

## 1. Identity and scope

- **Tradecraft's identity is the practice, not the mechanism.** What the repo is made of is the standards, memory, and judgment structure that turn frontier-model capability into trustworthy engineering; the mechanisms are only how that structure is carried. [ADR-001:15]
- **The belonging test asks what an artifact encodes, not what it does.** Name the standard, judgment rule, or recorded lesson this artifact enforces. If it can be named, the artifact belongs — even where a vendor ships the same underlying action, because a commit skill belongs only because and only insofar as it enforces our staging and verification standards. If no standard can be named, it is a capability wrapper and does not belong, however useful. Disputes are settled by that naming exercise, not by predictions about vendor roadmaps. [ADR-001:15]
- **The repo's durable asset is its accumulated discipline, not its wrappers.** Vendor feature releases are absorbed, not competed with. [ADR-001:29]
- **Anything that starts to look like an orchestration framework is presumptively out of scope and needs an argued exception.** [ADR-001:31]
- **Agents are retained only as a mechanism, used when structurally required for one of exactly three things:** *context isolation*, a subtask whose working material would pollute or overflow the parent; *adversarial independence*, where a reviewer must not share the author's context or it inherits the author's blind spots; and *parallelism*, fan-out over independent work. [ADR-001:17] [ADR-001:19] [ADR-001:20] [ADR-001:21]
- **Thin dispatch shells — two or three, at the composition layer — are the entire agent surface.** [ADR-001:30]
- **Methodology never lives inside a persona, and no workflow may depend on a persona existing.** Skills are methodology's home (§3). Persona agents as structural units cost real maintenance, and their removal lost nothing measured. The ruling reopens by one route: ledger or eval evidence that a persona-shaped structure caught or produced something the flat structure did not. [ADR-001:25]
- **Persona framing is adopted per skill on local evidence, and dropped where it does not help.** Whether casting a dispatch or skill prompt as an expert role improves output quality is open and the research is genuinely mixed, so the question is settled per task by ledger or eval evidence rather than in general. It starts as model judgment like everything else; it just never becomes load-bearing structure. [ADR-001:25]
## 2. Materials and the lifecycle

- **Every rule in this system is expressed in one of three materials:** model judgment (nothing written), prose (methodology the model reads), or code (contracts that execute). [ADR-002:11]
- **Material is assigned on two axes — checkability × durability — and by a lifecycle, never a static assignment.** Workflow is exactly what changes when models improve [ADR-002:14], so a one-axis rule ("if it can be violated silently, make it code") drives unstable rules into code, where they rot expensively. [ADR-002:17]
- **Every rule starts as model judgment. Promotion is earned, never speculative.** [ADR-002:21]
- **Judgment → prose:** when inconsistency across sessions has *actually cost something*, and the rule shapes judgment — what to consider, in what order, to what standard. Litmus: would a strong senior engineer need this instruction, or just the goal and constraints? Prose teaches; it never enforces. [ADR-002:23]
- **Prose → code:** only when the rule has (a) been violated at real cost, (b) survived change long enough to look stable, and (c) has a checkable form. Code is the retirement home for rules that have proven durable — never the first draft of a rule. [ADR-002:24]
- **Rule prose carries three things, and only the third belongs elsewhere.** The **specification** is the rule; numbers inside it — seat counts, pass counts, budgets — are part of the rule and cannot rot. The **reason** is the working principle a reader needs to apply the rule well and to notice when it stops applying; it is short, general, and stays inline, because a model reading mid-task will not follow a link and a rule whose point is invisible gets tidied away by whoever meets it next. The **evidence** is what proves the reason — counts, dates, incidents, prior findings — and goes behind a pointer into the evidence registry at `docs/architecture/evidence.md`: it is long, it drifts, it cannot be checked from the prose anyway, and reciting it spends every session's context serving a reader who is not there. Explain why; do not litigate it. A sweep deciding whether a rule is still load-bearing follows the pointer; a session applying the rule reads the reason and never needs to. [ADR-002:26]
- **Precision follows load, not grammar.** A figure that carries a decision — or that is a rule's only evidence — must be exact and checkable, because someone will act on it or revise a rule by it. A figure conveying a magnitude, so a reader grasps the shape of the thing, is held to *honest*, not *exact*: it must not mislead, and it does not need defending to the decimal. Writing every sentence to the first standard is how prose becomes unreadable and its review becomes an audit of rounding. [ADR-002:28]
- **A count nobody can reproduce is not evidence for anything.** A figure offered as a rule's basis must be reconstructible from the record; when it is not, it is withdrawn rather than incremented, so nothing is left behind to go stale. [ADR-003:33]
- **Evidence moves only if the pointer carries it, and only if the reader can reach it.** Verify the target contains the claim before cutting the recital — a citation that does not carry what it is attached to is worse than the text it replaced, because it converts a self-auditing statement into an unsupported one that *looks* supported, and no reader can tell from the prose. Reachability binds hardest in the shipped zone, where §4 governs the permitted pointer form. When the pointer cannot carry the claim or cannot be reached, the recital is not clutter — it is the only support the claim can have. [ADR-002:30]
- **Demotion is just as mandatory as promotion.** [ADR-002:32]
- **Prose is pruned when models stop needing it.** Every prose rule carries its exhibit — why it exists — so a future sweep can tell load-bearing from vestigial. [ADR-002:34]
- **A code guard whose condition can no longer recur is deleted, not maintained.** A test suite you are afraid to delete from is doctrine accreting in a compiler. [ADR-002:35]
- **The one exception that skips the lifecycle: boundary formats.** Formats and state at the GitHub boundary — markers, ledger rows, version stamps — are interchange formats whose whole point is durability across sessions and runtimes: stable by nature, and the site of every historical silent-violation incident. These are code from day one; the model never hand-writes them, it calls an emitter and a validator. The interim waiver in force while the emitter library does not yet exist is stated in §8. [ADR-002:37]
- **The second road into the lifecycle is admission by trial,** because promotion cannot serve everything: a rule can start as nothing and a mechanism cannot. [ADR-002:39]
- **A rule and a mechanism are told apart by what writing buys.** A **rule** is anything a competent session could do correctly from the goal and the constraints alone; writing it down makes that more reliable, but writing it is not what makes it *possible*. A **mechanism** is a thing that must be the *same thing* across sessions to work at all — a fixed name, shape, callable surface, or coordination point another party has to be able to find. A gate procedure is a mechanism: it is designed-in and non-overridable (§5), and the owner must know where to answer it. A checklist lens is a rule: a session applies it from the goal, which is why a recurring class is closed by promoting a lens rather than by admitting one. [ADR-002:41]
- **Boundary formats are neither a rule nor a mechanism, and nothing on the trial road reaches them.** A ledger row, a marker, a version stamp, and the vocabularies §8 grows by amendment are excluded by name, not by a preposition a reader has to notice: they are code from day one and they *skip* the lifecycle rather than entering it. [ADR-002:41]
- **What a trial admits is a mechanism's procedural specification** — its name, surface, shape, trigger, question, record format — and never the **advisory methodology** carried alongside it, which stays on the promotion road and is still earned by incident. A gate procedure's trigger and record ship with the trial; the guidance on writing a good one does not. A skill's steps ship; its rationale does not. This clause is what stops the road becoming a paragraph tax under a new name. [ADR-002:43]
- **The trial road exists because of a loop, and the loop closes only where a mechanism's absence is silent.** For a mechanism whose value cannot be shown without running it, "start as nothing" means *never start*: the promotion trigger cannot fire, because the incidents it demands are produced only by the mechanism running, and that verdict is indistinguishable from evidence weighing against the mechanism. The unqualified form is false, though — promotion's trigger is that inconsistency across sessions has actually cost something, and a mechanism's **absence** can cost something too. Where the absence produces its own incident, the ordinary road is open and the trial road is not needed. [ADR-002:45]
- **The owner may admit a mechanism as a trial, before any evidence exists.** Six properties govern it, each carrying the failure it blocks, because a property whose point is invisible is one a later reader tidies away. [ADR-002:49]
- **Trial property 1 — mechanisms only; rules keep the promotion road.** Blocks the paragraph tax accreting through a door built for something else. [ADR-002:51]
- **Trial property 2 — marked as a trial wherever it is used.** The marker is the word `trial` with its admission date, in the mechanism's own opening prose, at **every** site the mechanism is stated, so `git grep -in trial` returns every live one as a single class; without a fixed form, two sessions' trials are not greppable together and property 3 has nothing to fire on. Inside a shipped-zone skill, the marker's pointer back to this statute takes the form §4 requires. Blocks speculation laundering itself as evidence: a trial a reader cannot distinguish from an earned rule is worse than no trial, because it spends the credibility the earned ones paid for. [ADR-002:52]
- **Trial property 3 — cut at its review trigger unless evidence for it has arrived.** Removal takes no argument; retention takes one, made to the owner and recorded where the marker is. Until an instrument observes triggers, this is a norm rather than an operation and the obligation sits on whoever next reads the marker. Blocks accretion, which wins wherever the default is "keep." [ADR-002:53]
- **Trial property 4 — states its falsifier and review trigger before it runs, each in a unit something actually counts, and they are not the same kind of unit.** The **falsifier** is a claim about yield, counted in what the trial's instrument holds — by default the ledger (§8): rows, the fields on a row, and distinct `source` values. **Elapsed time is barred from a falsifier whatever the instrument records**, because it counts no yield at all — stated of the claim and not of the ledger's contents, since the instrument is no longer fixed. The **review trigger** is a claim about *opportunity* — how much chance the mechanism has had to fire — and may be counted in units of work, pull requests or uses, **because a trigger counted in the trial's own output can never fire on a trial that produced none**, which inverts property 3 against exactly the trial that most deserves cutting. The trigger's unit is free and its size is not: the owner grants it at admission (property 5), so a generous window is one the owner declined to refuse rather than one the trial took. Blocks a governing distinction written in a unit nothing carries, and blocks the proxy a session reaches for when the honest unit is banned. [D-69]
- **Trial property 5 — the owner admits it, and the admission is recorded where the convergence gate's is (§6).** Blocks the pretence that this is evidence: it is intent and risk appetite, judgment no model capability substitutes for. [ADR-002:55]
- **Trial property 6 — a trial produces, while it runs, the record its own promotion would demand, attributable to it.** The default instrument is the ledger and the default attribution is §8's optional `trial` token. **A trial whose evidence is not a defect names its instrument in its admission**, and that instrument must be durable, countable and attributable to the trial; the instrument is part of what the owner admits, and a trial whose instrument turns out not to count is cut like any other. **An instrument that is a new record format is a boundary format and takes §2's day-one-code rule, not this road** — its emitter and validator land before the trial starts, and §8's interim waiver reaches only the formats it names — the two record files, the entry skeleton and the citation tokens — so a trial wanting one either builds it first or names a surface that already exists; stalling instead reproduces, one level up, the never-start loop this road exists to break. Blocks a slower ratchet; this is what makes the road pay for itself, by manufacturing exactly the record promotion demands — and naming the instrument rather than assuming the ledger is what keeps that ratchet reachable by a mechanism whose value is that a defect is never made, which a defect ledger cannot express. [D-69]
- **Properties 3 and 6 are the structural argument, and neither works alone: a trial is self-liquidating** — promoted on the evidence it generated or cut for generating none, so it cannot sit and become furniture. **Where property 6 is excepted, its substitute carries this obligation**, since a trial producing nothing anyone can weigh is not self-liquidating whatever else the exception names. A promotion removes the marker in the commit that promotes it, and a cut reaches the mechanism's own text, every reference that accreted around it while it ran, **and any instrument built to carry it** — a record that outlives the trial it was built for is the accretion this property exists to prevent, arriving by the door a named instrument opens. [D-69]
- **The owner may admit a trial that cannot satisfy a property, and no property is waived without a substitute.** The admission states which property, why, and what stands in its place; it is recorded where property 5 puts the admission, and the marker carries the fact that a property was excepted, so `git grep -in trial` reaches the excepted trials alongside the ordinary ones without the whole statement being restated at every marker site. **A substitute that leaves property 3 nothing to fire on is no substitute** — that is the standard, not merely that something was named, and it reaches property 4 too, whose trigger is property 3's only operand: a trial nothing can cut is the furniture this road exists to prevent, and it arrives as easily through a substitute that does nothing as through no substitute at all. **Four properties take no exception:** 1, because excepting it admits rules as trials, which is the tax the road was built to exclude; 2, because a trial a reader cannot distinguish from earned prose is worse than no trial; 3, because excepting it excepts the point; and 5, because it is the property that grants exceptions. [D-69]
- **The trial road is open to any mechanism,** and a trial whose instrument is the ledger attributes its rows by §8's optional `trial` token on a ledger row and on a seat-record row, so `SELECT DISTINCT trial` enumerates *those* trials from the instrument itself. **It does not enumerate them all**, since property 6 admits an instrument that is not the ledger; property 2's marker is the enumerator that reaches every live trial, which is one more thing that marker is load-bearing for. [D-69]
- **An instrument that records only what a trial found cannot say it ran clean**, and such a trial names a run record in its admission. Where a record of the *run* exists alongside it, that record answers *whether*, and no separate naming is owed. Otherwise *ran and found nothing* and *never ran* are one silence, so property 3 reads the named run record for *whether* it ran and the instrument for *what it found*. **A seat trial is the worked example and needs no naming**: its instrument is the ledger, and §8 requires a seat record of every panel and routine review, whose `status` distinguishes `clean` from `ran` — a requirement §8 itself lists as unenforced, so the relief rests on a duty rather than on a guarantee — so a seat trial that produced no seat record at all, not one that produced no findings, is the case that cannot be retained on silence. [D-69]
- **The trial road binds only mechanisms admitted as trials.** Anything that reached prose or code by the ordinary road stays there, unmarked, and is not exposed to property 3. [ADR-002:64]
- **A cut trial leaves no record beyond the ones that already exist** — its pull request, and the append-only status line of the record that carried it. A dedicated graveyard would be a maintained corpus, the exit §6 forbids. The cost is real and is not hidden: a cut trial is then discoverable only by reading history, so its lesson resurfaces weakly and someone may try the same thing twice. [ADR-002:66]
- **Nothing caps how many trials run at once.** A number that reaches this prose becomes part of the rule and has to be right, so the cap is not written in advance; property 3 is the pressure, and if trials accumulate and it is observably not cutting them, that record earns the cap. [ADR-002:66]
- **This road's own falsifier is property 1's boundary failing in use.** Everything rests on a reader telling *a thing a competent session could do from the goal alone* from *a thing that must be the same across sessions*. If trials are admitted for things that had a judgment-tier form all along, that failure is the incident that earns a stronger material, and the road is demoted or replaced rather than defended. [ADR-002:68]
- **Two events say something about the trial road, and either is the trigger to re-read it.** The first is **the road running** — a ledger row carrying a `trial` token or whose `found_by` or `source` names a trial, or, for a trial whose instrument is not the ledger, that instrument's first record of it; the token is named because it is the attribution §8 minted for the mechanisms this road was opened past a review seat to serve, and a rule naming only the other two fields reaches a seat trial alone; naming only the ledger row here would miss the very trials property 6 was widened to admit. The second is **a mechanism the road could not admit**, because an admission that fails on a property is evidence about that property, and a road too strong to admit anything writes no row and so is unfalsifiable in exactly the case where it is wrong. Like property 3 this is a norm and not an operation: a failed admission leaves no record unless someone writes one, and its home is the work's issue, where property 5 already puts the admission it failed. [D-69]
- **Code volume stays proportional to *proven* stability.** That is the brittleness control: when models or requirements change, the demolition surface is small. [ADR-002:75]
- **Every promotion and demotion is a recordable event,** which is what lets process weight be governed by evidence. [ADR-002:76]
## 3. Cells, bundles and layers

- **The skill is the cell.** The unit of everything is the skill: a self-contained cell carrying whatever mix of materials its job requires — methodology prose, its own scripts and validators, its own tests, its own references. A skill about methodology may be pure prose; a skill wrapping a mechanical operation may be mostly script with a page of prose saying when to run it. The cell boundary, not the material, is what is uniform, which is why one loading and shipping story covers every skill. [ADR-003:17]
- **Methodology's home is the skill.** The cell is the only unit that can be read, tested and shipped with its methodology attached, which is why methodology never lives inside a persona and no workflow may depend on one existing. [ADR-001:25]
- **A piece becomes its own skill when it has an independent invocation trigger** — a situation where it should fire without the parent job being underway. If it only ever loads in service of one job, it is a `references/` file inside that job's skill, loaded on demand, because a cell stays cheap to carry only while its body is what actually fires. [ADR-003:19]
- **Directory grouping carries no semantics.** `skills/<area>/…` is purely organizational and confers no loading and no shipping behavior, so nothing may be inferred about a skill from where it sits in the tree. [ADR-003:19]
- **Dependencies point down, never sideways.** A skill may depend downward on the one sanctioned core library — boundary formats and genuinely shared primitives only (§2's exception) — and on nothing else. **No skill requires another skill.** Composition happens above the cells, not between them, so no skill's behavior can be changed by an edit to another skill. [ADR-003:23]
- **The bundle is the plugin.** Skills ship together in one plugin: discovered together, versioned together, firing independently. Independent versioning multiplies release-hygiene machinery that already hurt at N=1, and no second audience with a different upgrade cadence exists; because the no-sideways-dependency rule keeps a future split mechanical, this is revisited when a real second audience exists rather than pre-built for one. [ADR-003:27]
- **A pull request that changes any shipped-zone file bumps the plugin version before it merges.** The bundle is versioned as a unit, and the version is a consumer's only signal that installed and current differ. The unit is §6's — the pull request measured against its merge base — never the individual commit, and the new version must strictly increase as a semantic version. [ADR-003:29] [ADR-003:31] [ADR-003:35]
- **A guard that cannot determine its answer exits non-zero.** A guard that goes quiet when it cannot decide prints the same line as a clean pass, so every failure mode it meets becomes invisible; undeterminable is a failure, not a pass. [ADR-003:35]
- **Layer 1, state substrate** — durable state lives on GitHub, never in session memory. "State" here means structured records carried on issues, PR comments and checks — the boundary formats §2 enumerates — and those reads and writes go through the core library once it exists, so no session hand-writes an interchange format. [ADR-003:39]
- **Ordinary git operations are runtime plumbing, not boundary formats.** Commit and push are not structured records at the GitHub boundary, so skills may perform them directly rather than routing them through the core library. [ADR-003:39]
- **Layer 2, core library** — boundary-format emit, parse and validate, plus genuinely shared primitives. Small by constitutional intent: it is the one downward dependency every cell is allowed, so whatever is admitted to it is admitted to all of them. [ADR-003:40]
- **Layer 3, skills** — the cells, governed by this section's rules. [ADR-003:41]
- **Layer 4, composition** — commands, thin dispatch shells, and lanes. All sequencing and all coupling live here, explicitly, so coupling is cheap to see and change; composition is the **only** layer allowed to know the lane shapes, which is what keeps the coupling quarantined instead of diffused into the cells. [ADR-003:42] [ADR-003:48]
- **Layer 5, doctrine** — a deliberately tiny root file under an enforced size budget — **8,000 characters**, a figure that is part of the rule — carrying the constitution's pointers and not its content, because root instructions are always-loaded words that tax every session whether relevant or not. This is **the doctrine budget**; §4's lint enforces it and §9 binds the canonical root file to it. [ADR-003:43] [ADR-002:15]
## 4. Zones

- **The repository has two zones, with a mechanical wall between them.** The wall is what lets contributor docs be as heavy as they need to be without any of that weight taxing a consumer. [ADR-004:15] [ADR-004:27]
- **The repo-only zone** is `docs/`, CI workflows and their scripts, contributor rules, and this constitution. It may reference anything; consumers must never *depend* on it. [ADR-004:17]
- **The shipped zone** is `skills/`, the core library, composition-layer commands and shells, and the plugin manifest. [ADR-004:18]
- **Nothing in the shipped zone may reference the repo-only zone** — not a path, not a doc link, not a "see CONTRIBUTING" aside. The reference wall, not packaging exclusion, is the enforced boundary: it is what keeps the shipped zone *working* without the repo-only zone present. [ADR-004:18] [ADR-004:17]
- **A shipped-zone pointer into the repo-only zone takes the full `https://` URL form, and that form is lawful only while the repository is public.** A repo path does not resolve for a consumer; a web URL does. When the repository stops being public the form stops working, and evidence that lived only behind such a pointer stops being reachable. [ADR-002:30]
- **The packaging lint fails the build on** shipped→repo-only references, sideways skill dependencies (§3), and root doctrine over the doctrine budget (§3). The consumer experience is checked on every commit rather than audited after the fact, so "will the plugin experience be optimal?" is a build failure and not a review question. [ADR-004:20] [ADR-004:26]
- **Standards placement follows the zone rule.** A standard that downstream work must follow lives *in the skill that teaches it* (shipped); a rule about how *this repo* is built lives in the repo-only zone. When one idea needs both, the skill carries the standard and the repo doc carries only the local application — never the reverse, and never duplicated prose that can drift. [ADR-004:22]
- **The lint is the constitution's enforcement arm for the checkable subset only.** Path-form references are checkable and are enforced; name-form coupling stays a review concern, so a green lint is never a claim that the whole constitution held. [ADR-004:28]
- **When this statute changes, any lint rule enforcing the old text changes in the same commit.** A guard left enforcing repealed law reports green while checking a rule that no longer exists, which is worse than no guard. A statute rule with no lint rule enforcing it carries no such obligation. [ADR-004:28]
## 5. Interaction and gates

- **Every touchpoint answers one question: what does the human uniquely provide at this moment?** Three answers are possible, and each dictates a structure — a touchpoint that cannot say which of the three it is has not been designed. [ADR-005:17]
- **First answer — judgment only the human can supply**: intent, taste, priorities, risk appetite, and anything irreversible or outward-facing. These are **gates**: designed-in, non-overridable by pacing directives, and worth human time precisely because no model capability substitutes for them. [ADR-005:19]
- **The gate set is *framing* ("is this the problem?"), *convergence* ("is this the approach?"), *commitment* ("is this the bet?" — scope/plan approval), and *release* (merge, publish, delete).** The set is expected to evolve by evidence, not by accretion. [ADR-005:19]
- **The cut test: a gate must be able to state what the human uniquely decides at it.** A gate that cannot is automation wearing interaction's clothes, and is cut. [ADR-005:19]
- **Second answer — information the system can obtain: never ask.** A question containing a fact the agent could have looked up is a defect. [ADR-005:22]
- **Third answer — confirmation of the predictable: do not ask.** Act and report if reversible; if not, batch into an argued-case gate — per-item recommendation with its reasoning over the alternatives, the human's approve/modify/drop being the interaction, which is an informed one only where those alternatives are visible in this section's surfacing form. [D-61]
- **The check always runs; the question fires only when the outcome is genuinely indeterminate.** [ADR-005:27]
- **"Determined" has a narrow meaning: determined by a recorded human decision** — a standing rule, or a prior approval at the same or broader scope — **never by evidence the model has read on its own.** Intent, taste, priorities and risk appetite are exactly the inputs a model will believe it inferred from evidence, and inference does not determine a gate. [ADR-005:27]
- **A gate whose answer is covered by a recorded decision announces its conclusion, names that decision, carries a standing override, and proceeds.** [ADR-005:27]
- **Commitment is scoped by what the work's artifact states, not by its weight.** In the minimal lane its question does not fire where the affirmed artifact states the work's scope, because affirming the artifact as a whole approves that scope in the same act, leaving nothing for a second gate to uniquely decide; the session announces that conclusion, names the affirmed artifact as the decision covering it, and proceeds. [ADR-005:21]
- **Where the artifact states no scope, commitment has something left to decide, and fires.** So does it wherever a lane produces a design and a plan as separate artifacts: those are two decisions — *this is the shape*, and *this is the amount we are betting on it* — and commitment states what it uniquely decides and stands. [ADR-005:21]
- **Commitment is scoped, not deleted.** It stays in the gate set and its check still runs; only its question is answered early. Struck from the set it would read as the set shrinking by convenience, against the set's own rule that it evolves by evidence rather than by accretion. [ADR-005:21]
- **The owner's standing override to invoke commitment anyway is preserved wherever its question is answered early, and is exercised in conversation.** [ADR-005:21]
- **Consent travels with the decision.** Once the human approves a decision, everything entailed by it executes without re-asking; per-action re-approval is how approval fatigue destroys the gates that matter. [ADR-005:31]
- **One bound: consent never travels across a later gate type.** Commitment approval — the plan — never carries release consent — the merge, publish, or delete; each gate type's check still runs, answered by its own recorded decisions. [ADR-005:31]
- **The attended lane is conversational and gates fire inline.** Every question arrives as an argued recommendation — state, conflict, and evidence first, then the ask — batched at phase seams, never dribbled mid-stream. [ADR-005:35]
- **A decision put to the owner carries the live options, each with its pros and cons, and the recommendation among them.** This is what *argued* requires, on every surface a decision reaches the owner by — a gate, an argued-case batch, an `owner-pending` surfacing (§7), a typed halt report, a decision raised in conversation — because what makes a decision informed does not vary by the surface it arrives on. A bare menu withholds the judgment the surfacing exists to supply; a bare recommendation withholds the modify-space, and *approve / modify / drop* is an informed interaction only where the alternatives are visible. Where exactly one option is live, the surfacing says so and says what it rejected and why: a fabricated pair under-informs worse than the bare recommendation it was added to cure, because it presents a search that did not happen. This form is stated **also** in the review practice's skill, which §4's wall bars from citing this document, so a change to it lands at both sites in the same change. [D-61]
- **The unattended lane asks zero questions by construction.** Everything either proceeds by pre-approved rule or halts with a typed, legible halt report processed on the human's schedule; free-prose stops that could be mistaken for completion are forbidden. [ADR-005:36]
- **Every skill that can pause declares its pause discipline in its own prose** — ask-inline (attended lanes only) or typed-halt (safe in either lane). "Maybe ask, maybe halt" is the worst of both and is not a lawful shape. [ADR-005:38]
- **A pause discipline is about the skill's own stopping behaviour; lane *shapes* — sequencing and composition — remain the composition layer's knowledge alone (§3).** [ADR-005:38]
- **The release gate is designed-in but not enforced by the platform: nothing outside this statute stops an agent merging its own pull request.** The gate holds because the agent stops, and a rule that depends on the agent's compliance must be written where the agent reads it. [ADR-005:42]
- **The repository ruleset is the source of truth for what the platform enforces**, so any description of those settings that conflicts with the ruleset loses, and the text needs a commit. [ADR-005:42]
- **Pacing directives ("don't stop to ask") apply to preference questions and never to gates.** The lever to skip a gate is the option the gate itself offers. [ADR-005:48]
- **Question quality is reviewable: a transcript's questions can be audited against the second and third answers above.** [ADR-005:47]
- **Human time concentrates at the gate types that fire for the lane in hand, and at the batched exception queues of unattended runs; everything else is designed to cost nothing.** [ADR-005:46]
## 6. Process defaults

- **The minimal lane is the entrance.** One conversation takes work from intent to done; heavier lanes — staged design, deeper review, unattended runs — exist and are opt-in, chosen for cause rather than defaulted into. [ADR-006:17]
- **Quality is still the first constraint.** None of the above relaxes *coverage* — what gets looked at — only the ceremony around it. When lightness and a needed check conflict, the check wins; the ledger is how a check proves it is no longer needed. [ADR-006:129]
- **Review attaches to artifacts, not only to code.** A framing, a design, a plan, and an implementation are each reviewable artifacts, and a defect is cheapest caught at the artifact where it was introduced: a design flaw found in the design costs a conversation, and the same flaw found in the implementation costs the implementation. [ADR-006:18]
- **Escalation has two axes — *depth*, a wider or stronger review of one artifact, and *position*, reviewing an earlier artifact before building on it — and when risk justifies exactly one escalation, position usually beats depth.** [ADR-006:18]
- **In the minimal lane the pre-implementation artifact may be a stated approach a paragraph long, and its review *is* the convergence gate (§5).** The check always runs; what is opt-in is a dedicated adversarial challenge of the design or plan before implementation begins. [ADR-006:18]
- **The convergence gate fires before the first line of implementation, on any change carrying a pre-implementation artifact.** What the owner uniquely decides is §5's convergence question — *is this the approach?* — and the artifact is affirmed, amended, or rejected as a whole. [ADR-006:22]
- **Nothing below the trivial floor reaches the convergence gate — it, this gate, and no other.** Framing has no procedure, and the release gate is never skipped at any size; being below the floor is never a reason to skip a gate §5 still has firing. [ADR-006:22]
- **The trivial floor is a property of the change, not of its diff: a change is below it when it makes no decision** — it neither states, alters, nor removes a rule, nor introduces a mechanism or changes one's surface — **and a reader of the diff has nothing to affirm or reject.** [ADR-006:24]
- **A mechanism's surface is what another party relies on to use it, and that is not only its signature.** The test is reliance: a change is a surface change when a party using the mechanism correctly would now have to use it differently, or would get a different answer to the question it is called for. A script that refuses what it used to accept, a skill that gains a step its consumer must run, a gate procedure whose trigger moves — all above. Nothing a caller does or relies on moves — an internal rewrite, a clearer message for the same verdict, a fix to a case that never worked — and it stays below. [ADR-006:24]
- **The floor question is asked of the change you intend, before it is written, which is why it is reliance and not behaviour.** Whether a rewrite will in fact preserve behaviour is unknowable at the moment the floor is called; whether you intend to move what callers rely on is not. [ADR-006:24]
- **Both halves of the material boundary — rules and mechanisms — are named in the floor's test on purpose.** A mechanism is the one material that reaches the owner by admission rather than by incident (§2), so a test naming only rules would route it around the gate it most needs. [ADR-006:24]
- **The unit is the pull request measured against its merge base.** This is the floor's unit; §3 states the same unit for the version bump. A per-commit reading lets the same change answer differently by when it is split. [ADR-006:26]
- **Size is not the test and cannot be.** A change of a few lines across a few files can create a rule every later pull-request body must satisfy. [ADR-006:30]
- **Never below the floor, regardless of size:** anything that creates, changes or retires a rule; anything that introduces a mechanism or changes one's surface; and anything that changes what a guard accepts or rejects — the guards being `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py`, `tools/check_constitution.py`, and the CI workflow that invokes them, the ledger's *schema* included, since the lint validates its vocabularies. [ADR-006:32]
- **Appending a schema-conforming row to the ledger is below the floor** — it states no rule and changes no guard — so the ordinary output of every review needs no ceremony; **a row carrying a `found_by` value the ledger's vocabulary has not admitted (§8) is not an append but an amendment, and is above.** [ADR-006:32]
- **`AGENTS.md` and the constitution are *presumed* above the floor** — every session reads the one and is governed by the other — **and the presumption is rebutted only in the canonical form stated below. Silence does not rebut it.** A typo, a dead link, or a rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical is below the floor there as anywhere. [ADR-006:32]
- **The floor fails closed: a session that cannot tell is above it.** A wrongly-skipped gate costs an unbounded amount; a wrongly-run one costs the artifact — a paragraph, in the minimal lane — **plus a blocking round-trip to the owner**, which is the resource §5 exists to conserve, so the direction is chosen knowing it is not free. [ADR-006:34]
- **The declaration has a surface, and it is the pull request body.** The session declares its reading there — above the floor, or below it with the rule the change leaves untouched named; where the presumption attaches, that naming takes the canonical form below. The owner can overrule. Above the floor the affirmation record carries it too, and a *below*-floor declaration is not routed into that record, which exists only where there is an artifact — that would give the declaration a home for the one case that does not need it and none for the case the floor creates. A declaration with no recording surface is indistinguishable afterward from having skipped the gate, which is the whole failure being prevented. [ADR-006:36]
- **Stating the floor makes the judgment cheaper, never unnecessary.** [ADR-006:36]
- **Above the floor, work that has no issue files one**, since an issue is the pre-implementation artifact's home. [ADR-006:36]
- **The rebuttal has a canonical form, and the pull-request body carries one of the two statements below.** A singular *which rule* supplies no shape and has no principled answer in the case it meets most often: a typo leaves *every* rule untouched, so naming one is writable but arbitrary, while *no rule in this document is touched* is an absence claim about a whole document, which this repository's own review standard discharges only by reading to the end of it. [ADR-006:38]
- **Targeted**, where the change sits in a rule's neighbourhood: *"This change edits `<file(s)>`, where a reader would expect it to move `<the rule — quote the sentence that states it, not the paragraph around it, one quote per file named>`; it leaves that rule untouched because `<reason>`. It creates, changes or retires no other rule, introduces no mechanism and changes no mechanism's surface, does not change **what** `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py`, `tools/check_constitution.py` or CI accepts or rejects, and leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject."* [ADR-006:40]
- **Cosmetic**, where the change is a typo, a dead link, or a rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical: *"This change is a `<typo | dead link | rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical>` in `<file>`: `<what changed>`. It changes no rule's meaning, introduces no mechanism and changes no mechanism's surface, does not change **what** `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py`, `tools/check_constitution.py` or CI accepts or rejects, and leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject."* [ADR-006:42]
- **Both sentences of the chosen statement are required, and the words bind rather than the typography.** A session that writes only the first has dropped the never-below walk, which is the half that does the work. [ADR-006:44]
- **Where a pull request is *both* kinds it is Targeted**, whose second sentence is a universal over the whole change and covers the cosmetic part; the floor fails closed and that is the stronger branch. **Targeted names the rule the change might have been thought to touch** — the weaker, honest claim — and Cosmetic exists because that naming has no principled answer where every rule is untouched. [ADR-006:44]
- **Neither statement moves what is below the floor**: the never-below list and the triad ruling — typo, dead link, meaning-identical rewording — are unchanged, and the owner overrules either at release. [ADR-006:44]
- **A competing reading of the triad ruling is recorded rather than deleted**: that ruling can be read as itself disposing of the triad, in which case the Cosmetic statement restates a ruling already made rather than relaxing one. On either reading the floor's extension is unchanged; what moves is the discharge. [ADR-006:38]
- **Commitment's question does not fire below the floor; its check still runs.** The answer is covered by a recorded decision — this ruling — under §5's ordinary gate-vs-question machinery, and **not** its cut test, which removes a gate from the set and would contradict *scoped, not deleted*. The reason is the floor's own definition: a change below it leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject, and nothing to affirm or reject is nothing to bet on, so commitment has nothing it uniquely decides. [ADR-006:28]
- **A change carrying a genuine bet has something to affirm or reject and is above the floor by that fact** — doubtful cases with it, since the floor fails closed — so the below-floor genuine bet is empty by construction, answered from the definition rather than by a carve-out. [ADR-006:28]
- **Below the floor the session announces that conclusion, names this ruling as the decision covering it, and proceeds, in the pull-request body beside the floor declaration.** The owner's standing override to invoke commitment anyway is preserved below the floor as above it, and is exercised in conversation; above the floor, commitment follows §5 unchanged. [ADR-006:28]
- **The competing reading is recorded rather than deleted, because it is the one a careful reader reaches first**: §5 fires commitment *"where the artifact states no scope,"* and a below-floor change need carry no artifact — arguably the limiting case of stating none. What decides against it is that clause's own scope: it governs artifacts that *exist and omit scope* — a thin artifact, not an absent one. [ADR-006:28]
- **Where a below-floor change carries a small reversible scope choice, §5's third answer governs it in full**, and the report of such a choice is routed by the disposition rule (§7), never left in conversation. [ADR-006:28]
- **The pre-implementation artifact lives on the work's issue, as a comment.** An issue is the durable identity the work is discussed under, it survives branch deletion, and it does not break across multi-PR work — the two holes a branch file has. The artifact is written once, read by people and agents, and parsed by nothing, so it inherits no machine-parsed comment protocol. [ADR-006:45]
- **A revision is a new comment, latest wins.** Comment edits are invisible where the thread is read, and append-only ordering needs no reconciler. [ADR-006:45]
- **The artifact's shape is weighted to acceptance criteria and the boundary statement.** Criteria state what a nonconforming run looks like, so silence cannot pass as conformance; the **boundary statement** is the explicit list of what the work is *not* doing and why, which is what makes a later scope question answerable from the artifact instead of from memory. The weighting aims the shape and does not mandate a proportion. [ADR-006:46]
- **Rejected options take one of three exits — into a rule's prose as its *reason*, into an explicit falsifier that can expire, or the immutable pull-request snapshot — and never accumulate a maintained corpus.** [ADR-006:46]
- **The affirmation is given in conversation; approvals are not given on GitHub.** [ADR-006:47]
- **The durable record is a comment the session writes to the issue before the first commit**, naming the affirmed artifact comment by URL, stating that affirmation was conversational, and dated. **What that record buys is ordering, not authenticity** — its server-side timestamp precedes the first commit and stays checkable from the thread; it attests that the owner agreed and cannot prove it. [ADR-006:47]
- **Naming the artifact comment is what binds the record to a *revision*:** without it, a thread that affirms, revises, and implements passes the ordering check with the current artifact unaffirmed. [ADR-006:47]
- **The residual risk is named rather than mechanised.** A session that fabricates an affirmation is undetectable from the record, and the backstop is not a new mechanism but the release gate — already designed-in, already the owner's, and where a fabricated affirmation meets the person it was fabricated about. [ADR-006:47]
- **The review default is set at any position and scaled by the artifact's weight, and every review records which lane was chosen, and why, in its review report**, so the classification itself stays auditable. [ADR-006:48]
- **For routine changes: one adversarial pass that reads the artifact cold** — before any prior findings, self-review, or earlier ledgers are exposed to it — **then an evidence-bound defense, then mandatory re-validation of fixes.** There is no judge, and defense's evidence verdicts terminate. [ADR-006:48]
- **For substantial artifacts — newly written foundational prose, new scripts, high blast radius, and when in doubt this lane — a differentiated panel of four seats is the default, not an escalation**, then defense, then a judge whose job is independent re-execution of contested evidence, severity settling, and weighing each remedy's value against its price. Going below the default is bought only by ledger evidence, and a separate judge is earned by a panel-width ledger. [ADR-006:48]
- **The roster, its staffing, and its canonical seat names live in the practice's skill, `skills/adversarial-review`.** [ADR-006:48]
- **No clone seats: a seat is added for a lens or vantage the panel lacks, not for a second pass at one it has.** Stated honestly this is a judgment rather than a measurement — redundancy is not worthless, differentiation is simply what the seat record measures, and concurrence between passes has amplified a false finding — and it is revisable by `found_by` evidence in either direction. [ADR-006:48]
- **Every finding's finder is recorded (§8), so seats retire by ledger evidence rather than by argument — on yield.** §8 states what the instrument cannot see: the ledger holds only sustained findings, so it is structurally silent about a seat's precision, which is the axis a seat is actually questioned on. [ADR-006:48]
- **Recurring defect classes are closed by lens promotion (§2), never by adding seats, and that rule has no exception.** [ADR-006:48]
- **The fourth seat is selected by the artifact's shape**, stated here rather than only in the skill because a session reading the statute alone would otherwise seat an unconditional four: three seats stand, and the fourth is the claims-and-citations lens on substantially new prose, the revision-comparison lens on an amendment to existing governing prose, **both — at width five — when the artifact is both**, and the claims lens by default when it is neither. The names, charters and exhibits live in the practice's skill, which owns the roster. [ADR-006:50]
- **The axis that selects the fourth seat is the material, not the seats' relative yield**, and nothing here ranks them; **the both-shapes clause is a rule and not a judgment**, because the alternative was measured. [ADR-006:50]
- **Defense is mandatory at every depth: it is what makes width safe.** [ADR-006:50]
- **Fix re-validation is mandatory at every depth, executable rather than a re-read, and scoped to every branch the fix touched.** Every reading stage, defense included, shows shared blind spots that only live runs catch, and an escaped class recurs in its own fix's mirror image. [ADR-006:50]
- **Depth and position are escalated per-change by declared risk — wider panels, external fresh-session reviewers — and de-escalated only by ledger evidence.** In both directions, weight follows evidence, never cost arguments and never habit. [ADR-006:50]
- **The terminal stage prices what it rules, and that is not the lane-and-width clause above being relaxed** — that clause is unchanged, including its asymmetry. What is priced here is narrower and downstream: an individual **disposition** (this finding is real; is the remedy worth what it costs?) and the **marginal pass** (is another look worth running?). [ADR-006:52]
- **Anything lawfully priced leaves a record.** A priced-out finding writes a ledger row whose `ref`-surface reason states its price (§7), and a stop-or-run ruling is written in the review's final report — whereas a cost argument for *skipping* a check produces silence, which is the kind this statute forbids and will keep forbidding. [ADR-006:52]
- **The defense states each remedy's price** — regression surface and complexity delta, argued rather than scored. [ADR-006:52]
- **The terminal stage — the judge on panels, the defense on routine reviews — applies a two-clause test: *would acting improve the artifact*, then *is that improvement worth the price*.** [ADR-006:52]
- **A finding failing only the second clause is *sustained and priced out*, never dismissed**: it was real, and the record must say so. [ADR-006:52]
- **Severity and price are two halves and neither absorbs the other**: severity is defined in §8, and the price measures what the remedy costs. Without severity's meaning the second clause has no half to weigh against. [ADR-006:52]
- **The terminal stage's procedure and its exhibits live in the practice's skill.** [ADR-006:52]
- **Every review ends with a next-pass ruling — run or stop, each polarity carrying its price — written in the review's final report, and the default is to stop once a pass has sustained no high it will fix.** [ADR-006:54]
- **The next-pass ruling governs every look past the practice's mandatory post-fix floor, the fix cycle included.** That floor is owed and can never be priced away; every look after it — the next one in a fix cycle as much as a further whole pass — is ruled on and priced, because the cycle is where a review's unbounded time goes. This retires the former exit, *a clean look ends the cycle*, which was an asymptote wearing a termination condition's clothes. [ADR-006:54]
- **The operand is not bare `sustained`:** a priced-out high is sustained and by construction unfixed, so a default reading it could never fire on the artifact that produced one. [ADR-006:54]
- **The next-pass default rests on an owner ruling rather than on measurement** — it is risk appetite, §5's first answer, and the owner may set it. No measured threshold is claimed. [ADR-006:54]
- **Each review report records that pass's yield in the unit the practice's skill states**, so curves accumulate at no cost and the default is revised by citing them. [ADR-006:54]
- **For an implementation artifact, the review runs on the pull request, and running it is never a question.** Publish the branch and open the PR *first*, then run the review, then reconcile whatever automated reviewers posted while it ran; they start within seconds of the PR opening, so publishing first converts the external pass from a serial cost into a concurrent one. [ADR-006:56 "the review runs on the pull request, and running it is never a question"]
- **This sequence is about artifacts that have a branch and a pull request.** The upstream positions — a framing, a design, a plan — have neither, and their review is the convergence gate, which is a human gate and does ask; nothing here reaches them. [ADR-006:56 "this sequence is about artifacts that have a branch and a PR"]
- **Within this sequence, running the review is a check, not a gate (§5).** Approving the work approves its review, and an agent that finishes a change and hands the review back as a command for the human to run has invented a gate the charter does not have. [ADR-006:56 "running the review is a check, not a gate"]
- **One seam inside the sequence does lawfully ask, and only in an attended review**: the opening ledger read batches any open `owner-pending` rows into a single argued question at a phase seam (§7), and in an attended review that question is not suppressed to keep the sequence tidy. An unattended run reads the ledger for the evidence its shape and de-escalation choices turn on, leaves the rows as they are, and puts no question — §5's zero-questions construction takes no exception here, and halting at the seam instead would deliver the question no sooner than the durable row already does while costing the unattended lane its purpose. **Where the lane is undetermined the run is treated as unattended**, because the seam fails closed in the cheaper direction: a question wrongly withheld waits on a row that is durable by construction, while a question wrongly put breaches §5's zero-questions construction outright. [D-61]
- **The release gate is untouched by this sequence**: it ends at a reviewed pull request, and merging remains the human's. [ADR-006:56 "The release gate is untouched"]
- **The pull request body states what it closes**: `Closes #N` for every issue the change discharges, **or one line saying it closes none and why.** Closing keywords fire only from the pull-request body or from a commit landing on the default branch — never from a comment — so an issue whose work has landed otherwise stays open in silence, and *silence and a deliberate no are the same string*. [ADR-006:58]
- **This is stated as prose rather than a guard on purpose**: not every pull request closes an issue, so a check cannot fail hard without an escape hatch, and a warn-only check is the shape that gets ignored — the *closes none, because X* half is what would make a future guard checkable at all, by converting an absence into an assertion. [ADR-006:58]
## 7. Findings and their disposition

- **Filing is the exception — for findings.** This section governs findings, not work. A unit of work may hold an issue for the ordinary reasons any tracker exists: it is the durable identity the work is discussed under, the home its pre-implementation artifact lives in (§6), and the thing that survives a branch. What is being prevented is filing *findings* by default, two or three per unit of work, until the backlog is larger than anyone's intent; a repository with almost no issues is not evidence of health either. [ADR-006:59 "This section governs findings, not work"]
- **A finding's default disposition is *fix it now* or *drop it with a one-line reason*.** [ADR-006:61 "A finding's default disposition is *fix it now* or *drop it with a one-line reason*"]
- **A finding leaves the session through exactly one of five lawful exits, and the transcript is not one of them.** The exits are: the change that found it; a guard (§2); a `recorded` row carrying its one-line reason; an `owner-pending` row while the owner is away; or a filed issue with both remedy homes rejected. [ADR-006:65 "Every finding leaves the session through one of the homes above"]
  - **The transcript is not an exit because no stage takes it as input.** A finding written into a reply survives only if someone happens to ask, and a thing that depends on someone happening to ask is not a routing. [ADR-006:65 "the transcript is not one of them, because no stage takes it as input"]
  - **Two of the five are *remedy homes*: the change that found it, and a guard.** A guard fires when the condition recurs; an issue fires never. Wherever this statute says *both other homes rejected*, it names these two. [ADR-006:61 "Its two lawful homes are the change that found it, or a guard"]

- **Naming a finding in conversation is not a disposition, and the obligation sits with whoever noticed.** Surfacing a finding to the maintainer transfers information, never the routing — for every disposition, not only for `owner-pending` rows. [ADR-006:65 "Naming a finding in conversation is not a disposition"]
- **This rule is the permanently unenforceable kind, not the not-yet kind.** The defect is an *absence* of routing, visible only against an intent that was never written down: a tree where a session noticed something and merely said it is indistinguishable from a tree where the session noticed nothing. The rule raises the cost of the omission by naming it and cannot detect it. [ADR-006:71 "this is the permanent kind rather than the not-yet kind"]
- **Binding the exits adds no obligation to find more.** The bar for what counts as a finding is unchanged, and an observation is not a row. What is fixed is the exit, not the entrance. [ADR-006:67 "This adds no obligation to find more"]
- **Filing requires rejecting both remedy homes and passing the pickup test** — would this actually get picked up if time opened tomorrow? [ADR-006:61 "Proposing a tracked follow-up requires stating why both other homes were rejected"]
- **`recorded` and `filed` make different claims and are not interchangeable.** `recorded` says nobody will act on this; `filed` says someone will, at a named surface, and the row's `ref` carries that surface. Writing `recorded` with an issue named in the reason is the practice `filed` was minted to end. [ADR-006:102 "`recorded` says nobody will act on this, `filed` says someone will, at a named surface"]
- **A drop's one-line reason is carried on the row's `ref` surface.** A drop goes to a row with `disposition: recorded`; the reason lives where a later reader arrives, not in the reply. The cheap exit is bound because it is the exit under pressure: naming only the expensive homes as lawful closes three doors and leaves open the one nearest to hand. [ADR-006:67 "A drop goes where §5 already puts it: a row with `disposition: recorded`, its reason carried on the row's `ref` surface"]
- **A drop taken on cost states what the remedy would have cost, not merely that the finding was declined.** "Declined" is indistinguishable in the record from fatigue. [ADR-006:69 "it names **what the remedy would have cost**, not merely that the finding was declined"]
- **A finding *sustained and priced out* (§6) is real, and takes either `recorded` or `filed`.** `filed` is equally lawful where the remedy is routed to a live pickup home carrying the price — pricing a fix out of one change does not assert that nobody will ever act on it — and the filing precondition above still binds, so this is not a cheap road to `filed`. [ADR-006:69 "or `filed`, where the remedy is routed to a live pickup home carrying the price"]
  - **An audit of pricing reads both dispositions.** A rule naming only `recorded` would exclude priced-out findings that were lawfully filed. [ADR-006:69 "an audit of pricing reads both dispositions"]
  - **A priced-out drop that later bites writes its own row**, `caught` naming whichever stage re-found it — a later `adversarial-review`, or `post-merge` or `consumer` where it escaped that far — so mispriced drops surface as recurrence while well-priced ones age silently. [ADR-006:69 "a priced-out drop that later bites writes its own row"]
  - **No field, threshold or query ships with this.** No field distinguishes a priced-out row from any other `recorded` or `filed` row, and none links a recurrence to the drop it falsifies, so the audit reads both dispositions' `ref` surfaces by hand; a field is code and takes §2's recurrence-and-stability road. [ADR-006:69 "No threshold, field or query ships with this"]

- **A finding that turns on a decision only the owner can make is neither filed nor dropped: it is recorded with `disposition: owner-pending` and resurfaced from the ledger.** Every *attended* review's opening ledger read batches the open rows into a single argued question at a phase seam (§5); an unattended run reads the ledger, leaves the rows, puts no question, and so is not a surfacing — as is a run whose lane is undetermined, which §6 fails closed to unattended. A row still open after several such surfacings has failed the pickup test in fact and is re-dispositioned `recorded` with a one-line reason. The owner's queue lives inside the instrument that already gets read, rather than in a backlog that does not. [D-61]
- **`owner-pending` is a holding pen for unattended stretches, not a queue to accumulate in.** It exists so that work continuing while the owner is absent does not lose a decision it cannot make. When the owner is present and the row comes up, the conversation converts it to a decision; carrying it forward through a discussion in which it could have been settled is the failure, not the resurfacing. An agent that surfaces such a row owes §5's surfacing form — never a bare menu, never a bare recommendation. [D-61]
## 8. The ledger and the seat record

- **The ledger's home is `docs/ledger.jsonl`, one JSON object per line, with required fields `{id, date, artifact, severity, introduced, catchable, caught, source, disposition, found_by, ref}`.** [ADR-006:72 "Home: `docs/ledger.jsonl`, one JSON object per line with required fields"]
- **`source` names the review event that produced the row and `id` is unique within it, both strings, so the pair `(source, id)` is globally unique.** [ADR-006:72 "`source` names the review event that produced the row and `id` is unique within it"]
- **`date` is an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` day.** [ADR-006:72 "`date` is an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` day"]
- **`severity` is one of `high | medium | low`, and it measures harm-if-unfixed** — the harm of leaving the defect standing. It is not the remedy's price, which §6 rules is its separate half. [ADR-006:72 "`severity` is one of `high | medium | low`"] [ADR-006:52 "severity measures harm-if-unfixed"]
- **`disposition` is one of `fixed | reworded | recorded | owner-pending | filed`.** [ADR-006:102 "The set is now `fixed | reworded | recorded | owner-pending | filed`"]
- **`found_by` is a non-empty string naming the seat or stage that *first surfaced* the defect.** Lawful values: a canonical seat name from the review practice's roster, or `defense`, `judge`, `post-fix`, `external`, `ci`, `owner`, `consumer`, `authoring-review` (a defect its own author caught before any review stage ran), or `unrecorded` (rows predating the field). [ADR-006:72 "`found_by` is a non-empty string naming the seat or stage that first surfaced the defect"]
  - **A value is added to this list by a decision entry amending this section** — the same growth rule positions and stages take, because a field with no growth rule is a closed vocabulary wearing an open one's clothes. [ADR-006:72 "a value is added to this list by amending this section"]
  - **It is written as a lowercase name of letters, digits and hyphens, so one seat occupies exactly one bucket.** [ADR-006:72 "written as a lowercase name of letters, digits and hyphens so one seat occupies exactly one bucket"]
  - **`owner` is not decoration.** A maintainer functioning as an unrecorded finder silently excludes from the denominator exactly the catches an upstream review would be substituting for. [ADR-006:72 "**`owner` is not decoration**"]
  - **A merged finding's row carries its primary (first-surfacing) finder alone**; dual credit lives in the review report's merged ledger and in the seat record. [ADR-006:72 "a merged finding's row carries its primary (first-surfacing) finder"]
  - **`found_by` measures a seat's *yield* and is structurally silent about its *precision*.** The ledger holds only sustained findings — a disproved finding writes no row — so a seat filing thirty findings to land three is indistinguishable here from one filing three. Precision is the axis a seat actually gets questioned on; the seat record below carries it. This is the field's definitional home, and it is stated **also** in the review practice's skill, which the zone wall bars from citing this document — so, like the `sustained` gloss below, a change to it lands at both sites in the same change. [ADR-006:72 "it measures a seat's yield and is structurally silent about its precision"]

- **`ref` is an `https://` URL to the durable surface carrying that review's findings**, required because the row itself holds no description: without it a row proves a defect occurred but never what it was, and a later claim that a class *recurred* cannot be checked at all. [ADR-006:72 "`ref` is a URL to the durable surface carrying that review's findings"] [ADR-006:98 "`ref` is required and must be an `https://` URL"]
  - **It must survive a squash-merge and branch deletion.** A PR, an issue, or a squash-merge commit on the default branch does; a branch SHA may not. [ADR-006:72 "It must **survive a squash-merge and branch deletion**"]
  - **It must name findings individually.** A surface reporting only counts, severities and the highs does not discharge this field, because a reader arriving from a row still cannot learn what that row was. [ADR-006:72 "it must **name findings individually**"]
  - **Rows whose target does not meet the naming property are a recorded debt, not a passing grade.** The remedy is the review practice recording per-finding descriptions on the durable surface. It is **not** re-aiming a row at whichever surface reads richer: a row resolves by its `id`, and prose describing findings without naming them cannot answer what a given row was — the only question a reader arriving from a row is asking. [ADR-006:72 "That is a recorded debt, not a passing grade"]

- **The `artifact` vocabulary — what kind of material the defect sits in — is `constitution | repo-docs | work-prose | skill-prose | script | lint | tests | ci | packaging`.** [D-59]
  - **`constitution` denotes all three constitutional surfaces: this statute, the decision log, and the frozen ADR preamble (§12).** The value names a *kind* of material and not a file — constitutional material, one audience, one set of rules — and which surface a given defect sat in is recoverable from the row's `ref`. One value rather than three keeps every row written before the surfaces split still true, where minting values would leave each of them ambiguous between the statute and the record its rules came from. [D-53]
  - **`work-prose` denotes prose the constitution requires of a unit of work and homes outside the tree** — a framing, design or plan artifact on an issue, an affirmation record, a pull-request body, a review's report. **The test is where the material lives, not which position it sits at**: a value covering material on both sides of that line would carry two denotations at once, which is why widening `repo-docs` was rejected. One value covers every such surface rather than one value per surface, because the vocabulary grows only where a query wants a value and none yet distinguishes these surfaces from one another; enumerating them one at a time is what leaves the next mandated surface unwritable. [D-59]
  - **A value is added to this vocabulary by a decision entry amending this section**, the same growth rule `found_by`, positions and stages take. A field with no growth rule is a closed vocabulary wearing an open one's clothes; no value is added before a query wants one. [D-53]

- **`introduced` and `catchable` name artifact *positions*; `caught` names the *review stage* that actually found the defect. They are two axes, not one.** [ADR-006:74 "The remaining three fields are two axes, not one"]
  - **The position vocabulary is `framing | design | plan | implementation`, ordered as written, plus `unrecorded`, which is outside that order and is not a position at all.** [ADR-006:74 "`framing | design | plan | implementation`, ordered as written"]
  - **The stage vocabulary is `authoring-review | adversarial-review | post-fix | external | ci | post-merge | consumer | unrecorded`.** [ADR-006:74 "`caught` names the **review stage** that actually found it"]
  - **`introduced` no later than `catchable`, over the four ordered positions only.** [ADR-006:94 "the ordering between the axes (`introduced` no later than `catchable`, over the four ordered positions only)"]

- **A defect whose preventing decision belonged to an earlier position is `introduced` there even when no artifact existed to hold it** — the missing artifact is what made it invisible, not what made it absent. **That rule governs `introduced` alone**; extending it to `catchable` makes the retirement test key on a field forbidden to disagree with its neighbour. [ADR-006:74 "That rule governs `introduced` alone"]
- **`catchable` is a claim about detectability, and it is tested by writing the sentence.** A defect is `catchable` at position P when the sentence the P-artifact would have had to contain to prevent it is writable using only what was knowable at P. A bare "a design pass would have caught this" does not discharge the test; the sentence is constructed, and if constructing it requires knowledge that arrived later, P is not the answer and the test moves downstream. [ADR-006:76 "it is tested by writing the sentence"]
- **The burden attaches to the value, not to the deviation.** Any row whose `catchable` lands upstream — `framing`, `design` or `plan` — must carry the constructed sentence, written out on a durable surface: the review report where the finder produced one, and the row's own `ref` target where it did not. A row whose `catchable` is `implementation` rides on the test alone. [ADR-006:80 "**Any row whose `catchable` lands upstream — `framing`, `design` or `plan` — must carry the constructed sentence**"]
  - **`catchable` still defaults to `introduced` in the sense that no separate judgment is invented for it — but the default buys nothing.** If the defaulted value is upstream, the sentence is owed exactly as if it had been argued for. [ADR-006:80 "the default buys nothing"]

- **The classifier is the finder, not the artifact's author.** A defect's author judging whether their own artifact could have caught it is the interested-party problem; this rule moves the judgment off the party with the strongest interest. [ADR-006:84 "The classifier is the finder, not the artifact's author"]
  - **It is a disclosure, not a guarantee: nothing on the row records the classifier.** `found_by` records who *found* the defect, and this rule constrains who *classified* `catchable`; closing the gap needs a field the ledger does not have, which §2 earns by recurrence. [ADR-006:84 "It does not, however, make the rule checkable"]
  - **Where finder and author coincide, the row says so by `found_by` alone** — `owner` where the maintainer caught their own call, `authoring-review` where an agent caught its own work before any review stage ran. [ADR-006:84 "Where finder and author coincide the row says so by that field alone"]

- **`unrecorded` means one thing: the position was not judged.** It is not a fallback for a defect whose position *was* judged and fits nothing listed — that reading restores the double duty where one value covers both "we looked" and "nobody looked", and the retirement test cannot tell them apart. **A judged position with no lawful value is a signal that the vocabulary is short, and the row waits on the amendment rather than absorbing the gap silently.** [ADR-006:90 "`unrecorded` means one thing: the position was not judged"]
- **A new position or stage is added by a decision entry amending this section, editing the lint's single definition site.** A vocabulary that cannot grow files its consumers under the wrong heading: once a vocabulary is closed, the cheapest lawful move is always to file under an existing heading, and the reasons for doing so will always look good locally. [ADR-006:92 "**A new position or stage is added by amending this section**, editing the lint's single definition site"]
- **Grandfathering is stated in `source` values, never in dates.** `date` is a day, and this instrument's boundaries fall inside single days with rows on both sides of them; a grandfathering rule stated in days is one the ledger cannot evaluate. `source` names the review event and is unique by contract, so it separates what `date` cannot. [ADR-006:88 "The boundary is `source`, not `date`"]
  - **Every row already in the corpus is left as it stands.** Re-judging a closed corpus against a new test, by the same parties, is the interested-party problem operating retroactively and at scale. [ADR-006:86 "every row already in the corpus is left as it stands"]
  - **Rows carrying one of these `source` values read `unrecorded` for both positions**, on the checkable ground that the vocabulary in force could express no position at all: `full-adversarial-review-2026-08-15`, `pr2-panel-review-2026-08-15`, `pr2-postfix-2026-08-15`, `pr3-panel-review-2026-08-15`, `pr3-postfix-2026-08-16`, `pr4-panel-review-2026-08-16`, `pr4-standard-2026-08-16`, `pr4-delta-panel-2026-08-16`. [ADR-006:86 "Rows carrying a `source` that predates the split read `unrecorded` for both positions"]
  - **Rows carrying one of these `source` values keep `catchable` as the pre-detectability rule set it, and are read as introduction positions**: `pr6-panel-review-2026-08-16`, `pr6-owner-2026-08-16`, `pr6-postfix-2026-08-16`, `pr6-external-2026-08-16`, `pr8-routine-2026-08-16`, `pr8-postfix-2026-08-16`. [ADR-006:86 "rows whose `source` falls between the split and this amendment keep `catchable` as the old rule set it"]
  - **Every `source` not named in the two lists above is written under the detectability test and its burden rule.** [ADR-006:86 "This test binds on rows written from this amendment forward"]

- **What a row records when the catch arrives outside a review event.** `found_by: owner`, `ci` and `consumer` name finders that produce no review report. Such a row takes `source` as the event that surfaced it — a conversation, a CI run, a consumer report — named as durably as the surface allows, and `ref` pointing at whatever record exists. Because `ref` is required and must be an `https://` URL, a catch with no durable record cannot be written until one is made: post a comment naming the finding and point at that. **The row is never dropped for want of a surface; the surface is created**, because a catch left uncounted is the failure this field exists to prevent. [ADR-006:98 "What a row records when the catch arrives outside a review event"]
- **A row may carry `trial`, an optional lowercase token naming the trial whose evidence it is (§2).** It is optional — absent means the row is not a trial's evidence, which is almost every row — and **`SELECT DISTINCT trial` enumerates the trials whose instrument is the ledger**, so a session can find them without knowing a name in advance; a value that can only be queried by someone who already knows it attributes nothing. **It does not enumerate them all**, because §2's property 6 admits an instrument that is not the ledger; property 2's marker is what reaches every live trial, and this query answers a narrower question than a reader arriving here would assume. [D-69]
- **A run that found nothing is recorded outside the ledger, and that is a design decision rather than a shortfall.** A seat that finds nothing writes no rows, so *ran and found nothing* and *never ran* are one silence in a defect ledger. The distinguishing fact is per *run*, not per *defect*, and no defect ledger can carry it without admitting non-defect rows and becoming a different instrument. [ADR-006:106 "A run that found nothing is recorded outside this file"]
- **The seat record's home is `docs/seat-record.jsonl`, one JSON object per line, with required fields `{source, date, seat, model, runtime, lane, raw, merged, sustained, status, isolated}` and the same optional `trial`.** [ADR-006:106 "**`docs/seat-record.jsonl`**, one JSON object per line, required fields"]
  - **`status` is `ran | clean | failed`**: `clean` ran and found nothing, `failed` was still unusable after its one re-dispatch, and both carry zeros — without `status` they are the same row. [ADR-006:106 "`status` is `ran | clean | failed`"]
  - **`lane` is `panel | routine`.** [ADR-006:106 "`lane` is `panel | routine`"]
  - **`isolated` is a boolean recording whether the seat got its own worktree.** [ADR-006:106 "`isolated` is a boolean recording whether the seat got its own worktree"]
  - **`raw` is what the seat filed, `merged` what survived deduplication, `sustained` what the terminal stage kept — a priced-out finding included**, since the stage affirmed it as real and this field measures whether a seat's findings were real, not whether they were fixed. [ADR-006:106 "a priced-out finding included"]
  - **A merged finding credits every finder, so per-seat sums may exceed the merged list, by design.** [ADR-006:106 "a merged finding credits **every** finder"]
  - **The `sustained` gloss and the yield-versus-precision claim above are each stated here *and* in the review practice's skill on purpose, and a change to either lands at both sites in the same change.** The unqualified gloss is what a reader consults to learn what the field counts, and leaving the qualification at one of two definitional sites is the twin-site class this instrument has already committed once. [ADR-006:106 "leaving it at one of two definitional sites is the twin-site class"]

- **Every panel and every routine review writes a seat record.** [ADR-006:94 "the requirement that every panel and routine review *writes* a seat record at all"]
- **The two files are joined by `source`, and that join is a hint and not a key.** `source` granularity is per stage and rows are booked inconsistently against it — some reviews book defense-found rows under the panel's own `source`, others under a dedicated one — so a query may rely on the join for navigation and never for completeness. [ADR-006:108 "**That join is a hint and not a key today**"]
- **The entry skeleton and the citation tokens are hand-written and validated by `tools/check_constitution.py` under the same interim waiver, until the core library exists to own emit/validate.** They are formats created by this decision, and a format shipping without its validator is §2's day-one-code exception taken rather than discharged. [D-53]
- **Rows in both files are validated by the packaging lint until the core library exists to own emit/validate.** This is §2's boundary-format rule discharged in its smallest honest form. **A format therefore ships with its check rather than owing one**: a block claiming this waiver without a check is the day-one-code exception being taken rather than discharged, and this waiver *is* the lint. [ADR-006:110 "validated by the packaging lint until the core library exists to own emit/validate"]
- **The retirement test: what buys a standing pre-implementation review is `catchable` at an upstream position — `framing`, `design` or `plan` — recurring in the ledger; its absence is what retires one.** [ADR-006:112 "What buys a standing pre-implementation review"]
  - **A change to this test is recorded as a change to the test, including a change that leaves its wording untouched while moving what it selects.** A governing sentence rewritten in passing is the failure the record exists to prevent, and the case the record has actually missed is the mirror one — the sentence standing still while the term it selects on changes meaning. [ADR-006:118 "A reader diffing this section would see the test as untouched"]

- **The rules of §7 and §8 that no guard enforces are listed here, and the list is exhaustive over both sections.** A rule the lint does not carry must say so, or a reader assumes CI is holding a line that nothing holds; and every such rule appears under exactly one verdict. [ADR-006:94 "named together so a reader can tell which of §5's MUSTs the lint carries"]
  - **Unenforceable in principle**, and a reader waiting for a guard here is waiting for one that can never arrive: `catchable`'s detectability test, which asks what was knowable at a position — a judgment about the defect's content and not a fact any JSON row carries; and §7's routing rule, whose defect is an absence that leaves no trace to inspect. [ADR-006:96 "**One is unenforceable in principle**"] [ADR-006:71 "this is the permanent kind rather than the not-yet kind"]
  - **Unenforced for now** — new semantic rules with no incident record, and §2 earns code by recurrence, not by plausibility: the ordering between the axes; both `ref` properties (survives squash-merge and branch deletion; names findings individually); the correspondence between this statute's vocabularies and the lint's sets, which no check reads; the burden rule's constructed-sentence requirement; the finder-classifies rule; `found_by`'s growth rule; the requirement that a seat record is written at all; §7's filing precondition; §7's price-statement requirement on a cost-taken drop; and §7's `owner-pending` resurfacing and re-disposition duty. [ADR-006:96 "Nine are unenforced *for now*"]
  - **Two of those sit in the unenforced-for-now class on narrow grounds.** The burden rule is checkable in the negative — its trigger is `catchable` landing upstream, pure set membership on one field — but only where the sentence is on the `ref` surface; where it is permitted to live in a finder's report instead, `ref` must point at that report for a check to reach it. The finder-classifies rule is checkable only where authorship is recorded, and no field on the row records it. [ADR-006:96 "Two sit in the first class on narrow grounds worth stating"]

## 9. Cross-runtime and substrate

- **The practice is runtime-neutral by construction.** Skills are written tool-agnostically; anything Claude- or Codex-specific lives in a declared adapter surface at the composition layer, never in a skill body — which keeps that surface small and enumerable, so "does this work on Codex?" is a checklist rather than an investigation. [ADR-007:15] [ADR-007:27]
- **State on GitHub is the interchange layer.** Either runtime resumes any work from the durable record, which is possible only because nothing session-local is load-bearing (§10). [ADR-007:16]
- **Root instructions have one canonical home.** `AGENTS.md` is canonical because Codex reads it natively; `CLAUDE.md` is a pointer to it, never a fork of it. The doctrine budget (§3) applies to the canonical file. [ADR-007:17]
- **CI runs on both operating systems.** Tests run on Linux and Windows in Actions. [ADR-007:18]
- **The Linux leg is a proxy for Codex compatibility, not a Codex check.** Codex sandboxes are Linux with default Python, so the Linux leg stands in for them; a true Codex check means the suite exercised by a Codex agent with a defined failure condition, and Codex compatibility is never asserted beyond what is actually exercised. Such a check is pulled into existence when real Codex work arrives, under §11. [ADR-007:18]
- **The substrate is Python.** The core library and skill scripts use Python: present by default in Codex sandboxes, no build step, cross-platform, and the substrate models write with the fewest silent-corruption traps. [ADR-007:22]
- **PowerShell is rejected for new code.** It carries Windows-shaped assumptions, a long catalog of silent-corruption traps, and friction against Linux sandboxes. [ADR-007:22] [ADR-007:11]
## 10. Memory

- **The repo is the system of record for everything learned.** A lesson's durable home is one the material lifecycle (§2) already defines: prose in the skill it improves, carrying its exhibit; a code guard where it has recurred; a statute amendment with its decision entry where the lesson is constitutional; or a repo-only doc where it is maintainer-facing. **There is no fifth home called "memory."** [ADR-008:17]
- **Vendor memory is an inbox, never an archive.** Claude's auto-memory, Codex's equivalents, and any session-local store are capture surfaces — cheap places to note a lesson mid-task — and **no session-local or vendor-store state is load-bearing across sessions, machines or runtimes.** The wipe test defines the boundary: wiping every vendor store must cost convenience, never knowledge. Anything whose loss would matter has, by definition, not yet exited, and that is the defect to fix. [ADR-008:18] [ADR-007:16]
- **Landing is same-session by default.** The session that learns a lesson lands it in its home before ending — the memory analog of fixing a finding in the pull request that found it — because the context that makes the lesson writable exists only in that session. Capture-without-landing is the exception, for lessons that genuinely cannot land now (mid-dispatch, or the home is contested), and the capture note states its intended home. [ADR-008:19]
- **The inbox gets no meta-governance.** No admission contracts, no ledgers of the store's own history, no sweep machinery — governance of a pool is what the expensive-exits failure looks like from the inside. [ADR-008:20]
- **Every inbox entry carries its capture date, and an entry older than 14 days is lapsed** — landed immediately or deleted, nothing else. Because a capture note invoking the "cannot land now" exception must state its intended home, a lapsed entry with no home named is deleted without debate. [ADR-008:20]
- **If the inbox ever seems to need more governance than this, exits have gotten expensive again — fix the exits, don't govern the pool.** [ADR-008:20]
- **Personal working-style knowledge follows the same rule with a different home.** How the maintainer works and standing preferences land in the user-global instruction files both runtimes read (`~/.codex/AGENTS.md` canonical-style, `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`), or in a repo-only doc where the preference is project-scoped. Whether a project-scoped preference belongs in a public repo's repo-only zone or a private overlay is decided case-by-case at landing time. [ADR-008:22]
## 11. The predecessor

- **Tradecraft is a new project, not a continuation.** The predecessor is studied the way a post-mortem studies a system — its failures are as instructive as its successes, and starting over completely was chosen *because* many of its choices did not hold. [ADR-009:11]
- **Nothing carries by default.** No file, skill, prose, or code is ported. A capability exists in tradecraft only when real work pulls it into existence. [ADR-009:15]
- **Pull-based rewrite.** When work needs something the predecessor addressed, the skill is authored fresh at frontier weight with the predecessor open beside it as reference: its exhibits supply the *why*, its test cases supply the behavioral spec (re-expressed in Python), its design docs supply the rejected alternatives. Each pull is small, immediately exercised by the work that pulled it, and reviewed where the evidence says defects concentrate — the new prose. [ADR-009:16]
- **No presumption of correctness.** The predecessor's way of doing something is a data point, never a default. Every pull extracts the *lesson*, not the artifact, and asks explicitly: did this actually work there, and is the evidence for that success discriminating — or was it merely never falsified? Its failures are first-class evidence with equal standing. [ADR-009:17]
- **Citations over copies.** The predecessor is public; lessons are cited by link — issue, design doc, commit — not transcribed. Copied text drifts; a citation stays attached to its evidence. [ADR-009:18]
- **The predecessor ([agent-orchestra](https://github.com/Grimblaz/agent-orchestra)) is the evidence base: public, citable, and frozen for new practice-level work.** There is no forced cutover date — tradecraft grows exactly as fast as real work migrates. [ADR-009:19]
- **When work encounters something the predecessor solved, pull it into tradecraft properly** rather than keep doing it in the predecessor because the skill already exists there. [ADR-009:19]

## 12. Amending this statute

- **This statute is the always-current rulebook; the nine ADRs are a frozen historical preamble.** A rule's operative text lives here, and the ADR its citation names carries the record of how it was decided — so a reader learns the rule from one place and its provenance from another, rather than reconstructing the rule from a decision log. [D-53]
- **The decision log is `docs/architecture/decisions/`, one file per decision, named `D-<N>-YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md` where `<N>` is the pull request number that lands it.** Pull-request numbers are globally unique and need no allocation coordination, so concurrent branches cannot collide on a name. [D-53]
- **An entry is accepted when it lands on `main`; before merge it is a draft and is freely revisable.** The append-only guard evaluates against the merge base, which produces exactly this lifecycle with no extra state to track. [D-53]
- **An entry's shape is fixed:** `# D-<N>: <title>`, a `**Status:** Accepted YYYY-MM-DD (PR #<N>)` line, `## Context`, `## Decision` opening with either `**Statute delta:** <one sentence>` or the canonical string `**Changes no operative rule.**`, a `**Displaces:** [ADR-NNN:L], …` line naming every frozen rule the decision displaces where any are displaced, `## Rejected`, and `## Evidence`. A fixed shape is what lets a later session find a decision's parts without reading it whole. [D-53]
- **An accepted entry is immutable except for status-line appends of the supersession form below.** Re-authoring a decision after the fact is the interested-party problem operating on the record itself. [D-53]
- **The freeze admits no errata: a frozen ADR's text stands as it landed, typos included.** It is a historical record, and correcting a record is how a record stops being one; the cosmetic triad's below-floor ruling reaches the statute, never the preamble. [D-53]
- **Reversal of a landed decision is by superseding entry, never by revert.** This repository squash-merges and the log is append-only, so landing is one-way; that is stated here rather than discovered by a session trying to revert one. [D-53]
- **A frozen ADR's only lawful diff is a status-line append of the fixed form** `· Superseded in part by [D-N] (YYYY-MM-DD): ADR-NNN:L[, L…] — <one clause>`. An ADR superseded in whole is expressed by naming all of its rule lines, at once or over time. [D-53]
- **When a decision displaces a frozen rule, that append is obligatory in the same pull request**, and it is cross-checked against the entry's `Displaces:` field. Without it every arrival path — `AGENTS.md`'s citations, a skill's references, a search that lands in an ADR — presents stale law by default from the first amendment. [D-53]
- **A statute rule is a bold-led markdown list item at any depth, ending with its citation token, and a rule is identified by its bold lead-in.** The statute contains nothing but section headings and rule units; content outside that shape exits the citation regime silently, which is why the shape is the rule's unit and not merely its style. Depth carries no meaning — a nested rule is a rule — because an exemption written for indentation is an exemption from every check. [D-53]
- **A rule's citation is `[ADR-NNN:L]` for pre-split provenance and `[D-N]` thereafter.** File-and-line resolves against every ADR including the unnumbered ones and is stable because the files are frozen. Where a source line carries several claims, the citation may append a quoted fragment — `[ADR-006:48 "no clone seats"]` — as a reader's disambiguator. [D-53]
- **An above-floor change to this statute follows §6 unchanged** — issue, pre-implementation artifact, convergence gate, affirmation record before the first commit. The floor judges the *rule change*; the entry is its bookkeeping and adds no second gate. [D-53]
- **The amending pull request carries the statute edit, its `D-<PR#>` entry, every obligatory supersession pointer, and any lint rule enforcing the old text** (§4). One pull request carries the whole change, so no part of it can land without the others. [D-53]
- **A cosmetic change to a rule's body — a typo, a dead link, or a rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical — writes no entry; every other statute change writes one, below the floor or not.** A change to a rule's **bold lead-in** is not cosmetic: the lead-in is the rule's identity, and moving it reads as a rule removed and another added. A rewording that legitimately moves a citation declares the displacement in its entry like any other. **Recording and gating are independent questions**: being below the floor discharges a gate and never discharges the record, so a below-floor non-cosmetic change writes an entry opening `**Changes no operative rule.**` [D-53]
- **Non-rule constitutional content — an open ruling, an open question — lives in `docs/architecture/open-questions.md`.** It binds nobody, so it has no rule to live in here, and a live section inside the frozen preamble cannot be told from dead law. [D-53]
- **CI checks form, position and existence; review checks correspondence.** The guards check entry filenames, the entry's required-field skeleton, status-line pointer form, citation tokens and their targets, the displacement cross-checks, and the statute's rule-unit shape. Whether an entry's content describes the change, whether a `Changes no operative rule` declaration is true, and whether meaning was preserved are review's, and no guard is claimed to reach them. [D-53]
