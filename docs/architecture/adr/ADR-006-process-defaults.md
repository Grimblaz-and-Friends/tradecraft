## Context

The predecessor started heavy and spent years earning its way lighter: a five-stage pipeline as the entrance, a five-pass review panel, and filing-by-default that turned every unit of work into two or three tracked follow-ups (324 open issues; 40% untouched for 90+ days). Its own measurements pointed the other way: the widest review panel once sustained a *false* finding, while re-running fixes cheaply and reliably caught real defects the fixes themselves introduced (the per-cycle records are in evidence.md); the instrument for right-sizing process (a phase-containment ledger recording where defects were introduced vs caught) was the right idea, added years too late to shape the defaults.

## Decision

Invert the predecessor's defaults; keep its instrument from day one.

1. **The minimal lane is the entrance.** One conversation takes work from intent to done. Heavier lanes (staged design, deeper review, unattended runs) exist and are opt-in — chosen for cause, not defaulted into.
2. **Review attaches to artifacts, not only to code.** A framing, a design, a plan, and an implementation are each reviewable artifacts, and a defect is cheapest caught at the artifact where it was introduced — a design flaw found in the design costs a conversation; the same flaw found in the implementation costs the implementation. So escalation has **two axes**: *depth* (a wider or stronger review of one artifact) and **position** (reviewing an earlier artifact before building on it). When risk justifies exactly one escalation, position usually beats depth. In the minimal lane this costs almost nothing: the pre-implementation artifact may be a stated approach a paragraph long, and its review *is* the convergence gate (ADR-005) — the check always runs; what's opt-in is a dedicated adversarial challenge of the design or plan before implementation begins.

   **The convergence gate's procedure**, written here because it was settled in [#5](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/5), piloted end to end in [PR #6](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/6), and then present in **no tracked file at all** — `git grep -i affirm` returned nothing across the tree. ADR-008 requires a lesson to land in its repo home the same session. Both the settling and the pilot happened on 2026-08-16, and the session that did both ended without landing it — same-session is the standard ADR-008 sets, and it is the one that was missed, which is the failure this section had been committing about itself while prescribing it to others.

   - **It fires before the first line of implementation**, on any change carrying a pre-implementation artifact. What the owner uniquely decides is ADR-005's convergence question — *is this the approach?* — and the artifact is affirmed, amended, or rejected as a whole. Nothing below the trivial floor reaches it — **it**, this gate, and no other: framing has no procedure, and the release gate is never skipped at any size. Being below the floor is never a reason to skip a gate ADR-005 still has firing.

   **The floor is a property of the change, not of its diff: a change is below it when it makes no decision** — it neither states, alters, nor removes a rule, **nor introduces a mechanism or changes one's surface** — and a reader of the diff has nothing to affirm or reject. **A mechanism's surface is what another party relies on to use it, and that is not only its signature.** ADR-002 names what must stay fixed — *"a fixed name, shape, callable surface, or coordination point another party has to be able to find"* — and gives findability as the reason, which is a claim about what another party depends on. **This section rules, for its own floor, that the test is reliance:** a change is a surface change when a party using the mechanism correctly would now have to use it differently, or would get a different answer to the question it is called for. A script that refuses what it used to accept, a skill that gains a step its consumer must run, a gate procedure whose trigger moves — all above. Nothing a caller does or relies on moves — an internal rewrite, a clearer message for the same verdict, a fix to a case that never worked — and it stays below. **The question is asked of the change you intend, before it is written**, which is why it is reliance and not behaviour: whether a rewrite will in fact preserve behaviour is unknowable at the moment the floor is called, and whether you intend to move what callers rely on is not. Both halves of ADR-002's boundary are named on purpose: a mechanism is the one material that reaches the owner by admission rather than by incident, so a test naming only rules would route it around the gate it most needs.

   **The unit is the pull request measured against its merge base**, the unit `tools/check_version_bump.py` already states and gives its reason for; a per-commit reading lets the same change answer differently by when it is split.

   **Commitment's question does not fire below the floor; its check still runs.** The answer is covered by a recorded decision — this ruling — under ADR-005's ordinary gate-vs-question machinery, the same machinery that answers commitment early where an affirmed artifact states scope, and **not** its cut test, which removes a gate from the set and would contradict ADR-005's own *"scoped, not deleted."* The reason is the floor's own definition: a change below it leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject, and nothing to affirm or reject is nothing to bet on, so commitment has nothing it uniquely decides. That also answers the genuine-bet case, and answers it from the definition rather than from a carve-out: a change carrying a bet **has** something to affirm or reject and is above the floor by that fact — doubtful cases with it, since the floor fails closed — so the below-floor genuine bet is empty by construction. The session announces the conclusion, names this ruling as the decision covering it, and proceeds, **in the pull-request body beside the floor declaration this section already requires**; the owner's standing override to invoke commitment anyway is preserved below the floor as above it, and is exercised in conversation. Above the floor, commitment follows ADR-005 unchanged. **The competing reading is recorded rather than deleted, because it is the one a careful reader reaches first**: ADR-005 fires commitment *"where the artifact states no scope,"* and a below-floor change need carry no artifact — arguably the limiting case of stating none. What decides against it is the clause's own scope: it governs artifacts that *exist and omit scope* — its parenthetical is §2 permitting *"a stated approach a paragraph long,"* a thin artifact, not an absent one. Where a below-floor change carries a small reversible scope choice, ADR-005's third answer governs it in full: *"Confirmation of the predictable — do not ask. Act and report if reversible; if not, batch into an argued-case gate (per-item recommendation with reasoning; the human's approve/modify/drop is the interaction)"* — and the report of such a choice is routed by §4's disposition rule, never left in conversation. Sustained as `D1` on [PR #32](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/32), the first change in this repo to be below the floor **and** in a governing file.

   Size is not the test and cannot be: [PR #8](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/8) was +11/−0 across three files, the smallest **pull request** in this repo's history, and created a rule every later pull-request body must satisfy. Ten commits reached `main` before the ruleset required pull requests on 2026-08-15; **seven of the eight not named here sit on the never-below list** — four amended the constitution, one created `AGENTS.md`, the lint and CI, one touched eight ADRs and rewrote the lint. Two of the ten are below-floor exemplars: [`225e824`](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/commit/225e824) bumping CI actions off deprecated Node majors, safe because `python-version` is pinned so no guard's accept/reject set moved, and [`b98b569`](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/commit/b98b569) untracking `__pycache__`. Even the ungated era was overwhelmingly above-floor, which is the honest shape of the evidence and a narrower base than a taxonomy could rest on.

   **Never below the floor, regardless of size:** anything that creates, changes or retires a rule; anything that introduces a mechanism or changes one's surface; and anything that changes what a guard accepts or rejects — the guards being `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py`, and the CI workflow that invokes them, the ledger's *schema* included, since the lint validates its vocabularies. **`AGENTS.md` and the constitution are *presumed* above it** — every session reads the one and is governed by the other — **and the presumption is rebutted only in the canonical form stated below**: a typo, a dead link, or a rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical is below the floor there as anywhere. **Silence does not rebut it.** *(Appending rows to the ledger is below by this test — it states no rule and changes no guard — so the ordinary output of every review needs no ceremony; a row carrying a `found_by` value §5 has not admitted is not an append but an amendment, and is above. Owner ruling, 2026-08-16.)*

   **It fails closed: a session that cannot tell is above the floor.** A wrongly-skipped gate costs an unbounded amount; a wrongly-run one costs the artifact — a paragraph, in the minimal lane — **plus a blocking round-trip to the owner**, which is the resource ADR-005 exists to conserve, so the direction is chosen knowing it is not free.

   **The declaration has a surface, and it is the pull request body.** The session declares its reading there — above the floor, or below it with the rule the change leaves untouched named; **in `AGENTS.md` or the constitution, where the presumption attaches, that naming takes the canonical form below.** The owner can overrule. Above the floor the affirmation record carries it too. This is stated because the affirmation record defined below exists only where there is an artifact, so routing a *below*-floor declaration into it would give the declaration a home for the one case that does not need it and none for the case the floor creates; and because a declaration with no recording surface is indistinguishable afterward from having skipped the gate, which is the whole failure being prevented. Stating the floor makes the judgment cheaper, never unnecessary. [#11](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/11) stays open for the framing gate and for commitment's procedure in the lanes where it is retained; its pause-discipline item was discharged 2026-08-17, when the shipped skills were given their pause discipline in ADR-005's vocabulary ([#25](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/25)). Above the floor, work that has no issue **files one**: §4 already names an issue as the artifact's home and pre-emptively blesses filing for a unit of work, so this instructs what that section permits.

   **The rebuttal has a canonical form**, and the pull-request body carries one of the two statements below. The clause above was singular — *which rule* — and supplied no shape, and the case it meets most often has **no principled answer**: a typo leaves *every* rule untouched, so naming one is writable but arbitrary, while *no rule in this document is touched* is an absence claim about a whole document, which this practice's own standard — [`skills/adversarial-review`](../../../skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md) — discharges only by reading to the end of it. **One rebuttal has been accepted on the record** ([PR #32](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/32)), and it is findable only by knowing to look for it; two further below-floor declarations on files the presumption does not reach ([PR #29](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/29), [PR #47](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/47)) each invented their own shape under the same singular ask at the paragraph above, which is the drift this form ends. **The competing reading is recorded rather than deleted**, per this section's own practice: the post-colon triad ruling above can be read as itself disposing of the triad, in which case the cosmetic statement restates a ruling already made rather than relaxing one. On either reading the floor's extension is unchanged; what moves is the discharge.

   **Targeted**, where the change sits in a rule's neighbourhood: *"This change edits `<file(s)>`, where a reader would expect it to move `<the rule — quote the sentence that states it, not the paragraph around it, one quote per file named>`; it leaves that rule untouched because `<reason>`. It creates, changes or retires no other rule, introduces no mechanism and changes no mechanism's surface, does not change **what** `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py` or CI accepts or rejects, and leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject."*

   **Cosmetic**, where the change is a typo, a dead link, or a rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical: *"This change is a `<typo | dead link | rewording a reader can check is meaning-identical>` in `<file>`: `<what changed>`. It changes no rule's meaning, introduces no mechanism and changes no mechanism's surface, does not change **what** `tools/lint.py`, `tools/check_version_bump.py` or CI accepts or rejects, and leaves a reader nothing to affirm or reject."*

   **Both sentences of the chosen statement are required, and the words bind rather than the typography** — a session that writes only the first has dropped the never-below walk, which is the half that does the work. Where a pull request is **both** kinds it is Targeted, whose second sentence is a universal over the whole change and covers the cosmetic part; the floor fails closed and that is the stronger branch. Targeted names the rule the change **might have been thought to touch** — the weaker, honest claim — and Cosmetic exists because that naming has no principled answer where every rule is untouched. **Neither statement moves what is below the floor**: the never-below list and the triad ruling above are unchanged, and the owner overrules either at release.
   - **The artifact lives on the work's issue**, as a comment. This replaces the branch file [PR #6](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/6) piloted, which had two holes its own record named: multi-PR work breaks the "empty by construction" property for the whole interval, and the artifact's identity dies with the branch. An issue closes both, and it was excluded from the original choice by being ruled out rather than rejected (§4). The predecessor's 377-line comment protocol is not inherited with it: that cost bought a machine-parsed versioned record, and this artifact is written once, read by people and agents, and parsed by nothing. **A revision is a new comment, latest wins** — comment edits are invisible where the thread is read, and append-only ordering needs no reconciler.
   - **Its shape is weighted to acceptance criteria and the boundary statement.** Criteria state what a nonconforming run looks like, so silence cannot pass as conformance; the **boundary statement** is the explicit list of what the work is *not* doing and why, which is what makes a later scope question answerable from the artifact instead of from memory. The weighting comes from #5's experiment — of four clean catches, three came from criteria and one from the boundary, and nothing was caught by exploring options — **and that experiment is 8 rows, pre-selected and judged by one author, with 3 partials and 1 miss beside the 4**. It is enough to aim the shape and not enough to mandate a proportion; §5's disclaimer of it governs here too. Rejected options take one of the three exits [#5's investigation record](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/5#issuecomment-5305941143) settled — into a rule's prose as its *reason*, as an explicit falsifier that can expire, or the immutable PR snapshot — and never accumulate a maintained corpus.
   - **The affirmation is given in conversation; the durable record is written by the session.** The owner has ruled that approvals are not given on GitHub (2026-08-16), and the mechanism this bullet first carried was independently false anyway: it claimed the owner's reply bore "a timestamp the session cannot author", while the session authenticates as the owner (`gh api user`) and can post that reply itself. So the record is a comment **the session writes to the issue before the first commit**, naming the affirmed artifact comment by URL, stating that affirmation was conversational, and dated. **What that record buys is ordering, not authenticity** — its server-side timestamp precedes the first commit and stays checkable from the thread; it attests that the owner agreed and cannot prove it. The residual risk is named rather than mechanised: a session that fabricates an affirmation is undetectable from the record, and the backstop is not a new mechanism but the **release gate**, which is already designed-in, already the owner's, and is where a fabricated affirmation meets the person it was fabricated about. Naming the artifact comment is what binds the record to a *revision*: without it, a thread that affirms, revises, and implements passes the ordering check with the current artifact unaffirmed.
3. **Review default at any position, scaled by the artifact's weight.** For routine changes: one adversarial pass that reads the artifact **cold** — before any prior findings, self-review, or earlier ledgers are exposed to it — then an evidence-bound defense, then mandatory re-validation of fixes; there is no judge, and defense's evidence verdicts terminate. For substantial artifacts (newly written foundational prose, new scripts, high blast radius — and when in doubt, this lane, since going below the default is bought only by ledger evidence): a **differentiated panel of four seats is the default, not an escalation** — the roster, its staffing, and its canonical seat names live in the practice's skill, [skills/adversarial-review](../../../skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md) — then defense, then a judge whose job is independent re-execution of contested evidence, severity settling, and **weighing each remedy's value against its price** (a separate judge is earned by a panel-width ledger). Every review records which lane was chosen, and why, in its review report, so the classification itself stays auditable. This inverts this section's first version, which made one pass the default everywhere: the seat-level mining of the predecessor's review record ([issue #1](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1), 2026-08-15) overturned it — across the six review rounds where per-seat attribution survives (116 sustained findings), a single pass kept 12% of the sustained yield and lost nearly every critical and high, the *best* single seat chosen with hindsight still kept only 46%, seats' unique yields barely overlapped, and no seat was consistently strongest (the best seat's identity changed nearly every round). The same rounds set the width: the best three seats retained roughly 75–100% by round, the fourth seat's marginal yield stayed real (up to a fifth of a round's sustained findings, at times including highs), and the fifth's went thin — hence four, wider only by declared risk. Three bounds keep the panel honest: **no clone seats** — a seat is added for a lens or vantage the panel lacks, not for a second pass at one it has. Stated honestly this is a judgment rather than a measurement: the predecessor's same-lens pair did sole-find real defects, so redundancy is not worthless; differentiation is simply what the seat record measures, and concurrence between passes has amplified a false finding — in the recorded instance across two tiers, which is the weaker case and still held. Revisable by `found_by` evidence in either direction; **every finding's finder is recorded** (`found_by`, §5) so seats retire by ledger evidence — the instrument the predecessor never had, which is why its seat question stayed unanswerable across its whole measured corpus (~850 entries at mining, session-measured). **That instrument measures one axis of two, and the sentence above claimed both until 2026-08-16.** The ledger holds only sustained findings — a disproved finding writes no row — so `found_by` measures a seat's *yield* and is structurally silent about its *precision*: a seat filing thirty findings to land three reads identically to one filing three. Every seat this repo has been asked to reconsider was questioned on the axis the ledger cannot see, and the retirement promise was therefore unkeepable in exactly the case it gets invoked. The **seat record** closes it — per-seat `raw`, `merged` and `sustained` per review, in one fixed shape, joined to the ledger by `source`; its contract lives in the practice's skill with the roster and its rows live in this repo's seat-record file, named in this section. Recorded as a repair to this sentence rather than a new capability, because the gap was stated here as a solved thing for a day; and **recurring defect classes are closed by lens promotion (ADR-002), never by adding seats** — the predecessor's best-documented escape survived fifteen seat-readings across three artifacts plus two post-fix cycles, fell to a differently-positioned lens, and stopped recurring only when a checklist lens was worded to catch its class. **That rule has no exception, and one was drafted, reviewed and cut** — recorded because the attempt is instructive and because a rule stated as absolute should carry the record of the one time it was nearly softened. A "vantage gap" exit was added to license `revision-diff` (§3's fourth seat, below), on the ground that no lens reaches a class when every seat reads HEAD. The panel falsified it against its own admission test: the lens sentence was already written, a briefed seat acted on it, and the prior revision is one command from every seat in a git worktree — so what looked like a vantage the panel lacked was a sentence its dispatches lacked, which is a lens. The seat was kept and the argument dropped; the roster's shape-selection rule admits it without an exception. Two transferable lessons: a mechanism can be **right while its argument is wrong**, and dropping the argument is the cheaper repair; and an exception should be checked against its rule's **trigger** before it is written — this rule fires only when a class *returns*, and the exhibit was a single escape, so nothing had fired ([PR #19](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/19), `J1`).

   **The fourth seat is selected by the artifact's shape**, and this is stated here rather than only in the skill because a session reading the constitution alone would otherwise seat an unconditional four: three seats stand, and the fourth is the claims-and-citations lens on substantially new prose, the revision-comparison lens on an amendment to existing governing prose, **both — at width five — when the artifact is both**, and the claims lens by default when it is neither. The names, charters and exhibits live in the practice's skill, which owns the roster. **The axis that selects is the material, not the seats' relative yield**: the lens moved off the standing set outranks a retained seat on sustained yield in this repo's own ledger, and nothing here ranks them. The both-shapes rule is a rule and not a judgment because the alternative was measured — routing "both" to one seat left roughly eleven hundred words of new prose unchecked on the review that established it. Defense is mandatory at every depth: it is what makes width safe (in the predecessor's record it killed 40+ false or inflated findings and originated 8+ sustained ones, including a critical). Fix re-validation is mandatory at every depth, **executable rather than a re-read** — every reading stage, defense included, showed shared blind spots that only live runs caught — and scoped to **every branch the fix touched**, because the escaped class recurred three times in its own fix's mirror image. Depth and position are escalated per-change by declared risk (wider panels, external fresh-session reviewers), and de-escalated only by ledger evidence — in both directions, weight follows evidence, never cost arguments and never habit.

   **The terminal stage prices what it rules, and that is not the clause above being relaxed.** The sentence before this one governs **which lane runs and how wide** — and it is unchanged, including its asymmetry: escalation is bought by declared risk, de-escalation only by ledger evidence. What is priced here is narrower and downstream: an individual **disposition** (this finding is real; is the remedy worth what it costs?) and the **marginal pass** (is another look worth running?), an axis nothing governed before — which is why the #42 review ran three passes with no stage having chosen three. **The tell is that anything lawfully priced leaves a record** — a priced-out finding writes a ledger row whose `ref`-surface reason states its price (§4), and a stop-or-run ruling is written in the review's final report — whereas a cost argument for *skipping* a check produces silence, which is the kind this ADR forbids and will keep forbidding. So the defense states each remedy's price (regression surface and complexity delta, argued rather than scored) and the terminal stage — the judge on panels, the defense on routine reviews — applies a two-clause test: *would acting improve the artifact*, then *is that improvement worth the price*. A finding failing only the second is **sustained and priced out**, never dismissed: it was real, and the record must say so. **Severity and price are two halves and neither absorbs the other** — severity measures harm-if-unfixed, the price measures what the remedy costs. That severity sentence is **newly stated here**: this repo had named severity's values since day one and never its meaning, and clause (b) is what makes the omission load-bearing. The procedure and its exhibits live in the practice's skill.

   **Every review ends with a next-pass ruling — run or stop, each polarity carrying its price — written in the review's final report, and the default is to stop once a pass has sustained no high it will fix.** **It governs every look past the mandatory floor**: one prosecution look at every fix batch is owed and can never be priced away, and every look after that — the next one in a fix cycle as much as a further whole pass — is ruled on and priced. The cycle is deliberately inside its reach, because the cycle is where a review's unbounded time goes: its former exit, *a clean look ends the cycle*, was an asymptote wearing a termination condition's clothes, and this repo's record shows it never once fired — thirty seat rows, all `ran`, none `clean`; ten post-fix looks, all of them finding something. The operand is not bare `sustained`: a priced-out high is sustained and by construction unfixed, so a default reading it could never fire on the artifact that produced one. The mandate rests on an **owner ruling** (2026-08-18) rather than on measurement: this is risk appetite, ADR-005's first answer, and the owner may set it. What is *not* claimed is a measured threshold. The decay prior — roughly a one-third drop per pass — is **owner-supplied and recollection-grade**, one illustrative input to a continuation argument and never a yardstick a rule rests on ([registry](../evidence.md), which also carries the measured curves). Those curves are read with their widths attached: the #42 design review ran 9 sustained highs at a four-seat panel with a judge, then 4 sustained highs at a one-seat post-fix cycle, then 1 at a one-seat look with defense waived ([#42](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/42)) — two distinct widths and three terminal filters, so the three numbers are not one measurement, and in items rather than highs the same passes read 39 → 21 → 15, which decays more slowly. The ledger's own panel→post-fix pairs are the wider base — decaying ones (pr19 6→2, pr6 5→1, pr3 4→1) and non-decaying ones (pr30 0→0, pr13 0→1) — and their count is left to the registry's query rather than stated here, since it grows every time a review writes its own rows. **That review's third pass ran to completion**; what the owner's recorded direction stopped was a fourth, in the convergence declaration that closed it. The loop that fixes the prior is the ruling itself: each report records that pass's yield in a stated unit, so curves accumulate at no cost and the default is revised by citing them. This is the newest place in this section where a default arrives ahead of its evidence — §3 has others, recorded as judgments — and it is written that way on purpose: the alternative on offer was an unpriced status quo whose cost is already on the record.

   **For an implementation artifact, the review runs on the pull request, and running it is never a question.** Publish the branch and open the PR *first*, then run the review, then reconcile whatever automated reviewers posted while it ran. The ordering is the point: they start within seconds of the PR opening, so publishing first converts the external pass from a serial cost into a concurrent one. Scope, stated because the unqualified form of this paragraph collapsed two things it should not: **this sequence is about artifacts that have a branch and a PR.** The upstream positions §2 makes reviewable — a framing, a design, a plan — have neither, and their review is the convergence gate, which is a human gate and does ask. Nothing here reaches them. Within this sequence, **running the review is a check, not a gate** (ADR-005): approving the work approves its review, and an agent that finishes a change and hands the review back as a command for the human to run has invented a gate the charter does not have. One seam inside it does lawfully ask — §4's opening ledger read batches any open `owner-pending` rows into a single argued question — and that question is not suppressed to keep the sequence tidy. The release gate is untouched: the sequence ends at a reviewed PR, and merging remains the human's.

   **The pull request body states what it closes.** `Closes #N` for every issue the change discharges, **or one line saying it closes none and why**. The second half is the load-bearing one. Closing keywords fire only from the PR body or from a commit landing on the default branch — never from a comment — so an issue whose work has landed otherwise stays open in silence, and *silence and a deliberate no are the same string*. That ambiguity is not hypothetical: [PR #6](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/6) merged with no keyword, and four panel seats, a defense, a judge, a post-fix cycle and an external reconciliation all read that body without being able to notice, because a PR that lawfully closes nothing looks identical to one that forgot. Stated as prose rather than a guard on purpose: not every PR closes an issue, so a check cannot fail hard without an escape hatch, and a warn-only check is the shape that gets ignored — the "closes none, because X" half is what would make a future guard checkable at all, by converting an absence into an assertion. Recurrence is provable rather than arguable: the miss is ledgered (`O4`, `found_by: owner`, `caught: post-merge`).
4. **Filing is the exception — for findings.** **This section governs findings, not work.** A unit of work may hold an issue for the ordinary reasons any tracker exists: it is the durable identity the work is discussed under, the home its pre-implementation artifact can live in (§2), and the thing that survives a branch. Nothing here discourages that, and reading it as "this repo does not file issues" is a misreading that has already cost something — an upstream artifact was designed onto a branch file plus a pull-request snapshot, with known holes at multi-PR work and at branch deletion, because the issue option had been quietly ruled out rather than rejected. The predecessor's failure was **filing findings by default**, two or three per unit of work, until 324 sat open with 40% untouched for 90 days. That is the thing being prevented; a repository with almost no issues is not evidence of health, and this section is not an argument for one.

   A finding's default disposition is *fix it now* or *drop it with a one-line reason*. Its two lawful homes are the change that found it, or a guard (ADR-002 promotion) — a guard fires when the condition recurs; an issue fires never. Proposing a tracked follow-up requires stating why both other homes were rejected, and the proposal must pass the pickup test: would this actually get picked up if time opened tomorrow? A finding that turns on a decision only the owner can make is neither filed nor dropped: it is recorded with disposition `owner-pending` and **resurfaced from the ledger** — every review's opening ledger read batches the open ones into a single argued question at a phase seam (ADR-005), and a row still open after several such surfacings has failed the pickup test in fact, so it is re-dispositioned `recorded` with a one-line reason. The owner's queue lives inside the instrument that already gets read, rather than in a backlog that does not.

   **What `owner-pending` is for, stated because the mechanism reads as a parking space and is not one.** It exists so that work continuing *while the owner is absent* does not lose a decision it cannot make — a holding pen for unattended stretches, not a queue to accumulate in. **When the owner is present and the row comes up, the conversation converts it to a decision;** carrying it forward through a discussion in which it could have been settled is the failure, not the resurfacing. An agent that surfaces such a row owes a recommendation, not a menu. Recorded on the owner's own framing, 2026-08-16, the day the disposition was first used at all — one row, one conversation, converted the same day.

   **Naming a finding in conversation is not a disposition.** Every finding leaves the session through one of the homes above — the change that found it, a guard, a `recorded` row carrying its one-line reason, an `owner-pending` row while the owner is away, or a filed issue with the fix-here and guard homes both rejected — and **the transcript is not one of them, because no stage takes it as input.** A finding written into a reply survives only if someone happens to ask; that is what happened to `T1`, and a thing that depends on someone happening to ask is not a routing. A finding described in a reply is placed where no stage executes, which is `M12` in conversational form: *the new standard was filed where no stage could execute it — the lesson is inert as placed* (`M12`, [PR #3](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/3) — the words are recorded in [issue #5](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/5), not on the pull request, whose review wrote down no finding individually). The obligation sits with **whoever noticed**. Surfacing a finding to the maintainer transfers information, never the routing — the paragraph above already says this of `owner-pending` rows, where an agent that surfaces one owes a recommendation rather than a menu; it holds for every disposition, and this clause is the general case the special one was standing in for.

   **The cheap exit is bound too, and that half is what does the work.** *Drop it with a one-line reason* sits alongside *fix it now* as a default, and it is the disposition that costs least — so once the expensive homes are named as the lawful exits and the cheap one is not, the pressure routes precisely there, and the clause would close three doors while leaving open the one nearest to hand. A drop goes where §5 already puts it: a row with `disposition: recorded`, its reason carried on the row's `ref` surface. Written out rather than left as an inference across two sections, because drawing an inference is the thing a session under time pressure declines to do. **This adds no obligation to find more.** It does not lower the bar for what counts as a finding, and an observation is not a row; §4's opening paragraph governs what filing-by-default costs and is unchanged. What this fixes is the exit, not the entrance — a finding, once surfaced, does not leave by being mentioned.

   **A drop taken on cost states the price, and that is what makes the cheap exit auditable.** §3's two-clause test produces a disposition this section already has homes for: a finding **sustained and priced out** is real, and its row is the `recorded` drop above — **or `filed`, where the remedy is routed to a live pickup home carrying the price**, since pricing a fix out of one change does not assert that nobody will ever act on it, and this section's own filing precondition (both other homes rejected, pickup test passed) still binds. So no new value is added to §5, and none is needed until a query wants one; **an audit of pricing reads both dispositions**, and a rule that named only `recorded` would have excluded the first priced-out finding this repo ever wrote. What the standard adds is the *content* of the one-line reason: it names **what the remedy would have cost**, not merely that the finding was declined, because "declined" is indistinguishable in the record from fatigue and is exactly what this section spent its length preventing on the other exits. **The calibration is the point, and it costs nothing to run**: a priced-out drop that later bites writes its own row — `caught` naming whichever stage re-found it, most often a later `adversarial-review`, and `post-merge` or `consumer` where it escaped that far — so mispriced drops surface as recurrence while well-priced ones age silently, and *are our drops well-priced?* stops being settled by whoever read the last review. **It is not yet a question the ledger can answer on its own**: no field distinguishes a priced-out row from any other `recorded` drop — nor from any other `filed` row, now that both dispositions are lawful for one — and none links a recurrence to the drop it falsifies, so the audit reads the `ref` surfaces of **both** dispositions by hand. **No threshold, field or query ships with this** — a field is code, so it takes ADR-002's recurrence-and-stability road, not the incident road that earns prose.

   **Prose that cannot be enforced in code, and this is the permanent kind rather than the not-yet kind** (the distinction §5's own unenforced list turns on). The defect is an *absence* of routing, visible only against an intent that was never written down: there is no artifact to inspect, no run to fail, and a tree where a session noticed something and said it is indistinguishable from a tree where the session noticed nothing. The clause raises the cost of the omission by naming it and cannot detect it. Its exhibit is [#10](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/10) — two findings narrated in one session and routed nowhere, one of which reached its home only because the maintainer asked whether it had (`T1`, `T2`, `found_by: owner`).
5. **The ledger exists, with a defined shape.** Home: `docs/ledger.jsonl`, one JSON object per line with required fields `{id, date, artifact, severity, introduced, catchable, caught, source, disposition, found_by, ref}`. Field contracts: `date` is an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` day; `source` names the review event that produced the row and `id` is unique within it, both strings, so the pair `(source, id)` is globally unique; `severity` is one of `high | medium | low` — stated here because the lint has enforced that set since day one while this section named the field and never its values, which is the prose-and-code correspondence gap this section warns about, pointing the other way; `disposition` is one of `fixed | reworded | recorded | owner-pending | filed`; `found_by` is a non-empty string naming the seat or stage that first surfaced the defect (a canonical seat name from the skill's roster, `defense`, `judge`, `post-fix`, `external`, `ci`, `owner`, `consumer`, `authoring-review` for a defect its own author caught before any review stage ran, or `unrecorded` for rows predating the field — **and a value is added to this list by amending this section**, the same growth rule positions and stages take, because a field with no growth rule is a closed vocabulary wearing an open one's clothes) — **`owner` is not decoration**: the maintainer has been functioning as an unrecorded finder, and the decisions that reshaped this very ADR arrived that way, so without the value the instrument's denominator silently excludes exactly the catches an upstream review would be substituting for, written as a lowercase name of letters, digits and hyphens so one seat occupies exactly one bucket — a merged finding's row carries its primary (first-surfacing) finder, the review report's merged ledger is where dual credit lives, and this field is what lets a seat be retired or added on evidence instead of argument **on one axis of two: it measures a seat's yield and is structurally silent about its precision**, because a disproved finding writes no row, so a seat filing thirty findings to land three is indistinguishable here from one filing three. Precision is the axis a seat actually gets questioned on, so the promise failed exactly where it was invoked; the seat record defined below carries per-seat raw, merged and sustained counts to close it. Written here, at the field's definitional home, because the first repair of this claim was made in §3 alone and scoped itself to "the sentence above" — leaving this sentence, the one a reader consults to learn what the field buys, stating the unqualified version. That is the twin-site class §3's own review standards name, committed inside the amendment that named it ([PR #19](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/19), `M5`); `ref` is a URL to the durable surface carrying that review's findings — because the row itself holds no description, so without it a row proves a defect occurred but never what it was, and a later claim that a class *recurred* cannot be checked at all. Two properties. The second was **under-specified and unenforced** until 2026-08-16, not absent: the prior text asked for a surface "carrying that review's findings *in full*", which already demanded more than counts and highs — this amendment sharpens those words rather than supplying a property that was never there, and an earlier draft of this sentence claimed otherwise while deleting the very words that carried the weaker form. It must **survive a squash-merge and branch deletion**: a PR, an issue, or a squash-merge commit on the default branch does; a branch SHA may not. And it must **name findings individually** — a surface reporting only counts, severities, and the highs does not discharge this field, because a reader arriving from a row still cannot learn what that row was. **The historical corpus does not meet this property and is not claimed to** — most rows point at a real record of a review that never wrote down what each finding was ([the rate, its bias, and the re-aiming experiment are in the registry](../evidence.md)). That is a recorded debt, not a passing grade, and it is why this property binds reviews from here rather than being claimed as satisfied. The remedy is the review practice recording per-finding descriptions on the durable surface, since counts, severities and the highs are what it records today. It is **not** re-aiming rows at whichever surface reads richer: that was drafted in this amendment and reverted once measured, because a row resolves by its `id`, and prose describing findings without naming them cannot answer what `M25` was — the only question a reader arriving from a row is asking. The artifact vocabulary — what kind of material the defect sits in — is `constitution | repo-docs | skill-prose | script | lint | tests | ci | packaging`.

   **The remaining three fields are two axes, not one.** `introduced` and `catchable` name **artifact positions**: where the defect was made, and the earliest position from which it could have been caught. Their vocabulary is §2's own list of reviewable artifacts — `framing | design | plan | implementation`, ordered as written — plus `unrecorded`, which is **outside that order** and is not a position at all. `caught` names the **review stage** that actually found it: `authoring-review | adversarial-review | post-fix | external | ci | post-merge | consumer | unrecorded`. A defect whose preventing decision belonged to an earlier position is `introduced` there even when no artifact existed to hold it — the missing artifact is what made it invisible, not what made it absent. **That rule governs `introduced` alone.** Until 2026-08-16 it governed both, and the reason given was that `catchable` is the field the retirement test keys on — which made the test key on a field forbidden to disagree with its neighbour. Nine rules keep the axes honest.

   **`catchable` is a claim about detectability, and it is tested by writing the sentence.** A defect is `catchable` at position P when the sentence the P-artifact would have had to contain to prevent it **is writable using only what was knowable at P**. A bare "a design pass would have caught this" does not discharge the test; the sentence is constructed, and if constructing it requires knowledge that arrived later, P is not the answer and the test moves downstream. This is the method [#5's experiment](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/5#issuecomment-5305941143) already ran, promoted from an experiment to the field's definition — the promotion is what it earns; **its 4-of-8 result mandates nothing**, having been run on rows pre-selected and judged by one author, and no threshold, rate or tolerance anywhere in this section rests on it.

   The shape the axis exists to capture is `introduced: design, catchable: implementation` — made early, detectable only once the artifact existed. **No row in the corpus is that shape**, and one appearing is not required for the test to be correct; if none ever does, the axis reads flat and that is a result the instrument can now honestly produce, rather than one its own rules guaranteed.

   **The burden attaches to the value, not to the deviation.** **Any row whose `catchable` lands upstream — `framing`, `design` or `plan` — must carry the constructed sentence**, the product of the test above, written out on a durable surface: the review report where the finder produced one, and the row's own `ref` target where it did not (`owner`, `ci` and `consumer` produce no report, per the rule below). A row whose `catchable` is `implementation` rides on the test alone. `catchable` still defaults to `introduced` in the sense that no separate judgment is *invented* for it — but the default buys nothing: if the defaulted value is upstream, the sentence is owed exactly as if it had been argued for.

   The first draft of this rule burdened deviations, and that burdened nothing. It required argument for an *upstream deviation* — `catchable` earlier than `introduced` — which the ordering property below forbids outright, so the argued class was empty across all sixteen position pairs while all six lawful deviations rode the free path. Worse, the direction that buys process needs no deviation at all: `introduced` is upstream nearly always, so the identity default lands `catchable` upstream for free. Measured at the time: **26 of the 26 rows whose `catchable` was upstream had arrived that way unargued.** Attaching the burden to the value closes both — the test now has to be run on precisely the rows the retirement test counts, which is the only place its answer changes anything.

   **The classifier is the finder, not the artifact's author.** `found_by` already names the finder on every row, so this costs no new field. **It does not, however, make the rule checkable** — an earlier draft of this sentence claimed it did, and that was false: `found_by` records who *found* the defect, and the rule constrains who *classified* `catchable`. Nothing on the row records the classifier, and on this rule's own first application every value was written in one commit by the artifact's author — the party the rule names. Stated as the disclosure it is rather than the guarantee it was drafted as; closing it needs a field the ledger does not have, and ADR-002 earns that by recurrence. A defect's author judging whether their own artifact could have caught it is the interested-party problem [#5](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/5) named as this investigation's weakest point; it is not solved here, only moved off the party with the strongest interest. Where finder and author coincide the row says so by that field alone, and the coincidence is readable rather than hidden: `found_by: owner` where the maintainer caught their own call, and `found_by: authoring-review` where an agent caught its own work before any review stage ran. That second value is added by this amendment because the case had none — the roster seats are barred from the author by the independence rule, `owner` is false for an agent, and `unrecorded` is reserved for rows predating the field — so the honest row was unwritable and the guess would have been silent, the lint accepting any lowercase token.

   **This test binds on rows written from this amendment forward, and every row already in the corpus is left as it stands.** Re-judging a closed corpus against a new test, by the same parties, is the interested-party problem operating retroactively and at scale — and it would manufacture exactly the variance this amendment must not be able to produce on demand. The corpus is a recorded debt, on the precedent `ref`'s second property set in the same section. Rows carrying a `source` that predates the split read `unrecorded` for both positions; rows whose `source` falls between the split and this amendment keep `catchable` as the old rule set it, and are read as introduction positions.

   **The boundary is `source`, not `date`, and the difference is not pedantry.** `date` is a day, and this instrument's whole history to now — the pre-split corpus, the split, and this amendment — happened inside a single day, and the rows carrying that date fall on **both sides of every boundary named above**. A grandfathering rule stated in days is one the ledger cannot evaluate. `source` names the review event and is unique by contract, so it separates what `date` cannot. This is the same defect the retirement test's own record spent three paragraphs on — a governing distinction written in a unit the instrument does not carry — caught here before it landed rather than after.

   **`unrecorded` means one thing: the position was not judged.** It is not a fallback for a defect whose position *was* judged and fits nothing listed — that reading would restore exactly the double duty `authoring` was retired for, where one value covers both "we looked" and "nobody looked" and the retirement test cannot tell them apart. A judged position with no lawful value is a **signal that the vocabulary is short**, and the row waits on the amendment rather than absorbing the gap silently. That is what makes the growth trigger fire instead of rot.

   **A new position or stage is added by amending this section**, editing the lint's single definition site. A vocabulary that cannot grow files its consumers under the wrong heading: the predecessor's token schema declares its *"closed five-value phase enum is **deliberately not extended**"*, and a distinct flow's checkpoints are consequently emitted under another phase's name, with an explicit instruction not to add one ([open-for-work § Gate-decision tokens](https://github.com/Grimblaz/agent-orchestra/blob/main/skills/open-for-work/SKILL.md#gate-decision-tokens)). Stated fairly, that source defends its own mapping as apt and gives a mechanical reason — a new value fails validation before reaching its reconciler. The exhibit is the *cost structure*, not an admission: once a vocabulary is closed, the cheapest lawful move is always to file under an existing heading, and the reasons for doing so will always look good locally.

   **Ten properties of this section are stated and not enforced in code**, named together so a reader can tell which of §5's MUSTs the lint carries: the ordering between the axes (`introduced` no later than `catchable`, over the four ordered positions only); both `ref` properties stated at the top of this section; the correspondence between this prose's vocabularies and the lint's sets, which no check reads; `catchable`'s detectability test; the burden rule's sentence requirement; the finder-classifies rule; `found_by`'s growth rule, which the lint cannot hold because it validates that field by form alone; the requirement that every panel and routine review *writes* a seat record at all, which no check can see because a missing file is indistinguishable from a repo that has none; and §4's both-homes-rejected precondition on `disposition: filed`, which is an argument and not a field. The last two arrived with the 2026-08-17 amendment, and the count moved with them — an earlier draft of that amendment moved the number alone and left the enumeration and the split below it untouched, which is the defect this paragraph's own last clause forbids. A rule the lint does not carry must say so, or a reader assumes CI is holding a line that nothing holds — and every such rule appears in this list under exactly one verdict, because an earlier draft split one constraint across two and gave it opposite answers.

   Two reasons sit behind that list, and they are not the same reason. Nine are unenforced *for now* — new semantic rules with no incident record, and ADR-002 earns code by recurrence, not by plausibility. **One is unenforceable in principle**: `catchable`'s detectability test asks what was knowable at a position, which is a judgment about the defect's content and not a fact any JSON row carries. Distinguishing them matters, because a reader waiting for a guard that can never arrive will mistake a permanent property of the instrument for a backlog item. Two sit in the first class on narrow grounds worth stating. The **burden rule** is checkable in the negative — its trigger is `catchable` landing upstream, pure set membership on one field — but only where the sentence is on the `ref` surface; where the rule permits it to live in a finder's report instead, `ref` must point at that report for the check to reach it. The **finder-classifies rule** is checkable only where authorship is recorded, which today is nowhere on the row.

   **What a row records when the catch arrives outside a review event.** `found_by: owner`, `ci`, and `consumer` name finders that produce no review report. Such a row takes `source` as the event that surfaced it — a conversation, a CI run, a consumer report — named as durably as the surface allows, and `ref` pointing at whatever record exists. `ref` is required and must be an `https://` URL, so a catch with no durable record cannot be written until one is made — post a comment naming the finding and point at that. The row is never dropped for want of a surface; the surface is created, because a catch left uncounted is the failure this field exists to prevent.

   **Three vocabulary gaps, closed together because they are one amendment** ([#17](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/17), folded into [#16](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/16)). Each was a consumer already needing a value this section could not express, and amending §5 three times in a week is how a vocabulary acquires half-designs that do not compose.

   **`disposition` gains `filed`.** The set is now `fixed | reworded | recorded | owner-pending | filed`. §4 makes filing the exception and requires both other homes rejected first — but a finding that lawfully clears that bar had no value to say so, and rows were written `recorded` with an issue named as the reason. That is the closest lawful value and **not the same claim**: `recorded` says nobody will act on this, `filed` says someone will, at a named surface. The row's `ref` carries the issue. This is §5's own rule applied to itself — a judged value with no lawful slot means the vocabulary is short.

   **A row may carry `trial`**, an optional lowercase token naming the trial whose evidence it is. ADR-002's second road admits a mechanism before any evidence exists, and two of its six properties need a row attributable to the trial that produced it; nothing on a row could say that, so the road shipped **open only to review-panel seats**, whose names are already lawful in `found_by`. `trial` lifts that: a gate procedure, a skill or a script can now be admitted and produce countable evidence. Two properties. It is **optional** — absent means the row is not a trial's evidence, which is almost every row, and a required field defaulting to a sentinel would put the sentinel on 359 rows to serve none of them. And **`SELECT DISTINCT trial` enumerates the live trials**, so a session can find them without knowing a name in advance; a value that can only be queried by someone who already knows it attributes nothing.

   **A run that found nothing is recorded outside this file, and that is a design decision rather than a shortfall.** A seat that finds nothing writes no rows, so *ran and found nothing* and *never ran* are one silence here — the defect ADR-002 names inside its own escape hatch. The distinguishing fact is **per run, not per defect**: it is about which seats were staffed, which no defect ledger can carry without admitting non-defect rows and becoming a different instrument. So it lives in a companion the same shape as this one — **`docs/seat-record.jsonl`**, one JSON object per line, required fields `{source, date, seat, model, runtime, lane, raw, merged, sustained, status, isolated}` and the same optional `trial`. `status` is `ran | clean | failed`: `clean` ran and found nothing, `failed` was still unusable after its one re-dispatch, and both carry zeros — without `status` they are the same row, which is the collapse being closed, reproduced one level down. `lane` is `panel | routine`. `isolated` is a boolean recording whether the seat got its own worktree; it exists because that rule was prose with nothing to check it against and was violated on this amendment's own review. `raw` is what the seat filed, `merged` what survived deduplication, `sustained` what the terminal stage kept — **a priced-out finding included, since the stage affirmed it as real and this field measures whether a seat's findings were real, not whether they were fixed** — and a merged finding credits **every** finder, so per-seat sums may exceed the merged list, by design. That qualification is stated here and not only in the practice's skill because the unqualified gloss is what a reader consults to learn what the field counts, and leaving it at one of two definitional sites is the twin-site class this section has already committed once (`M5`, [PR #19](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/pull/19)).

   Both files are joined by `source`. **That join is a hint and not a key today**, because `source` granularity is per stage and the corpus is inconsistent about it — some reviews book defense-found rows under the panel's own `source`, others under a dedicated one. Naming that rather than asserting a join that does not hold.

   Rows in both files are validated by the packaging lint until the core library exists to own emit/validate (ADR-002's boundary-format rule, discharged in its smallest honest form). **The seat record therefore ships with its check rather than owing one** — the review practice's earlier draft put these counts in a pull-request comment claiming this section's waiver, which was not available to it: this waiver *is* the lint, and a block with no check is the day-one-code exception being taken rather than discharged.

   **The retirement test, and the full record of its rewriting.** What buys a standing pre-implementation review is **`catchable` at an upstream position — `framing`, `design` or `plan` — recurring in the ledger**; its absence is what retires one. The sentence has been rewritten twice, and **both halves changed**, so both are recorded here rather than one. A third change followed, in which **the sentence did not change at all** and what it means changed more than either rewrite — recorded below on the ground that this paragraph exists for changes to the test, not for changes to its characters.

   *The second conjunct.* The original read `"catchable at design, caught at implementation"`. The 2026-08-16 split made `implementation` a position rather than a stage, so `caught: implementation` became unwritable, and the amendment's first draft silently substituted `caught: adversarial-review` — a value **every row in the corpus already carried**, which made the conjunct select everything and discriminate nothing. It is therefore **dropped rather than replaced**.

   *The first conjunct.* The original said `catchable at design`; this text says `framing`, `design` **or** `plan`. That broadening is defensible on its merits — a pre-implementation review covers all three positions, so a defect catchable at any of them is evidence for one — but it was made silently, in the paragraph whose stated purpose is recording exactly this kind of change, and a first correction of that paragraph then *misdescribed* the original as having paired the broadened clause with `caught: implementation`, which it never did.

   *The redefinition, 2026-08-16.* `catchable` stopped being set by the `introduced` rule and became a detectability claim with its own test. The test's characters are identical either side of that change; its content is not, because the term it selects on now means something the old rule forbade it to mean. **A reader diffing this section would see the test as untouched, which is why it is written down here.** The prior two failures were both a sentence changing while the record stayed silent; this is the mirror case — the record speaking while the sentence stays still — and the paragraph would have missed it on its own wording, which asked only whether the sentence had been rewritten.

   Recorded at this length, and three times over, because a governing sentence rewritten in passing is the failure this section exists to prevent, and the correction reproduced the defect it was correcting. The second conjunct's substitution was caught by the defense stage of the amendment's own review, after four seats read the new text forward and none diffed it against the old; the first conjunct's broadening was caught only by the post-fix pass over that correction. The redefinition was caught by neither, because it was a stated acceptance criterion of the change that made it — which is the only one of the three mechanisms that ran *before* the defect could land.

   **That test was rigged until 2026-08-16** — the vocabulary it replaced held one value per field across every row then written ([registry](../evidence.md)), so the absence was guaranteed by the schema and the loop could return only one verdict. An evidence loop whose instrument cannot express the finding it tests for is not evidence. The corpus predating the split carries `unrecorded` for both positions, on the checkable ground that the old vocabulary could express no position earlier than the prose — not on any claim about what its authors did or did not judge. **A schema that can express variance is not yet an instrument that has shown any**, and this amendment's own rows are the first evidence of how far that gap runs. They exercise `framing`, `design`, `implementation`, `post-fix`, `external` and `authoring-review`, so `introduced` and `caught` do vary. **`catchable` does not.** It is identical to `introduced` on every row in the corpus — the formulation is left self-maintaining on purpose. An earlier draft of this sentence replaced it with a literal count, which was stale within the hour: eight rows landed between the read and the writing, and the figure shipped wrong in three places here and twice on the pull request. A constant that has to be re-verified on every merge is a claim this section cannot keep; the query is in the registry for anyone who wants the number as of a revision.

   **The second rigging, and why the first fix did not reach it.** The 2026-08-16 split fixed the *schema* — the vocabulary could finally express an upstream position — and left the *rule* intact, and the rule was doing the same work: a defect whose preventing decision belonged upstream took that position in both fields, "which is nearly always". So the test still keyed on a field that could not disagree with its neighbour, and the loop could still return only one verdict. The split's own text recorded the identity as an observation about the corpus; it was a consequence of the rule, and stating it as data about the rows read as though more rows would settle it. **They would not have.** Under the old rule no possible corpus separates the axes, so the second amendment is not a refinement of the first — it is the same defect one level up, found because the first fix's stated caveat was read as a live question rather than a closed one.

   This is also the honest bound on what the present amendment claims: it removes a rule that forced the answer. It does not show the axis separating, and it cannot, since it declines to reclassify the corpus, and the rows written since have not separated the axes either. **An instrument that no longer guarantees its own answer is not yet an instrument that has produced one** — the same sentence the split earned, now owed by its successor.

   The case where they *can* differ is worth naming, because it is the one the position axis was really built to measure: a defect **introduced early and detectable only later** — `introduced: design, catchable: implementation` — the class of thing that cannot be sorted out before the artifact exists. No row in this corpus is that shape yet. Until one is, the retirement test is answered by `introduced` wearing `catchable`'s name, and that is a weaker instrument than this section previously claimed to have built.
6. **Quality is still the first constraint.** None of the above relaxes *coverage* — what gets looked at — only the ceremony around it. When lightness and a needed check conflict, the check wins; the ledger is how a check proves it is no longer needed.

## Consequences

- Process weight is a managed quantity with an evidence loop, not a ratchet.
- The backlog measures intent: everything in it passed the pickup test, so it stays small enough to be real.
- Heavier process must recruit evidence to exist, which means the ledger gets consulted, which keeps the loop honest.
