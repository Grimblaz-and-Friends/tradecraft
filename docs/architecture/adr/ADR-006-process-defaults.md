# ADR-006: Process defaults — start light, escalate on evidence

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-15 · Amended 2026-08-15 (review made artifact-positional, not implementation-only) · Amended 2026-08-15 (differentiated panel made the default for substantial artifacts; seat attribution added to the ledger — evidence: the seat-level mining of the predecessor's review record, [issue #1](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1))

## Context

The predecessor started heavy and spent years earning its way lighter: a five-stage pipeline as the entrance, a five-pass review panel, and filing-by-default that turned every unit of work into two or three tracked follow-ups (324 open issues; 40% untouched for 90+ days). Its own measurements pointed the other way: the widest review panel once sustained a *false* finding, while re-running fixes cheaply and reliably caught real defects the fixes themselves introduced (the per-cycle records are in evidence.md); the instrument for right-sizing process (a phase-containment ledger recording where defects were introduced vs caught) was the right idea, added years too late to shape the defaults.

## Decision

Invert the predecessor's defaults; keep its instrument from day one.

1. **The minimal lane is the entrance.** One conversation takes work from intent to done. Heavier lanes (staged design, deeper review, unattended runs) exist and are opt-in — chosen for cause, not defaulted into.
2. **Review attaches to artifacts, not only to code.** A framing, a design, a plan, and an implementation are each reviewable artifacts, and a defect is cheapest caught at the artifact where it was introduced — a design flaw found in the design costs a conversation; the same flaw found in the implementation costs the implementation. So escalation has **two axes**: *depth* (a wider or stronger review of one artifact) and **position** (reviewing an earlier artifact before building on it). When risk justifies exactly one escalation, position usually beats depth. In the minimal lane this costs almost nothing: the pre-implementation artifact may be a stated approach a paragraph long, and its review *is* the convergence gate (ADR-005) — the check always runs; what's opt-in is a dedicated adversarial challenge of the design or plan before implementation begins.
3. **Review default at any position, scaled by the artifact's weight.** For routine changes: one adversarial pass that reads the artifact **cold** — before any prior findings, self-review, or earlier ledgers are exposed to it — then an evidence-bound defense, then mandatory re-validation of fixes; there is no judge, and defense's evidence verdicts terminate. For substantial artifacts (newly written foundational prose, new scripts, high blast radius — and when in doubt, this lane, since going below the default is bought only by ledger evidence): a **differentiated panel of four seats is the default, not an escalation** — the roster, its staffing, and its canonical seat names live in the practice's skill, [skills/adversarial-review](../../../skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md) — then defense, then a judge whose job is independent re-execution of contested evidence and severity settling (a separate judge is earned by a panel-width ledger). Every review records which lane was chosen, and why, in its review report, so the classification itself stays auditable. This inverts this section's first version, which made one pass the default everywhere: the seat-level mining of the predecessor's review record ([issue #1](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/1), 2026-08-15) overturned it — across the six review rounds where per-seat attribution survives (116 sustained findings), a single pass kept 12% of the sustained yield and lost nearly every critical and high, the *best* single seat chosen with hindsight still kept only 46%, seats' unique yields barely overlapped, and no seat was consistently strongest (the best seat's identity changed nearly every round). The same rounds set the width: the best three seats retained roughly 75–100% by round, the fourth seat's marginal yield stayed real (up to a fifth of a round's sustained findings, at times including highs), and the fifth's went thin — hence four, wider only by declared risk. Three bounds keep the panel honest: **no clone seats** — a seat is added for a lens or vantage the panel lacks, not for a second pass at one it has. Stated honestly this is a judgment rather than a measurement: the predecessor's same-lens pair did sole-find real defects, so redundancy is not worthless; differentiation is simply what the seat record measures, and concurrence between same-lens passes has amplified a false finding. Revisable by `found_by` evidence in either direction; **every finding's finder is recorded** (`found_by`, §5) so seats retire by ledger evidence — the instrument the predecessor never had, which is why its seat question stayed unanswerable across its whole measured corpus (~850 entries at mining, session-measured); and **recurring defect classes are closed by lens promotion (ADR-002), never by adding seats** — the predecessor's best-documented escape survived fifteen seat-readings across three artifacts plus two post-fix cycles, fell to a differently-positioned lens, and stopped recurring only when a checklist lens was worded to catch its class. Defense is mandatory at every depth: it is what makes width safe (in the predecessor's record it killed 40+ false or inflated findings and originated 8+ sustained ones, including a critical). Fix re-validation is mandatory at every depth, **executable rather than a re-read** — every reading stage, defense included, showed shared blind spots that only live runs caught — and scoped to **every branch the fix touched**, because the escaped class recurred three times in its own fix's mirror image. Depth and position are escalated per-change by declared risk (wider panels, external fresh-session reviewers), and de-escalated only by ledger evidence — in both directions, weight follows evidence, never cost arguments and never habit.
4. **Filing is the exception.** A finding's default disposition is *fix it now* or *drop it with a one-line reason*. Its two lawful homes are the change that found it, or a guard (ADR-002 promotion) — a guard fires when the condition recurs; an issue fires never. Proposing a tracked follow-up requires stating why both other homes were rejected, and the proposal must pass the pickup test: would this actually get picked up if time opened tomorrow? A finding that turns on a decision only the owner can make is neither filed nor dropped: it is recorded with disposition `owner-pending` and **resurfaced from the ledger** — every review's opening ledger read batches the open ones into a single argued question at a phase seam (ADR-005), and a row still open after several such surfacings has failed the pickup test in fact, so it is re-dispositioned `recorded` with a one-line reason. The owner's queue lives inside the instrument that already gets read, rather than in a backlog that does not.
5. **The ledger exists, with a defined shape.** Home: `docs/ledger.jsonl`, one JSON object per line with required fields `{id, date, artifact, severity, introduced, catchable, caught, source, disposition, found_by, ref}`. Field contracts: `date` is an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` day; `source` names the review event that produced the row and `id` is unique within it, both strings, so the pair `(source, id)` is globally unique; `disposition` is one of `fixed | reworded | recorded | owner-pending`; `found_by` is a non-empty string naming the seat or stage that first surfaced the defect (a canonical seat name from the skill's roster, `defense`, `judge`, `post-fix`, `external`, `ci`, or `unrecorded` for rows predating the field) — a merged finding's row carries its primary (first-surfacing) finder, the review report's merged ledger is where dual credit lives, and this field is what lets a seat be retired or added on evidence instead of argument; `ref` is a URL to the durable surface carrying that review's findings in full — the pull request or issue where its record lives — because the row itself holds no description, so without it a row proves a defect occurred but never what it was, and a later claim that a class *recurred* cannot be checked at all. It must name a surface that survives a squash-merge and branch deletion: a PR or issue does, a branch SHA may not. The artifact/position vocabulary is `constitution | repo-docs | skill-prose | script | lint | tests | ci | packaging`, and `introduced`/`catchable`/`caught` name the earliest phase each was true of (`authoring`, `authoring-review`, `adversarial-review`, `ci`, `post-merge`, `consumer`). Rows are validated by the packaging lint until the core library exists to own emit/validate (ADR-002's boundary-format rule, discharged in its smallest honest form). This is what makes the position axis governable: "catchable at design, caught at implementation" recurring in the ledger is the evidence that buys a standing pre-implementation review; its absence is what retires one.
6. **Quality is still the first constraint.** None of the above relaxes *coverage* — what gets looked at — only the ceremony around it. When lightness and a needed check conflict, the check wins; the ledger is how a check proves it is no longer needed.

## Consequences

- Process weight is a managed quantity with an evidence loop, not a ratchet.
- The backlog measures intent: everything in it passed the pickup test, so it stays small enough to be real.
- Heavier process must recruit evidence to exist, which means the ledger gets consulted, which keeps the loop honest.
