# The dispatch contract

**Loaded when** you are writing the dispatch that sends a review role its work — a seat, a defense, a judge; the dispatch itself is copied from `../references/dispatch-template.md`. A recipient reading a dispatch it was handed does not need it.

**Every dispatch is built in three parts, in this order**: the **shared block**, a **dispatcher's note** where there is one, and the **recipient's own part**. The shared block is the assignment — everything a recipient needs, carried verbatim or by link and never the dispatcher's paraphrase, every predecessor stage's output whole — and it is byte-identical across every recipient dispatched against the same diff base, but for what the roster withholds from `cold-read` (`../references/roster.md`), which the dispatch names rather than replacing with a selected substitute. A preamble puts per-recipient framing ahead of it, and a summary can lose what its source carried: **calling attention is lawful; filtering and restating are not.** What every recipient's output must carry, and the revision with what lands the recipient there, are assignment content and sit in the block — the same thing is asked of every recipient, and the route differs by tree rather than by recipient. The dispatcher's note is labeled as such and additive; no seat takes one, because a cold vantage sits among them and aimed attention is what that vantage is defined against. The recipient's part is its identity and its lens brief in this skill's own words — which the `cold-read` seat does not take — its working location where that differs, and the shape its own role's output takes.

**Role separation, judging-seat staffing and the record of every launch are the `engagement` cell's; the judge is also never a finder.** The job declares its capability.

**A dispatch whose subject is text the change edits gets a working root whose copy of that text is the change's own**, and is told that any always-on text reaching it otherwise is not its governing text: a runtime injects the repository's own doctrine and charter into every session it starts, from wherever it resolves them, so a recipient can read the rule the change replaced and rule against it, and naming the root alone has not stopped it.

**The dispatch names its revision and landing route.** A root is not a revision: a tool-cut root otherwise opens on the default rather than the text under review. Building a tree at a revision, for any instrument's run, is the `experience-session` cell's; the one line a recipient runs on a root it cannot rebuild, and what to do when that line fails, are the template's. A tool-cut attached root is **seat-launcher-incompatible**: this launcher refuses it before checkout, so use one that permits recipient positioning.

**And the recipient states, in its output, the revision it actually worked at**, or that it reached none, and whether the tree it worked in was its own or shared with any other recipient — what makes a stage that judged replaced text legible to someone who was not in it.

**Every dispatched recipient works in its own tree, cut detached at the revision the dispatch names**. That is what isolation is here, and it is owed whatever the recipient was dispatched to do: a seat that only reads leaves the same residue as one that writes. **A recipient dispatched to write owes one thing beyond it — its write boundary is that tree**, or concurrent readers see files change underneath them mid-review. A capability-`execute` dispatch is dispatched to write for this rule and names that boundary. It also gives the seat a time bound sized for its job and tells it to bank findings to a named file in its tree as it goes, because a timeout can return no transcript. Detached, because a pull request opened from a branch made at the change re-proposes the reviewed change under an unrelated title.

**A dispatched recipient's tree becomes removable when the review that dispatched it closes** — its final report posted — and not before: a seat that returned unusable output is re-dispatched once (`../references/arbitration.md`), and trees outlive the sessions that cut them, so a rule owed by the dispatching session reaches none already standing. **Removal covers the branch the tree's creation made**, where one still stands — a branch nothing removes is what outlives its tree.
