# Writing the pre-implementation artifact

**Loaded when** you are writing a pre-implementation artifact, revising one after a verdict, or deciding whether one is finished. A session settling the brief does not need it -- the brief is the cell's, and this is what reads it; a seat working a dispatch it was handed does not need it either.

**Then the artifact is written, and a cold seat settles it.** A seat with none of the conversation's history judges whether the artifact's reading would achieve the brief. **A verdict that it would settles the artifact, as does a verdict of `not settleable` discharged by the fork being carried** — which the label states, **settled by a discharged `not settleable`** and never the word bare, so no stage inherits it as a reading a seat affirmed. Carrying an adverse verdict settles nothing, because the check exists to answer whether the reading achieves the brief and a transcribed no is that question answered against you. Until then you are holding a **draft**: labelled as one, complete enough to argue with rather than complete, carrying every question still open as open. **The verdict names the text it judged**, and an artifact edited after its verdict is a draft again — the seat judged what it read, not what replaced it. A draft that reads like a settled artifact has already taken a decision nobody gave it, so the verdict exists before the label does, and citing a verdict nobody has written is that defect wearing the settled word.

**Where what the change delivers is the truth of some text, that text is drafted before the artifact and the artifact is written against the draft.** The class is recognised by what *done* is: where the change succeeds or fails on whether some claim the text makes is **true of what it describes** — correcting a false or stale description is the standing case — the drafted text is the only thing criteria can be written against. Where the fork is design instead, a new mechanism or a new binding rule, nothing outside the text makes the result true or false, the shape is the decision, and the artifact stays first. Criteria over prose that does not exist can only describe a hypothetical: [five rounds argued about imaginary text, and the false sentence shipped anyway](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/355). **That drafted text is neither committed nor posted**, so nothing about the affirmation, or about the artifact being settled before the first commit, moves. **The artifact carries that drafted text**, which is how the seat that settles it and the implementer who builds from it both hold text committed and posted nowhere. [D-364]

**What the dispatch carries, what cold means, the bar the seat applies, the three verdicts, what bounds the re-runs and the two-round cap that sends the next question to the owner, and what a draft keeps between them: `../references/cold-seat.md`**, loaded when you dispatch any cold seat, run the check, read a verdict back, or price a revision after one -- that file's own condition, and not a narrower one.

**A draft is not built from, and waiting is not a licence.** The wait is where the rule breaks under a competent session acting reasonably: the verdict is slow, the work looks obvious, and building starts. Slow is answered by waiting. **Where the check cannot be got at all, the artifact carries a truncation in place of the verdict** — what was tried, what went unjudged, and why no party in reach could supply the seat. That settles it, and every rule keyed to a settled artifact then reads correctly; what the label carries is **settled by a recorded truncation**, never the word bare, so no stage inherits it as a judged reading. A verdict of *no* is not this: an adverse verdict is a verdict, and it settles nothing. **A check somebody withheld is not this either.** The route covers a check **no party within the session's reach can supply** — no way to dispatch one exists, or every attempt failed. Where a bound someone set is what removed it, a dispatch forbidding the check being the standing case, it was withheld rather than unobtainable: the artifact stays a **draft**, and the withholding goes back to whoever set that bound, reported where this cell already sends a call the session made. **This is the one route to settled that needs no other party's act**, which is why that last element is required at all.

**The draft carries every adverse verdict it receives, one line per point a verdict lands on** — naming the claim, criterion or choice rather than the finding's prose, from the first verdict onward; the lines are collected directly under the draft's purpose header and stay there once it settles. **A second line naming the same point is evidence about the point rather than about the sentence.** Repairing what a verdict named is the right response once; when the next one lands on the same point — the wording having changed in between is the norm, not a reason it does not count — stop reworking it and name which of three it is. The claim is **false about the tree**, which no wording repairs: probe it, and write from what comes back. The passage is the **wrong shape** for what it covers: restate it whole. Or each draft **decides the same thing differently**, which is what a fork looks like when a session keeps closing it silently: it takes the contingent form below and goes to the owner argued, the work proceeding on the recommendation. [D-304]

**A fork that is the owner's goes in the brief**, since the brief is what they affirm — so an artifact's criteria are written against the ruling the affirmed brief carries. A fork the brief does not reach, because nothing about it could have made the brief wrong, stays open into the artifact and takes the contingent form below. [D-155]

**Where the issue leaves open a question that is theirs under the fork test this cell states, the artifact carries the fork rather than closing it.** Its criteria are the ones that hold whichever way they rule; the fork goes to them in the argued form the cell states, at release or at a stop where the brief itself must change, and any criterion that turns on their answer is written against the recommendation, marked as contingent, and names what replaces it. **An artifact whose criteria presuppose an answer the issue leaves open has closed a fork silently** — the mirror of asking where no fork exists. [D-155]

- **Acceptance criteria** are the change's success definition, and its review judges against them — so what they name is what every stage of that review inherits. **Write each one as something a consumer of the result can do, or no longer does wrongly** — whoever works under the built result, most often a session, in a situation the change exists for. A criterion about the artifact *conforming* — a count, a section present, a wording faithful, a file untouched — is lawful where it argues its own consumer-facing load, which it states; written for its own sake it manufactures the bookkeeping class, and a criterion is the one thing that makes such a finding look like a defect in the work. They state what must be true of the result, not what it may cost. [D-166]
- **Each criterion names the check that would falsify it** — a criterion nobody can check is a wish. The diff settles what the diff can settle, a command settles what a command can run, and what only use shows is settled by putting the situations to a session that has none of this change's history, or by the built result in use; the criterion says which. **Naming the check does not order it run**: the criterion states how the claim could be falsified, and what a change owes in runs is not this rule's to say. **A criterion on the truth of a claim names the run that settles it** — execute what the claim describes, and compare — never a property of the wording: [fifteen rows binding removal, locatability and order let a false replacement ship, because none asked whether what replaced the overstatement was true](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/355). [D-166] [D-364]
- **A criterion a plausible wrong implementation would also pass measures nothing.** Say what failing looks like before writing it; where the artifact carries a fork, a criterion held to survive it must be falsifiable on **every** branch it survives — the cheapest cross-branch criterion is one that is empty on a branch. [D-166]
- **A criterion claiming a piece of text moves a reader — toward a destination or away from one — names which of two quantities it claims**, because the two come apart: that the reader *goes* where the text says, which is its **reach**, or that a reader's *stated reason* names this text. **Reach is falsifiable only where nothing else the reader loads routes that same situation anyway, or where what does routes it too weakly to mask the difference.** Two routes have defeated it in a run and apply in either polarity, though only the first has been measured where the text keeps a reader away: the destination's own description settling the situation — its summary sentence, or another of its clauses — and an always-on surface naming the destination. **A third is reasoned rather than measured and applies only there, where it is the ordinary case: the reader's own indifference.** A reader with no pull toward that destination declines it whether or not the text is there, and the situations that test such text at all are near misses, where the pull is weakest by construction — so reach is the quantity least likely to survive there. Least likely, not unavailable: text stripped of its exclusions has been measured opening the destination they excluded. **Warrant is falsifiable where reach is not, and it fails the same way**: where the destination's name or summary sentence lets a reader reconstruct this text's substance, they give that substance as their reason whether or not the text is there. [D-338] [D-320] [D-345]
- **One figure rule is this cell's, and it scopes to the whole artifact rather than to these criteria.** A **forecast** — any figure about a tree that does not exist yet — belongs **outside** it, in the PR body, the decision entry, or the review's report, each written after the tree exists. [D-155]
- **A boundary statement** — the explicit list of what the work is *not* doing and why — records the scope agreed, so a later scope question is answerable from the artifact rather than from memory. It does not forbid the work covering more; the diff is what records what was actually done.

**Settled, the artifact is the handoff contract — which is also the test for whether it is finished.** From there the work can leave the conversation entirely, to a fresh session or another runtime, so nothing load-bearing may be left in chat: an implementer who was not there builds the agreed thing from the artifact and the brief it carries, alone. Handoff across runtimes rests on that property and nothing else.

**A premise the artifact rests on may need testing before you assert it** — that is a spike, which tests one and commits nothing.

**Where a check bears on the artifact, the artifact carries what came back rather than the fact that you looked.** It carries a spike's result, **including a spike that did not resolve**; where you considered one and did not run it, it carries the material you consulted instead — the enumeration, the query and the tree it ran on, the file you opened. [D-155]
