# Holding the unattended stretch

**Loaded when** coordinating the stretch from an affirmed implementation brief to release readiness, taking one of its dispatched stages, staffing a judging seat, or preparing its reports and records.

## The holder and the implementer

**The holding session designs the change with the owner through affirmation and owns the whole stretch that follows.** It runs each stage explicitly through the `work` entrance, writes any holder-authored job that stage needs, and receives every return, remaining the owner's counterpart through the reviewed pull request and its posted report. One holding session per change, never one for many, because its context grows from the first turn through every stage. It edits no file in the implementation tree and takes no judging seat. Where responsibility changes at affirmation, the handoff rule (`../references/the-handoff.md`) governs the exception.

For a lane that owes an artifact and cold settlement, post the whole-change reading with the `work` cell's marker, replacing `RESULT` from that marker's vocabulary:

```text
<!-- tradecraft:holder-reading:v1 result=RESULT -->
```

An affirmed mechanical lane has no judged artifact to read and manufactures no holder-reading marker.

On the holder's explicit `run build`, the entrance cuts a branch and separate implementation worktree below the holder checkout's ignored `.claude/worktrees/`, registers and publishes that branch, verifies its remote head, and dispatches the builder there. Later stages resolve the same root internally, so the holder command names no protected path and judgment reads the change. Before cutting the worktree, the entrance writes `/.claude/worktrees/` to the holder checkout's repository-local exclude file, which is not committed and is not visible in `git status`. The recorded guard status describes whether the holder checkout's project settings declare the write-refusing pre-tool hook, not whether a runtime enforced it. When declared, that hook rereads the machine-local registry on every edit, write, notebook edit or shell call and refuses an implicated write; without the declaration, before/after revision and status snapshots detect a breach but do not enforce the boundary.

**The material handing a party its job states the tools and permission or approval route it must be started with to perform it, and a launcher capable of checking that declaration refuses a launch that would not supply it**, because instructions cannot supply a capability the launch withholds.

**Where the lane owes an artifact, the holder resumes its implementer only for substantive artifact repairs after a cold return. The build goes to a fresh builder reading the settled artifact; a mechanical build instead receives the exact affirmed brief and lane reason. That builder resumes for every prescribed repair after build, judgment or use**, because those later stages depend on the tree and choices that builder already holds; this includes small fixes. A holder session identifier and a builder session identifier may never be the same in a dispatch record, because a separate process is the enforceable half of that role boundary. **Source-verification rule:** every durable account the holder authors or finalizes on the unattended stretch, whose factual claims are established by the change tree, GitHub state, or a stage return, goes to a fresh implementer for source verification before release. This property, rather than a roster of surface names, selects the account; for example, it reaches a review report draft when that draft makes such claims. It excludes the affirmed implementation brief, which is the owner's settled term; a dispatch as an instruction, while its factual shared block is source-checked with the eligible account it belongs to rather than by a separate check of every dispatch; and the implementer's artifact and commit message, whose own rules retain their checks. **The default report sources are the executable floor, any required experience session or current-head no-use carrier, and the connected reviewers' in-thread dispositions; a terminal ruling and fix batch are sources only where the owner bought a panel or those stages actually ran.** Where a panel was bought, its merged list, defense and terminal ruling remain stage-return producers the source check compares against, not accounts it assesses. The fresh implementer receives the exact account drafts with the affirmed brief, implementation tree, pull request state, the durable dispatch records required by this cell's `dispatch-records.md`, and the unabridged returns and in-thread dispositions that produced their claims. Its instruction says to compare every verifiable claim with its producer and return what cannot be verified rather than repairing, reinterpreting, or silently narrowing it. **During a fix batch, writing parties run serially in the change's implementation worktree and branch:** the resumed builder finishes and commits that batch before a fresh installer writes eligible repository content, and neither writes while the other does; source-ready accounts that precede a fix batch remain under their own installation procedure. The implementer returns at the extent its dispatch names. A recipient needing another party returns that need to the holder; recipients dispatch no pipeline stage themselves. Each instrument retains its own ordering, evidence, isolation and settlement rules.

**For a Codex implementer**, use `lib/dispatch_implementer.py`, which applies the explicit model and effort, workspace-write with automatic approval review, JSON return capture and a named session id without `--last` or `--ephemeral`. The dispatch tells the implementer to report a sandbox refusal and request approval to retry it, since stopping at the first failure suppresses that route. A fresh turn returns the id its later `--resume SESSION_ID` takes; a completed turn with no trustworthy id returns its product but cannot qualify as resumable. If approval is rejected or unavailable, report that inability to the holder. The [launch spike](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/593#issuecomment-5653831839) and [resume by id](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/593#issuecomment-5654461236) demonstrate the capability; the `dispatch-records.md` entry in this cell carries the launcher and record contract, including the manual path for a Claude implementer.

## A surprise from build onward

**From the start of build onward, a surprise is compared first with Shape and then with the reader cells of the affirmed implementation brief.** If Shape is wrong, stop: the term no longer covers the change, so its amendment goes to the owner with the cost of the ask stated. If a reader cell would now read differently, write only the affected row as a proposed contingent row, carry the recommendation and its strongest case against, and continue the run on that recommendation; changing an affirmed row is the first post-affirmation owner door, and its ask states its cost. A question the record or precedent can answer goes through the assigned coordinating session first, or through the holder's recorded check when none is assigned. If no cell changes, the holder decides and records the surprise with the reason and continues.

The posted row uses the one ask block and mark in `../references/the-ask.md`, naming the row-changing door and the ask's cost; as the reading amendment in `../references/the-brief.md`, it carries the contingent row's criteria into the settled artifact without buying a fresh verdict, and is not an edit to the affirmed item. The holder gives the builder the affected row, the recommendation, and the contingent alternative, so continuing never turns into choosing silently.

**A seat judges a contingent row on both readings.** Its surviving criteria must be falsifiable whether the owner keeps the affirmed row or accepts the proposed one.

## Staffing judgment

**Every judging seat is a fresh dispatch on the other vendor from the implementer's**, with only the history its instrument permits: the artifact's cold reader, review seats, defense, judge, and the experience session's consumer. By default Claude holds the implementation brief, Codex implements, and Claude staffs judging seats. The comparison is with the implementer, whatever vendor holds the implementation brief. Each launch names its model and reasoning effort; freshness does not erase the predecessor evidence a role is meant to receive.

**The experience consumer also runs on the runtime the change's users run.** Both requirements apply. Where no qualifying consumer can be supplied, record the missing use and why under the instrument's omission route.

**An experience consumer takes the cold judging effort (`max` on Claude).** The run must notice unbriefed friction while operating the result, so `run use` classifies it as cold rather than borrowing an ordinary review seat's effort. An owner's recorded per-change instruction wins over this default without moving the stage between vendors.

**Staffing is read from the actual returned vendor.** A return from the requested vendor records `qualified`; an availability fallback records `degraded`, with requested and actual vendor and the reason. A degraded return does not complete a cross-vendor or fresh stage unless that stage carries its same-vendor reason, because capability alone does not restore the independence the requested staffing bought. Where the fallback cannot supply the declared capability, no judgment returns and the instrument's missing-seat or omission route applies.

## Returns and records

**Every return comes back to the holder, is posted to the change's record whole — including any verbatim copy of the affirmed brief it carries, because later stages read the posted comment rather than the holder's bundle — and travels onward whole.** A file is the transport where available; the artifact's cold check takes its prescribed inline carrier. The holder passes the source itself, with only the labeled additions the receiving instrument permits and the withholding its cold boundary requires. It merges findings as an index of the source reports; the judging stage rules on the work.

**The holder writes any required use job, reports and records from the returns, and sends each eligible account through the Source-verification rule when its sources are ready.** Once the executable floor, any required experience session or current-head no-use carrier, and the connected reviewers' in-thread dispositions have supplied the default sources — joined by the terminal ruling and fix batch only where the owner bought a panel or those stages actually ran — the release-ready pull request body and any report draft are checked before posting. The holder-owned release-report handoff names the required gate's current-head verdict and identified run; where it is not green, the handoff directs the holder to restate the path-departures paragraph at that head with the bypass and its reason. The release report carries an owner ask only through one of the two post-affirmation doors and states its cost. A returned correction stays with its holder-authored account and any revision changing a returned claim is checked again. Only an account that needs a posted panel report's URL waits for that posting; another source-ready account is verified and installed under its own procedure. Once posting supplies the report URL or another source a dependent account needs, the same fresh implementer may resume to verify, install, and commit that eligible repository content. This keeps authorship with the party that saw the run and gives its claims an independent checker without making the checker a review stage. The holder retains every dispatch through this cell's `dispatch-records.md` contract, attaches its own usage at close, and posts the read-time cost report's separately labelled raw usage, dated rate-card equivalent and bill/plan status; none is a dispatch halt. It opens the pull request after the build return, posts any bought panel's report, and delivers the outcome to the owner; merging remains the owner's.
