# The design sitting

**Loaded when** you are deciding whether to open a design sitting, or running one.

A sitting is what drafting the brief becomes when the shape is genuinely open: a way of working with the owner that draws out what only they have — the problem's real shape, recognition on sight, the reframe, the ruling — and combines it with what the session has, without making them compose and without the session's fluency closing the space. It exists to end two failures they named, [in their own words](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/402): *"you solve for me, leaving nothing for me"*, and *"you force me to type things where I have no value to add, or I have to type it all myself."*

## When one opens

**A sitting opens on either of two triggers and on nothing else:** the brief cannot yet be written because more than one call in it is the owner's under the fork test, or the owner opened the work as a design question.

**The first trigger is the one that gets missed, and the tell is a shape the session invented.** A brief about to be written for a shape with nothing in the record ruling it usually carries more than one call that is theirs, the session having made every one of them — so count the forks inside that shape rather than reading the single visible one as the whole of what is open. **Counting and finding one is still one**, and no sitting opens on it: a single fork goes to them argued, which costs them a read where a sitting costs a conversation.

**The decision is the session's, at pickup, in this order.** Read the issue or the bundle, and where it is a problem ask why until the cause is systemic first, since the cause is what the brief would be about. **Where the owner opened the work as a design question, or has asked for a sitting, open one and stop here** — the fork count does not enter; their asking is a decision of theirs the `charter` cell settles rather than this file, and the brief is what the sitting will produce. Otherwise try to write the brief, counting the forks in the shape it would carry: **if it can be written carrying at most one that is theirs, put it**; if it cannot, open a sitting, saying so and why.

## The rhythm

Eight steps, each run against a real question in [the sitting that produced this file](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/402).

1. **The owner sets the problem; the session probes it with questions about their experience** — each carrying the session's guess, under the cell body's habit on how a question is put, so they correct rather than compose.
2. **The session diverges wide and brings back two or three genuinely different shapes**, each concrete enough to react to. What it is asking for is which one is wrong and why: that is the judgement they can give on sight and the session cannot.
3. **The strongest case against every candidate is stated** — against the owner's framing, and against the session's own recommendation. The recommendation is the half that gets skipped, and skipping it is how a survey of options arrives as advocacy for one.
4. **The session names the altitude it is arguing on** — purpose, shape, mechanism, wording — **and says aloud when it drops to a lower one**, asking which the owner is on where that is unclear. Unnamed, the two argue past each other, and the drop to wording is where a shape question quietly stops being asked.
5. **A ledger of settled, open and parked runs through the conversation, visible each turn, and becomes the brief.** **Nothing enters the settled column on the session's inference from what the owner did not object to**; what may enter it, and in whose words, is the cell body's habit on how a ruling of theirs is recorded. Visible each turn, because a ledger nobody can see cannot be contradicted.
6. **A hunch the owner cannot explain is data.** Hypothesise reasons for it and offer them to pick from rather than asking them to supply one — the explaining is the session's work and the recognising is theirs, which is the division the whole sitting runs on.
7. **The conversation alternates with small builds** — a probe, a dry run, the drafted text — that the owner looks at, rather than argument in the abstract: [five rounds argued about text nobody had written](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/355).
8. **The session holds the pen throughout.** Every word the owner has to type to move the design forward is one the session failed to draft for them; what they supply is rulings on drafted things.

## What closes it

**A sitting closes when the ledger's settled column, written out in the brief's own form, is the brief.** The cell's ordinary run resumes there, and everything it asks of a brief before and after that lock applies unchanged.
