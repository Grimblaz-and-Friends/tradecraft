# engagement

**Purpose:** keep the surfaces the owner enters to the decisions that are genuinely theirs, and make each one cheap for them to act on. **Audience:** any session working the stretch between the owner's attention and the next call on it — putting a decision to them, or building under one already settled. **Success:** the decisions that reach the owner are the ones genuinely theirs; they can read one brief and know what it is, why it matters, and what they are being asked; the artifact a session leaves behind states what the work is for without them in the room; and a session can say which side of the boundary any stage of the work falls on, and where a question arising on the far side goes.

## The plain brief

Every surface the owner enters opens with one, and so does the artifact, which carries the brief because it binds there.

> **In plain terms:** a few sentences of plain English, set off from everything around them, that explain the thing to the owner as a person — what it is, why it matters, what they are being asked.

- **Form:** a blockquote opening with a bold lead-in — `> **In plain terms:** …` — **immediately before what it briefs**. On a document that is the top, with nothing above it but a title; on a message ending in a decision it is directly above the ask, because everything between a brief and its ask is what the reader has to hold to connect them.
- **Plain English, and no term the owner would have to look up.** It explains; it does not compress. Issue numbers, decision citations, file paths, and a figure or the command that derives it, are not plain English, and they belong below.
- **What earns a place:** what this is, why it matters, what the owner is being asked. **What is disqualified:** anything that would only matter once they had already decided — implementation detail, supporting evidence, enumerations, the alternatives rejected on the way here.
- **Length is not a count.** The bar is readable in one pass, and longer is lawful where there is a reason for it. The disqualifier is what keeps a brief short; a word limit would reject a good six-sentence brief for being seven, which is why concision is stated as a rule about content rather than length.
- **A brief may put something out of scope, and what it puts out binds.** Saying *not this* is how the owner sets an edge they can be held to and a later session cannot quietly cross. It is never a required part: a brief naming no exclusion has excluded nothing rather than everything, and the edges are then the session's reading, recorded in the artifact's boundary statement. That is the difference the review's fix bar turns on — what the affirmed brief put out is the owner's, what only the artifact excluded is the session's.
- **Affirmed, it binds; describing, it is accountable.** A brief the owner affirmed is the term of the agreement, and the material under it interprets that term: where the two conflict the brief governs, and where the brief is silent the material does. Everywhere else a brief describes something that already exists — a review's findings, the state of the work — and there it yields to its subject, so a brief that misrepresents its body is a defect found in that body's review. **Authority follows affirmation, not placement**: what the owner approved is what they can be held to, and a rule keyed to a list of surfaces drifts as surfaces are named.

**What it is not:** a summary of everything beneath it, or a home for detail. The deep material sits around it — the brief does not have to carry it, it has to make carrying it unnecessary for the decision at hand.

**The conversation with the owner is the engagement surface**, and a decision put to them there is this skill's own. Three more are this cell's to name: a review's final report, where the brief opens the report as posted; a message handing work back after time away, which also carries where the work now stands, since on returning the expensive part is reconstructing that before the decision makes sense; and the pre-implementation artifact, which carries the affirmed brief **because it binds there**, not to brief a reader who settled it already.

## A decision put to the owner

**A decision is the owner's when it is a genuine fork**: what turns on the pick is something they would care about — what gets built, what it costs, what others see, what they live with day to day — and undoing a wrong pick costs them something an edit does not undo: habit, muscle memory, what others already saw. Both halves are required — one live option can satisfy the first — and everything else is the session's to decide. The statement of the test is the `charter` cell's — or an adopting repository's own doctrine, where that is where it lives; the calibration below is this cell's.

**Asking where no fork exists is a defect rather than politeness.** The tell is that nothing turns on their answer — the session would proceed the same way whatever they said; that is a report, and putting it as a question spends their attention for nothing. One live option is not by itself the tell: a real search that narrowed to one can still turn on their answer, and the fork test above decides it either way.

- **Theirs:** affirm, amend, or reject what a change is for before it is built; merging; anything outward-facing or destructive; a name, or anything else they live with day to day; spending materially beyond what the task implied.
- **The session's:** implementation approach, file layout, tool choice, and the order work is done in.

**What is theirs arrives argued:** the live options, each with its pros and cons, then the recommendation among them — above the brief, which sits last, immediately before the ask it briefs. Where exactly one option is live, say so and say what was rejected and why — the reasoning is what makes that case informative, and a fabricated second option under-informs worse than none, because it presents a search that did not happen.

**A call the session made is reported with its reason** where the work is already recorded — the work's issue, the PR body, the decision entry, or the review report — and never only in chat. The fork test is what licenses deciding; the report is what keeps deciding from becoming deciding silently.

## The pre-implementation artifact

The artifact settles what is being built before it is built, and lands on the work's own issue. It **opens with the affirmed brief, quoted verbatim from the comment that carries it**, and governed by it, then its own purpose header — the one place a governing document opens with something ahead of that header, because the term it is held to outranks its own statement of itself; the rest is what a reader who was not in the conversation needs in order to build the agreed thing. Its audience is the sessions that build and review from it, so it is written for them rather than presented to the owner.

**The brief is settled with the owner; the artifact is the session's reading of it.** What gets built is the owner's call, so the brief is drafted and revised with them in conversation and posted as its own comment when it locks — **it locks once nothing still open could invalidate it**, a question that would only add detail being the artifact's business rather than a reason to hold. **Locked is not affirmed**: locking is the session's judgement that the brief is ready to be put, and the affirmation is the owner's answer to it. A premise that could make the brief wrong is tested before it locks, not after.

**Then the artifact is written, and a cold seat settles it.** A seat with none of the conversation's history judges whether the artifact's reading would achieve the brief. **What settles the artifact is a verdict that it would, or a verdict of `not settleable` discharged by the fork being carried** — which the label states, **settled by a discharged `not settleable`** and never the word bare, so no stage inherits it as a reading a seat affirmed. Carrying an adverse verdict settles nothing, because the check exists to answer whether the reading achieves the brief and a transcribed no is that question answered against you. Until then you are holding a **draft**: labelled as one, complete enough to argue with rather than complete, carrying every question still open as open. **The verdict names the text it judged**, and an artifact edited after its verdict is a draft again — the seat judged what it read, not what replaced it. A draft that reads like a settled artifact has already taken a decision nobody gave it, so the verdict exists before the label does, and citing a verdict nobody has written is that defect wearing the settled word.

**What the dispatch carries, what cold means, the three verdicts and what bounds the re-runs, and what a draft keeps between them: `references/cold-seat.md`**, loaded when you run the check rather than when you read this.

**A draft is not built from, and waiting is not a licence.** The wait is where the rule breaks under a competent session acting reasonably: the verdict is slow, the work looks obvious, and building starts. Slow is answered by waiting. **Where the check cannot be got at all, the artifact carries a truncation in place of the verdict** — what was tried, what went unjudged. That settles it, and every rule keyed to a settled artifact then reads correctly; what the label carries is **settled by a recorded truncation**, never the word bare, so no stage inherits it as a judged reading. A verdict of *no* is not this: an adverse verdict is a verdict, and it settles nothing.

**The draft carries every adverse verdict it receives, one line per point a verdict lands on** — naming the claim, criterion or choice rather than the finding's prose, from the first verdict onward; the lines are collected directly under the draft's purpose header and stay there once it settles. **A second line naming the same point is evidence about the point rather than about the sentence.** Repairing what a verdict named is the right response once; when the next one lands on the same point — the wording having changed in between is the norm, not a reason it does not count — stop reworking it and name which of three it is. The claim is **false about the tree**, which no wording repairs: probe it, and write from what comes back. The passage is the **wrong shape** for what it covers: restate it whole. Or each draft **decides the same thing differently**, which is what a fork looks like when a session keeps closing it silently: it takes the contingent form below and goes to the owner argued, the work proceeding on the recommendation. [D-304]

**A fork that is the owner's goes in the brief**, since the brief is what they affirm — so an artifact's criteria are written against the ruling the affirmed brief carries. A fork the brief does not reach, because nothing about it could have made the brief wrong, stays open into the artifact and takes the contingent form below. [D-155]

**An amendment is a further comment, never an edit.** A recording of a settled conversation is not rewritten to reflect a later one; the later one is recorded too, naming what it changes. Which route it takes follows from who owns what: **an amendment that changes the brief is re-agreed with the owner**, because the brief is what they approve; one that changes only the reading is the session's, reported in that same comment and deliberately needing no second verdict, since a re-check per reading amendment costs more than it buys. **The latest comment governs, and every amendment obliges a pointer to it from the artifact** — the handoff sends an implementer to the artifact and the brief it carries alone, so an amendment nothing points at is invisible exactly there, and the embedded copy is the stale one they would build from. An amended **brief** obliges more: the artifact is settled again against it, because the reading was judged against a term that has since changed.

**Where the issue leaves open a question that is theirs under the test above, the artifact carries the fork rather than closing it.** Its criteria are the ones that hold whichever way they rule; the fork goes to them in the argued form above, at release or at a stop where the brief itself must change, and any criterion that turns on their answer is written against the recommendation, marked as contingent, and names what replaces it. **An artifact whose criteria presuppose an answer the issue leaves open has closed a fork silently** — the mirror of asking where no fork exists. [D-155]

- **Acceptance criteria** are the change's success definition, and its review judges against them — so what they name is what every stage of that review inherits. **Write each one as something a consumer of the result can do, or no longer does wrongly** — whoever works under the built result, most often a session, in a situation the change exists for. A criterion about the artifact *conforming* — a count, a section present, a wording faithful, a file untouched — is lawful where it argues its own consumer-facing load, which it states; written for its own sake it manufactures the bookkeeping class, and a criterion is the one thing that makes such a finding look like a defect in the work. They state what must be true of the result, not what it may cost. [D-166]
- **Each criterion names the check that would falsify it** — a criterion nobody can check is a wish. The diff settles what the diff can settle, a command settles what a command can run, and what only use shows is settled by putting the situations to a session that has none of this change's history, or by the built result in use; the criterion says which. **Naming the check does not order it run**: the criterion states how the claim could be falsified, and what a change owes in runs is not this rule's to say. [D-166]
- **A criterion a plausible wrong implementation would also pass measures nothing.** Say what failing looks like before writing it; where the artifact carries a fork, a criterion held to survive it must be falsifiable on **every** branch it survives — the cheapest cross-branch criterion is one that is empty on a branch. [D-166]
- **A criterion claiming a piece of text moves a reader — toward a destination or away from one — names which of two quantities it claims**, because the two come apart: that the reader *goes* where the text says, which is its **reach**, or that a reader's *stated reason* names this text. **Reach is falsifiable only where nothing else the reader loads routes that same situation anyway, or where what does routes it too weakly to mask the difference.** Two routes have defeated it in a run and apply in either polarity, though only the first has been measured where the text keeps a reader away: the destination's own description settling the situation — its summary sentence, or another of its clauses — and an always-on surface naming the destination. **A third is reasoned rather than measured and applies only there, where it is the ordinary case: the reader's own indifference.** A reader with no pull toward that destination declines it whether or not the text is there, and the situations that test such text at all are near misses, where the pull is weakest by construction — so reach is the quantity least likely to survive there. Least likely, not unavailable: text stripped of its exclusions has been measured opening the destination they excluded. **Warrant is falsifiable where reach is not, and it fails the same way**: where the destination's name or summary sentence lets a reader reconstruct this text's substance, they give that substance as their reason whether or not the text is there. [D-338] [D-320] [D-345]
- **The rule for figures scopes to the whole artifact, not to these criteria.** A **forecast** — any figure about a tree that does not exist yet — belongs **outside** it, in the PR body, the decision entry, or the review's report, each written after the tree exists. [D-155] A figure **measured** on a tree that exists is stated as the command that derives it and the tree it runs on, **never as the number**: the artifact freezes once settled, so a number in it is one a seat inherits rather than checks. [D-194]
- **A boundary statement** — the explicit list of what the work is *not* doing and why — records the scope agreed, so a later scope question is answerable from the artifact rather than from memory. It does not forbid the work covering more; the diff is what records what was actually done.

**Settled, the artifact is the handoff contract — which is also the test for whether it is finished.** From there the work can leave the conversation entirely, to a fresh session or another runtime, so nothing load-bearing may be left in chat: an implementer who was not there builds the agreed thing from the artifact and the brief it carries, alone. Handoff across runtimes rests on that property and nothing else.

**A premise the artifact rests on may need testing before you assert it** — that is a spike, which tests one and commits nothing.

**Where a check bears on the artifact, the artifact carries what came back rather than the fact that you looked.** It carries a spike's result, **including a spike that did not resolve**; where you considered one and did not run it, it carries the material you consulted instead — the enumeration, the query and the tree it ran on, the file you opened. [D-155]

## The two halves, and the boundary between them

The owner's attention concentrates at two moments and the stretch between them runs without them. Which stages fall where is the map; a session that cannot tell which side it stands on is the failure this exists to stop.

**Theirs, up to and including the affirmation:** whether the work is picked up at all, drafting and settling the brief in conversation, and any fork surfacing while settling it. **The affirmation is the boundary.** **The unattended stretch, from there to a change ready for merge** — which means on a pull request whose review has run and closed, every finding dispositioned, external reviewer comments reconciled, and the report posted: the artifact and the cold seat that settles it, implementation, the guards the change owes, commit and push and the pull request, the experience session the change bought or the line declining it, the review, the fixes. **Theirs again at release:** the report and the outcome delivered, and the merge. Filing sits outside all three — it puts something on the board rather than starting work. **Pinging inside the stretch spends the attention the affirmation bought.**

**Nothing substitutes for the affirmation, and a session that cannot reach the owner for an affirmation its work owes waits.** One route, not two: slow and unreachable get the same answer, because neither licenses proceeding. Work owing no brief owes no affirmation and is not held here. **A directive the owner wrote is not one either** — it is what a brief is drafted from, and the affirmation is their word on the brief that reads it back to them, which nothing else supplies. Their having asked for the work is why it is lawful to draft; it is not the agreement on what drafting produced.

**The truncation that settles an unobtainable cold check does not extend to it.** That check judges the session's own reading, which the session owns and can be accountable for leaving unjudged; the affirmation is the owner's term, which the session does not own and cannot supply for them. A truncated verdict leaves a reading nobody checked; a truncated affirmation would leave a term nobody agreed — not a degraded agreement but the absence of one.

**Waiting for a decision the owner has not made is not the stalling the charter forbids.** That rule bars refusing or stalling a decision they *have* made; a brief they have not yet seen is not one, and neither is a directive of theirs that no brief has yet read back — obeying it is what the drafting does, and the decision still owed is on the reading.

**Waiting is not idling.** Everything the affirmation would unlock except the work itself stays available: draft the brief to the point where it could lock, test the premises that could invalidate it, and post it on the work's issue **labelled a draft**, marking which sentences are the session's restatement rather than the owner's own words. That is not the locking post above, and the label is what keeps the two apart. Then hand back in the form above.

**A question arising inside the stretch takes three tests, in this order, the first match governing.**

1. **Does answering it change what the change is for?** The work **stops** — the one thing that stops it. Post what you found and wait, because that is an amendment to what they affirmed.
2. **Does the affirmed brief, or the map above, settle it?** Settled the same way, no report owed beyond the diff. The map settles every stage it assigns, which is why *do I open the pull request?* is answered here.
3. **Otherwise decide it and report it with your reason** — and where this cell makes the matter the owner's, that report is an **argued ask they rule at release**, carried where this cell already sends a reported call — the work's issue, the pull request body, the decision entry, or the review report — rather than a settled call. The work does not wait on the ruling: proceed on your recommendation.

**One restraint, on all three tests above.** Do not perform an act whose effect this branch cannot take back — whichever way you read the matter, and whether or not you think the call is your own. **An act the map or the affirmed brief already accounts for is exempt**: a stage is accounted for by being assigned, and filing by being placed outside the bands. **The exemption covers performing an accounted-for act at the cost the change warrants and reaches no further** — not the increment beyond that, a panel or a fan-out widened well past what the change warrants, and not an irreversible side-act taken inside it, which is reached whatever that act costs because a side-act is never what it needs in order to complete. So the restraint reaches three things: an act no edit at review retracts that the map does not account for; the increment by which an accounted-for act exceeds what the change warrants; and an irreversible side-act inside one. **A stage run at the cost the change warrants is performed**, and reaching for the restraint there is the misreading that halts the stretch. Where the work cannot continue without a withheld act, the affirmed brief no longer covers what the change requires, and test 1 applies.
