# Licensing a deletion from a cold-seat A/B

**Loaded when** the output of a cold-seat A/B would be the removal of the text under test — not when the run only asks what a reader does under it.

**Deletion requires a run in which no seat, in either arm, cited the text or its substance. Anything else keeps** — one citation by one seat is enough, and the text is then recorded as tested-and-kept rather than untested. The asymmetry is deliberate, and it is the cost asymmetry: three seats an arm can show that text nobody reaches for is not carrying its situation, and cannot show that text one seat leaned on is safe to remove. Keeping inert text costs characters. Deleting live text costs a session routed where the text existed to stop it — the failure the text was written to prevent, which no guard catches and which surfaces as somebody's bad change months later.

**What counts as a citation, because the rule above cannot be applied without it: a decline reasoned from the absence of a positive trigger is not one**, even where its content coincides with the excluded material's. A seat attributing the exclusion *to the text* — *"its entry explicitly excludes push-rejection recovery"* — is citing, in whichever arm it appears. Score both arms by the same reading, and say who scored: a run scored by the dispatching session is not blind to arm, and a later reader can only weigh that if it is stated.

**Everything a cold-seat A/B owes holds here first.** The planted control, the void rule, the near-miss docket and the forced verdict are not relaxed by a deletion being the point — they are what a null has to survive before it means anything. A deletion licensed by a void run is licensed by nothing.
