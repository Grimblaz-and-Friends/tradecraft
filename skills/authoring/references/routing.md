# Where content lives

**Loaded when** you are deciding which home a piece of content belongs in — a skill, an always-on surface, a decision entry, a record — or which material a rule takes instead. A session writing or revising a single document whose home is settled does not need it, but a session putting one idea in two places is siting content again.

This file is the charter principle applied: governing prose states the concept and its reason and carries at most one example, and a rule is a guard, a script, an exhibit, or unwritten. The homes below are where a concept goes; the materials after them are where a rule goes instead.

## The four homes, for a concept

- **Methodology** — how work is done, what to consider, in what order, to what standard → a skill. A piece is its own skill when it has an independent trigger: a situation where it should fire without the parent job underway. Where the trigger is not independent and a cell already serves it, it is a `references/` file inside that skill. Where no cell serves it at all, the answer is a new cell — the burden sits on cramming rather than on creating.
- **What must hold before any context loads** → the practice's always-on surface: a shipped charter where it travels to every repository that adopts the practice, the repo's own root doctrine where it does not. Both are last resorts because every session pays their reading cost.
- **Rationale** — why this shape was chosen, what was rejected → the repo's decision log, one frozen entry per decision, written in the change that lands it. Rationale informs future judgment; it never binds it.
- **What happened** → append-only exhaust: a report. Never create a record that must be maintained after its append.

When one idea needs both a shipped standard and a local application, the standard ships — in the skill that teaches it, or in the practice's own always-on surface where it must bind before any skill fires — and the repo's own files carry the application, never the reverse, and never duplicated prose that can drift.

## The four materials, for a rule

Prose binds only a reader who read it first, and a rule reaches only the case it names — so a rule written as prose is charged to every session and spent on the one that happened to remember it. Four materials, cheapest first:

**A rule leaves prose for a guard or a script's own message only where the reader who loses the sentence receives that guard or script** — otherwise the move replaces a rule with silence.

- **A guard.** What can be checked is a lint, a schema, a test. What survives in prose is the concept, plus the guard's name so a reader knows the check exists; restating the guard's reasoning is paying twice for one answer.
- **A script's own message.** What a session must not get wrong about running something is said by that thing at the moment of the mistake, not in a paragraph read two days earlier.
- **An exhibit.** The incident a rule came out of is a decision entry, cited from the concept it explains. The argument lives there, where a reviser is already told to look; what merely happened is exhaust, and takes the record above.
- **Unwritten.** What is left is the reader's judgment, and a sentence that makes that judgment for them is the sentence to cut.

**A guard or a script is built only for a concept prose already states in one sentence.** Otherwise it enforces something nobody agreed.

When prose moves, move its reason too, because a rule without its reason cannot be applied beyond its literal example. Delete sentences that state no concept or merely repeat an example; keeping them makes the governing text harder to find and use.
