# Where content lives

**Loaded when** you are deciding which home a piece of content belongs in — a skill, an always-on surface, a decision entry, a record — or which material a rule takes instead, **or editing an always-on surface at all**, because that is when the outflow below is owed. A session writing or revising a single document whose home is settled does not need it — but a session putting one idea in two places is siting content again, whatever it thinks it is doing, and does.

This file is the charter principle applied: governing prose states the concept and its reason and carries at most one example, and a rule is a guard, a script, an exhibit, or unwritten. The homes below are where a concept goes; the materials after them are where a rule goes instead.

## The four homes, for a concept

- **Methodology** — how work is done, what to consider, in what order, to what standard → a skill. A piece is its own skill when it has an independent trigger: a situation where it should fire without the parent job underway. Where the trigger is not independent and a cell already serves it, it is a `references/` file inside that skill. Where no cell serves it at all, the answer is a new cell — the burden sits on cramming rather than on creating.
- **What must hold before any context loads** → the practice's always-on surface: a shipped charter where it travels to every repository that adopts the practice, the repo's own root doctrine where it does not. Budget at least one of them, because a budget is what forces the routing decision to be real; both are the last resort, after a guard and after skill prose.
- **Rationale** — why this shape was chosen, what was rejected → the repo's decision log, one frozen entry per decision, written in the change that lands it. Rationale informs future judgment; it never binds it.
- **What happened** → append-only exhaust: a review report, an index row. Never create a record that must be maintained after its append.

When one idea needs both a shipped standard and a local application, the standard ships — in the skill that teaches it, or in the practice's own always-on surface where it must bind before any skill fires — and the repo's own files carry the application, never the reverse, and never duplicated prose that can drift.

## The four materials, for a rule

Prose binds only a reader who read it first, and a rule reaches only the case it names — so a rule written as prose is charged to every session and spent on the one that happened to remember it. Four materials, cheapest first:

- **A guard.** What can be checked is a lint, a schema, a test. What survives in prose is the concept, plus the guard's name so a reader knows the check exists; restating the guard's reasoning is paying twice for one answer.
- **A script's own message.** What a session must not get wrong about running something is said by that thing at the moment of the mistake, not in a paragraph read two days earlier.
- **An exhibit.** The incident a rule came out of is a decision entry, cited from the concept it explains. The argument lives there, where a reviser is already told to look; what merely happened is exhaust, and takes the record above.
- **Unwritten.** What is left is the reader's judgment, and a sentence that makes that judgment for them is the sentence to cut.

**A guard or a script is built only for a concept prose already states in one sentence.** Otherwise it enforces something nobody agreed: whether a cell body ought to be any particular size is still an open question [#328], and the merge job built on the ceiling anyway filed one pool item per cell over it — five across two runs twenty minutes apart — until the owner retired it [D-544].

## Outflow: the same order, read backwards

**Whenever an always-on surface is edited, look for what now has a cheaper home** — every edit, not once a budget is reached, and never sized to what is being added: a session that reads it as a matched cost either refuses a lawful addition or forces a bad move to pay for one. The ceiling is what applies size pressure; the outflow is what makes the look happen, and a surface that only ever empties under pressure empties once and refills.

Four moves, cheapest first, each a rule leaving for a home that already holds it: to a guard plus the guard's name, to the script's own message at the moment of the mistake, to the cell where it binds and nowhere else — `cell-structure.md` carries the further test that destination must pass — or to a decision entry's citation. **A rule leaves whole or not at all**: one that moves without its reason strands the clause a later reader needs in order to know when it stops applying, and afterwards it reads complete, so nothing finds it.

**Deleting is lawful where the sentence states no concept and no move above is open to its rule** — reached after the four, never before them — and the change that removes it names which it was, where amendments are recorded. A second example of a concept already illustrated is such a sentence: prose carries one. This reverses the bar that stood here, which kept every sentence absent measured evidence that it did not bind [D-261] and so kept armour, restatement and lore beside the rules that had earned their place.

**The obligation is discharged by a move, a deletion, or a refusal, and a refusal is written where amendments are recorded** — the decision entry, or the PR body where no entry is warranted. Where nothing has a cheaper home, say so and say why. Mechanical work owes nothing at all.

**Measure the whole surface, not the file you are editing** — a shipped charter, an adopting repository's own doctrine, and every cell's name and description, which load whether or not the cell fires. Routing a rule from one of those into another is not an outflow; it is the same cost under a different heading, and it reads as a reduction to anyone measuring one file. **What the write-up then says about that measurement is under the figure rule**: `frozen-documents.md`, in this cell.
