# What a cell carries

**Loaded when** you are creating a cell, restructuring one, writing or revising a description, or writing prose that names or relies on another cell.

- **The description is the triggering surface a cell owns, and it is always loaded.** State the triggers explicitly and in the third person, and state non-triggers where a sibling could be mistaken for it, because a body is not available until the description has already routed the reader. Keep sibling descriptions mutually exclusive so competing summaries do not hide the right cell.
- **A cell is a focused `SKILL.md` plus optional depth.** What every firing needs stays in the body; what only one trigger needs moves to `references/`, because unrelated prose makes every firing pay for a branch it will not use. A depth file opens with its load condition rather than another purpose header, since the cell's header governs it.
- **A cell with depth carries an index in its body.** Give one entry per `references/` file: the trigger that opens it, the path, and a one-line précis. A reader deciding whether to open a file has not read it yet, so write the entry for that decision. Inline pointers remain useful, but they do not replace the index because a buried pointer is discoverable only after the reader reaches it.
- **A shared standard has exactly one owner.** Where one cell needs a rule another owns, point at that cell rather than copying its prose, because copies drift. The reserved form is `` `<name>` cell `` on one line; use an ordinary unbackticked name for every other sense. Pointers may not form a cycle because cells in a cycle must move together. A shipped cell may not point at a repository-only cell because an adopter receives the former without the latter. A standard moves only to a cell whose trigger still reaches every reader who needed it; finding the same words at the destination does not establish that route.
