# What a cell carries

**Loaded when** you are creating a cell, restructuring one, writing or revising a description, or writing prose that names or relies on another cell.

- **The description is the triggering surface a cell owns, and it is always loaded.** A cell's name and description sit in every session whether or not the cell fires; the body costs nothing until it does. So the description states its triggers explicitly and in the third person, states its non-triggers wherever a sibling could be mistaken for it, and stays mutually exclusive with its siblings' — the failure mode is under-triggering, and siblings compete for attention that thins as the always-on set grows ([IFScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538); and on what attached tooling costs, [MCPGauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12566)). **Where your practice's always-on surface declares a roster, that roster routes as well** — the names of the cells your consumers receive, so a session that does not know which cell owns the rule it needs sees the whole set and cannot mistake a partial list for it; a cell they never receive is routed by whatever surface they do read. **A roster that excludes any cell says which**, or its completeness is the one thing it claims and gets wrong. **It names them and does not say when to load them**: a roster line stating a load condition is a second always-on copy of a description your consumers already hold, which is the copy the next bullet refuses, and it drifts the way any second copy does ([this practice's own did, four hours after it landed](https://github.com/Grimblaz-and-Friends/tradecraft/issues/503)). **A roster line is always-on in every runtime**, so a cell added at a full surface pays for its name as well as its description. A guard checking a description is present, parseable and inside a ceiling is the floor beneath this standard, not the standard — your practice sets that ceiling and states it where the guard lives, because a ceiling a writer cannot read is one they size by imitation.
- **A cell is built as a small `SKILL.md` plus its depth, and that is the shape rather than a move available under pressure.** What every firing needs stays in `SKILL.md`; what one trigger among several needs sits in `references/`. The test is a disqualifier, not a size: a cell is too big when a session loads prose it had no use for, which a small cell can do and a large one need not. **So a body carrying one-trigger prose is unfinished, whatever it measures** — writing such a rule into the body and leaving it there is the defect, not the size that eventually reveals it. A `references/` file opens with its own load condition rather than a purpose header; it is depth inside the cell, and the cell's header governs it.
- **A cell that has depth carries an index of it, and the index is the authority on what depth exists.** One entry per `references/` file, in the body: the trigger that opens it, the file, and a one-line précis of what is there. A reader deciding whether to open a file has not read it yet, so the entry is written for that decision rather than lifted from the file's own load condition. Inline pointers stay lawful wherever a paragraph wants one; what they stop being is the record — a depth file reachable only from a sentence buried mid-body is reachable only by a reader who happens to be at that sentence. Your practice states where the completeness check lives, as it does for the circle check above; nothing here ships you either.
- **Meeting a ceiling is a reading, never a reason to cut.** A ceiling on a cell's body is a reminder to apply the rule above — shed the one-trigger prose — and where that is not what is wanted, the answer is to report the measurement rather than to trim, to stall, or to leave the surface unedited for fear of it. **Whether that growth becomes work is a judgment somebody makes on reading it**, which is why the reading goes where your practice's readings are read rather than into a work item of its own: the population a ceiling measures is your cells, and cells keep growing, so a mechanism that files on the measurement fills a tracker with one standing item per cell and each says what one line of a reading would have said. **Nothing about a ceiling is a reason to decline to add text.** A ceiling that refuses an addition teaches avoidance, and avoidance is what leaves the one-trigger prose where it is.
- **One ceiling in this practice does not work that way, and a writer meets it without warning otherwise.** A cell body normally costs nothing until its cell fires, so shedding depth relieves it. **The `charter` cell's body is different: it is a member of every always-on row**, charged to every session in every adopting repository whether the cell fires or not. It therefore still meets a ceiling that fails the lint and names removal — and **the move this section teaches is refused there outright**: the charter cell may hold nothing but its `SKILL.md`, because a rule routed into its own `references/` would be available to a reader and not binding on one, the adoption instruction loading only that file completely. Shedding would relieve the row arithmetically, by exactly what moved; what stops it is that the depth would not bind. So what answers a full charter is the admission order's: route the rule to a cell, compress a reason to its citation, or admit it deliberately.
- **Cells are self-contained, a shared standard has exactly one owner, and a cell that needs it points at the owner.** A skill carries whatever mix of prose, scripts and tests its job needs. **Where its job needs a rule another cell owns, it points at that cell rather than carrying a copy**, holding none of its text. Two half-owners of one sentence kept in agreement by hand is never lawful: the instruction to keep copies in step is the mechanism by which they drift. **The bound is that pointers may not run in a circle** — cells in a ring must move together from then on, which is the coupling this rule exists to prevent. The reserved form makes that checkable, and your practice states where its check lives; nothing here ships you one. **A pointer at your practice's always-on surface costs nothing and closes nothing**: it is loaded in every session already, so following it loads no prose and it can be no hop of a circle — the `charter` cell is that surface here. **One direction stays refused**, where a practice keeps cells its consumers never receive: such a cell may point at one that ships, and never the reverse, because a consumer holds the shipped cell and not the other. Where every cell ships, that refusal is empty. A reference is written as the cell's name, in the reserved form `` `<name>` cell `` on one line — the `charter` cell — because the name is the part that survives relocation where a path does not, though a runtime may qualify it on the way in. Reserved means what it says: write any other sense of the word unbackticked, or a guard reading the form will answer for it. **Naming a sibling to exclude it is not naming it as a pointer**, and the way to keep it from becoming one is to state that job rather than the cell that holds it: a guard reads the form, not the sentence around it, so a backticked name inside an exclusion is an edge like any other. Where two cells both need one standard it gets one owning cell and the other points at it. The always-on surface stays the last resort, for a rule that must bind before any cell loads rather than for one two cells share. **A standard moves only to a cell that reaches the reader it is taken from.** The mover names the route that reader now takes — the destination's own load condition covering their situation, or an always-on surface naming the destination — and checks every hop of it by reading what each hop states. **Finding the words in the destination is not that check**: a search for the source's own phrasing answers a question about text where the question is about the rule. Where no route holds, what widens is the cheapest surface that closes it — a load condition, then a pointer, then a description; returning the rule to its source restores the second copy.
