# What a cell carries

**Loaded when** you are creating a cell, restructuring one, writing or revising a description, or writing prose that names or relies on another cell.

- **The description is the triggering surface a cell owns, and it is always loaded.** A cell's name and description sit in every session whether or not the cell fires; the body costs nothing until it does. So the description states its triggers explicitly and in the third person, states its non-triggers wherever a sibling could be mistaken for it, and stays mutually exclusive with its siblings' — the failure mode is under-triggering, and siblings compete for attention that thins as the always-on set grows ([IFScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538); and on what attached tooling costs, [MCPGauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12566)). **Where your practice's always-on surface declares a roster, that roster routes as well** — one line per cell your consumers receive, saying when to load it; a cell they never receive is routed by whatever surface they do read. That line is a load condition and never a second description: the description stays the authority on what the cell triggers on, and a roster line restating it is the second copy the next bullet refuses. What the roster buys is a session that does not know which cell owns the rule it needs, and so cannot recognise the description that would have routed it. A guard checking a description is present, parseable and inside a ceiling is the floor beneath this standard, not the standard — your practice sets that ceiling and states it where the guard lives, because a ceiling a writer cannot read is one they size by imitation.
- **Depth leaves the body when only some firings need it.** What every firing needs stays in `SKILL.md`; what one trigger among several needs sits in `references/`, behind a pointer carrying a one-line précis of what is there and when to load it. The test is a disqualifier, not a size: a cell is too big when a session loads prose it had no use for, which a small cell can do and a large one need not. A `references/` file opens with its own load condition rather than a purpose header; it is depth inside the cell, and the cell's header governs it.
- **Cells are self-contained, a shared standard has exactly one owner, and a cell that needs it points at the owner.** A skill carries whatever mix of prose, scripts and tests its job needs. **Where its job needs a rule another cell owns, it points at that cell rather than carrying a copy**, holding none of its text. Two half-owners of one sentence kept in agreement by hand is never lawful: the instruction to keep copies in step is the mechanism by which they drift. **The bound is that pointers may not run in a circle** — cells in a ring must move together from then on, which is the coupling this rule exists to prevent. The reserved form makes that checkable, and your practice states where its check lives; nothing here ships you one. **A pointer at your practice's always-on surface costs nothing and closes nothing**: it is loaded in every session already, so following it loads no prose and it can be no hop of a circle — the `charter` cell is that surface here. **One direction stays refused**, where a practice keeps cells its consumers never receive: such a cell may point at one that ships, and never the reverse, because a consumer holds the shipped cell and not the other. Where every cell ships, that refusal is empty. A reference is written as the cell's name, in the reserved form `` `<name>` cell `` on one line — the `charter` cell — because the name is the part that survives relocation where a path does not, though a runtime may qualify it on the way in. Reserved means what it says: write any other sense of the word unbackticked, or a guard reading the form will answer for it. **Naming a sibling to exclude it is not naming it as a pointer**, and the way to keep it from becoming one is to state that job rather than the cell that holds it: a guard reads the form, not the sentence around it, so a backticked name inside an exclusion is an edge like any other. Where two cells both need one standard it gets one owning cell and the other points at it. The always-on surface stays the last resort, for a rule that must bind before any cell loads rather than for one two cells share. **A standard moves only to a cell that reaches the reader it is taken from.** The mover names the route that reader now takes — the destination's own load condition covering their situation, or an always-on surface naming the destination — and checks every hop of it by reading what each hop states. **Finding the words in the destination is not that check**: a search for the source's own phrasing answers a question about text where the question is about the rule. Where no route holds, what widens is the cheapest surface that closes it — a load condition, then a pointer, then a description; returning the rule to its source restores the second copy.
